Executive Pulse453 words

#Macro signal

Risk-on close to July, oil the outlier: US indexes finished the last session of July markedly higher, with the S&P 500 up 0.7% and the Dow booking a fourth straight winning month, as investors looked past rising bond yields on the strength of megacap earnings. The dissonant note is crude, with WTI up nearly 4% on the day to roughly $87. For leaders, the signal is a market comfortable paying up for AI and consumer strength while energy costs quietly reintroduce input-cost risk. Plan for a second half where demand looks fine but margins face a fuel and rates squeeze.

#Tech industry

  • Enterprise AI's "platform moment": Industry analysis this week frames a shift from scattered AI tools to governed "systems of intelligence" tied directly to business outcomes. Strategic implication: the buying decision is moving up from individual tool procurement to platform and governance choices made at the executive level. The window for letting teams adopt AI point solutions bottom-up is closing, because the cleanup cost is now visible on the balance sheet.

#AI at the leadership level

The governance bill for two years of bottom-up AI adoption is coming due. Fresh survey data has 67% of executives believing their company has already suffered a data leak from an employee using an unapproved AI tool, 55% describing internal AI use as a "chaotic free-for-all," and 79% saying AI apps are being built in silos. Meanwhile only 29% see significant ROI from generative AI and 23% from AI agents. The pattern is a direct governance failure, not a technology one. Sprawl without a control layer produces both the security exposure and the weak returns. Expect the Chief Data and AI Officer mandate to harden from evangelism to control, and expect boards to start asking who owns the AI risk register.

#Leadership & org design

IRIS Software Group appointed its first Chief Data and AI Officer this week to lead global data strategy, a responsible-AI framework, and enterprise governance under a single role. The structural move worth noting is the consolidation of data strategy and AI governance into one accountable seat rather than splitting them across a CDO and a separate AI function. For org-builders, the lesson is that a fragmented AI mandate is what produced the silo-and-leak problem above. One accountable owner with authority over both the data layer and the AI policy is the emerging fix.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If an auditor asked us today to list every AI tool touching customer or proprietary data, who owns each one, and what data it can reach, could we answer in an hour? If not, that gap is our real AI strategy right now.

Practitioner Pulse432 words

#Try this now

  • Codex Remote, now GA on ChatGPT plans: OpenAI moved Codex Remote and workspace agents to general availability this week, with new admin controls over how agents run inside Business, Enterprise, and Edu. The practical unlock: you can hand an agent a scoped task, let it run remotely against a repo or workspace, and review the output as a diff rather than babysitting a chat. Worth 20 minutes to test on one real backlog task and see where it lands versus where you still need to intervene.
  • Vertesia Studio Assistant: An agentic assistant embedded inside the builder that writes prompts, tests the same task across different models, learns from execution, then refines and publishes the agent. This is the emerging shape of prompt work: you stop hand-tuning a single prompt and instead let the tool run the model bake-off for you. Even if you never adopt Vertesia, the pattern is the point. Treat prompts as tested, versioned assets, not one-off strings.
  • BridgeApp orchestration layer: Launched July 27, it connects people, agents, tasks, and context in one workspace and is built to move a work item from to-do to finished pull request without tool switching. The idea worth stealing regardless of vendor: the friction in agent workflows is now the handoffs between tools, not the model. Map where your team loses context between systems before you buy anything.

#Case study worth stealing

Akeneo, Agentic Ziggy inside the Product Cloud: Akeneo shipped an agentic orchestration layer that coordinates specialist agents for data modeling, schema mapping, enrichment, and continuous quality checks on product data. Rather than one general agent trying to do everything, they assigned narrow agents to narrow jobs and put a coordinator on top. Any team drowning in product or catalog data can adapt the structure. Decompose the messy job into specialist steps, give each step a checkable output, and orchestrate rather than automate end to end. The quality-check agent running continuously is the detail most teams skip.

#Framework or mental model

Orchestration over autonomy: The through-line across this week's releases (Vertesia's Process Engine, BridgeApp, Akeneo) is a deliberate middle ground between fully autonomous agents and rigid deterministic automation. Autonomous agents are hard to trust and hard to debug. Rigid automation cannot handle variance. Orchestration defines the process as a diagram or spec, then lets agents fill the steps under supervision. For digital leaders, the takeaway is to stop asking "can an agent do this whole job" and start asking "what is the smallest process I can define and hand to supervised agents this quarter."

AI Stack Daily547 words

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Everseen acquires the Viztel portfolio from RadiusAI: Extends Everseen's Vision AI platform from the checkout lane to the entire store floor. Signal: retail computer-vision is consolidating from point solutions (loss prevention at the till) into full-store sensing platforms. The buyers want one vendor covering the whole physical funnel, not a stitched-together set of cameras.
  • Edge226 completes acquisition of AnyClip: Merges video content processing with performance advertising. Signal: the video and performance-ad stacks are collapsing into each other. As creative becomes machine-generated and machine-optimized, owning both the content pipeline and the buying engine is becoming one capability rather than two vendors.
  • ActiveCampaign ships a Google Ads connector for Active Intelligence: Marketers can now push ActiveCampaign data into Google Ads and launch Performance Max campaigns from inside ActiveCampaign. Signal: the mid-market automation platforms are racing to become the single console for owned and paid, not just email. The CRM is trying to eat the ad-buying seat.

#Category watch

Agentic commerce infrastructure: Algolia enhanced its Agent Studio for building and optimizing onsite agentic commerce experiences the same week Akeneo shipped an agentic layer over product data. The category forming here is the plumbing beneath AI shopping agents: search, product data quality, and agent orchestration as a connected stack. The players are the search and PIM vendors repositioning fast, and the bet is that when shopping shifts from browsing to delegated agents, whoever owns clean product data and the retrieval layer owns the transaction.

#Stack intelligence

The consolidation pattern this week is vertical, not horizontal. Vendors are not buying competitors for scale, they are buying up and down their own value chain (checkout to full store, content to ad buying, CRM to paid media). For anyone building a martech org, the practical read is that the "best of breed versus suite" question is reopening. The suites are re-absorbing the adjacent point solutions, and the integration tax you pay for best-of-breed is rising as each vendor tries to own more of the flow.

#Foundations concept of the day

Product Information Management (PIM): A PIM is the system of record for everything you know about a product, its attributes, descriptions, images, specs, pricing rules, and channel-specific variants, kept clean and consistent so it can flow to every place the product appears. It matters at scale because a retailer with a hundred thousand SKUs across a website, marketplaces, and retail-media placements cannot manually keep each listing correct. Real example: a consumer-electronics brand selling on its own site plus Amazon plus a retailer's app uses a PIM so a single spec change (say a revised battery rating) propagates everywhere at once. This is why the agentic-commerce vendors are converging on product data. AI shopping agents are only as trustworthy as the PIM feeding them.

#Watch list

  • CRM platforms moving into ad buying: ActiveCampaign's Performance Max integration is a small move that signals a large one. Watch whether the larger CRM and marketing clouds follow, because owning the paid-media seat from inside the CRM changes the buying center for ad budgets.
  • Retail vision platforms: With Everseen consolidating full-store sensing, watch which grocery and big-box retailers standardize on a single vision vendor. That decision sets the data layer for in-store retail media next.

Markets Signal384 words

#Macro pulse

  • Canada: The S&P/TSX Composite closed 35,226.14, down 279.70 (-0.79%), dragged by weak mining shares as gold fell and by a sharp Telus drop after the telecom cut its full-year outlook. USD/CAD near flat at 1.4013, keeping the loonie soft around the 1.40 handle, which supports Canadian carrier costs on domestic spend but raises the bill on USD-denominated fuel and aircraft.
  • USA: Indexes closed the last session of July higher. S&P 500 +0.7% to 7,489.72, Nasdaq +1% to 25,373.85, Dow +276.97 (+0.53%) to 52,485.03, with the Dow posting a fourth straight winning month. Amazon surged on earnings, and the tape looked past rising bond yields.
  • Asia: Nikkei 225 jumped 4.03% to 64,362.02, a standout move, with Shanghai Composite +0.72% and Hang Seng roughly flat at +0.10%.
  • Oil: WTI crude spiked to about $86.80, up 3.84% on the day. This clears the 2% flag and matters directly to airline margins. A move of this size on jet fuel input costs offsets a meaningful slice of a strong demand quarter.

#Today's industry move

Two vertical-integration deals closed in the martech and vision stack. Everseen acquired the Viztel portfolio from RadiusAI to extend retail computer vision from the checkout lane to the full store, and Edge226 completed its acquisition of AnyClip, merging video content processing with performance advertising. Both are consolidation up the value chain rather than for scale.

#AI infrastructure watch

The nuclear-for-AI thread continues to firm up. Nvidia and Valar Atomics are progressing a nearly waterless, roughly 30-megawatt AI data center in Utah powered by a small modular reactor, after demonstrating Blackwell chips running on the microreactor live. The constraint shaping AI two years out is not chips, it is power and water, and the hyperscalers are now solving for both at the site level.

#Signal or noise?

The oil spike is the signal to watch. One day is a blip, but a nearly 4% move into a quarter where airlines are otherwise seeing strong demand is the kind of input-cost pressure that compounds if it holds.

#Watch tomorrow

Whether crude holds above $85 into the new week. If it does, it reframes the airline margin story from demand-led optimism to a cost-side squeeze, and it will show up in carrier and travel-name sentiment first.
