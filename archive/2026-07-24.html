Executive Pulse497 words

#Macro signal

A new tariff regime lands under a new legal theory: The administration is imposing 10% to 12.5% duties on 60 trading partners, backfilling a blanket 10% tariff that Congress let expire this week. The affected countries account for 99.4% of US imports, and separate threats target generic pharmaceuticals at up to 200% by 2029. The strategic tell is not the rate, it is the mechanism. These are enacted under Section 301 of the 1974 Trade Act rather than the emergency authority that courts already struck down, signaling that tariff exposure is now a durable, structurally-entrenched cost of doing business, not a negotiating pose that fades with the next headline. For tech, product, and marketing leaders, this means input-cost and supply-chain assumptions in every 2027 plan should be re-baselined upward now, and pricing models rebuilt to absorb persistent duty drag rather than treating it as a one-time shock.

#Tech industry

  • AI is now the stated reason for structural headcount cuts, not a background efficiency story: Block is cutting roughly 40% of its workforce with leadership explicitly crediting AI-plus-flatter-teams, while Monday.com cut 20% and SAP reorganized product and engineering leadership around its autonomous AI suite. Strategic implication: the market is beginning to reward AI-driven margin structurally, and boards will increasingly ask why headcount is not falling even as AI spend rises. The narrative has flipped from "AI augments the team" to "AI resizes the team," and that reframes every operating-plan conversation.

#AI at the leadership level

The Chief AI Officer went from novelty to norm in twelve months. An IBM study of 2,000 CEOs found 76% now have a CAIO, up from 26% a year ago. But the governance underneath is hollow: only 12% describe their AI governance as mature, and the single most common cause of failed AI projects is undefined responsibility between CIO, CDO, and business units. The forcing function arrives this month. The EU AI Act's high-risk obligations become mandatory in August 2026, and board members carry personal liability. The lesson for leadership is that appointing an AI owner is not the same as assigning accountability, and regulators are about to make that distinction expensive.

#Leadership & org design

The bottleneck to AI value is cultural, not technical. In a 2026 leadership survey, 93.2% named cultural challenges, not model limitations, as the principal barrier to adoption, and a Mercer study found 98% of executives planning organizational redesign inside two years with 99% expecting at least some AI-driven workforce reduction. The practical read: the highest-leverage AI work right now is redesigning workflows and decision rights, not procuring better models. Leaders who treat this as a technology purchase will underperform leaders who treat it as an operating-model redesign.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If AI genuinely delivered on our roadmap, what would our org chart and headcount look like in eighteen months, and what are we doing today to design toward that answer rather than defend against it?

#Try this now

  • Claude Cowork scheduled tasks (remote sessions): Cowork now runs sessions on Anthropic's servers, so scheduled jobs fire even with your laptop closed. In the left sidebar hit "Scheduled," then "New task," and "Create with Claude." Set a recurring 6am job that reads a client's email threads, transcripts, and recent news, builds a briefing doc, and leaves the follow-up email drafted but unsent. When it needs approval it pings you and resumes after you reply. Twenty minutes to wire up one recurring prep task that saves a standing hour every week. Rolling out on Max first, then other paid tiers.
  • Build a Skill instead of a mega-prompt: The Skills pattern loads a ~60-token header at session start and only pulls the full ~2,500-token body when the skill actually fires, roughly 98 percent of the cost deferred until use. Five registered skills cost ~300 tokens at startup versus ~12,500 for the same content pasted into a system prompt. Practical move: take your most-repeated prompt (a brief template, a QA checklist, a positioning format) and package it as a named skill so it fires on demand instead of bloating every conversation and degrading quality.
  • Split urgent vs. batch AI work with a cheaper processing tier: OpenAI Flex Processing routes non-urgent tasks to a lower-cost tier. Separate real-time customer-facing calls from overnight batch work (content tagging, transcript summaries, catalog enrichment) and send the batch jobs to Flex. Same output, materially lower spend on work no one is waiting on. One afternoon to audit which of your AI jobs are actually latency-sensitive.

#Case study worth stealing

A San Francisco retailer, context-aware image personalization: The team swapped hero and product imagery based on the visitor's location and device type rather than serving one static creative to everyone, and logged a 43 percent lift in mobile conversion. The approach is cheap to copy because it does not need a full personalization engine: start with two or three device-and-geo segments, swap the single highest-traffic image per segment, and measure against a holdout. Broader benchmarks put behavior-based personalization at 5 to 15 percent revenue lift, so the outsized mobile number here came from matching creative to the exact moment of the visit, not from more data.

#Framework or mental model

Compaction vs. Clearing vs. Memory: Three distinct moves for managing what an AI agent "sees," because accuracy drops as the context window grows even when all the right information is present (context rot, measured at 13.9 to 85 percent degradation). Compaction summarizes the whole window when it nears the token limit. Clearing drops stale, re-fetchable data from inside the window. Memory pushes information out of the window into external storage so it survives across sessions. Digital leaders running any multi-step agent or long research workflow should decide per-task which lever applies: summarize a bloated thread, drop a giant pasted doc you can re-pull, or write durable facts to a memory file rather than re-feeding them every run.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Sprinklr & 6sense: Both shipped MCP (Model Context Protocol) servers this month. Sprinklr's Summer '26 release added a first-party connector that lets Copilot, ChatGPT, and Claude query its customer intelligence directly. 6sense's MCP server is in open beta, GA in August, exposing proprietary GTM data to any AI agent. Signal: MCP is fast becoming the standard integration surface for martech. The API era rewarded whoever owned the connectors. The agent era rewards whoever makes their data callable by someone else's agent. Platforms are racing to be a queryable source rather than a destination UI.
  • Broadsign: Ran what it calls the first fully agentic out-of-home ad campaign, with a buy-side agent (Draft Digital) transacting against Broadsign's sell side. Media planning and buying that required human coordination at every handoff collapsed into agent-to-agent protocols. Signal: The agentic buying pattern is jumping from search and social into channels long considered manual. OOH going agent-to-agent means the workflow compression is structural, not channel-specific.
  • Edge226 / AnyClip: Edge226 closed its acquisition of AnyClip, merging video content processing with performance advertising. ML models analyze video frames and auto-generate contextual ad tags for multichannel campaigns. Signal: Contextual targeting keeps absorbing AI to compete with identity-based buying. As signal loss persists, understanding the content becomes the durable substitute for tracking the user.

#Category watch

Agentic commerce protocols: The plumbing under AI shopping is splitting into three camps. Stripe and OpenAI's Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP) powers ChatGPT checkout. Google's Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP), launched at NRF, spans discovery to post-purchase. Anthropic's MCP, now donated to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation, is the data connectivity layer. This week Shoppable's universal checkout plugin went live inside ChatGPT, putting multiretailer cart-building in front of 900M weekly users. For anyone building a martech org, the strategic question is no longer only "which retail media network do we buy," it is "which agent protocols does our commerce and data stack need to speak so an AI intermediary can find, price, and transact our products without a human touching the funnel." Getting stranded on the wrong protocol is the new integration risk.

#Stack intelligence

The integration layer is being rebuilt around agents, not dashboards. For a decade the pattern was point-to-point connectors feeding a warehouse and a UI a human logged into. The emerging pattern is: platform exposes an MCP or protocol endpoint, and orchestration happens agent-to-agent above it. Practical consequence for stack builders: evaluate every incumbent vendor on whether it has a first-party agent-callable interface, not just a REST API and a pretty console. A tool that can only be operated by a person clicking through it is becoming a liability in a stack where the operator is increasingly a model. Buy-vs-build shifts too. You no longer build the connector, you build the orchestration policy and the guardrails that decide what your agents are allowed to do.

#Foundations concept of the day

Data clean rooms: A clean room is a secure, neutral environment where two parties match and analyze their data without either side seeing the other's raw records. A retailer has purchase histories tied to loyalty IDs. A brand or a media platform has ad exposure data. They match on a common key inside the room, learn that exposed shoppers bought 12% more, and both walk away with the aggregate insight while the individual-level data never leaves its owner. This matters at scale because it is how measurement survives the collapse of third-party cookies and cross-site tracking. It is the privacy-safe bridge between first-party data islands. Real example: retail media networks are the main driver of adoption right now, yet fewer than half of US networks currently offer clean room capability, which is exactly why the ones that do can charge for closed-loop proof advertisers increasingly demand. A clean room is what lets a walled garden prove ROI without opening the wall.

#Watch list

  • The protocol standards war (ACP vs UCP vs MCP): Not a news event, a slow-moving tectonic shift. Whichever protocols reach critical merchant and agent mass will decide how discovery and checkout route for the next decade. Watch which retailers commit to which camp, and whether the camps converge or fragment commerce into agent silos.
  • IAB Tech Lab CTV Ad Portfolio Guidance: Finalized July 22 after public comment. Standardization signal for connected TV inventory description and signaling. Worth tracking because clean CTV standards are the precondition for programmatic CTV to trade with the fluidity of open-web display.

Markets Signal410 words

#Macro pulse

Risk-off session driven by two forces: a Middle East oil shock and AI capex sticker shock. Canada: S&P/TSX Composite slipped 0.82% to 35,193, an energy rally failing to offset weakness in mining, tech, and financials after a fresh all-time high the day prior. USD/CAD held near 1.408, essentially flat. USA: the selloff was tech-led. Nasdaq dropped 2.15% to 25,138, S&P 500 off 1.21% to 7,408, Dow down 0.97% to 51,712. Alphabet fell 7% and Tesla lost roughly 14% on earnings. Asia extended the pain overnight into July 24: Nikkei down about 2.5%, Hang Seng off 1% to ~24,893, Shanghai down 1.2% to ~3,830, and Korea's KOSPI fell hard enough to trigger sidecar curbs. Oil: WTI surged roughly 6% to the low-90s per barrel on renewed Strait of Hormuz tensions, well past the flag threshold and now up about a fifth on the month.

#Today's industry move

Alphabet posted a blowout quarter (revenue $119.8B, Cloud up 82% to $24.8B) and the stock still fell 7%, its worst day in over a year. The reason: 2026 capex guidance lifted to $195B-$205B, with a warning that 2027 spending rises "significantly." The market is no longer rewarding AI revenue growth on its own. It now wants to see the return on the buildout. For any digital leader, that is the shape of the coming budget conversation: spend is assumed, ROI is the question.

#AI infrastructure watch

The capex reset is a double signal. Alphabet's $200B midpoint feeds directly into the chip and power stack, and analysts framed the hyperscaler spree (roughly $630B-$725B across the Big Five in 2026) as a cushion under NVIDIA and AMD rather than a threat. NVIDIA still captures well over half of every AI hardware dollar, AMD around 7-9%. The named structural risk is not a spending slowdown but hyperscaler custom silicon eroding that grip over time. Energy and cooling remain the quiet gating constraint behind every one of these capex lines.

#Signal or noise?

Part of a larger trend: the oil spike is a live geopolitical risk, but the durable shift is investors demanding proof of AI return on investment, which will reprice both the infrastructure and application layers through year-end.

#Watch tomorrow

Whether the Middle East premium in crude holds or fades, because a sustained WTI above $90 flows straight into jet fuel crack spreads (Delta and United already guiding fuel costs $4B-$6B higher for 2026) and reshapes travel margins fast.
