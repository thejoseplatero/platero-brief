Executive Pulse757 words

#Macro signal

Two pieces of national-scale infrastructure failed in the same 48 hours, and neither operator could name a root cause while it was happening. At 3:36 p.m. ET Friday, the FAA's en route and terminal radar and communications became unstable with 3,500 planes in the air. It ordered only the third nationwide ground stop in 25 years and lifted it around 8:15 p.m. More than 5,700 flights were cancelled by Friday night and 980 were already cancelled for Saturday. Officials said there was no sign of a cyberattack, but no cause has been published. The day before, Microsoft's Exchange Online was down more than 21 hours across 19 Microsoft 365 services, including Copilot's access to work data, and hit government offices in Japan, Australia, Singapore and Portugal. Recovery then caused a retry storm, with devices opening up to 70 percent more connections. Strategic point: the risk is concentration more than any single vendor. When one shared system fails, every organization that depends on it fails together, and there is no competitor to switch to that afternoon. For tech, product and marketing leaders, the question is which customer journeys stop when one upstream system stops, and whether customers have a degraded path when that happens. Airlines found out on Friday evening. (Skift, PPC Land)

#Tech industry

  • Nvidia fell again Friday and dragged the Nasdaq down 0.9 percent, with earnings now due Tuesday under an interim CEO: Technology was the biggest weight on Friday's market, a day after Jensen Huang's resignation, though the S&P 500 still posted its first weekly gain since early October. Nvidia reports on 17 November with CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO and Harvey C. Jones as its first independent chair. Strategic implication: Tuesday's numbers are about demand, but the questions on the call will be about succession and who sets direction. That is a preview of what any company faces when its strategy lives in one person. (La Nación/AP)
  • Europe's privacy regulators closed the main legal loophole for cross-site ad tracking: The European Data Protection Board adopted guidelines on 10 November saying cross-site tracking will rarely pass the "legitimate interest" test, so it now effectively needs consent. Separately, an EU Advocate General advised that the court was wrong to scrap Google's 1.49 billion euro AdSense fine. Strategic implication: In Europe, the value of first-party relationships just went up and the value of rented third-party audiences went down. Any growth plan that assumes stable European targeting reach should be re-run. (PPC Land)

#AI at the leadership level

Germany's digital industry association published a voluntary code for AI at work, and its most useful rule is one line: every AI agent gets a named owner and a kill switch. The BVDW's "KI-Knigge" has 17 parts and 94 orientation points. It also covers practical habits such as capping prompt iterations so people do not loop endlessly on a model's output. The owner and kill switch rule turns a vague accountability principle into something auditable. For any agent in production, someone can be asked who owns it and how fast it can be stopped, and an empty answer is a finding. Boards asking for AI governance should ask for that inventory first: every agent, its named owner, its stop mechanism, and the last time the stop was tested. (PPC Land)

#Leadership & org design

Tripadvisor is undoing its own reorganization a year later, and the outgoing CEO has said why. In November 2025 Tripadvisor consolidated Viator and Tripadvisor experiences "under one team, strategy, and roadmap" as part of an experiences-led reset. Skift reported on 13 November that the company is reversing that merger and cutting Cruise Critic staff by 60 percent. Outgoing CEO Matt Goldberg said the combination "hasn't proved out as we expected," and experiences revenue fell 3 percent in the third quarter. The lesson is about what gets merged. Viator is a marketplace that sells to people who already know they want a tour. Tripadvisor serves people still deciding where to go. Putting them on one roadmap meant one team was always trading off two different customers. Before combining teams, check whether they share a customer and a success metric. If they share only a category name, keep them separate and share the platform underneath. (Skift, Skift, 2025)

#Question worth asking your team this week

If our single most important upstream system went down for four hours this Friday afternoon, which customer journeys would stop completely, what would customers see, and who would decide what we tell them?

#Try this now

  • Move ChatGPT Ads campaigns to Target CPA before the old bid types disappear: OpenAI replaced automated bidding in ChatGPT Ads with "intelligent bidding." Advertisers can now choose Target CPA or Target CPC, and Max Bid and Max CPC stop working on existing campaigns on 1 January 2027. Spend 20 minutes now. Clone one live campaign onto Target CPA with a target set at your trailing 30-day cost per conversion. Run it beside the original through Black Friday. You will go into January with a baseline instead of finding out what the default does to your budget during the cutover week. (PPC Land)
  • Export Amazon Ads history before it is deleted: Amazon's old report pages go read-only on 17 December, and any report not moved to unified reporting is gone on 31 January 2027. Only 85 of the 156 unified reporting dimensions apply to Sponsored Products, so some cuts you use today may not come across. This week, list every scheduled report your team or agency uses. Rebuild each one in unified reporting and pull a full raw export of the last 24 months into your own warehouse. The year-over-year comparison for Q4 2027 depends on it. (PPC Land)
  • Run your own B2B search cost audit using the Grey Matter method: A Grey Matter study tracked 1,349 keywords across 14 advertisers from April 2025 to September 2026. It found the same B2B clicks cost 21 percent more year over year, and the typical account's cost per click rose 36 percent, with the index moving from 100 to 132. The authors say it is not a controlled experiment. The method is still easy to copy. Pick your 50 highest-spend exact-match keywords and compare cost per click for the same months year over year. Then split the change into price inflation and mix shift. If you cannot explain at least half of the increase, it is the auction, and that changes how you defend next year's budget. (PPC Land)

#Case study worth stealing

TIME, agents for coordination and people for the deal: TIME uses AI agents to assemble proposals and troubleshoot campaigns, but its COO Mark Howard says agents closing deals is "not part of the strategy." The reasoning is that premium ad sales run on relationships, and the sellers were losing too much of their week to coordination work: chasing inventory, re-cutting proposals, answering delivery questions. Any digital team with a sales, partnerships or account management function can copy the split. List the steps between "client asks" and "client signs," move everything that is assembly or status chasing to agents, and protect the conversation itself. Measure it by seller hours spent with clients, not by tickets the agent closed. (AdExchanger)

#Framework or mental model

The 8.8 percent problem: New IAB research finds that consumers who get an AI brand recommendation are 2.5 times more likely to visit that brand's site within a week, but only 8.8 percent of those visits arrive tagged as AI referrals. The rest show up as direct, branded search or organic, so your analytics give the credit to whatever channel the visitor came through last. Google's new help page on identifying Search traffic in server logs admits the method "isn't exact" and says nothing about AI Mode or Gemini referrals. Before any team cuts an AI visibility budget for low referral numbers, compare branded search and direct traffic before and after a change in AI answer share. Those channels are where the influence lands. (PPC Land)

#Rabbit hole

Shoppers want to steer, not be driven: Two pieces of research landed in the same week and point the same way. Salesforce found 76 percent of more than 2,500 respondents do not want a personal AI agent buying for them. Shift Browser's 2027 State of Browsing Report, based on 1,005 US users, found 64 percent want a browser they steer even if it means more decisions, and 42 percent worry about AI taking actions they did not intend, even though 68 percent used an AI tool instead of search in the last six months. Both come from vendors with something to sell, and Shift published no margin of error. The pattern is still worth an hour for anyone designing agent features: people want AI to do the work and leave the commitment to them. A confirm step before purchase, payment or account change is the feature, not a drag on conversion. (PPC Land)

#Vendor & platform moves

  • IAG launched IAG Media, a standalone UK company selling ads across its five airlines: Skift reported on 12 November that the parent of British Airways, Iberia, Aer Lingus, Vueling and LEVEL has set up IAG Media with two arms: a products and platform unit that builds ad technology on connectivity, inflight entertainment and AI, and an agency unit for sales, campaigns and creative. Inventory includes Starlink login portals, seatback screens, websites, apps, PDF boarding passes and an audience extension product that reaches travellers on third-party platforms. Targeting runs on destination, cabin and customer profile across about 122 million annual passengers. Executives gave no revenue targets, and say ad load will range from nothing to standard pre-show ads depending on the passenger and cabin. Signal: Free Wi-Fi costs about $300,000 to $500,000 per aircraft to install plus recurring fees, and advertising is how airlines plan to pay for it. With United's Kinective Media, Lufthansa Group building its own and now IAG, the traveller media network is a standard airline business line, set up beside loyalty as a capital-light revenue unit. (Skift)
  • Viant bought Waymark, a generative AI video tool for CTV ads: According to Viant's 8-K, Waymark turns brand inputs into complete video ads with scripts, visuals and voiceover. AdExchanger reports a $20 million price, and Waymark keeps its team, platform and pricing. Signal: A DSP is buying creative production because the scarce input in CTV is advertisers who have a video ad, not inventory. Small and mid-sized advertisers locked out of TV for lack of a spot can now be onboarded inside the buying platform. That pulls creative, media and measurement into a single vendor. (SEC 8-K, ExchangeWire)
  • Google made Gemini 4 Hydrogen the default model in AI Mode for every user worldwide: Rolled out 12 November alongside the November core update. Google says it handles misspelled and complex questions better, fans queries out across more subtopics to surface more links, and builds richer answers such as tables and custom tools. Signal: The answer engine changed under every brand's feet in the middle of a core update and two weeks before Black Friday. Query fan-out means a page can win citations for subtopics it never ranked for, and lose them just as quietly. Visibility tracking has to happen at the subtopic level, and it has to be rerun after every model change. (PPC Land)

#Category watch

Traveller media networks: IAG Media follows the retail media playbook, which takes logged-in customer data plus owned surfaces and sells reach on and off property. Airlines add two things retailers lack, a captive cabin audience on Wi-Fi portals and seatback screens, and intent data that names a destination, date and cabin before the trip happens. The open questions are the same ones retail media spent five years on: whether closed-loop measurement against bookings and spend can be offered without exposing customer data, and whether low-frequency travellers give enough reach on their own. Expect partnerships across loyalty programs, card issuers and hotel groups to fill the frequency gap, and expect clean-room deals to be the price of admission for large advertisers.

#Stack intelligence

Platform reporting is being rebuilt around the platform, not the advertiser. In the same week, Amazon set a 31 January 2027 deletion date for any report not moved to unified reporting, with only 85 of 156 dimensions covering Sponsored Products. Google published guidance on finding Search traffic in server logs that admits it "isn't exact" and leaves out AI Mode and Gemini referrals. The IAB found only 8.8 percent of AI-driven visits show up as AI referrals. Each platform redefines its own reporting on its own schedule, and the historical series breaks every time. Enterprises that keep a raw daily export of every platform's spend and outcome data in their own warehouse can rebuild any view. Enterprises that rely on in-platform reporting lose their year-over-year baselines at each migration. Own the data layer, rent the execution. (PPC Land)

#Foundations concept of the day

Legitimate interest vs consent: Under the EU's GDPR, every use of personal data needs a legal basis. The two most common in marketing are consent, where the person actively says yes, and legitimate interest, where the company argues its business need outweighs the person's privacy expectations and the person can object afterward. Legitimate interest is attractive because nothing has to be asked up front, so no one gets the chance to say no. On 10 November the European Data Protection Board adopted guidelines saying cross-site ad tracking will rarely pass the legitimate interest test. Strategically, that means any audience built by following people across other companies' sites now needs consent in Europe. First-party audiences built on your own sites, apps and loyalty program keep their advantage. Retail is the example to watch: a grocer's loyalty-based retail media network relies on its own customer relationship, while a third-party retargeting network that tracked those same shoppers across the web now has to ask permission it often will not get. (PPC Land)

#Watch list

  • ChatGPT Ads bidding: OpenAI replaced automated bidding with "intelligent bidding," added Target CPA and Target CPC, and retires Max Bid and Max CPC for existing campaigns on 1 January 2027. Goal-based bidding is the feature search advertisers expect, and it is the clearest sign yet that ChatGPT is being built as a performance channel, not an awareness buy.
  • Apple's ATT concession in Germany: Under binding commitments to the German competition authority, apps can ask users who declined tracking to reconsider once after implementation and then once a year, with a prompt that drops the word "track." It applies only in Germany, with implementation due within four months. If opt-in rates rise there, expect other regulators to ask for the same terms. (PPC Land)

#Macro pulse

Friday ended a winning week on a soft note. US indexes slipped as Nvidia fell again, oil barely moved, and Treasury yields rose. The FAA's nationwide ground stop came after the close and is a weekend problem for airline operations, not a market move yet.

Canada: The TSX closed Friday at about 36,106, down 31 points, or 0.09 percent, almost flat. Energy gained, with Canadian Natural up 1.2 percent and Suncor up 0.9 percent. Technology lagged on the Wall Street selloff, with Celestica down more than 5 percent. Banks were subdued, with RBC down about 0.2 percent and National Bank down 0.5 percent. USD/CAD finished near 1.4269, up 0.08 percent, with the Canadian dollar close to a one-month low and the 2026 high around 1.429 to 1.430 still within reach. The Canada 10-year yield rose to about 3.86 percent as markets priced a Bank of Canada hike before year-end. A weak loonie keeps raising the Canadian-dollar cost of US-dollar fuel bills and aircraft leases for Canadian carriers. (Trading Economics)

United States: Friday close: S&P 500 down 30.73 points, or 0.4 percent, to 7,561.67. Dow up 50.46 points, or 0.1 percent, to 52,347.55. Nasdaq Composite down 229.82 points, or 0.9 percent, to 25,136.27. Russell 2000 down 0.3 percent to 2,790.49. Technology was the biggest drag. For the week, the S&P 500 rose 2.5 percent, the Dow 2.1 percent and the Nasdaq 2.3 percent, the first weekly gain since early October. The rate backdrop has not changed since Friday morning's 6.5 percent annual wholesale inflation print: yields rose again, and a December Fed hike is still fully priced. (La Nación/AP)

Asia (week ending 13 November): The Nikkei 225 gained about 6.5 percent on the week, its strongest week since June, as tech stocks recovered from the previous week's selloff and demand built for the Anthropic IPO. Advantest, Tokyo Electron and Recruit Holdings led. The Hang Seng ended the week on a second straight heavy loss, down 1.6 percent Friday to about 23,638. (Trading Economics)

Oil: Quiet after Thursday's jump. WTI settled about 0.2 percent lower near $88.56 and Brent was flat near $95.84, putting crude on track for a second straight weekly decline even after the "no longer need a deal with Iran" comments. Supply is coming back: Saudi output rose 26 percent in October, Iraq lifted production, and US crude stocks rose 4.66 million barrels in the week to 6 November, the biggest build since August. That supply rebuild is the only thing keeping a ceiling on jet fuel while the Strait risk premium stays in the price. (Trading Economics)

#Today's industry move

The FAA grounded every departing US flight for more than four hours on Friday afternoon. Radar and communications at en route and terminal control centres became unstable at 3:36 p.m. ET with 3,500 aircraft airborne. The stop was lifted around 8:15 p.m. More than 5,700 flights were cancelled Friday and 980 Saturday flights were cancelled before the weekend began. It is only the third nationwide ground stop in 25 years, and the 2023 one lasted about two hours. No cyberattack has been indicated and no root cause has been published. Expect US carriers to report the cost in fourth-quarter results, and expect Canadian carriers with heavy transborder schedules to take knock-on disruption through the weekend. (Skift)

Also in travel: Tripadvisor is reversing its 2025 merger of Viator and Tripadvisor experiences and cutting Cruise Critic staff by 60 percent, after the outgoing CEO said the combination "hasn't proved out." IAG set up IAG Media, a standalone ad business across its five airlines and about 122 million passengers, to recover the cost of free Starlink Wi-Fi. (Skift, Skift)

#AI infrastructure watch

The financing side of the AI buildout is showing its cost.

  • Meta borrowed 9 billion euros across six euro bond tranches, with maturities out to 2065 and coupons up to 6.4 percent. Hyperscalers are now funding data centre capex with 40-year paper at rates that reflect a 5 percent-plus US Treasury. (PPC Land)
  • Nscale lost $1.02 billion in six months as it heads for a New York Stock Exchange listing. Neoclouds are building ahead of contracted demand and asking public markets to fund the gap. This listing will test that appetite the week after Anthropic prices. (PPC Land)

#Signal or noise?

Signal: the AI trade recovered this week, but the companies building the capacity are paying more for money, borrowing longer and losing more on the way, so the cost of AI infrastructure is rising even as demand holds.

#Watch tomorrow

Weekend Iran headlines will set Monday's oil open, and the FAA's weekend recovery will show whether Friday's cancellations clear by Monday. Next week: Nvidia reports Tuesday, 17 November, and Anthropic prices Wednesday.
