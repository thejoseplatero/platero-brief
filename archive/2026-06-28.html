#Macro signal

Geopolitics is now a structural input to operating models, not a scenario plan: Supply-side disruption driven by geopolitical tensions, tariffs, energy insecurity, and demographic pressure is persistently raising the cost of growth across energy-intensive industries, explicitly including aviation. EY's Q2 geostrategic analysis flags that nearly 75% of CEOs are localizing production or reorganizing supply chains around regional blocs. The G7 summit (June 15 to 17) managed divergences among members but resolved nothing structural. For Air Canada, fuel cost volatility and vendor dependencies are now structural, not cyclical. The strategic implication: your operating model needs stress-testing against a "persistent fragmentation" baseline, not a "return to normal" one. Risk-adjusted growth planning, not just growth planning, is the new default posture.

#Tech industry

  • Apple opens the AI platform: Apple confirmed iOS 27, iPadOS 27, and macOS 27 will let users choose which AI model powers Apple Intelligence, ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude. The most significant platform openness decision Apple has made in a decade. Strategic implication for product leaders: the AI layer is becoming a consumer-facing choice, not hidden infrastructure. Loyalty will flow to experience quality, not to which model is underneath. Teams building AI-powered customer journeys need to design at the UX layer, not just the model selection layer.
  • US federal AI governance closing in: The White House issued an EO in early June establishing a voluntary framework for pre-release AI model review for cybersecurity and national security assessments. A 269-page Great American AI Act draft was unveiled in Congress. The EU AI Act enforcement begins August 2026. The regulatory window is closing, organizations without documented AI governance structures are now accumulating compliance risk, not just reputational risk. Microsoft's launch of its proprietary MAI model family (7 in-house models, MAI-Thinking-1 as the flagship reasoning model) signals enterprise AI entering a phase of vertical integration, compressing the window for differentiation through tooling and raising the bar for proprietary data and workflow advantages.

#AI at the leadership level

The CDO role is crystallizing around a specific accountability gap: only 29% of organizations have comprehensive AI governance plans, and only 22% have governance processes for the board's own AI usage, yet 66% of directors are already using AI for board work. That gap is a liability. The emerging expectation is that CDOs and senior digital leaders own data lineage, training data quality, model input controls, and AI output auditing, not just data strategy. The framing is shifting from "AI strategy" to "AI accountability." Deloitte and Harvard governance research are both converging on the same point: AI risk must be owned at the business level (named executive accountability), not delegated to IT. If your org doesn't have a named AI governance owner, that's a board-level gap.

#Leadership & org design

IMD's 2026 leadership research shows high-performing organizations prioritize AI adoption at 74% vs. 56% for lower performers, and the specific differentiator is strategic deployment rather than scattered experimentation. The organizations pulling ahead connect AI, talent development, and operating model into a coherent system rather than running parallel initiatives. Separately, multiple sources confirm org charts are flattening and authority is becoming distributed, but the leadership requirement is harder, not easier. Leaders who perform in this model influence outcomes without positional authority, operating through clarity of context and direction. For a Director managing product, design, Martech, AdTech, and a design system simultaneously, the coherence challenge, connecting a wide portfolio into a single operating logic, is the work.

#Question worth asking your team this week

"Where in our portfolio are we using AI as a point tool, and where are we using it as a system, and what would it concretely take to convert the former into the latter before Q4?"

It takes multi-step tasks, breaks them into stages, ships output into the thread, and has an "ambient mode" that proactively flags things across channels you've invited it to. For Jose: set it up in a BrainStation planning channel or Air Canada product channel this week. Bonus signal: the Anthropic Claude Code team reports Claude Tag now writes 65% of their product team's code, including most of what built Claude Tag itself.
  • Prompt Library as Team Infrastructure: The highest-ROI PM AI investment right now isn't a new tool, it's a shared prompt library. The pattern: each prompt includes (1) product stage, (2) target audience, (3) constraint, (4) desired output format. Store in Notion or Confluence. For BrainStation: make building a 5-prompt PM starter kit a live classroom exercise. Students leave with something immediately usable, and the exercise teaches structured thinking, not just tool mechanics.
  • Claude Design: Anthropic launched a design workspace that generates complete prototypes, one-pagers, decks, and marketing assets, aimed at non-designers: PMs, founders, marketers. If you're not living in Figma daily, this is worth 20 minutes. Practical test: feed it a product brief and see if it outputs a deck worth sharing. Strong classroom demo for the "AI as collaborator, not replacement" conversation with design students.

#Case study worth stealing

Grubhub + Braze, Agentic Onboarding at Scale: Grubhub rebuilt their student onboarding as a multi-stage agentic journey using Braze. Instead of a fixed email sequence, an AI agent watches user actions and personalizes each next step in real time, adjusting timing, channel, and offer based on what each user actually did. Result: 836% ROI increase, 20% more orders, 188% rise in student signups. The specific tactic Jose can adapt: map your highest-drop funnel segment (new loyalty members at Air Canada, or new BrainStation enrollees) and replace the static onboarding sequence with a branching agent that responds to what users actually do in the first 72 hours. The tech is table-stakes now; the differentiator is having the behavioral logic mapped well enough to hand it to an agent.

#Framework or mental model

Data Contextualization → Reasoning Transparency → Performance Feedback: From recent research on how human mental models evolve when working with AI. Three mechanisms drive whether people get better at working with AI over time: (1) AI surfaces data in context, not raw; (2) AI shows its reasoning, not just output; (3) users see when AI was right or wrong (feedback loop). Most teams skip #2 and #3, which is why AI adoption plateaus. Jose can apply this directly: in AI workshops, build all three into the exercise, ask participants to prompt for reasoning, then check the output against reality. This is also a product design lens: AI features that hide reasoning create weaker user mental models and lower trust over time.

#Teach this tomorrow

Live prompt library build: Give BrainStation PM students a single user interview transcript (real or synthetic), then have them construct three structured prompts to extract different things from it, themes, unmet needs, and a draft opportunity statement. Constraint: each prompt must include role, context, output format, and one specific constraint. Debrief on how the structure changed output quality. Works because it's tactile, immediately transferable, and surfaces the "garbage in, garbage out" insight experientially rather than conceptually.

#Category watch

Agentic CDPs: The CDP category is bifurcating. One side: standalone CDPs (Segment, Lytics, mParticle) adding AI orchestration and agentic layers on top of their existing unification infrastructure. Other side: data platform players (Databricks CustomerLake, Snowflake marketing data cloud) building CDP functionality from the lakehouse layer up, skipping the separate-vendor model entirely. The key battleground is where marketing teams will live, in a CDP UI optimized for marketers, or querying a lakehouse via agents. Enterprise orgs building net-new stacks face a real fork: buy a mature CDP (faster time-to-value, richer activation connectors) or bet on the data platform (higher ceiling, longer ramp, no extra contract). The outcome of Databricks' private preview will shape this decision across Fortune 500 procurement cycles in H2 2026.

#Stack intelligence

Two architectural patterns are competing for the enterprise martech stack in 2026. Pattern A (Zeta-Palantir model): governance data layer → identity and intelligence layer → execution layer. Separate vendors, but tightly integrated with explicit data lineage at each handoff. Pattern B (Databricks CustomerLake model): single data platform does it all, unification, profiling, activation, measurement, consumption-billed, no layer separation. Pattern A favors orgs with existing heterogeneous stacks and compliance requirements. Pattern B favors orgs already standardized on a lakehouse who want to collapse vendor count. The practical signal for org-builders: platform selection is now the most consequential martech decision, not tool selection. Picking Snowflake vs. Databricks vs. BigQuery now means picking your future CDP's ceiling.

#Foundations concept of the day

Data Clean Rooms: A data clean room is a secure, privacy-preserving compute environment where two parties can run joint analytics on combined datasets without either party seeing the other's raw data. Think of it as a controlled lab: you bring your customer list, a media partner brings their audience data, the clean room runs overlap analysis and attribution queries, and neither party walks away with the other's PII.

How it works: queries run inside a walled compute environment with strict rules on permitted outputs (aggregate counts only, minimum thresholds to prevent re-identification). Results, "X% of people exposed to this ad bought within 30 days", come out. Raw records do not. Major platforms: Google PAIR, Amazon Marketing Cloud (AMC), LiveRamp Clean Rooms, AWS Clean Rooms, Snowflake Data Clean Room.

Why it matters at scale: retail media networks (Amazon, Walmart Connect, Instacart) use clean rooms to let CPG brands measure whether ad spend drove sales, without handing over transaction-level purchase data. In travel: an airline can share loyalty segment data with a hotel partner to measure co-marketing lift without exposing individual itineraries.

Current state: fewer than half of US retail media networks currently offer clean room capabilities (Mars United Commerce, Q2 2025). Amazon Marketing Cloud went free for all Sponsored Ads advertisers in late 2025, compressing the cost barrier and accelerating adoption. The frontier shift: clean rooms are evolving from *measurement tools* (report after the fact) to *activation engines* (seed targeting segments in a DSP directly from clean room outputs, in near-real-time).
