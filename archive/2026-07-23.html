Executive Pulse419 words

#Macro signal

Oil is back as the swing variable in the rate story: Brent pushed toward $97 to $99 a barrel on renewed Middle East escalation, and markets are now pricing roughly a one-in-three chance of a Fed rate hike this month, a scenario that was near zero weeks ago. The strategic implication is that the "rates only go down from here" planning assumption baked into a lot of 2026 budgets is no longer safe. Leaders in travel, retail, and any discretionary-demand business should stress-test their second-half plans against a scenario where energy-driven inflation keeps money expensive and consumer wallets tighter.

#Tech industry

  • Alphabet's capex shock: Q2 revenue rose 24 percent to $119.8B and Google Cloud grew 82 percent, yet the stock fell more than 6 percent because Alphabet raised full-year capex guidance to $195B to $205B and warned 2027 will climb "significantly." Free cash flow turned negative. Strategic implication: the market is no longer clapping for AI spend on faith. It now wants the revenue line to visibly outrun the infrastructure bill, and the bar for justifying AI capex just moved from "strategic" to "show me the return."

#AI at the leadership level

The tension inside enterprises is widening, not closing. A 2026 survey finds 79 percent of organizations now report challenges adopting AI, up double digits from last year, and 54 percent of C-suite executives say AI adoption is "tearing their company apart." The gap is not strategy, 42 percent feel their strategy is ready, it is infrastructure, data, risk, and talent. The leadership lesson is that AI maturity is now an operating-model problem, not a vision problem. The organizations pulling ahead are redesigning how work flows and who owns what, not writing better AI strategy decks.

#Leadership & org design

The pattern in AI-native transformation is a shift from adding AI tools to redesigning the operating model around agents. Firms scaling AI are reorganizing delivery so autonomous agents handle end-to-end workflows inside defined guardrails, which changes what human roles exist and what they are accountable for. The practical takeaway for any leader: the reorg is the strategy. If your AI plan does not name which workflows change hands and which roles get redefined, you have a tool rollout, not a transformation.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If our AI investment had to defend itself the way Alphabet's capex just had to in front of the market, could we point to revenue it moved, or only activity it enabled?

Practitioner Pulse427 words

#Try this now

  • OpenAI Presence (enterprise agent runtime): A deployed layer for running trusted AI agents across voice and chat with policies, guardrails, simulations, evals, and a fixed set of approved actions. The practitioner move is not "buy it," it is copy the shape: before you ship any customer-facing agent, define the approved-action list and the eval set first, then let the model operate only inside that box. Twenty minutes spent writing the action allowlist saves you the incident.
  • ActiveCampaign Google Ads connector for Active Intelligence: Marketers can now pipe CRM data straight into Google Ads and launch Performance Max campaigns from inside ActiveCampaign. Worth a look if you run lifecycle plus paid acquisition, because it collapses the audience-handoff step where first-party signal usually leaks. Test it on one segment, measure whether the PMax audience actually reflects your CRM traits before you route budget through it.
  • Superhuman Docs (Coda) campaign hub from one prompt: Docs AI builds a working campaign workspace, tasks, calendars, dashboards, live Jira and Salesforce data, from a single prompt. Useful for spinning up a launch war-room in minutes instead of hand-wiring a template. The real test is whether the live-data views stay accurate a week in, so pressure-test the Jira and Salesforce sync before you trust the dashboard in a standup.

#Case study worth stealing

Bank of America, splitting the assistant by audience: Erica was extended past consumer banking into CashPro for business clients and into an employee-facing "Erica for Employees" that fields IT and HR queries. The internal deployment cut calls to the help desk by 55 percent, and the CashPro integration cut live-chat volume by 42 percent. The adaptable tactic is the segmentation: they did not build one universal bot, they cloned the engine and retrained the context and approved actions per audience. Any digital org sitting on a support assistant can get a second win by pointing the same infrastructure at internal ops, where the queries are narrower and the hallucination risk is lower.

#Framework or mental model

The Context Pyramid: A layered way to engineer what an AI agent actually knows at runtime, stacking system prompt, durable memory, tools, and retrieval rather than stuffing everything into one prompt. The insight for practitioners is that agent reliability is a context-architecture problem, not a prompt-wording problem. When an agent underperforms, the fix is usually a missing layer, no persistent memory, or retrieval pulling the wrong documents, not a cleverer instruction. Audit a failing agent by asking which layer is starving before you touch the prompt.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • ActiveCampaign: Shipped a Google Ads connector for its Active Intelligence engine, letting marketers push CRM data into Google Ads and launch Performance Max directly from the platform. Signal: The mid-market martech players are racing to become the activation layer for the walled gardens. Owning the audience-to-PMax handoff is how they stay sticky as buyers consolidate tools, and it is another data point that the CRM, not the DSP, is where first-party audiences increasingly get assembled.
  • OpenAI Presence: A production runtime for enterprise agents across voice and chat, with built-in policies, guardrails, simulation, evals, and approved actions. Signal: The model vendors are moving up the stack into governed deployment, the exact territory CX and martech suites assumed was theirs. Expect the "agent platform" category to get squeezed between foundation-model vendors selling the runtime and application vendors embedding agents natively.

#Category watch

Incrementality measurement and identity resolution: Q1 2026 dealmaking cooled across adtech and martech, but the acquisitions that did close clustered on incrementality measurement, identity resolution, and AI-driven performance optimization. That is where the money is voting. As signal loss and walled-garden opacity persist, the ability to prove causal lift, not just report attributed conversions, is becoming the defensible category. Anyone building a stack should treat measurement as a first-class layer, not a reporting afterthought.

#Stack intelligence

AI-native martech firms are commanding more than 2x the M&A multiple of traditional martech, reaching roughly 7.8x revenue while legacy martech trades at a discount to the broader SaaS median. The practical read for an org-builder: the "AI wrapper on a legacy CRM" era is ending, and buyers are paying for tools where the AI is the architecture, not a bolt-on. When you evaluate a vendor, ask whether ripping out the AI leaves a functioning product or a hollow one. The valuation gap says the market now prices that difference explicitly.

#Foundations concept of the day

Performance Max (PMax): Google's fully automated, goal-based campaign type that spends across Search, Display, YouTube, Gmail, Discover, and Maps from a single campaign, with the algorithm deciding placement, bid, and creative mix to hit a conversion or value target. You feed it audience signals, creative assets, and a goal, and give up granular channel control in exchange for cross-inventory optimization. It matters at scale because it concentrates spend and decisioning inside Google's black box, which is efficient but erodes the advertiser's visibility into what actually drove results. Cross-industry example: a retailer that pipes loyalty-tier data into PMax as an audience signal gets better targeting, but loses the ability to see which channel converted which segment, the classic automation-for-control trade that every programmatic buyer eventually confronts.

#Watch list

  • CRM-as-activation-layer: Watch whether more martech suites follow ActiveCampaign in building direct walled-garden connectors. If the CRM becomes the default place audiences are built and launched, the standalone DSP and CDP categories both lose ground.
  • Model vendors selling governed runtimes: OpenAI Presence is a template. If Anthropic and Google ship comparable governed agent-deployment layers, the independent "agent orchestration platform" startups face a hard squeeze.

#Today's industry move

Alphabet reported Q2 revenue up 24 percent to $119.8B with Google Cloud up 82 percent, but shares fell more than 6 percent after it raised full-year capex guidance to $195B to $205B and free cash flow turned negative. The market is now demanding that AI revenue visibly outpace AI infrastructure spend, a shift in sentiment that reprices the entire hyperscaler complex.

#AI infrastructure watch

The buildout keeps escalating. Roughly $725B in hyperscaler AI capex was confirmed across Q1 2026 earnings, and Alphabet's raised guidance plus its "significantly higher" 2027 warning extends the curve. The constraint story is energy and water: Nvidia's new DSX factory design uses direct liquid cooling to drop water chillers, and its Valar Atomics collaboration targets a nearly waterless, small-modular-reactor-powered data center. Power and thermal, not chips alone, are becoming the binding limit on what AI can scale to.

#Signal or noise?

Signal. The oil spike is event-driven, but the capex-versus-return scrutiny on Alphabet is a durable regime shift in how the market underwrites AI spending, and it will shadow every hyperscaler print this season.

#Watch tomorrow

Whether Brent breaks $100. A clean move through that line would harden Fed-hike expectations and put fresh pressure on both airline margins and discretionary-demand equities.
