Executive Pulse444 words

#Macro signal

Oil falls on Iran diplomacy hopes: WTI crude dropped 3% to settle near $89 and Brent fell nearly 4% after reports that Pakistan, backed by China, is pushing to revive US-Iran talks, even as US strikes continued overnight. Why it matters: energy is the swing variable in the current inflation and margin picture, and a diplomatic path would ease both input costs and geopolitical risk premia. The implication for leaders is that a chunk of this quarter's cost pressure is contingent on a negotiation, not a trend. Do not bake a lower fuel or energy line into next quarter's plan until the talks are more than a report.

#Tech industry

  • Chip and memory selloff on capex fears: Sandisk fell 11% and the Nasdaq slid 0.6% as investors questioned whether AI infrastructure spending can keep compounding. Strategic implication: the market is starting to price the risk that AI capex outruns near-term returns. Boards should expect harder questions on payback timelines for AI investments, not just on ambition.
  • Four hyperscalers plan $710B in 2026 capex: The spend is concentrating compute, energy, and data center capacity in a handful of hands. Strategic implication: every enterprise's AI roadmap now depends on infrastructure it does not control and cannot forecast the price of. Build optionality across providers rather than betting the roadmap on one.

#AI at the leadership level

The governance gap is now the headline risk. Survey data shows 60% of executives expect their board to intervene over a botched AI strategy, and 58% say many of their peers lack the fundamental knowledge to make sound AI decisions. The role is shifting: leaders are being asked to redesign how work is done, not to approve a technology purchase. The CDO mandate is moving from tooling to operating-model design, and the leaders who cannot make that shift are the ones boards are preparing to override.

#Leadership & org design

Despite heavy investment, 79% of enterprises report struggling with AI adoption, which points to an execution problem rather than a technology one. The organizations pulling ahead are the ones treating AI as a work-redesign project owned by the operating leadership, not an innovation project parked in a lab. The practical move is to name a single accountable owner for redesigning a specific high-volume workflow end to end, and to measure the containment rate rather than the number of tools deployed.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If our board intervened tomorrow on our AI strategy, what is the one workflow we could show them running autonomously end to end, with a real containment number attached, and not just a pilot?

#Try this now

  • UserTesting MCP Server: A Model Context Protocol server that pipes participant recruitment, test creation, and results straight into Claude, ChatGPT, and Figma Make. Instead of context-switching to a research platform, you brief a test from inside the tool you already design in and pull results back into the same thread. Worth 20 minutes if your research still lives in a separate tab from where decisions get made. The real move is treating discovery as a call you make mid-workflow, not a project you schedule.
  • Zapier AI Agents (now GA): Multi-step agents built from natural-language prompts across 7,000+ apps, with conditional logic, error recovery, and multi-branch handling. This is the practical bridge for teams that want agentic automation without a platform engineering lift. Build one that watches a lead source, enriches it, routes it, and logs the exception cases it cannot handle. The skill to practice is writing the fallback path, not the happy path.
  • Background coding agents with diff review: The newest coding tools run long tasks asynchronously and surface a diff review interface before you accept changes. The pattern to internalize is the audit gate: the agent does the work, you approve the delta. Port that habit to any AI workflow you run. Never let an agent commit; make it propose.

#Case study worth stealing

Tesco, AI-personalized Clubcard challenges at scale: Tesco delivered individually personalized loyalty challenges to millions of Clubcard members, using purchase history to set each member a relevant goal rather than blasting one generic promotion. It lifted engagement and proved 1:1 marketing works at population scale, not just in a segment of one. The adaptable tactic: you do not need a new channel to personalize, you need a value metric per member and a rule that generates the next best challenge from their own behavior. Any loyalty or retention team sitting on transaction data can build a version of this before touching a new martech line item.

#Framework or mental model

The containment rate: Borrowed from support operations, containment rate is the share of interactions an autonomous system resolves end to end without human handoff. One marketplace hit 75% containment across 900,000 weekly self-service voice sessions. The value is that it forces an honest number on "AI-powered": not how many queries the agent touched, but how many it actually closed. Digital leaders can apply it to any agent rollout as the single metric that separates a demo from a deployment. If you cannot state your containment rate, you have a pilot, not a system.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Affinity launches Affinity Ascend: The relationship-intelligence CRM shipped an agent platform that automates workflows across the investment lifecycle, from sourcing to diligence to portfolio tracking. Signal: vertical CRMs are turning their proprietary relationship graphs into agent substrates. The moat is not the automation, it is the domain-specific data the agent runs on. Horizontal players cannot copy the graph.
  • UserTesting ships an MCP server: Research tooling is now callable from Claude, ChatGPT, and Figma Make. Signal: MCP is becoming the default distribution layer for point tools. The platforms that expose a clean MCP interface get pulled into every AI-native workflow; the ones that stay walled behind a UI get skipped. Integration strategy is now protocol strategy.
  • AI-native martech commands 2x multiples: M&A data shows AI-native martech firms clearing 7.8x revenue, more than double the multiple of traditional vendors, with acquisition activity up 13% over 2024. Signal: the market is repricing the whole category by how AI-native the architecture is, not by revenue quality. Bolt-on AI does not earn the premium; agent-native design does.

#Category watch

Retail media and loyalty personalization: The category is shifting from segment-based offers to per-member agentic personalization. Tesco's individually generated Clubcard challenges and Amazon's Rufus assistant (300M users, shoppers 60% more likely to purchase) show the pattern maturing from recommendation to autonomous action inside the journey. The players who win own both the first-party purchase graph and the agent that acts on it. For anyone building a martech org, this is the clearest near-term case where owning the data layer beats renting a better model.

#Stack intelligence

The consolidation wave is producing a two-layer stack decision: own the data and identity layer, rent the execution and model layer. AI-native tools distribute through MCP rather than through pre-built connectors, which lowers switching costs at the execution layer and raises the strategic value of the data layer that stays put. The practical build implication is to stop over-investing in integrations that MCP will commoditize, and concentrate spend on the first-party data and identity assets that agents will compete to access.

#Foundations concept of the day

Identity resolution: The process of stitching fragmented signals (email, device IDs, loyalty numbers, cookies, in-app IDs) into a single persistent view of one person across touchpoints. It works by matching deterministic keys where you have them (a login) and probabilistic signals where you do not (device, behavior, location). It matters at scale because every downstream capability, personalization, attribution, suppression, frequency capping, depends on knowing that these ten events are one human. Real example: a grocery retailer that resolves the in-store Clubcard swipe to the same profile as the app session and the website visit can set one member one challenge across all three; a retailer with three unlinked identities sends three disconnected messages and looks like spam.

#Watch list

  • MCP as the new integration standard: watch how many enterprise martech vendors ship MCP servers this quarter. The count is a leading indicator of which tools survive the agent transition.
  • Vertical agent CRMs: Affinity in investment, and whichever players move next in legal, healthcare, and real estate. The template is proprietary graph plus lifecycle agent.

#Macro pulse

Weekend read on Friday's close. Canada: the TSX gained over 150 points Friday as falling oil eased inflation worries, though the Canadian dollar slipped to 70.96 cents US from 71.01, a modest move that still nudges Canadian carrier international revenue the wrong way. USA: mixed and volatile. Dow +0.5% to 51,947, S&P 500 essentially flat at 7,412, Nasdaq -0.6% to 24,976 on a chip and memory selloff; all three finished the week lower. Oil: WTI fell 3% to settle at $89.31 and Brent dropped nearly 4% to $96.78 after reports of a possible revival of US-Iran talks, the first down day for crude in a week. A sub-$90 WTI is a tailwind for airline fuel costs if it holds.

#Today's industry move

Sandisk tumbled 11% Friday, leading a broader memory and chip pullback as investors questioned whether AI infrastructure capex can keep compounding. The move dragged the Nasdaq lower even as the Dow recovered, a rotation out of the AI hardware trade and into everything else.

#AI infrastructure watch

The capex-fatigue selloff sits against a backdrop of four hyperscalers planning north of $710B in 2026 infrastructure spend. The energy constraint is now explicit: Nvidia's Blackwell chips have been run off a Valar Atomics microreactor in Utah, and the companies are weighing a 30MW nearly waterless data center, a public admission that the grid cannot serve AI at scale and that behind-the-meter nuclear is becoming the plan rather than the pitch.

#Signal or noise?

Signal. Friday's chip selloff is one day, but the question underneath it, whether AI capex outruns near-term returns, is the trend that will set the tone into earnings season.

#Watch tomorrow

Whether the US-Iran diplomacy reports firm up or fade. If talks gain traction, oil stays soft and the airline cost picture improves; if the strikes escalate, Friday's crude drop reverses fast.
