Executive Pulse427 words

#Macro signal

Nearly three-quarters of CEOs are now their company's chief decision-maker on AI, and four in five are more optimistic about AI ROI than a year ago. AI has moved from a delegated technical initiative to the center of the CEO's own agenda, touching strategy, operations, culture, risk, and talent all at once. For any leader still routing AI decisions through a CTO or a committee, this is the signal that ownership has already shifted at the top of the market.

#Tech industry

  • S&P Global restructured its Market Intelligence unit on July 6 to accelerate agentic AI solutions and platform capabilities, pairing the move with executive leadership changes. Strategic implication: even data and analytics incumbents are reorganizing around agents as the primary product form, not a bolt-on feature.
  • Qualcomm is targeting $15 billion in AI data center chip revenue by 2029, a direct challenge to Nvidia built on chips that skip high-bandwidth memory entirely. Strategic implication: the AI infrastructure market is still young enough for a credible new entrant to challenge an incumbent's core business with a different architecture bet.

#AI at the leadership level

More than half of enterprise 2026 AI investment is now going to agents specifically, and AI-first CEOs who align around five core operating plays saw 17% higher revenue growth over the past three years than peers who did not. But confidence has a ceiling: 60% of CEOs admit they have intentionally slowed their own AI implementation over concerns about errors and malfunctions, even while nearly all expect measurable ROI in 2026. The tension is not indecision, the leaders most bullish on the upside are also the most aware of what an uncontrolled failure would cost.

#Leadership & org design

The enterprises actually converting AI agents into ROI share one pattern: none used a traditional waterfall rollout. Every verified case study, from customer service automation to contract review to fraud detection, started small, learned, and expanded, with a median time-to-value of 5.1 months. Org design implication: a big-bang enterprise AI rollout is now a documented anti-pattern, not just a cautious preference.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If an AI agent in production made a costly, visible error tomorrow, does your organization actually know who owns that failure, or does everyone quietly assume it belongs to someone else until it happens?

Practitioner Pulse324 words

#Try this now

Context engineering over prompt engineering: the shift practitioners should make now is from crafting a single clever prompt to designing the system that feeds an agent the right memory, tools, and information at the right time. A shared, context-specific prompt library, templated by product stage, audience, constraint, and format, is one of the highest-ROI investments a product team can make this quarter, and it is buildable in an afternoon.

#Case study worth stealing

Klarna, AI agent handling customer support at scale: Klarna's AI agent saved the company $60 million and now handles the workload equivalent of 853 employees. The transferable pattern is not the specific chatbot, it is the staged rollout: start with a narrow, well-bounded task, measure real resolution rates, and only expand scope once the agent has earned trust on the data. Any team piloting an AI agent for support or triage should copy the sequencing, not just the tool.

#Framework or mental model

Prompt, context, and intent engineering as three distinct layers: prompt engineering steers a single answer, context engineering gives an agent the memory and tools it needs across a session, and intent engineering constrains an agent that acts autonomously over a longer horizon so it does the right thing without being watched at every step. Digital leaders evaluating an "AI workflow" tool should ask which of these three layers it actually operates at, since most tools claiming agentic behavior are still only doing the first.

#Rabbit hole

Worth 20 minutes: enterprise AI agent adoption data shows banking and insurance leading deployment at 47% while healthcare and government trail at 18% and 14%. The gap tracks almost exactly with regulatory density, a useful predictor for where agent adoption will hit friction next in any org.

AI Stack Daily453 words

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Revmatics acquired DataFeedWatch from Cart.com: a cash-and-stock deal combining DataFeedWatch's product-feed distribution across 18,000+ brands and thousands of shopping channels with Revmatics' self-improving agentic AI platform, Lumara. Signal: whoever controls how product data is formatted and distributed at that scale effectively controls how AI shopping agents rank and surface products, a chokepoint most marketers have not priced in yet.
  • Canva launched Canva Grow 2.0 at Cannes Lions: an AI-native workflow unifying ad creation, publishing, and performance analytics into one platform. Signal: this directly targets the fragmented martech stack problem and pressures Adobe, HubSpot, and Salesforce to accelerate their own AI-native integrations rather than bolt AI onto existing point products.

#Category watch

Agentic commerce infrastructure: the Revmatics/DataFeedWatch deal signals a new category forming around who controls the data layer AI shopping agents actually read from. As more purchase journeys start with an AI agent rather than a search box, owning the product-feed distribution layer becomes as strategically important as owning the storefront itself.

#Stack intelligence

The pattern across this week's platform moves, Canva's unified workflow and Revmatics' agentic feed play, is consolidation pressure from one direction: marketing teams want fewer tools that do more, not more tools that do less. Any team evaluating a new point solution should ask whether it survives the next 12 to 18 months of platform consolidation, or whether its function gets absorbed into an adjacent all-in-one play.

#Foundations concept of the day

Agentic commerce and the product feed as a chokepoint: a product feed is the structured data, price, availability, images, attributes, that retailers and brands send to shopping channels so their products can be found and purchased. Historically this was a technical plumbing problem. As AI shopping agents increasingly do the searching and comparing on a customer's behalf, whichever company controls the format and distribution of that feed data effectively controls visibility, the same way SEO controlled visibility in the search era. Cross-industry example: a grocery chain and a fashion retailer both compete on price and selection, but the one whose product data is structured for AI agents to parse correctly gets recommended more often, regardless of whether it actually has the better product.

#Watch list

  • Qualcomm's data center chip push: targeting $15 billion in revenue by 2029 with a memory-light architecture aimed squarely at Nvidia's core business, worth watching whether this becomes a genuine third option for AI infrastructure buyers.
  • Agentic commerce chokepoints: as more purchases originate from AI agents rather than search, watch which company ends up owning the data-formatting layer between brands and those agents.
