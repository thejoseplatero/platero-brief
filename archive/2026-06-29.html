Executive Pulse544 words

#Macro signal

The Fed's pivot from "cut" to "hold-or-hike" is now the base case. Following the June FOMC, the median 2026 PCE projection was revised up to 3.6% (core 3.3%), and BofA is now modeling three quarter-point *hikes* this year, reversing prior cut expectations and pushing the benchmark toward 4.25 to 4.5%. Long-end yields across advanced economies hit ~20-year highs even as the S&P prints record highs, a market betting on resilient growth (real GDP ~2.2%, unemployment 4.3%) while bracing for higher-for-longer money. Strategic read: the era of cheap capital underwriting "grow now, monetize later" is over. Every AI/martech investment case now has to clear a higher hurdle rate. Leaders should reframe AI spend from "innovation budget" to "cost-to-serve and margin defense", the framing that survives a restrictive-rate boardroom.

#Tech industry

  • Qualcomm in early talks to buy Tenstorrent ($8 to 10B). A serious bid to break the Nvidia/AMD duopoly on AI silicon via open RISC-V (Jim Keller's shop). Combined with Goldman raising 2026 AI capex forecasts to ~$800B and the rise of "Sovereign AI" (NAVER scaling to gigawatt capacity on Nvidia DSX; France, Saudi, Japan funding domestic stacks), the signal is consolidation *and* fragmentation at once. Implication: the compute/model layer is becoming geopolitical and contested, reinforcing the "own the data layer, rent execution" thesis. Don't bet the enterprise on a single vendor's roadmap when the substrate is this volatile.

#AI at the leadership level

The governance gap is now the board's problem, not IT's. Per Informatica's 2026 CDO survey, 69% of orgs have deployed GenAI and 47% agentic AI, but 76% admit governance isn't keeping pace with employee usage, and 60% of executives say their board is likely to intervene over a botched AI strategy. The Conference Board found 93% cite *cultural*, not technical, barriers as the top adoption hurdle. The CDO role is shifting from "data plumbing" to *being the accountable owner of the AI-risk-vs-velocity tradeoff at the board table*. For an airline sitting on rich loyalty and behavioral data, the moat isn't the model, it's governed, trusted, usable data plus the org literacy to act on it.

#Leadership & org design

The flattening is concrete now, not theoretical. Meta is moving toward a 1:50 manager-to-engineer ratio using internal AI agents for the PM/admin layer; GitLab's "Act 2" restructuring collapses eight management layers and mandates daily AI use; Cloudflare's CEO said the cuts hit "the measurers", middle management, finance, internal audit. The emergent winning role is the "AI orchestrator": senior people who don't produce more output but *evaluate, steer, and exercise taste* over what agents generate. Practical takeaway for a design/PM/martech org: the differentiated skill is judgment and "knowing what good looks like," not throughput. Restructure teams around taste and verification, not headcount-driven execution.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If AI agents handled the execution layer of our martech and design work tomorrow, what judgment calls would still have to run through a human, and have we actually built our org chart around those, or around the work that's about to be automated?

#Try this now

  • Loop engineering (stop prompting, start designing loops): Boris Cherny, head of Claude Code at Anthropic, says he doesn't prompt Claude anymore, "I have loops running that prompt Claude and figuring out what to do." The move is from writing one prompt at a time to building a small repeatable loop that discovers a task, does the work, and verifies the result with minimal input. The practitioner exercise: pick one PM workflow that repeats weekly (e.g. triaging support tickets into themes, drafting release notes) and write it once as a loop, purpose, the steps, and a verification check, instead of re-prompting each time. Worth 20 minutes to find your first loop candidate. (thenewstack.io/loop-engineering, addyosmani.com/blog/loop-engineering)
  • Figma Code-to-Canvas / /prototype-to-figma: Shown at Config 2026. If you've vibe-coded a prototype (Cursor, Claude Code, Figma Make), run the /prototype-to-figma skill against your localhost URL and Figma pulls each screen onto the canvas as editable frames mapped to your design system, plus a summary and styles page. AI-generated code becomes structured, design-system-aware Figma layers ready for handoff, not throwaway mockups. Open it today against any small prototype to see the round-trip. (figma.com/blog/code-on-the-figma-canvas, help.figma.com, "Workflow lab: Code to canvas")
  • Context-specific prompt library (team asset, not personal hack): The highest-ROI move for a PM org right now is a shared prompt library where each prompt is templated with product stage, audience, constraint, and format, not clever one-offs. Build one shared doc this week with 5 prompts your team re-runs (synthesis, PRD draft, competitive teardown, user-story expansion, exec summary). It's the cheapest version of "loop engineering" any non-engineer can ship today. (productside.com/the-ai-product-management-workflows-2026)

#Case study worth stealing

Adore Me, agent that drafts to 95%, human finishes the last 5%: Adore Me (e-commerce lingerie) used Writer AI Studio to attack repetitive writing. Marketplace product descriptions went from a ~20-hour-a-month process to 20 minutes; stylists who hand-write 400 to 500 word notes over a thousand times a day saw note-writing time drop 36%. Critically, conversion was unchanged between human- and AI-written descriptions, so the writers were redeployed to higher-impact creative work, not cut. The stealable pattern: target the repetitive, high-volume writing where AI gets you 95% there and the human adds the last 5% of voice, and measure a guardrail metric (conversion) to prove quality held. The question for any content-heavy org: which copy is high-volume, low-variance, and safe to template? (writer.com/blog/adore-me-customer-story, consumergoods.com)

#Framework or mental model

Context engineering = the OS mental model (CPU / RAM / OS): From Karo Zieminski's "Context Engineering for Product Builders", treat the LLM as the CPU, the context window as RAM, and you (the practitioner) as the operating system deciding what gets loaded into RAM and when. Anthropic frames context engineering as "the natural progression of prompt engineering": the skill is no longer phrasing a request, it's curating the right tokens, durable state, retrieved knowledge, and per-call runtime context. It's why structured agent systems (CLAUDE.md, skills, MCP) matter: they're how you, the OS, manage limited RAM. (karozieminski.substack.com, anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)

#Case study worth stealing

Salomon creative sprint, 1,600+ creatives, zero physical shoots: Salomon produced 1,600+ on-brand ad creatives across six markets and four aspect ratios in an 8-week sprint by defining constraints *before* generating: structured visual prompts (mood, lighting, lens, wardrobe, framing), 13+ modular templates built once and scaled across 1:1 / 4:5 / 16:9 / 9:16, and localized copy. The lesson: the leverage is in the constraints, not the model. Define the structure first, then generate at scale. (trypencil.com/blog/case-studies/salomon, pragmatic.digital)

#Rabbit hole (optional)

Anthropic's "Effective context engineering for AI agents", the canonical write-up on token curation, compaction, and the discipline of deciding what context an agent actually needs. An hour here will make every prompt-library and loop decision above sharper, and it's the conceptual backbone behind context-aware agent system design. (anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Databricks (CustomerLake): Databricks formally entered MarTech with CustomerLake, an "agentic CDP" built natively on the lakehouse, unifying identity resolution, audience building, and activation, and replacing campaigns with continuous "infinity" agent loops. Signal: The CDP category is being eaten from below by the data platform. When the warehouse ships native customer-data + agent tooling, the standalone CDP loses its reason to exist as a separate layer. Forrester is openly treating this as the litmus test for whether agentic marketing is real. This validates the "own the data layer, rent execution" thesis, the data layer is now where the intelligence lives, not the application.
  • Zeta + Palantir (Cannes, Jun 23): Zeta is rearchitecting its Data Cloud on Palantir Foundry, pulling in Foundry's ontology, governance, and versioned/auditable data layer. Zeta projects $100M+ annual revenue from the deal. Signal: The differentiator for enterprise agentic marketing isn't the model, it's governance. A versioned, auditable data layer is what lets a marketing agent act autonomously inside a regulated business (bank, hospital, airline) without breaking compliance. Governance is becoming the moat.
  • Adobe + Semrush: Adobe is using its Semrush acquisition to build a GEO (generative engine optimization) tool, AI agents that recommend and implement changes to improve a brand's visibility inside LLM answers. Signal: SEO is forking into GEO. As discovery shifts from search results to LLM-generated answers, "rank on Google" becomes "get cited by ChatGPT/Gemini." Adobe is bundling this into its lock-in stack, worth watching as an Adobe-dependency vector.

#Category watch

Agentic CDPs: The CDP is mid-transformation from a passive profile store into an autonomous decisioning engine. Databricks (CustomerLake), Zeta+Palantir, and the incumbents (Salesforce Data Cloud, Adobe RT-CDP) are all racing to bolt agents onto unified data. The real question for buyers isn't "which CDP", it's "do I buy a CDP at all, or does my warehouse/lakehouse already do this?" For an org-builder, this is the moment to avoid committing to a thick application-layer CDP that the data platform will commoditize within 18 months.

#Stack intelligence

The "buy a clean room" decision is dissolving into "use the clean room features already embedded in your stack", retail media networks and cloud platforms are shipping native clean-room functionality, so standalone clean rooms are losing share. The same pattern is now visible one layer up with CDPs and the lakehouse. The architectural lesson: capabilities that arrive as standalone point solutions get absorbed into the platform beneath them within a couple of cycles. The buy-vs-build call for a new org should bias toward thin, swappable execution tools sitting on a fat, owned data foundation, because the foundation keeps absorbing the tools, not the reverse.

#Foundations concept of the day

First-party data strategy & identity graphs: First-party data is data you collect directly from your own customers through your own channels, purchases, app activity, loyalty sign-ups, email engagement, site behavior. It is yours, you have consent for it, and (unlike third-party cookies) no regulator or browser can switch it off. An *identity graph* is the system that stitches all those fragmented signals, an email here, a device ID there, a loyalty number, an app login, into one persistent profile of a single person across channels and devices. Why it matters at scale: with third-party cookies gone and privacy law tightening (GDPR enforcement hardening, 20+ US state laws live, EU AI Act fully applicable Aug 2026), the identity graph built on first-party data *is* the targeting and measurement infrastructure. No graph, no addressable audience. Cross-industry example: Starbucks' loyalty program isn't really about free coffee, it's a first-party data and identity-graph machine. Every order ties a payment, a location, a preference, and a time-of-day to one known profile, which is why Starbucks can personalize offers without buying a single third-party audience. An airline loyalty program is structurally the same asset: it is the identity graph, and it should be treated as core MarTech infrastructure, not a marketing perk.

#Watch list

  • GEO (Generative Engine Optimization): With Adobe now building tooling here, watch whether GEO becomes a standalone budget line or a feature inside existing SEO/content suites. The brands that figure out how to be cited inside LLM answers will own a discovery channel competitors can't see yet.
  • The lakehouse-as-CDP shift: Track whether Snowflake responds to Databricks CustomerLake with its own native agentic CDP. If both warehouse giants ship one, the independent CDP vendors (mParticle, Segment/Twilio, Tealium) face an existential repositioning, likely pivoting to "composable activation layer on top of your warehouse."

#Macro week in review

A risk-off week dominated by an AI-infrastructure cost scare and a hawkish Fed reset, partly offset by collapsing oil.

  • Equities: US benchmarks sold off into the weekend. S&P 500 closed ~7,354 (down ~2% on the week); NASDAQ closed ~25,298 (down ~4.6%), its worst stretch since April 2025, driven by a brutal chip rout, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index plunged ~10% intraweek before a partial rebound. The trigger: investors suddenly repricing the *cost* of AI infrastructure rather than the demand for it. The TSX bucked the trend, closing ~34,980 (modestly positive on the week, near its record above 35,000). Canada's index is structurally light on megacap chips and heavy on gold (Agnico Eagle, Barrick) and financials, so it sidestepped the semiconductor pain. Shopify +4.6% Friday on its Spring product rollout; Celestica -6.6% tracking US tech weakness.
  • USD/CAD: ~1.4233 (June 24). The loonie sits weaker than the early-month five-bank consensus (~1.38), a softer Canadian labour market and trade uncertainty outweighing stable commodity support. Implication for Canadian carriers: a weak CAD is a mixed bag, it inflates USD-denominated costs (fuel, aircraft, USD debt service) but flatters the CAD value of US/international revenue and makes Canada a cheaper inbound destination for US travelers this summer.
  • Oil / jet fuel: The week's biggest macro move. WTI fell ~4% Friday to ~$68.86, the lowest since February, capping a roughly -22% month. The driver is diplomatic: US to Iran progress toward reopening the Strait of Hormuz unwound the Middle East risk premium. This is a direct, material tailwind for airline cost structures, fuel is the single most volatile line in airline margins, and a 20%+ drop heading into peak summer flying is a meaningful margin gift.
  • Fed / BoC: The Fed held at 3.50 to 3.75% on June 17 (unanimous, Kevin Warsh's first meeting as Chair), but the dot plot flipped hawkish, median year-end 2026 rate now 3.8% (up from 3.4% in March), and traders are pricing a possible *hike* as early as October. This, not the hold, is what reset the cost of capital and pressured long-duration tech.
  • Macro theme into next week: Cheaper oil + a weak loonie is a near-ideal demand-and-cost setup for a Canadian carrier's summer, but a hawkish Fed and a possible rate *hike* path is a headwind to consumer discretionary demand in H2. Watch whether travel demand holds as credit gets more expensive.

#MarTech & AdTech

  • The Trade Desk vs. AppLovin, the divergence hardened: TTD down ~44% YTD (decelerating to ~12% revenue growth, 30% EBITDA margins) while AppLovin's AI-driven ad engine delivers ~24% growth at ~85% EBITDA margins (down ~17% YTD but far outperforming). The market is paying for AI-native ad infrastructure and punishing the legacy independent DSP. Signal: in adtech, "independent and neutral" is losing to "AI-optimized and closed-loop."
  • Salesforce: Continued its agentic shopping spree, new marketing agents that qualify leads, generate content, launch and optimize campaigns autonomously; Intercom rebranded to Fin and folded into the Salesforce agentic stack. Reinforces the convergence-of-martech/adtech/salestech thesis. The signal: the agent layer is becoming the product, and the platforms are racing to own the execution surface.
  • Adobe: Experience Platform continues to position as the unified creative+marketing+analytics+commerce spine, a significant lock-in surface worth watching. No new break this week, but the agentic moves from Salesforce raise the competitive pressure on Adobe's experience layer.

#AI models & APIs

  • The big shift, "tokenmaxxing" to efficiency: The week's defining AI story. Enterprises are reining in runaway token spend; the Lindy CEO publicly moved 100% of traffic from Claude to DeepSeek for cost. This is the demand-side mirror of the chip selloff, the market is questioning unit economics, not capability.
  • IPO race: Both OpenAI (potentially Sept 2026, ~$730B valuation) and Anthropic (~$965B round, confidential filing) are moving toward public markets, arguably to lock in valuations *before* AI spend rationalizes. Anthropic's run-rate revenue surpassed $30B with 1,000+ customers spending $1M+/yr.
  • Coding as the battleground: Microsoft and Google are pushing hard into AI coding to catch Anthropic (Claude Code) and OpenAI. Talent is flowing toward Anthropic (two senior Gemini contributors departing Google) while Shazeer left Google for OpenAI. The frontier-lab talent market is reshuffling.

#Travel & airline tech

  • Cabin and connectivity modernization is now table stakes: Air Canada modernizing all A320/A321 interiors with new in-flight tech; 55% of its regional jet fleet now has next-gen free Wi-Fi; A220-300s getting XL bins from March 2026; A321XLR entered service. United is the aggressor on premium (Polaris suites, 250+ new aircraft by 2028).
  • The real signal, operational AI: American, Delta, United and others are deploying predictive analytics to pre-empt disruption (instant travel waivers, proactive rerouting before hubs collapse). The signal: the competitive frontier in airline tech is shifting from hardware (seats, Wi-Fi) to *software that prevents operational chaos*, a product and data problem.

#AI Infrastructure Stack

Raw materials & hardware

  • AI memory and chip components led the selloff, the cost scare started at the hardware layer, where high-bandwidth memory and advanced packaging supply remain the binding constraints on how fast capacity scales.

Chips & semiconductors

  • The SOXX dropped ~10% intraweek on AI-infrastructure *cost* fears before rebounding. NVIDIA fundamentals stayed strong (record FY26 revenue $215.9B; Data Center compute $60.4B, +77% YoY; networking $14.8B, +199% YoY) and it trades at a relatively modest ~25x forward. The selloff was a sentiment/valuation event, not a demand break.

Cooling & energy

  • Meta locked in ~6.6 GW of nuclear across Constellation (20-yr Clinton, IL deal), Vistra (2.1+ GW Ohio/PA), TerraPower and Oklo, the clearest sign that power, not chips, is becoming the gating factor. US data center demand has surged from 23 GW (2023) to 42 GW (2026). On cooling, MIT-spinout Ferveret reported ~15% efficiency gains over state-of-the-art liquid cooling; Equinix/CPP's $4B atNorth deal earlier in the year bought Nordic liquid-cooling and heat-reuse expertise.

Data centers & cloud capex

  • Hyperscaler 2026 capex commitments stand at ~$750B, the number underwriting the entire AI buildout. The market's question this week wasn't whether the spend happens, but whether the *returns* justify it.

Stack insight

The whole week was one trade: the market repricing AI from a demand story to a cost story. Every layer flashed the same signal, chips selling off on cost fears, Meta racing to nail down nuclear power, enterprises swapping premium models for cheaper ones. The 12 to 18 month read: AI capability is no longer the constraint; the constraints are power and unit economics. Whoever controls cheap energy and efficient inference wins the next phase, not whoever has the biggest model.

#Funding rounds worth noting

  • Baseten, $1.5B Series F: AI *inference* infrastructure. The largest round of the week, and the category is the tell, capital is flowing to the layer that makes AI cheaper to run, not bigger to train. Directly downstream of the efficiency shift.
  • Mirendil, $200M seed (a16z, Kleiner, NVIDIA backing): frontier lab for AI R&D systems. Seed-stage mega-checks signal sustained appetite at the frontier despite the public-market jitters.
  • Upscale AI, $190M Series A extension (to ~$500M total); Assort Health, $120M Series C; Taktile, $110M Series C. AI captured ~81% of global venture funding in Q1; the spree continued, increasingly anchored by sovereign funds and hyperscalers rather than traditional VCs.

#M&A signal

  • SpaceX acquiring Anysphere (Cursor) for ~$60B dominated the week, a non-traditional acquirer buying a top AI coding company. This is consolidation of the AI *coding* layer by an industrial/space player, blurring the line between frontier compute, defense, and developer tooling. Combined with the Salesforce agentic acquisitions, the pattern is clear: the application and tooling layers are consolidating fast, and the acquirers are no longer just the usual software incumbents.

#The week's big bet

The market stopped asking *what AI can do* and started asking *what AI costs*. The chip rout, the Fed's hawkish flip, enterprises swapping Claude for DeepSeek, and Meta sprinting to lock down 6.6 GW of nuclear are all the same bet expressed four ways: the next phase of AI is won on energy and unit economics, not raw capability. The implication for digital leaders: design the org and the martech stack around *cost-efficient, model-agnostic execution*. Own the data layer and the agent orchestration; treat the underlying model as a swappable, commoditizing input. The teams that get cheap, reliable AI into production will out-execute the teams chasing the frontier.
