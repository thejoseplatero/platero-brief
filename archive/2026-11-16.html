#Macro signal

US Thanksgiving travel is forecast to shrink for the first time since 2008, with fuel and airfare doing the damage. AAA projects 81.5 million Americans will travel 50 miles or more between 24 and 30 November, down 0.1 percent from 81.6 million last year. Excluding 2020, it is the first decline in AAA's Thanksgiving projection since the 2008 recession, though AAA notes older forecasts used a shorter window. Gas is near $4 a gallon, a level AAA says has never been exceeded on Thanksgiving. Hopper puts the average domestic round trip at $402, up 31 percent year over year. Car trips are forecast down 0.3 percent and domestic air down 0.2 percent, while trains, buses and cruises rise about 4 percent and Amtrak expects record ridership. A Bank of America Institute report from 9 November found fewer travelers trading down from premium, and slowing airline transaction growth suggests budget travelers are skipping trips entirely. The strategic read is a split consumer: the top end keeps spending, the price-sensitive end exits rather than trades down. For product and marketing leaders, that argues against blanket discounting and for knowing which segment a promotion is actually reaching. A discount aimed at a customer who has already decided not to travel buys nothing. (Skift)

#Tech industry

  • Microsoft's Exchange postmortem shows why "just roll back" is not a recovery plan: The preliminary report traces last week's outage to a lock-contention bug introduced in a July update that only surfaced on higher-core-count gateway servers. Microsoft's load testing had focused on weaker hardware. When Teams traffic failed over on 12 November while about 14 percent of gateway capacity was offline, servers stalled and pushed load onto peers that hit the same bug, until over 80 percent were unresponsive. Because the defect had been in production for three months, a standard rollback was not viable, and engineers had to write corrective code. Automated recovery was not designed for a broad unresponsive state. Strategic implication: rollback plans age. The longer a change sits in production, the less a rollback helps, so recovery planning has to cover latent defects, not just the last release. (The Register)
  • The Nvidia story is now a trade policy story: Semafor reported on 15 November that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is flying with the president to China for APEC, while Treasury, State, Defense and USTR have pushed for months to blacklist Megaspeed, the company at the centre of Jensen Huang's exit. Commerce holds the rulemaking pen and has not acted. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on CNBC that if Nvidia were a bank, "the OCC would be in there right now." Nvidia reports on 17 November under interim CEO Colette Kress. Strategic implication: when a supplier's governance failure becomes a policy fight between agencies, the supplier no longer controls its own timeline. Any roadmap that assumes stable access to Nvidia capacity in Asia should carry a policy scenario. (Semafor via AI Weekly, La Nación/AP)
  • The AI listing window is narrowing before Anthropic's debut: The Financial Times reports the ten largest US IPOs of 2026, including SpaceX, SK and Cerebras, trade an average of 20.5 percent below their offer prices. Strategic implication: public markets are repricing AI infrastructure stories. Enterprises signing multi-year AI commitments should expect vendors under pressure to show revenue, which usually means firmer pricing and harder renewals. (FT via AI Weekly)
  • Canada's lawful-access bill faces Big Tech at the Senate this week: Bloomberg reports Meta, Alphabet and Apple are lobbying Ottawa to strip a Bill C-22 clause that would let the public safety minister issue secret six-month metadata-retention orders. Signal has threatened to leave Canada, and a Senate committee is set to hear from Meta, Signal and Tailscale on Monday. Ottawa says the bill creates no encryption backdoors. Strategic implication: Canadian firms holding customer metadata could inherit retention obligations they cannot disclose to customers. Privacy and legal teams should map what that would mean before the bill is final. (Bloomberg via AI Weekly)

#AI at the leadership level

The rails for AI agents as customers went live in one week, and most companies were opted in without deciding anything. On 13 November, Sierra released Poppy 1.0, a protocol that lets a person's AI agent find a company and negotiate which actions it may take. Every Shopify store in more than 175 countries now publishes a Poppy file automatically, and Meta's Muse agent looks for one on every site it visits by default. Cisco, Delta, Intuit, Twilio and Zoom are among more than 100 launch companies. The same day, OpenAI announced ChatGPT Wallet with American Express, Mastercard and Visa, letting its agents buy without seeing real card numbers, with biometric approval on each purchase. OpenAI's merchant playbook asks retailers not to block agents. Its earlier Instant Checkout launched in September 2025 and shut in March 2026, and a Blend test found bot protection turned away a shopping agent at 29 of 200 retailers. Read together: the payment and discovery layers are now solved enough that the open question is policy, not technology. Leadership teams need an explicit answer to which customer actions an agent may complete, which require a human, and who owns that list. A default chosen by a platform partner is still a decision, just not one the company made. (Sierra via AI Weekly, PPC Land)

#Leadership & org design

Tech hiring data shows the talent pyramid turning into a column. The Hacker News "Who is Hiring" thread had 244 postings by 14 November, down 37 percent from 387 at the same point last year, and 2026 is averaging the fewest monthly postings since 2013. AI now appears in 51.6 percent of postings, up from 30 percent two years ago, and MCP in 4.5 percent, up from 0.3 percent a year ago. The shape matters more than the volume: junior roles sit at about 3 percent of postings, staff-level roles at about 12 percent and roughly quadrupled since 2019, and front-end postings have fallen from 27 percent in early 2022 to 6.6 percent. Organizations hiring only senior people who can direct AI are saving money now and borrowing against the future, because staff engineers are grown from juniors. Leaders should decide deliberately how many entry-level seats they keep and what those people learn, rather than letting the pipeline close by default. (HN Trends)

#Try this now

  • Tag every Google Ads click with the new AI Max match label: Google added a ValueTrack parameter, {search_term_matchtype}, that records whether a search term matched through your keyword or through AI Max. It returns "e", "p" or "b" for exact, phrase and broad, and "a" for AI Max expansions and keywordless matches. Until now, an AI Max expansion on an exact keyword looked identical to a true exact match in URL data. There is no setting to switch on. Add it to your tracking template beside {matchtype}, keeping {lpurl} at the start so final URL expansion does not throw 404s. A click where {matchtype} is e, p or b and {search_term_matchtype} is "a" is an AI Max expansion. It is worth 20 minutes because the label follows the lead into your CRM and revenue data, where the interface reports never reach. Smarter Ecommerce found the share of exact match impressions served through AI Max went from about zero in January 2025 to nearly 29 percent by July 2026 across 383 million EMEA ecommerce impressions. Know which of your "exact" revenue is actually Google's expansion before you set 2027 budgets. (PPC Land)
  • Audit audiobook campaigns now that Amazon ads run inside Audible: Amazon put Sponsored Products ads inside Audible as a US open beta. Active audiobook campaigns become eligible automatically on existing bids and targeting, with no opt-out and no separate Audible bid. Audible placements are free during the beta, with no end date or post-beta price announced. Results land under "Other on Amazon," and attributed sales now include Audible purchases, which may restate earlier figures. If you publish, sell or advertise audiobooks, snapshot this month's campaign reporting today so you can separate real lift from restated attribution later, and decide now what you will do when billing starts. (PPC Land)
  • Write the brief before you hand a brand task to an agent: In a sponsored MarTech piece, Optimizely describes building account-specific landing pages for 800 prospects. Its account-based marketer spent three days writing a 17-page brief on how she would build the ideal personalized page, and that brief was loaded into the agent that produced the pages. The working principle is "process before magic": when output is generated at volume, review the instructions the agent receives, not every page it produces. Pick one repeatable content task this week, have its best human practitioner write the brief they wish a new hire had, and use that as the agent's instructions. Treat it as a vendor describing its own practice, not an independent result. (MarTech)

#Case study worth stealing

eBay, a written line between assisted shopping and agent checkout: eBay's User Agreement bars "buy-for-me agents, LLM-driven bots, or any end-to-end flow that attempts to place orders without human review" unless eBay has given prior express permission, while still allowing some AI search and agent-assisted navigation. The clause took effect on 20 February, but it bit this month when The Verge reported that Meta's Muse agent had lost reliable access to eBay listings, intermittently by 12 November. HUMAN Security had estimated Muse accounted for 72 percent of daily AI agent requests, so eBay applied the rule to the biggest agent, not a marginal one. Its spokesperson called it a "selective approach" aimed at unauthorized scraping and training on its data. The tactic any commerce, travel or booking team can adapt is to write the policy at the level of the action: which agents may browse, which may compare, and which may complete a purchase without a human review step. The gap to avoid is the one eBay left open, since it has not said which agents hold permission or how a developer applies. (PPC Land)

#Framework or mental model

Relevance has an expiration date: Julie Zwissler argues in MarTech that personalization decays. A download, a product-page visit or an event registration keeps triggering automated messages long after the customer's need has moved, so the test before every send should be "Is this relevant now?" rather than "Is this personalized?" Her checklist: audit sequences for triggers built on stale behavior, design each touchpoint around the customer's next decision rather than the next campaign step, treat a pause as a valid move when there is nothing useful to add, and judge touchpoints by whether they make the next interaction easier. She cites research with 3,300 consumers in which 81 percent ignore messages that are not relevant to them. Digital leaders can apply it to any lifecycle program by putting an age limit on every trigger: a browse or search signal older than the typical decision window should stop firing on its own. (MarTech)

#Rabbit hole

Invoca's 2026 Broken Funnel Report: A survey of 451 US marketers at companies with 100 or more employees found 44 percent of B2C marketers have conversational AI agents live in at least one channel, yet only 19 percent can reliably send agent conversion data back to ad platforms and 26 percent can tie an agent conversation to the campaign, keyword or ad that produced it. Only 27 percent use revenue or booked outcomes as the main measure of agent performance, and only 27 percent say their agents can see a customer's prior digital journey. Attribution breaks most often at dealer, franchise or local locations, then chat and phone. It is a vendor survey from a call-tracking company, so weigh it accordingly, but it is a useful hour for anyone about to report on an agent pilot: the question to answer first is whether the agent's outcomes flow back into the systems that decide media spend. (MarTech)

When the vendor that runs a brand's email, search and personalization also scores its odds of being cited by an assistant, AEO stops being an SEO side project and becomes a line in the customer engagement platform contract.   • Google added a parameter that shows when an "exact match" click was really AI Max: The new ValueTrack parameter {search_term_matchtype} returns "e", "p" or "b" for exact, phrase and broad, and "a" for AI Max broad expansions and keywordless matches. The older {matchtype} parameter labels an AI Max expansion with the keyword's own match type, so those clicks looked like exact match in URL data. Smarter Ecommerce found the share of exact match impressions served through AI Max expansions rose from about zero in January 2025 to nearly 29 percent by July 2026, across 383 million EMEA ecommerce impressions. The help page carries no date, and parts of Google's guidance are inconsistent. Signal: The parameter changes nothing about matching, bidding or price. It only restores the ability to measure downstream, in analytics, CRM and call tracking. Teams that pass click parameters into their own data layer can now separate keyword intent from platform-inferred intent. Teams that rely on the platform UI cannot. (PPC Land)
  • Amazon put Sponsored Products ads inside Audible, free during beta: Open beta in the US since 9 November for vendors, managed-service and self-service advertisers, and authors. Active audiobook Sponsored Products campaigns serve on Audible automatically with existing bids and targeting, with no separate Audible bid, budget or exclusion. Results roll into the "Other on Amazon" placement, and attributed sales now include Audible purchases, which may restate earlier figures. Amazon has not said how Audible will be priced after the beta. Signal: Another owned surface absorbed into the same campaign by default. The pattern is the retail media playbook applied across a subscription property: no setup, no separate control, a blended placement line. Measurement gets harder each time a surface is added without its own row. (PPC Land)

#Foundations concept of the day

Keyword match types: In search advertising, an advertiser bids on keywords, and a match type tells the platform how closely a person's actual search has to resemble that keyword before the ad can show. Exact match is the tightest: the search must mean the same thing as the keyword. Phrase match allows searches that include the keyword's meaning with extra words around it. Broad match lets the platform show the ad for anything it judges related. Match types matter at scale because they are the main lever separating what the advertiser chose to pay for from what the platform decided was relevant, and that split drives cost, lead quality and reporting. Over the last few years platforms have loosened every type, so "exact" now includes close variants and inferred intent, and AI features such as Google's AI Max can serve ads on searches no keyword covers. A travel example: a hotel group bidding exact match on "downtown Toronto hotel" may now pay for a click on a broader "best area to stay in Toronto" query. Without a parameter like {search_term_matchtype} passed into its own analytics, the booking report would credit that sale to the exact keyword. (PPC Land)

#Macro week in review

Hot inflation, a 10-year Treasury above 5 percent, a weaker loonie and $88 to $96 crude all hit at once. Meanwhile the most important company in the AI stack lost its founder-CEO in midweek.

It jumped 0.67 percent to 36,411.72 on Wednesday, fell 0.75 percent to 36,137.19 on Thursday as gold dropped 2.8 percent, then finished Friday nearly flat near 36,106 (Trading Economics). Canadian Press reported the prior Friday's level at about 35,789, which puts the week up roughly 0.9 percent. The November relief on fuel bills is real but fragile. It depends on supply rebuilding faster than the Strait of Hormuz risk premium grows.

Fed and Bank of Canada: Markets fully price a quarter-point Fed hike at the 8 to 9 December meeting, after both the hot CPI print and October PPI rising to 6.5 percent year over year, the highest since November 2022. Governor Waller had said core CPI "around 0.3% or higher" could justify a half point (CBS News). The Fed's financial stability survey found 87 percent of market contacts naming AI as a salient risk, up from 50 percent in the spring (CNBC via AI Weekly). In Canada, the Bank of Canada hiked to 2.50 percent on 28 October and decides next on 10 December. Monday's softer core CPI pulled back expectations of a December hike, and Governor Macklem says decisions are now "meeting-by-meeting" (Trading Economics).

Consumer data: The most recent sentiment reading is the University of Michigan preliminary November index, released 6 November. It rose to 51.0 from 48.0, a three-month high, as year-ahead inflation expectations fell to 4.3 percent from 4.7 percent (Investinglive). The final reading comes 20 November. Travel demand is already cracking at the edges. AAA forecasts 81.5 million Thanksgiving travellers, down 0.1 percent and the first projected decline since 2008 outside of 2020, with domestic air travel down 0.2 percent. Hopper puts the average domestic Thanksgiving round trip at $402, up 31 percent (Skift).

Theme for next week: Fares are rising faster than demand. When fuel costs are passed through to fares and consumers face 5 percent Treasury yields, holiday volume stalls even as sentiment improves. Airlines will lean on yield over volume into December. Loyalty and ancillary revenue become the margin buffer.

Stack insight

Demand for compute is not the constraint. Applied, Cisco and Google's TPU backlog all confirm it is accelerating. The constraints are power, permitting and the price of money. With Treasuries above 5 percent, 40-year hyperscaler bonds, neocloud losses and a stalled GPU-collateral market, AI capacity over the next 12 to 18 months will go to whoever holds contracted megawatts and cheap balance sheets. Expect inference prices to stay firm and capacity to be rationed toward the largest buyers. Enterprise buyers should not assume the cost curve keeps falling on schedule.

#Funding rounds worth noting

  • Mirendil, $1 billion Series A at $5 billion: Founded by researchers from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind and xAI to automate frontier AI research. It came five months after a $200 million seed (AI Weekly). Investors are now paying for automating AI research itself.
  • LangChain, $300 million Series C at $3 billion: Led by CapitalG, more than double last year's valuation (AI Weekly). The orchestration layer between models and enterprise systems is holding value.
  • Imprint, $130 million at about $1.9 billion: Agent-ready co-brand cards (Upstarts Media). Loyalty payments are being rebuilt for agents.
  • Dreamteam, $53 million: AI-native CRM (AI Weekly).
  • Xilect, RMB 500 million angel round: Robotics chip joint venture of MetaX and UBTech (TechNode). China is funding its own silicon for embodied AI.

#M&A signal

The deal of the week is OpenAI's acquisition of Odessia (Skift). Model labs are moving downstream into transactional verticals, and travel comes first because it is high-value, high-frequency and already API-addressable. Tripadvisor's unwinding of its experiences merger shows the opposite: incumbents de-consolidating because combinations did not pay (Skift). In MarTech, Salesforce's pending Braze deal continues the suite consolidation of engagement into CRM (CX Today). Google is reportedly chasing TypeSafe's eight-week-old Jev, and acqui-hires of very young AI teams keep going (AI Weekly).

#The week's big bet

OpenAI buying a booking agent the same week Imprint shipped an MCP server for co-brand rewards is the clearest sign of where this is going. The AI layer intends to own the moment of purchase in travel, and the loyalty and payment rails are already being rebuilt so agents can transact against them. Nvidia's leadership shock and the 5 percent Treasury show the infrastructure underneath is getting more expensive and more fragile. The bet on agents taking over the transaction keeps getting funded anyway. For digital leaders in travel and MarTech, the next 12 months come down to whether an agent can price, book and credit loyalty directly. Airlines that cannot be booked that way will lose the customer relationship to whoever can.

#The convergence watch (loyalty data → media networks)

IAG launched IAG Media in London on November 12, putting British Airways, Iberia, Aer Lingus, Vueling and LEVEL under a single advertising sales brand pitched at 122 million travellers a year. Until now each airline ran its own ad strategy, mostly selling pre-roll on seatback screens through outside vendors. IAG Media takes distribution in-house and sells inflight entertainment, the Starlink Wi-Fi login portal, airport lounges, onboard magazines and a generative-AI creative studio as one package (Aviacionline, Paddle Your Own Kanoo). The unit is led by Nacho Tovar, formerly Iberia's director of innovation and digital transformation, whose stated goal is "to make IAG Media the benchmark media platform in the travel sector." IAG Media CCO Josh Peckham frames it as "one unified story" across the five carriers, with targeting by destination and traveller profile (Adweek). IAG calls the model capital-light, and the press coverage does not disclose ad tech partners, measurement methodology or revenue targets.

The detail that matters most is the template IAG says it is copying. IAG Media is explicitly modelled on IAG Loyalty, the Avios business, whose operating profit rose 17.7 percent to £391 million from January to September 2026 while British Airways' operating profit fell 8.8 percent and Vueling's fell 17.4 percent over the same period (Aviacionline). That is the convergence thesis stated as a group structure: the loyalty currency business is already outperforming the flying business, and the group is now building a second non-flying unit on the same audience. A media network spun up as a sibling to a separately run loyalty company is a different organisational bet from United's, and it is the one worth studying for any carrier where loyalty sits as its own P&L.

The new inventory is the Starlink login page. IAG is installing Starlink on more than 500 aircraft and targets 50 percent of its long-haul fleet by end of 2026, with Wi-Fi free on four of the five airlines. A free connectivity session that requires a login is an authenticated, in-environment, multi-hour impression, and it converts what was a cost centre into the media network's anchor placement. Every carrier rolling out free Wi-Fi tied to loyalty login is building the same asset whether or not it has a media team yet.

Context that sharpens the read: last month United reorganised Kinective Media, folding its media sales and operations team into the MileagePlus partnerships team after a string of leadership departures, with CCO Andrew Nocella writing "This puts our partner strategy and media capabilities together" (Adweek). The category's first mover has concluded that media and loyalty partnerships are one team, and the newest entrant launched with media modelled on its loyalty company from day one. Both are converging on the same answer: the media network is a loyalty partnership product with more inventory, not a standalone ad business.

#Airline loyalty & digital

  • American AAdvantage / World of Hyatt: The partnership ended November 15. Members who had not linked accounts by 11:59 p.m. CT lost the ability to redeem AAdvantage miles for Hyatt rewards, with linked members keeping some redemption and status-earning access through February 28, 2027 (TheTravel). Signal: a reciprocal airline-hotel tie unwinding is a reminder that partner earn and burn is now evaluated on economics, not breadth. The link-by-deadline mechanic is the useful pattern: it converts a partnership exit into a data capture event, rewarding members who connect identities and quietly retiring those who never engaged.

#Hotel & travel loyalty

  • Accor and Hard Rock (November 9): Properties across four Hard Rock brands can opt in to the ALL Accor booking and loyalty platform, gaining access to more than 125 million ALL Accor members, with point conversion and status matching with Unity by Hard Rock proposed but no rates, tiers or timetable published. Hard Rock keeps its own direct channels, and Ennismore will help expand the brand internationally (Yahoo Travel). Worth watching: this is loyalty-as-distribution, a member base licensed to a brand that does not want to be absorbed. For coalition-style programs it is a template for adding high-affinity partners without full integration, with the member file as the product being sold.
  • WorldHotels Rewards (announced November 16): Best Western Rewards and legacy WorldHotels Rewards merge into one program launching January 5, 2027, covering 70 million members, more than 4,000 hotels and 18 brands. Tiers collapse to Bronze, Silver (5 nights) and Gold (20 nights), points never expire, and free status match from other hotel programs is built in. Existing Diamond and Diamond Select members are mapped to Gold with grandfathered bonuses (PR Newswire). Worth stealing: a 5-night first elite rung paired with milestone bonuses at 5, 10, 20 and 30 nights is engineered for the mid-frequency traveller the big programs underserve. Standing status match is an acquisition offer aimed at every competitor's elite base.
  • Airbnb moves on hotels (November 12): Co-founder Nathan Blecharczyk said Airbnb is expanding into hotel bookings to compete with Expedia and Booking.com, citing 650 million user accounts and saying not offering hotels "forced customers to go elsewhere" (Travel Weekly). Worth watching: a 650 million account base adding hotel inventory is another large authenticated audience moving toward full-trip intent, which is the same raw material travel media networks sell.

#MarTech

  • Attentive (November 9): Under new CEO Eric Miao, Attentive described an agentic operating model in which marketers set goals, budgets and approval rules and the system acts within them, and made its MCP server generally available so ChatGPT and Claude can build segments and edit campaigns and journeys directly (Agile Brand Guide). Relevance to loyalty stacks: once a third-party assistant can edit journeys through MCP, the engagement platform's UI stops being the control surface. Governance moves to the approval rules and the segment definitions, which is exactly where a loyalty team needs its own policy layer, independent of whichever vendor executes.
  • Twilio 2026 Customer Insights Series (November 9): 86 percent of companies believe customers are satisfied with AI customer service, against 51 percent of consumers, and 78 percent of consumers have tried to bypass an AI agent (Agile Brand Guide). Relevance: a 35 point perception gap is the warning label for automating elite service. For high-value members, the escape hatch to a human is part of the tier benefit, not a cost leak.

#AdTech & retail media

  • The Trade Desk Q3 (November 9): Revenue of $736 million, down 0.5 percent year over year, the first decline since its 2016 IPO, with about 3 points from a one-time partner deal and Q4 guidance of at least $755 million, roughly 11 percent below Q4 2025. Walmart's DSP, built on Trade Desk technology, renewed without exclusivity, and its retail data marketplace claims access to over 80 percent of top US retailers' sales (PPC Land, The Trade Desk). Relevance: the largest independent DSP is shrinking while commerce data becomes the pitch. Walmart dropping exclusivity tells every first-party data owner that the data holder, not the pipe, holds the leverage, which strengthens a carrier's hand in any DSP or clean room negotiation.
  • Shopify Campaign Autopilot (November 10): Shopify's agentic ad tool lets merchants set the price they will pay per sale across Meta, Microsoft Advertising, Snapchat and its own Shop app, with Shopify taking the media risk rather than charging per impression or click, and now extending into SEO (AdExchanger). Relevance: pay-per-sale with the platform absorbing media risk is a direct challenge to CPM-priced retail media. A travel media network that can only sell impressions will face buyers who have been offered outcome pricing elsewhere.
  • Retail media consolidation pressure (November 16): An AdExchanger column cites WARC data that Amazon and Walmart took 85.5 percent of US retail media spend in 2025, eMarketer's expectation that they capture 89 percent of 2026 growth, WARC's forecast that non-Amazon retail media growth slows to 9.8 percent in 2027, and NIQ data that only 53 percent of CMOs think networks measure adequately. It also notes Tesco, B&Q, John Lewis and Waitrose piloting dunnhumby's cross-retailer network alliance (AdExchanger). Relevance: opinion, but the numbers are the ones every airline media pitch competes against. The long tail is being squeezed, and the defensible networks will be the ones with inventory nobody else has and measurement buyers can compare.

#Strategic implication

The week's move is IAG choosing to build its media network as a sibling of its loyalty company rather than as a feature of its airlines, at the same moment United folded Kinective into its MileagePlus partnerships team. Two of the largest airline groups have now independently placed media next to loyalty partnerships, and IAG has published the reason: Avios profit up 17.7 percent while the flying businesses declined. For a CDO or VP Digital at a major carrier, the question is no longer whether to build a media network but where it reports, and the evidence now points to the loyalty partnerships P&L. The practical watch item is the Wi-Fi login page: if free connectivity is on the roadmap, its authentication flow is the anchor ad placement, and it should be designed with loyalty identity and consent capture before the media team exists, not retrofitted after.
