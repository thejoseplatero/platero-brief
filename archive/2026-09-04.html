Executive Pulse1,010 words

#Macro signal

Two labour markets diverged on the same morning, and oil is up 10 percent on the week for reasons no central bank can fix. The US added 162,000 jobs in August against a 53,000 consensus, with upward revisions to June and July and unemployment steady at 4.1 percent. Canada lost 42,000 against an expected gain of 15,000, with the public sector down for a third straight month and wage growth for permanent employees falling to 2.0 percent from 3.0 percent. Simultaneously, WTI sits near $91 after strikes on tankers in and around the Strait of Hormuz led major producers and trading houses to suspend transit. September Fed hike odds moved to 58 percent from 49.4 percent overnight, and the two year yield reached its highest level since January 2025.

The strategic read is that the rate environment is now being set by a supply shock rather than by demand, and rate policy is a poor instrument against supply shocks. Leaders should stop planning for the easing cycle. Any 2027 plan whose economics depend on cheaper capital arriving next year needs a version that assumes it does not. For anyone with a North American footprint, the more consequential point is that the two economies are no longer moving together: input costs, consumer demand, hiring conditions, and currency now diverge across a border that most corporate plans still treat as one operating region.

#Tech industry

  • OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra on September 3, and declared it crossed the Critical threshold for cybersecurity capability under its own Preparedness Framework. The model rolls out across ChatGPT tiers, the API, Azure, and Bedrock, with major gains claimed in coding, cybersecurity, and scientific discovery. Enterprise access is off by default and administrators must deliberately enable it. Strategic implication: a frontier lab has shipped a commercial product while publicly stating it meets its own highest risk classification in a specific domain, and has pushed the enable or disable decision onto enterprise administrators. That is a liability transfer as much as a safety control. Whoever in the organization flips that switch is making a risk decision on behalf of the company, and in most companies today that person is an IT administrator responding to a ticket from an enthusiastic team. This belongs on a risk register with a named accountable executive before it becomes a default on setting in six months.
  • Adobe acquired Rilo, and Nvidia raised server prices more than 15 percent. One is an incumbent buying agentic workflow orchestration rather than building it. The other is the first clear pass through of AI infrastructure cost into the price of the hardware, driven by memory supply that is sold out through 2026. Strategic implication: the capability layer is consolidating into suites at the same moment the cost layer is inflating. Enterprises that have been treating AI spend as a growing but predictable line should model what happens when their vendors reprice, because the vendors' own input costs just moved and multi year agreements signed during the land grab period were priced on a different cost base.

#AI at the leadership level

The governance question has moved from what the model can do to who is allowed to turn it on. GPT-6 Astra ships disabled for enterprise workspaces. Tenable launched a service to vet third party agents, skills, and MCP servers before deployment. Genesys and others are bundling their own AI control planes into their applications. Each of these exists because the decision rights around AI deployment are currently undefined in most organizations, and vendors are filling the vacuum with defaults.

The consequence is that governance is arriving as a set of vendor specific toggles rather than an enterprise policy. An organization running six major platforms will soon have six enable or disable decisions, six inspection regimes, and six audit trails, each owned by whichever team administers that platform. Nobody designed that. It accumulates. The board level question is not whether the company has an AI policy document. It is whether a single named executive can currently answer which agents are running, on whose data, with what authority to take action, and who approved each one. In most organizations the honest answer today is no, and the number of switches is growing faster than the ability to inventory them.

#Leadership & org design

Deloitte's State of AI in the Enterprise, drawing on 3,235 leaders, splits the field into thirds: 34 percent deeply transforming with AI, 30 percent redesigning key processes around it, and 37 percent still using it at a surface level. The finding worth acting on is structural rather than statistical. Organizations are beginning to flatten as AI absorbs routine execution, and some are merging technology and people leadership functions so that systems design and workforce design evolve together instead of in sequence. Separately, Writer's survey found 95 percent of executives saying roles and team structures are changing because of AI, and 54 percent of C-suite respondents saying adoption is tearing their company apart. The top organizational response to the skills barrier is broad workforce AI fluency at 53 percent, ahead of targeted upskilling at 48 percent.

Read together, these point to a specific failure mode. The organizations struggling are the ones treating AI adoption as a technology rollout with a training programme attached, which leaves the org chart untouched while the work underneath it changes. The ones making progress are changing reporting lines. Merging technology and people leadership is not a cosmetic move. It puts the person who decides what gets automated and the person who decides what happens to the affected roles in the same accountability, which is the only structure where those two decisions get made against each other rather than in separate meetings six months apart.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If our vendors turned on every AI capability they currently ship disabled by default, what would be running in our environment on Monday, who inside this company would have approved it, and how long would it take us to produce that list?

#Try this now

  • GPT-6 Astra, and the admin toggle that comes with it: OpenAI released Astra on September 3 across ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise, plus the API, Azure, and Bedrock, with claimed state of the art results on computer workflow tasks across professions. The detail most teams will miss is that enterprise access is off by default and a workspace administrator has to enable it, because the model meets the Critical threshold for cybersecurity capability under OpenAI's own Preparedness Framework. Worth 20 minutes this week: find out who in your organization holds that toggle, and whether anyone has briefed them on what they are deciding. If a team is already blocked waiting on it, that is a live example to bring to whoever owns AI risk, and a much better conversation starter than a policy memo.
  • Batch actions in agent browser control: Anthropic's browser use tool went generally available as a distinct toolset, giving the model the page's accessibility tree alongside the rendered view so it targets elements semantically rather than by screen position. Paired with that, computer use now supports batch actions, where several tool_use blocks arrive together, the application executes them in order, and results come back as a group. The practical technique to steal, regardless of which vendor you use: stop designing agent flows as one action per model call. Every round trip you eliminate removes latency and a chance for the model to lose the thread. Audit any browser or UI automation you are running and count how many model calls it takes to complete one logical step. If the answer is more than a handful, the flow is the bottleneck, not the model.
  • Pre deployment vetting for third party agent components: Tenable launched CyberAgents Exchange AI Inspector, which inspects community built agents, skills, MCP servers, and multi agent playbooks before they are deployed, using model analysis plus researcher review. The transferable practice does not require buying it. Most teams are installing MCP servers and community skills with less scrutiny than they would apply to an npm dependency, because the install takes thirty seconds and feels like configuration rather than code. Write down what your team currently has installed, who added it, and what data or actions each one can reach. For most teams that list has never been made, and making it takes an afternoon.

#Case study worth stealing

Adobe, buying a six person team to skip a build cycle: Adobe acquired Rilo, a startup founded in 2025 by two IIT Bombay alumni, in a deal that licences the core technology and absorbs the six person engineering team while the standalone product shuts down. Rilo's system let people without technical backgrounds describe a complex workflow in plain English and have it executed, and Adobe is folding that into agentic marketing capability across the enterprise suite. The tactic worth adapting is the shape of the deal rather than the price. Adobe did not buy a customer base or revenue. It bought a working implementation and the people who understood why it worked, in a category it had already decided it needed to own, at a point where building it internally would have cost more time than the market was going to allow. For a digital team, the same logic applies one level down: when a capability is clearly on your roadmap and clearly not your differentiator, the honest comparison is not build versus buy on cost. It is build versus buy on when the window closes. Adobe's window was measured in quarters because three different vendor categories are converging on workflow orchestration at once.

#Framework or mental model

The AI-SDLC ladder, doing to delegating to automating: a mental model for engineering and product leaders that describes three distinct stages of working with AI. First you do the work with AI assisting you. Then you delegate discrete work to agents and review the output. Then you automate whole workflows and supervise the system. The value is in what it says about the bottleneck. At each rung the limiting factor moves upward, and by the third it is no longer any single station in the delivery process. It is the quality of human judgment applied at the top, deciding what should be built and what good looks like. Where this applies for digital leaders: most teams claiming to be at the automate stage are actually at delegate, because they have agents producing output but no clear specification of what correct means, so a human still inspects every result. The diagnostic question is whether your team could tell you what a bad output looks like before seeing one. If not, adding more automation increases throughput of work nobody can evaluate.

#Rabbit hole

The reason Nvidia is raising server prices more than 15 percent on systems shipping early next year is memory, not logic. HBM is reported sold out through 2026, which quietly makes memory suppliers the gatekeepers of AI capacity expansion rather than the chip designers everyone watches. Pull that thread alongside the power story, roughly 9.8 gigawatts of nuclear capacity committed by hyperscalers across 13 deals with only about 19.6 percent of it actually flowing, and FERC's show cause orders to six grid operators covering regions serving 200 million people. What emerges is a picture where the constraint on what AI can do in eighteen months is not model research. It is memory allocation contracts and interconnection queues. Worth an hour for anyone whose 2027 plan assumes inference gets cheaper on schedule.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Adobe, acquisition of Rilo: a licence and team deal for a 2025 founded Indian startup whose product let non technical users describe a complex marketing workflow in plain English and have it executed. Adobe takes the six person engineering team and the core technology, and the standalone app shuts down. Signal: the marketing cloud incumbents have decided that workflow orchestration is a layer they must own rather than integrate. That is a change in the buy versus build calculus for everyone downstream. Orchestration bought by the suite vendor arrives pre coupled to the suite, which means the natural-language workflow layer becomes another surface that raises switching cost rather than lowering it. Anyone evaluating an independent orchestration vendor this quarter should assume every major suite will have shipped or bought one within four quarters, and should price the independent option on portability rather than features.
  • Tenable, CyberAgents Exchange AI Inspector: a security vetting service for community built agents that inspects agents, skills, MCP servers, and multi agent playbooks before deployment, combining model based analysis with human researcher review. Signal: third party agent components are now being treated as a software supply chain, with the same pre deployment inspection posture as dependencies. The marketplace model that platform vendors have been racing toward, certified agents resold inside the walls, only works if something scans what gets listed. Expect certification and scanning to arrive together in CDP and marketing cloud agent marketplaces, because a marketplace without an inspection layer is an unbounded liability the platform owner cannot price.
  • Proofpoint, SOC Analyst Agent: converts natural language questions into structured investigation findings across the vendor's own data, in private preview with general availability targeted for end of Q3. Signal: the pattern to note is not the agent, it is the scope. The agent reasons only over data the vendor already holds. That is the emerging default for enterprise vendor agents, and it quietly reinforces the walled garden, because the agent is most useful to whoever holds the most of your data.

#Category watch

Agentic workflow orchestration: this was a startup category eight days ago and is now an acquisition target. Adobe bought Rilo for it. Genesys shipped Navigator and Orchestrator into Genesys Cloud this week. Sabio partnered with Gentoro to run agentic orchestration across programmatic buying, creative assembly, and audience processing for CTV. Databricks has pushed an agentic CDP out of the data platform side. The category is being approached from three directions at once: the marketing suite coming down from the application layer, the data platform coming up from the warehouse, and the point solution coming in sideways through a channel like CTV. Whoever owns orchestration owns the sequencing of every downstream tool call, which is a more durable position than owning any single execution surface. The strategic question for a martech org is which of those three directions the orchestration layer should enter from, and the answer is usually the one closest to where the first party data already lives, because orchestration bought at the application layer will keep asking the warehouse for permission.

#Stack intelligence

Two moves this week point the same way and they are worth reading together. Adobe bought the layer that turns plain English into an executed workflow. Tenable shipped the layer that inspects agent components before they run. Neither is a model, and neither is an execution channel. Both sit in the space between intent and action, which is exactly the space that had no owner eighteen months ago. The practical consequence for stack architecture is that the enterprise now needs three things it did not budget for: a place where workflows are expressed, a place where third party components are vetted, and a place where actions are authorized before execution. Most stacks have zero of the three, and the vendors currently offering them are each bundling their version into a product the enterprise bought for another reason. The org that ends up with four orchestration layers and three inspection layers did not choose that architecture. It accumulated it, one adjacent purchase at a time. The defensible move is to name an owner for the intent to action layer now, before the fourth vendor ships their version of it into a product already under contract.

#Foundations concept of the day

Retail media networks: an advertising business run by a retailer, selling ad placements against its own shopper data and inventory, both on its own properties and increasingly off them. The mechanism is simple and it is the reason the category took $128 billion in advertiser spend in 2026. A retailer knows who bought what, because the purchase happens inside its own system. So it can serve an ad and then confirm the resulting sale in its own transaction records, which is called closed loop attribution. Everyone else in digital advertising has to infer conversion. A retail media network observes it. That difference is worth an enormous premium, and it is why the model spread from retail into every industry with a logged in customer base and a transaction record: airlines and hotels selling media against travel intent, banks against spend categories, delivery platforms against basket composition, healthcare pharmacies against refill patterns. The strategic point for a senior leader is that a retail media network is not an advertising product with a data requirement. It is a data asset with an advertising business attached, and its margin comes from the measurement, not the impression. Which means the build decision is really a question about identity resolution and transaction data quality, and any organization that cannot reliably connect an ad exposure to a purchase record for the same human being does not have a retail media network. It has inventory.

#Watch list

  • Vendor agents scoped to vendor data: Proofpoint's SOC agent, Specter's private markets research agent, and the Genesys suite all reason primarily over data their own platform holds. If this becomes the norm, the agent layer will re fragment enterprise data along vendor lines just as CDP consolidation was starting to undo it, and the cross vendor question will get answered by whichever agent has the most context rather than the best reasoning.
  • Nvidia's server price increase passing through to SaaS pricing: server prices are rising more than 15 percent on systems shipping early next year, driven by memory cost, with HBM sold out through 2026. Application vendors currently pricing agents per seat or per action are absorbing that increase. The first martech vendor to reprice an AI feature explicitly on compute cost rather than value will mark the end of the land grab pricing period, and every multi year contract signed before that point should be read for what it says about cost pass through.

#Macro pulse

Canada: the August Labour Force Survey landed at the same hour as the US print and went the other way. Employment fell 42,000 against a consensus gain of 15,000, with full time down 35,900 and the public sector shedding 20,000 for a third consecutive month of losses. Unemployment held at 6.4 percent only because the participation rate slipped to 65.0 percent. The line that matters most is wages: average hourly earnings for permanent employees grew 2.0 percent year over year, down from 3.0 percent and below the 3.0 percent estimate. That is a demand signal, not a labour supply story. USD/CAD sat near 1.3806 into the release, down about 0.35 percent on the week on a softer US dollar, a hawkish Bank of Canada September message, and firm crude. A weaker loonie flatters reported international revenue for Canadian carriers, but 2.0 percent wage growth against 6.4 percent unemployment is the domestic discretionary travel demand picture, and it is deteriorating.

USA: nonfarm payrolls came in at 162,000 against a 53,000 to 55,000 consensus, the strongest month in five, with unemployment steady at 4.1 percent and average hourly earnings up 0.3 percent in line. Prior months were revised up, July from a loss of 23,000 to a gain of 21,000 and June to a gain of 31,000. The household survey added 569,000 employed and 683,000 to the labour force. Restaurants and bars led at 59,000 and government education added 42,000. Fed funds futures repriced a September hike to 58 percent from 49.4 percent the prior day, and the two year yield hit its highest since January 2025. Futures were mixed rather than sharply lower, which is the market splitting the difference between a strong economy and a more expensive one. This comes one day after equities posted their best session in a month on Fed governor Waller leaning toward holding.

Asia: Hang Seng closed up 1.75 percent while the Shanghai Composite slipped 0.30 percent, continuing the pattern where offshore China exposure outruns the mainland tape. Regional benchmarks traded mixed following the technology led Wall Street rally.

Oil: WTI around $90.56 to $91.80, up roughly 10 percent for the week. The cause is not demand. Two supertankers exiting the Strait of Hormuz were struck by projectiles late on August 31, the US struck two Iranian tankers on September 2, and Iran responded with roughly 25 ballistic missiles and drones at bases in Bahrain, Kuwait, and Erbil. Major producers and trading houses have suspended shipments through the strait, and loaded supertankers including the Eagle Veracruz and the Front Beauly have slowed or stopped near the approaches. A 10 percent weekly move in jet fuel input cost is the single largest number in this brief for airline margins, and it arrives in the same week that a hot US labour print raised the odds the Fed responds to inflation with a hike rather than relief.

#Today's industry move

Adobe acquired Indian marketing intelligence startup Rilo in a licence and team deal, absorbing a six person engineering team and shutting down Rilo's standalone app. Rilo, founded in 2025 by IIT Bombay alumni, let non technical users describe complex marketing workflows in plain English and have them executed. Terms were undisclosed. This is the second Indian acquisition for Adobe after Rephrase.ai in 2023, and it is a talent and capability buy aimed squarely at agentic workflow orchestration inside the enterprise suite. The signal for anyone negotiating with a marketing cloud vendor: the incumbents are buying the orchestration layer rather than building it, which means the layer is arriving faster than most stack roadmaps assume and it will arrive already coupled to the suite.

#AI infrastructure watch

The cost of the buildout is now being passed through. Nvidia has told major customers that prices on servers containing its AI chips are rising more than 15 percent in many cases, effective on systems shipping early next year, including Vera Rubin and Grace Blackwell configurations. The stated driver is soaring memory cost, and the HBM market is reported sold out through 2026, which makes memory suppliers the gatekeepers on capacity expansion rather than the logic vendors. Separately, Nvidia widened its partnership with MediaTek alongside a $3.9 billion overseas convertible bond sale largely bought by Nvidia, deepening the custom silicon and data centre tie.

Power remains the harder constraint. Hyperscalers have committed roughly 9.8 gigawatts of nuclear capacity across 13 deals, and only about 19.6 percent of that committed power is actually flowing. FERC's June 18 show cause orders to six grid operators, covering regions serving about 200 million Americans, required tariff justification or reform within 60 days and reliability reports within 30. NRG is closing on a 1.2 gigawatt hyperscaler deal against a Texas data centre pause. Chips can be bought at a 15 percent premium. Electrons on a signed interconnection cannot.

#Signal or noise?

Signal, and it is a squeeze from both ends: a US labour market strong enough to justify a hike is colliding with a supply driven oil shock the Fed cannot fix with rates, while Canada's own labour market contracts and wage growth falls to 2.0 percent, which sets up a policy divergence that will show up in the currency long before it shows up in bookings.

#Watch tomorrow

Whether Hormuz transit resumes. The equity and rates reaction to payrolls is a one week story that the September 15 to 16 FOMC will settle, but a sustained shipping suspension turns a 10 percent weekly crude move into a structural fuel cost reset, and every Q4 capacity, fare, and ancillary revenue assumption built at $80 crude has to be rerun at $95.
