Executive Pulse558 words

#Macro signal

Central banks are tightening into an equity drawdown, all at once. The Fed's minutes leaned toward a hike, Japan's core inflation hit 2.2 percent and pushed December Bank of Japan hike bets higher, and Canada's October CPI held at 3.1 percent, keeping a Bank of Canada hike live for 9 December. Markets answered with a sixth straight S&P 500 loss, a 2 percent drop on Thursday, and a 5 percent fall in the Nikkei on Friday. The strategic point: the usual cushion of rate cuts arriving when equities wobble is not on offer this time. For tech, product and marketing leaders, 2027 planning should assume tighter credit, a softer discretionary consumer and finance partners who will ask harder questions about AI spend that has not yet shown up in margin.

#Tech industry

  • Anthropic is now a public company worth about $1.76 trillion: Shares closed day one at $97.17, 17 percent above the $83 offer, after opening 34 percent higher. Strategic implication: the most important AI vendor in many enterprise stacks now reports quarterly, discloses risk factors and faces public pressure on margins. Vendor diligence can now use public filings instead of press releases, and pricing behavior will start to answer to investors.
  • Frontier labs are building their own infrastructure layer: Reporting this week describes Anthropic leasing more than 1GW of dedicated U.S. capacity to reduce reliance on Amazon, Google and Microsoft, which are simultaneously its suppliers, investors and competitors. Implication: concentration risk in the AI stack now runs in both directions, and enterprise buyers should map which hyperscaler sits under each model they depend on.

#AI at the leadership level

Chief AI Officers can see the value but mostly cannot move the money. IBM's Institute for Business Value surveyed 1,667 senior AI leaders across 33 geographies: only 18 percent have full control over AI investment decisions, 15 percent have full authority over AI workforce strategy, and 75 percent say governance, risk or compliance delays AI-driven action. Only 29 percent say their organizations reallocate resources quickly when performance data shows an opportunity. The high-impact group delivers AI ROI at more than double the rate of peers, and nearly all of them have AI embedded directly in workflows, against 59 percent of everyone else. Deloitte's board survey the week before found the same gap one level up: 49 percent of directors report limited or no oversight of how AI money splits between infrastructure and workforce enablement. The title exists. The budget authority mostly does not.

#Leadership & org design

HubSpot is reorganizing product teams around customer outcomes instead of product lines. Under returning CEO Brian Halligan, the company says it will stop organizing around individual Hubs and expects $65 to $75 million in restructuring charges, mostly in Q4 2026. The move matters beyond CRM: when AI agents can cross product boundaries in a single workflow, an org chart drawn around products creates handoffs the customer feels and the agent cannot see. Organizing by outcome is expensive and political, which is why most companies wait for a CEO change to do it.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If the AI budget had to be defended in a flat-revenue, higher-rate 2027, which three initiatives would we fund again, and who in this room actually has the authority to stop the rest?

#Try this now

  • Build a 2027 scenario plan in Google Analytics that compares named ad platforms, not channel groups: On 19 November Google Analytics said its cross-channel budgeting (in beta since January) now supports source platform dimensions, so projection and scenario plans can put individual platforms side by side. It needs a year of campaign data from at least one Google and one non-Google channel, runs on desktop only, and moves no money: any shift still has to be made by hand in each ad account. Scenario curves freeze when the plan is created, so build a fresh one after each major change. Use it this week as a challenger to your current 2027 media split, not as the answer, because the model is run by Google, which is also one of the platforms being ranked. If its recommendation moves money toward Google, ask for an independent read before acting. (PPC Land)
  • Check whether your photo and model releases cover AI-generated use: A model filed suit in federal court in California on 18 November alleging that a menswear brand, after changing owners, turned 2021 shoot photos (paid in clothing, no signed release) into AI-generated images and video for Instagram ads. The claims include false endorsement and right of publicity, and he asks for an injunction against any AI depiction without written consent. These are allegations, but the exposure is common: content archives bought with a company, or licensed years ago, rarely mention synthetic reuse. Spend 20 minutes with legal listing which archives your creative team or agency feeds into generative tools, and whether the releases name AI or derivative use. An "AI-generated" label does not supply consent. (PPC Land)
  • Replace one Black Friday discount send with a guarantee: A MarTech piece published 20 November lays out a no-discount holiday playbook: member first access and full-price bundles instead of percentage-off emails, points that buy priority shipping or a returns line, carrier-cutoff arrival dates on product pages, a price-match guarantee through 24 December, and returns extended to 31 January. The supporting data is survey-based (Narvar and eTail Insights found 70 percent of shoppers call an accurate delivery date at checkout very important; Kenco found 82 percent rate return policies as important) and the retailer example is hypothetical, so treat it as a test. Pick one send in next week's calendar, swap the discount for a guarantee, and compare margin per order, not just conversion. The same logic applies to any loyalty program: points that buy service carry no markdown cost. (MarTech)

#Case study worth stealing

Walmart, pricing its owned traffic as media: Walmart's global ad sales rose 25 percent in the quarter to 31 October, its slowest pace in two years, but Walmart Connect in the US grew 36 percent, and CFO John David Rainey said US ecommerce is "pretty close to break-even without advertising." Read that the other way round: advertising is what turned the online business profitable. The approach was to treat every search, product page and app session as inventory with a price, and to sell suppliers access to the shopper at the moment of choice. Any digital organization with high-intent first-party traffic (a booking flow, a loyalty app, a marketplace) can run the same exercise: estimate what a partner would pay to reach that audience at that moment, and set it against the cost of running the channel. The counterweight is in the same data: Walmart's international ads fell 15 percent, so the model works where the audience is large and the purchase intent is clear, and not automatically elsewhere. (PPC Land)

#Framework or mental model

If you can change it, you are accountable for it: IAB Tech Lab's SupplyChain object 1.1, published 18 November, widens who must be listed in the chain of companies handling an ad request. The test it uses is capacity, not behavior: "if you have the ability to overwrite something that was already there... you will become a node," even if you never do and never get paid. The same test works well beyond ad tech. When mapping an AI agent workflow, a content pipeline or a customer data flow, list every system and vendor that could alter the data or output, not just the ones that are supposed to, and give each one a named owner and an audit trail. (PPC Land)

#Rabbit hole

The hidden risks of auto-generated creative: A 34-minute MarTech piece from 19 November on how AI makes it "easier to publish mistakes at scale," because one flawed asset spreads across ads, email and every localized version at once. It walks through public cases, including Sports Illustrated's product reviews published under AI-generated author headshots, and ends with a seven-part control framework covering review depth, rights checks, asset records, vendor terms and incident response. The legal points are general and depend on jurisdiction, but the controls make a solid starting checklist for any team scaling generative creative in 2027. (MarTech)

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Bending Spoons agreed to buy Mailchimp from Intuit for $3.68 billion: Announced 19 November, the price is less than a third of the roughly $12 billion Intuit paid in 2021, and Intuit will put $368 million of the proceeds back into new Bending Spoons shares. Closing is expected in early 2027, subject to regulatory approval. Mailchimp's quarterly revenue was reported at $313 million, down 1 percent. Bending Spoons already owns Airtable, Eventbrite, Evernote, Vimeo, WeTransfer and Brightcove, and is known for buying mature software, cutting costs and raising prices. Signal: Standalone email and SMB marketing automation is being priced as a cash-flow asset, not a growth platform. Any team on a mature point tool owned by a cost-focused buyer should plan for price increases, slower roadmaps and support changes, and check contract terms on renewal pricing and data export now. (MarTech, PPC Land)
  • Databricks announced CustomerLake Conversions, sending value signals to nine ad platforms: Brands can define conversion events in the warehouse (for example, a first purchase from a customer with high predicted lifetime value) and push them, with audiences, to Amazon Ads, Google, LinkedIn, Meta, Microsoft Ads, Reddit, Snapchat, The Trade Desk and TikTok through conversion APIs and reverse ETL. Results come back into Databricks. Epsilon, Merkle and Omnicom are agency partners. No availability date, pricing, customers or performance data were given. Signal: The data warehouse is moving into the activation layer that CDPs and tag managers used to own. The value signal that platform bidding models learn from is now being produced where the customer data already lives. (PPC Land)
  • AWS made RTB Fabric Modules generally available: Third-party modules now attach directly to the bid traffic running on AWS's real-time bidding network, filtering or enriching requests before they reach the buyer, and are billed through the AWS invoice. Launch modules come from Pixalate (invalid traffic blocking and CTV show data), WURFL (device profiles) and Picnic (inventory quality). Limits include two modules per flow by default, and vendor modules default to passing traffic through if they fail. Signal: Ad verification and data enrichment are being bought as infrastructure, inside the cloud, rather than as standalone SaaS. The cloud provider becomes the marketplace, the network and a customer at once, which is worth a line in any vendor risk review. (PPC Land)

#Category watch

Purchase-data measurement: On 19 November Circana became a lead partner for The Trade Desk's Open Measurement, a sales measurement framework due this quarter, with Circana's purchase data feeding both audience building and sales lift measurement, two days after Attain was named its first data partner. Retail media has always had this closed loop. Open-web and CTV buyers are now getting it from third-party transaction data. The unresolved issue, which PPC Land raises directly, is that the same dataset choosing the audience and then grading the campaign can flatter the results. For a martech org, the practical rule is to keep the measurement source independent of the targeting source on at least a share of spend, or hold out a control group the vendor cannot see. (PPC Land)

#Stack intelligence

The budget model and the signal pipeline are the two layers to own. In one day, Google Analytics added platform-level budget scenarios run on Google's own model, and Databricks pitched itself as the source of the value signals every platform bids on. Both are claims on the same decision: who tells the organization where the next dollar goes and what a good customer is worth. If the planning model sits with a media seller and the conversion values are defined inside each ad platform, the brand is renting its own judgment. The architecture pattern emerging is to define customer value (margin, lifetime value, cancellation-adjusted revenue) once in the warehouse, push it to every platform from there, and use platform-native planners as one input next to an independent mix model, not as the plan. (PPC Land, PPC Land)

#Foundations concept of the day

Reverse ETL: ETL (extract, transform, load) is the traditional process of pulling data out of operational systems such as a booking engine, point of sale or app, cleaning it, and loading it into a central data warehouse for analysis. Reverse ETL runs the other way: it takes modeled data out of the warehouse (a segment, a lifetime value score, a churn risk flag) and syncs it back into the tools that act on it, such as the email platform, the CRM, the call center or an ad platform's conversion API. It matters at scale because it lets one definition of "high-value customer" drive every channel, instead of each tool keeping its own copy and its own logic, which is the usual cause of a customer getting a win-back offer the day after a big purchase. It is also the core of the "composable CDP" approach, where the warehouse holds the customer profile and a lighter sync layer handles activation. Cross-industry example: a subscription streaming service computes churn risk in its warehouse each night and syncs it to its email tool and to Meta, so at-risk subscribers get a retention offer while ad spend is shifted away from people already likely to stay. Databricks' CustomerLake Conversions, announced this week, is reverse ETL pointed at ad platform bidding. (PPC Land)

#Watch list

  • AI answers now sit on most ecommerce searches: BrightEdge reports Google AI Overviews appear in 85 percent of US ecommerce searches, up from 24 percent in April, with Amazon, Walmart and Best Buy named in 39, 13 and 8 percent of retailer-naming shopping answers. The figures are vendor-reported with no sample size, but the direction matches the click-loss research of recent weeks. In the same news cycle Profound put its AI visibility tracking inside Microsoft Teams, a sign this category is moving from a dashboard into daily workflow. (Agile Brand Guide)
  • Ask YouTube on the living room screen: YouTube's quarterly TV update says its conversational AI search, desktop-only in July, now runs on TVs and game consoles, with AI video summaries on the watch page. YouTube held a record 15.8 percent of US TV viewing in Nielsen's August Gauge. TV discovery is shifting from browsing and keywords to questions, which changes how video content needs to be titled and described. Nothing has been said yet about ads in these answers. (PPC Land)

Markets Signal589 words

#Macro pulse

Canada: The S&P/TSX Composite fell 549.32 points, or 1.59 percent, to 34,028.73 on Thursday, a second heavy day after Wednesday's 2.09 percent drop took it to its lowest close since June. Materials and technology led, and decliners outnumbered advancers by more than five to one. October CPI held at 3.1 percent with core trim and median at 1.8 and 2.0 percent, so the Bank of Canada's 9 December decision is now a genuine hike-or-hold debate, with unemployment up to 6.7 percent arguing for a hold.

USA: The early rally reversed into a broad rout. Dow down 1,101.11 points, or 2.2 percent, to 49,097.06, with only one of 30 stocks higher. S&P 500 down 2 percent to 7,175.83, its sixth straight loss and now 8.2 percent below last month's record. Nasdaq down 2.3 percent to 23,879.20 after trading up as much as 2 percent. More than 90 percent of S&P 500 members fell, and analysts could not point to a single trigger beyond AI positioning and Wednesday's hawkish Fed minutes.

Asia: Friday was a capitulation-style session. CNBC reported the Nikkei 225 closed down 5.06 percent at 56,436.65, hit by the Wall Street tech sell-off and a core inflation print of 2.2 percent, the year's high, which raises odds of a Bank of Japan hike in December. The Hang Seng was down about 2 percent late in the session, extending its five-month low.

Oil: WTI recovered from Wednesday's 3.2 percent fall, trading roughly $83 to $85 through Thursday as Hormuz tanker attacks offset IEA surplus warnings. Brent near $92 to $93. Lower crude than the summer peak is the one cushion airline cost lines have right now.

#Today's industry move

Anthropic closed its Nasdaq debut at $97.17, up 17 percent on the $83 IPO price. The stock opened at $111.50 and touched $115.10 before fading with the market. The $81.3 billion raise plus a $26 billion private placement from seven strategic partners, including Google and Nvidia, brings total proceeds to $107.3 billion. At the close the fully diluted valuation is about $1.76 trillion. The fade from a 34 percent pop to 17 percent on a day the Nasdaq 100 fell 2.2 percent says demand for the asset is real but not immune to the tape.

#AI infrastructure watch

  • Nvidia beat and the market shrugged: Fiscal Q3 revenue of $115.8 billion against $109.2 billion expected, Q4 guidance of $127.4 to $132.6 billion against $125.3 billion. Shares still reversed lower, with semiconductors down more than 25 percent since early October and the leadership vacuum after the CEO's resignation unresolved. Numbers are no longer the constraint on the stock. Governance and multiple are.
  • Anthropic is moving from renting compute to leasing its own capacity: Reporting this week describes more than 1GW of planned U.S. capacity under long-term leases, operated by third parties on its behalf, with Google potentially backing lease payments. This sits alongside cloud commitments of about $111 billion with Google, $110 billion with Amazon and $161 billion in largely non-cancelable Broadcom equipment leases. Model labs are becoming infrastructure tenants with balance sheets that look like utilities.

#Signal or noise?

Signal: a sixth straight S&P loss, a 5 percent Nikkei drop and three central banks leaning hawkish at once is a regime question about AI valuations meeting tighter money, not a one-day blip.

Whether the Asian sell-off carries into Friday's North American open, and how ANT trades on day two as the first real test of public demand for a frontier lab.
