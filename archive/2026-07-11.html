Executive Pulse496 words

#Macro signal

AI spend is doubling, and CEOs now own the decision: Corporations expect to roughly double AI spending in 2026, from about 0.8% to 1.7% of revenues, and nearly three quarters of CEOs now say they are their organization's primary decision maker on AI, twice last year's share. Why it matters strategically: AI has crossed from an IT line item to a CEO-owned bet, which means the accountability for return sits at the top and the tolerance for vague pilots is falling. The implication for tech, product, and marketing leaders: expect to defend AI investment in ROI terms to a CEO who now considers it their call, not yours.

#Tech industry

  • OpenAI ships GPT 5.6 and ChatGPT Work: A new tiered model family paired with an integrated AI workspace unifying chat, coding, and productivity. Strategic implication: the assistant is becoming the operating surface for knowledge work, which pressures every SaaS vendor whose value was a dedicated interface a worker no longer needs to open.
  • Accenture and Google Cloud target the mid-market: A suite of pre-built agentic solutions aimed at companies with $300M to $3B revenue. Strategic implication: agentic AI is being packaged for firms without the engineering bench to build it, compressing the capability gap between enterprises and their smaller, faster competitors.

#AI at the leadership level

The constraint on AI has moved from model capability to organizational readiness. Adoption is now bottlenecked by organizational design, fragmented data, and a shortage of implementation talent, not by whether the model is smart enough. Agency and vendor AI capability is outrunning clients' ability to approve, fund, govern, and measure it. The CDO mandate is shifting accordingly: the job is no longer sourcing technology, it is building the governance, data architecture, and operating model to deploy it responsibly at scale. The organizations that win the next 18 months will be the ones that fixed their data and decision rights, not the ones that picked the best model.

#Leadership & org design

The pattern in this week's CEO research is a widening execution gap: near-universal belief that AI agents will produce measurable returns in 2026, paired with candid admission that most organizations cannot yet approve, fund, and govern deployments fast enough to capture them. The practical read for a leadership team is that the binding constraint is internal velocity, not external technology. The highest-leverage move right now is redesigning the approval, funding, and measurement path for AI work so it moves at the speed of the capability, rather than adding more pilots to a queue no one can clear.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If our AI capability is now ahead of our ability to approve, fund, and measure it, what is the single approval or governance bottleneck we could remove this quarter that would unlock the most stalled value?

Sources: BCG AI Radar 2026: CEOs take the lead, AI news week of July 10, AI Update July 10 2026

#Try this now

  • Claude Sonnet 5 for agent workflows: Anthropic's new midsize model plans, uses tools (browser, terminal), and completes multi-step work at a fraction of frontier-model cost. If you have an internal automation stalled because the "smart enough" model was too expensive to run at volume, re-price it against Sonnet 5 this week. The economics of agentic workflows just shifted, and most teams are still budgeting against last quarter's rate card.
  • Nano Banana 2 Lite for creative production: Google's fast, low-cost image model (available to developers June 30) is built for the exact grind marketing teams hate, ad variants, product visuals, social creative at volume. Point it at your next campaign's creative brief and generate 20 variants before you brief an agency. Use it to widen the funnel of ideas, not to replace the final asset.
  • ChatGPT Work + GPT 5.6: OpenAI's integrated workspace unifies chat, coding, and productivity in one surface with the new tiered model. Worth 20 minutes to see whether it collapses the tool-switching tax your team pays jumping between a chat window, a doc, and a code sandbox.

#Case study worth stealing

Salesforce commerce customers, exposing catalogs to agent surfaces: Salesforce made its Shopper, Buyer, and Merchant agents generally available July 6, with native integration into ChatGPT and Google Search AI Mode coming. The number that matters: retailers running their own shopper agents grew sales 59% faster than those on the sidelines. The adaptable tactic is not "buy Agentforce." It is that your product and pricing data now needs to be legible to autonomous agents, not just to human browsers. Any digital org should be asking whether its catalog, inventory, and commerce APIs are agent-readable this quarter, because the shopping surface is moving from your site to someone else's assistant.

#Framework or mental model

Context engineering (write, select, compress, isolate): As agents run longer and accumulate history, the context window fills with noise and decisions degrade, a failure mode now called context rot. The discipline that replaces prompt-tweaking has four moves: write (persist context outside the window), select (retrieve only what is relevant now), compress (summarize to save tokens), isolate (give sub-tasks their own clean context). A 2026 survey found 82% of data leaders say prompt engineering alone is no longer sufficient for production. Where digital leaders apply it: any agent you deploy internally will live or die on what information it sees at each step, so treat context design as an architecture decision, not a copywriting one.

Sources: Anthropic Claude Sonnet 5 and Google image models, Salesforce Agentforce Commerce GA, Context engineering guide

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Salesforce Agentforce Commerce: Shopper, Buyer, and Merchant agents went generally available July 6, with native placement inside ChatGPT and Google Search AI Mode on the roadmap. Signal: the commerce vendors are racing to make themselves the plumbing behind third-party agent surfaces, not the destination. The buy interface is decoupling from the brand's own site, and the platform that owns the agent-to-catalog connection captures the transaction.
  • HubSpot Revenue Hub: Commerce Hub was renamed Revenue Hub with quoting, tax, financing, and payments folded in, while Breeze AI spread into global search, email composition, mobile meeting prep, and a Microsoft 365 Copilot integration. Signal: the mid-market suites are consolidating point features into fewer, agent-fronted hubs. Every standalone tool doing one of those jobs just lost shelf space inside the suite.

#Category watch

Agentic commerce infrastructure: The category forming fastest right now sits between the CRM/commerce suites and the consumer AI assistants. Salesforce, and by parallel strategy Adobe and HubSpot, are all repositioning as infrastructure for AI agents rather than interfaces for humans. The players to watch are whoever controls the agent surface (OpenAI, Google) and whoever controls the structured commerce data (the suites). The open question for anyone building a stack: when the shopper is an agent, does your martech investment make your data more agent-legible, or just prettier for a human who is no longer the one clicking?

#Stack intelligence

The consolidation pattern this week is suites absorbing adjacent point solutions under an agent layer, HubSpot pulling payments and tax into Revenue Hub, Salesforce fronting commerce with three agents. For anyone running a stack, the buy-vs-build calculus is shifting: the value is no longer in the feature, which the suite will absorb, but in the proprietary first-party data and the orchestration logic that sits above any single vendor. Own the data layer and the decisioning, rent the execution surfaces, because the execution surfaces are the part being commoditized into agents this year.

#Foundations concept of the day

Clean rooms: A data clean room is a secure, privacy-safe environment where two parties (say a retailer and a brand, or an airline and an advertiser) match and analyze their data without either side seeing the other's raw records. Only aggregated, anonymized results come out. It works by matching on hashed identifiers inside a neutral space, so a brand can learn "your loyalty members who saw my ad bought 20% more" without ever receiving a customer list. Why it matters at scale: as third-party cookies disappear, clean rooms are how first-party data gets activated across partners without violating privacy law. Real example: only 48% of US retail media networks currently offer clean room capability, which makes it a live differentiator, not table stakes, for any data owner building an ad business.

#Watch list

  • Agent-readable catalogs: the quiet infrastructure race is making product, inventory, and pricing data consumable by autonomous agents. Watch which vendors ship agent-facing commerce APIs, because that plumbing decides who captures agent-driven sales.

Sources: Salesforce Agentforce Commerce GA, HubSpot July 2026 updates, First-party data and clean rooms in retail media

#Macro pulse

Markets are closed for the weekend, so this is the read off Friday's close. The tape ended the week firmly risk-on, led by a rebound in the AI trade and softer oil.

  • Canada: The S&P/TSX Composite gained roughly 0.3% Friday to close near 35,305, adding to a solid week. Falling crude is a mixed signal for the index given its energy weighting, but the broad move was up.
  • USA: The S&P 500 closed Thursday at 7,543.64 (+0.8%) and pushed higher again Friday toward 7,575. The Nasdaq led, closing Thursday at 26,206.89 (+1.3%) on renewed AI momentum, with the Dow up to roughly 52,637. Breadth favored semiconductors and AI names.
  • Oil: WTI (August) slipped about 67 cents to near US$71.41 and Brent eased 0.4% to US$76.01, despite the ongoing Middle East conflict. For a Canadian carrier, softer crude is a tailwind on fuel cost even as it pressures the petro side of USD/CAD.

#AI infrastructure watch

The signal of the week sits below the application layer. Nvidia and startup Valar Atomics ran Blackwell AI chips off the Ward250 microreactor in Utah on July 1, the first small modular reactor to power a data center, and are weighing a 30-megawatt, nearly waterless facility using Nvidia's water-efficient cooling. This is the concrete expression of the theme that AI capex is now energy-constrained and the industry is going behind the meter to escape grid limits, following the Microsoft-Constellation and Amazon-Susquehanna nuclear pattern.

#Signal or noise?

Signal. The equity move is one week of momentum, but the energy-for-compute buildout is a multi-year structural constraint, and behind-the-meter nuclear is the clearest tell yet on what will gate AI capacity in 12 to 18 months.

#Watch tomorrow

Whether the AI-led rally has legs into next week's US CPI print, and whether crude keeps sliding despite the Middle East war, since both feed directly into airline cost and consumer discretionary demand.
