Executive Pulse418 words

#Macro signal

AI circular-financing fear reprices the chip complex: Asian semiconductors sold off hard Tuesday, South Korea leading, with SK Hynix down roughly 14.7% and Samsung down 13.4%, as investors questioned whether the wave of Nvidia mega-deals (a $500B-plus SK Group pact, ~$750B in commitments cited across recent deals) is real demand or capital circulating among the same handful of players. US chip names (Nvidia, Micron, AMD, SanDisk) slid pre-market. Why it matters strategically: the market is now stress-testing the assumption that AI infrastructure spend is durably funded. For tech, product, and marketing leaders, the implication is to pressure-test your own AI roadmap's dependency on vendors whose economics rely on a financing loop that could tighten. Assume compute may not stay cheap or abundant on the current curve.

#Tech industry

  • The system of record becomes the agent runtime: Salesforce's Summer '26 release (Agentforce GA, Tableau MCP, direct OpenAI catalog integration) signals incumbents converting their data-of-record position into the orchestration layer for agents. Strategic implication: whoever holds your operational data is trying to become the platform your agents run on. Boards should decide deliberately whether to concentrate on one runtime or preserve optionality, because this choice is a multi-year lock-in, not a tooling preference.

#AI at the leadership level

Enterprise data keeps converging on the same picture: ~72% of firms have agentic AI in production, ~61% of CEOs are actively adopting, yet ~60% lack formal governance and ~79% report challenges despite heavy investment. McKinsey's read is that value only appears after organizations change how work is organized, not when they adopt. The governance gap is the exposure a CDO should be naming out loud: production agents without an accountability and audit layer is operational and regulatory risk sitting on the balance sheet unpriced.

#Leadership & org design

The recurring finding this month: agentic AI success is roughly 70% people and change management, 30% algorithm. The specific failure mode is real and nameable, organizations have super-users producing extraordinary results but no mechanism to propagate those practices enterprise-wide. The high-performing move is to treat internal diffusion as a designed system (documented patterns, an internal registry, embedded champions), not as something that happens by osmosis once the tool is licensed.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If our AI roadmap assumes compute keeps getting cheaper and more available, what breaks in our plan if that curve flattens for 18 months, and which of our bets are quietly dependent on a single vendor's financing staying loose?

Practitioner Pulse395 words

#Try this now

  • UserTesting MCP Server: The research platform now exposes participant recruitment, test creation, and results as MCP tools that plug directly into Claude, ChatGPT, and Figma Make. The practical move: wire it into whatever agent already sits in your design workflow so a "run a 5-person unmoderated test on this flow" request fires without leaving the canvas. Worth 20 minutes to see how much of the recruit-to-insight loop you can collapse before a decision is already made.
  • Agentforce Help Agent (GA): Salesforce shipped a prepackaged autonomous service agent with guided setup, deployable in minutes, priced at a flat $2 per resolution. Even if you never buy it, the pricing is the artifact worth studying: outcome-based, not seat-based. Model your own AI feature economics against "what does one resolved job cost" instead of "what does a license cost."
  • Describe-to-agent builders: Vertesia's Studio Assistant and Coda's Docs AI both now take a plain-language spec ("an agent that checks competitor pricing each morning and summarizes it") and generate, test across models, and publish the agent. Use one this week to stand up a small internal monitoring agent you keep meaning to build. The skill to practice is writing the spec tightly, not the plumbing.

#Case study worth stealing

CVS Media Exchange, CorIQ closed-loop measurement: CVS built an AI measurement layer on top of its ExtraCare loyalty data that ties ad exposure to actual purchase, closing the loop retailers usually leave open. The tactic any digital org can adapt: the value was not the AI model, it was that they already owned a clean, consented first-party identity spine (the loyalty program) to attach it to. Before buying a personalization or measurement tool, audit whether you have a logged-in data asset worth measuring against. The model is cheap, the data spine is the moat.

#Framework or mental model

70 percent people, 30 percent algorithm: Fresh enterprise research on agentic AI adoption keeps landing on the same split, success with AI agents is roughly 70 percent change management and 30 percent technology. Organizations now have super-users getting extraordinary results but no mechanism to spread those practices. For any digital leader rolling out AI: your bottleneck is not model quality, it is the distribution system for turning one power user's workflow into the team's default. Build the spread mechanism before you buy more seats.

AI Stack Daily586 words

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Salesforce Summer '26: Agentforce Help Agent and Customer Service Portal hit GA this month, plus Tableau MCP, which lets AI agents query the analytics engine directly for grounded answers, and an OpenAI integration wiring the product catalog straight into ChatGPT. Signal: the CRM is repositioning as an agent runtime, not a database of record. When the incumbent stack of record starts pricing per resolved outcome and exposing its catalog to third-party assistants, the buying question shifts from "which tool" to "whose agent orchestrates the others."
  • Retail media platform arms race: Amazon Ads shipped Dynamic Creative TV, Walmart Connect launched self-service Meta campaigns built on Walmart shopper data, and CVS Media Exchange introduced CorIQ, a closed-loop measurement layer on ExtraCare loyalty data. Signal: retail media is consolidating around whoever owns the logged-in identity spine plus measurement. The networks are no longer selling inventory, they are selling their first-party graph as an activation and attribution utility.
  • AI-native valuation premium: AI-native MarTech firms are commanding more than 2x the M&A multiple of traditional MarTech (7.8x revenue vs a sector trading at a ~7% discount to broad SaaS). Signal: the market is pricing a hard split. Retrofitting AI onto a legacy suite is not clearing the bar, being architected around it is. Expect more acqui-hires of AI-native point solutions by suites that need the multiple story.

#Category watch

Retail media networks: The center of gravity moved this year from selling ad inventory to activating first-party data in real time, creative automation, closed-loop measurement, and audience intelligence. The players separating from the pack (Amazon, Walmart, CVS) all share one trait: a consented, logged-in identity asset the AI attaches to. For anyone building a martech org, the lesson is that a retail media network is really a first-party data business wearing an ad-sales costume.

#Stack intelligence

The practical consolidation pattern this month: enterprises are letting the system of record (CRM, commerce platform) become the agent orchestration layer rather than buying a standalone orchestration tool. Salesforce exposing MCP and outcome-based agent pricing is the tell. The buy-vs-build decision emerging is narrower than last year, build your first-party data spine and your prompt and eval discipline in-house, rent the agent runtime from whoever already holds your data of record. Owning the orchestration layer yourself is losing favor for anyone not at hyperscaler scale.

#Foundations concept of the day

Closed-loop measurement: The ability to connect a specific ad exposure to a specific downstream purchase by the same identified person, closing the loop between spend and outcome. It works when the platform holds both the exposure log and the purchase record under one consented identity, usually a loyalty program or logged-in account. It matters at scale because without it, ad budgets are optimized against proxies (clicks, reach) instead of revenue, and CFOs increasingly refuse to fund proxies. CVS's CorIQ is the clean example, ExtraCare loyalty data is the spine that makes exposure-to-purchase attribution possible. Any business with a logged-in audience, a bank, an airline, a grocer, can build the same loop.

#Watch list

  • Outcome-based AI pricing: Agentforce's flat $2-per-resolution model is an early signal that agent economics may decouple from seat licenses entirely. If it spreads, every SaaS pricing page gets rewritten.
  • MCP as the integration standard: UserTesting, Salesforce Tableau, and others are shipping MCP servers within weeks of each other. Watch whether MCP becomes the de facto connective tissue of the enterprise stack, and who controls the registry if it does.

Markets Signal353 words

#Macro pulse

  • Canada: TSX Composite closed the prior session at 35,568, up +0.56%, holding near highs and largely shrugging off the tech-led weakness elsewhere thanks to its energy and materials weighting.
  • USA: S&P 500 hovering around 7,400 (down ~0.17%), Nasdaq futures off nearly 1% pre-market as the chip sell-off deepened. Dow held up better, a rotation out of AI/semis rather than a broad risk-off. Nasdaq Composite closed the prior session at 24,932.
  • Asia: Split tape. Nikkei 225 jumped +1.92% to 41,650, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng ended flat at 17,523. South Korea was the pain trade, semiconductor names (SK Hynix ~-14.7%, Samsung ~-13.4%) led a regional chip rout on AI-financing valuation worries.
  • Oil: WTI slid back toward the mid-$80s after topping near $93 intraday, easing an input-cost worry for carriers. A tailwind for airline margins if it holds.

#Today's industry move

Salesforce's Summer '26 release put Agentforce Help Agent and Customer Service Portal into GA at a flat $2-per-resolution price, alongside Tableau MCP and a direct OpenAI catalog integration. The pricing model is the signal: outcome-based agent economics decoupling from seat licenses, a template every enterprise SaaS vendor will be pressured to answer.

#AI infrastructure watch

The whole stack repriced on circular-financing fear. Nvidia's cluster of mega-deals (a $500B-plus SK Group collaboration spanning AI factories and 2+ gigawatts of Korean data centers, ~$750B in aggregate commitments cited) triggered doubt about whether AI capex is genuine end demand or capital circulating among the same players. Memory makers took the brunt (SK Hynix, Samsung double-digit drops). This is the layer that sets what AI can do in 12-18 months, and the market just questioned its funding base.

#Signal or noise?

Signal. One-day chip drawdowns are common, but the circular-financing critique is a structural question about how AI infrastructure is capitalized, and it will keep resurfacing until deal economics are more transparent.

#Watch tomorrow

Whether US chip names stabilize or the Korean memory rout spreads to US hyperscaler capex sentiment, and any Nvidia or hyperscaler commentary that reframes the mega-deals as end demand rather than vendor financing.
