Executive Pulse492 words

#Macro signal

Middle East escalation reprices energy and risk: Fresh US strikes on Iranian targets sent WTI crude up more than 4% to roughly $75 on July 13, with equity futures soft and semiconductors leading the slide. For leaders, this is a reminder that the inflation fight is not over on the demand side, it can be reignited from the supply side overnight. May US CPI already ran at 4.2%, its highest since 2023, driven largely by energy. Any 2026 plan built on a smooth disinflation glide path needs an energy-shock branch. The implication for tech, product, and marketing leaders: model a scenario where input costs and consumer discretionary spend move against you at the same time, because an oil spike does both.

#Tech industry

  • Capital rotates from software to AI infrastructure: Adobe, Salesforce, and The Trade Desk all hit 52-week lows this month as investors pulled money out of traditional SaaS and pushed it into AI infrastructure names, even though Adobe posted record revenue of $6.62B up 13%. Strategic implication: the market is pricing a real risk that generative AI erodes the value of premium software subscriptions. Every software-dependent business should have a clear answer to "why does our seat-based pricing survive agents doing the work?"
  • The agent platform war goes to protocols: Google rebranded Vertex AI to the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform and pushed the Agent2Agent protocol into production. Strategic implication: the competitive frontier is moving from model quality to who owns the orchestration and interoperability standard. Betting your stack on a single vendor's agent protocol is a lock-in decision, treat it like one.

#AI at the leadership level

The uncomfortable number this week: roughly 95% of organizations report zero ROI from generative AI investments, and the diagnosed cause is not the technology, it is the operating model. The gap is between deployment and transformation. Notably, line-of-business leaders are now the largest AI decision-making group at 46%, ahead of CIOs and CTOs. AI budget authority is shifting out of central IT and into the functions. Enterprises where senior leadership actively shapes AI governance capture materially more value than those that delegate it to technical teams.

#Leadership & org design

The winning pattern in the 2026 studies is to treat AI as a capability and workflow redesign, not a tool rollout. Organizations scaling AI successfully are redesigning how decisions get made and how authority is distributed, not bolting a copilot onto an unchanged process. The practical move for a leader this quarter: pick one core workflow and redesign it around what agents can now do, rather than spreading a thin layer of AI assistance across everything and getting no ROI anywhere.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If line-of-business owners now hold most of the AI decision rights, what is our operating model for governing dozens of function-led AI initiatives so they compound into one capability instead of fragmenting into forty disconnected pilots?

Practitioner Pulse394 words

#Try this now

  • Hightouch Lifecycle Studio: Runs multi-stage lifecycle campaigns directly off your data warehouse with a visual builder for touchpoints, transactional milestones, and real-time audience logic. No copying data into a separate marketing cloud first. If your customer data already lives in Snowflake, BigQuery, or Databricks, map one onboarding or win-back journey here this week and compare the audience freshness against whatever your ESP is doing on a nightly sync. The activation-from-warehouse pattern is worth the 20 minutes even if you do not switch.
  • ActiveCampaign Google Ads connector for Active Intelligence: New connector pushes your first-party ActiveCampaign segments straight into Google Ads and can spin up Performance Max campaigns from them. The practical win is closing the loop between owned audiences and paid without a manual list export every week. Wire one high-intent segment into a PMax test and watch whether match rates hold.
  • Typeface Ad Agent and Video Agent updates: Ad Agent now auto-resizes a single creative into channel-specific variants, and Video Agent added scene-level editing so you can fix one shot without re-rendering the whole cut. For a lean team shipping paid social across five placements, this collapses the resize-and-version busywork that eats a designer's afternoon.

#Case study worth stealing

ActiveCampaign, closing the owned-to-paid loop from one segment source: Rather than treating email lists and ad audiences as two disconnected systems, the new connector lets a single governed segment drive both lifecycle email and Performance Max spend. The tactic any digital team can copy without buying the tool: pick your best-performing owned segment, define it once in your source of truth, and make that same definition the seed for paid acquisition. The value is not the integration, it is refusing to let "email audience" and "ad audience" drift into two different truths maintained by two different people.

#Framework or mental model

The extraction tax: Every time you copy customer data out of the warehouse into a separate marketing tool, you pay a tax in staleness, duplicated identity logic, and sync lag. The warehouse-native activation movement (Hightouch, Databricks CustomerLake, Snowflake clean rooms) exists because practitioners realized the better move is to keep the data where it lives and plug capabilities in around it. Before your next MarTech purchase, ask what extraction tax it charges and whether the same job could run against the data in place.

AI Stack Daily544 words

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Databricks CustomerLake: Launched as an "agentic CDP" embedded natively in the lakehouse, with Adstra (identity resolution) and Bloomreach as launch partners. Signal: The CDP is migrating down into the data infrastructure layer itself. First the marketing clouds absorbed CDPs (Salesforce Data Cloud, Adobe Real-Time CDP), now the data platforms are absorbing the activation layer from below. The standalone CDP is being squeezed from both directions.
  • Google Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform: Google renamed Vertex AI to the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform and folded Agentspace into one product, shipping a no-code agent builder for Workspace, 200-plus models including third-party Claude, and the production Agent2Agent protocol. Signal: The hyperscalers are racing to own the agent orchestration layer, not just the model. Whoever owns the protocol for how agents talk to each other owns the switching costs.
  • ActiveCampaign and Typeface: ActiveCampaign shipped a Google Ads connector that launches Performance Max from owned segments, Typeface added channel-specific ad resizing and scene-level video editing. Signal: The mid-market MarTech tools are converging on the same feature set, owned-audience-to-paid loops plus generative creative variants, which compresses what used to be three vendors into one.

#Category watch

Agentic CDPs: The category is shifting from "unify customer data in a dedicated platform" to "run identity resolution and activation as agents against data that never leaves the warehouse." Databricks CustomerLake, Snowflake clean rooms, and Salesforce Data Cloud are the players. The open question nobody has answered cleanly: autonomous identity resolution at machine speed creates privacy obligations, EU and US state rules on automated decision-making, that most enterprise teams have not mapped. The tech is ahead of the governance.

#Stack intelligence

The warehouse-as-CDP pattern is now the default architecture question, not a fringe one. Enterprises building a new customer stack in 2026 are increasingly deciding to keep the lakehouse as the source of truth and buy thin activation and identity layers around it, rather than buying a monolithic marketing cloud that owns a second copy of the data. The buy-vs-build line has moved: buy the model and the activation connectors, own the data layer.

#Foundations concept of the day

Data clean rooms: A clean room is a secure, neutral environment where two parties match and analyze their data against each other without either side seeing the other's raw records. A retailer and a brand can learn that the same 40,000 people bought both products and measure a campaign's lift, without the retailer handing over its customer list or the brand exposing its buyers. It matters at scale because in a world with fewer third-party cookies, clean rooms are how walled gardens and first-party data owners collaborate legally. Real example: an airline with logged-in loyalty data and a hotel chain can run a clean-room match to size their shared audience and sell a joint media package, with neither exposing individual identities.

#Watch list

  • Adstra: Its identity-resolution role inside CustomerLake is a tell for where independent identity vendors go as CDPs collapse into data platforms. Watch whether more identity specialists become embedded launch partners rather than standalone products.
  • Agent2Agent protocol adoption: If A2A gets traction beyond Google, cross-vendor agent interoperability becomes real and the orchestration layer, not the model, becomes the strategic control point.

#Macro week in review

Canada: The S&P/TSX composite held up well through the prior week, advancing 0.76% to close near 35,200 on July 9 with broad positive breadth, before slipping about 74 points Monday morning as financials and base metals led a pullback. USD/CAD stayed tied to oil, a rising crude price supports the loonie, which cushions Canadian carrier international costs even as it complicates USD-denominated revenue translation. USA: A choppy week. Semiconductors sold off hard to open the prior week (Nasdaq down 1.16% on July 7, Micron off 4.7%), then chips led a sharp rebound mid-week (Nasdaq up 1.30% July 9). Monday July 13 opened risk-off again: Dow roughly flat, S&P 500 down about 0.3%, Nasdaq down about 0.7% as Asian chip weakness in SK Hynix and Samsung dragged Micron and Sandisk. Asia: Asian chipmakers were the week's pressure point, SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics fell hard on the Monday session, exporting weakness into US semiconductor names. Oil / jet fuel: WTI jumped more than 4% to roughly $75 on July 13 after fresh US strikes on Iranian targets, though crude is still down about 7% over the trailing month. A supply-driven spike is the worst kind for airlines, it raises fuel cost without any accompanying demand strength. Rates and data: The June US CPI print lands Tuesday July 14. Consensus looks for a soft headline near 3.9% annual, but that softness is energy base effects, not underlying cooling, and May already ran at 4.2%. Q2 bank earnings (JPMorgan, Goldman, Citi, Wells, BofA Tuesday, Morgan Stanley and BlackRock Wednesday) set the tone. Theme into next week: Supply-side inflation risk from the Gulf is back on the table just as earnings season opens. Travel and consumer discretionary sit directly in the crossfire of higher fuel and squeezed sentiment.

#MarTech & AdTech

  • Adobe, Salesforce, The Trade Desk: All three hit 52-week lows this month as capital rotated out of application-layer software into AI infrastructure. Adobe touched a seven-year low near $218 despite record $6.62B quarterly revenue up 13%. Signal: the market is pricing the risk that agents erode seat-based SaaS value, the application layer is on the defensive.
  • Databricks CustomerLake: Launched as an agentic CDP inside the lakehouse with Adstra and Bloomreach as partners. Signal: activation and identity are migrating into the data platform, squeezing standalone CDP vendors.

#AI models & APIs

  • Google Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform: Vertex AI rebranded and consolidated with a no-code agent builder, 200-plus models including Claude, and the production Agent2Agent protocol. Strategic implication: the fight is moving from model quality to owning the agent orchestration and interoperability standard.
  • Anthropic Sonnet 5 and Claude Fable 5: Sonnet 5 shipped at near-Opus performance with introductory $2/$10 per-million pricing, and Claude Fable 5 was redeployed July 1 after an export restriction was lifted. Implication: frontier-adjacent capability keeps getting cheaper, which pressures per-token pricing across the board.
  • OpenAI GPT-5.6: Arrived as three tiers (Sol, Terra, Luna) plus an Ultra mode, with enterprise agent usage via Codex reportedly at three million weekly users. Implication: the majors are segmenting by cost-per-intelligence, not just raw capability.

#Travel & airline tech

  • WestJet Rewards overhaul: WestJet is moving from a currency-based to a points-based system (100 points per prior $1) and, for the first time, letting members reach up to Platinum status without flying, via RBC co-brand card spend. Signal: the direct Canadian competitor is copying the credit-card-driven status model, loyalty status is decoupling from flying.
  • Aeroplan and Club Avolta: Members now earn Aeroplan points across roughly 1,900 Avolta airport retail and F&B outlets. Signal: coalition earn surface is expanding into physical travel retail, more everyday touchpoints feeding the loyalty ledger.

#AI Infrastructure Stack

Raw materials & hardware

  • Water and siting constraints: The Nvidia-Valar nuclear demo centered on a "nearly waterless" 30MW design in Utah. Signal: water and land, not just power, are becoming gating constraints on where compute can physically go.

Chips & semiconductors

  • Semiconductor whipsaw: Chips drove both the prior week's selloff and its rebound, then led Monday's decline again on Asian weakness. Signal: the AI trade is now concentrated enough in a handful of chip names that they set the tape for the whole market.

Cooling & energy

  • Nvidia and Valar Atomics microreactor: On July 1 the two ran a live demo powering Blackwell silicon off Valar's Ward250 microreactor, described as the first small reactor to power a data center. Signal: AI capex is now energy-constrained, not chip-constrained, grid interconnection queues run five to seven years, and microreactors are the workaround.

Data centers & cloud capex

  • The $4 trillion framing: Nvidia's Jensen Huang reiterated that operators may spend up to $4T upgrading infrastructure over five years. Signal: the spend thesis has shifted from GPUs to the full stack of power, cooling, networking, and orchestration.

Stack insight

The through-line this week: the binding constraint on AI has moved down the stack from chips to electrons. Nuclear-adjacent power deals, waterless cooling, and multi-year grid queues now determine what compute comes online in 12 to 18 months. For anyone planning AI-dependent products, capacity and cost are increasingly a function of energy availability, not model releases, and energy is far less elastic than software.

#Funding rounds worth noting

  • Mobile UA agent startup, $7M Series A (led by The Raine Group): Builds user-acquisition agents for mobile game studios. Signal: agentic automation is moving into performance marketing execution, not just content generation.

#M&A signal

Ad tech and MarTech dealmaking cooled in the first half of 2026 (roughly 10 AdTech acquisitions through June versus 36 in all of 2025), with AI the one hot ticket. The recent marquee deals remain Salesforce-Informatica (about $8B) and Adobe-Semrush. The pattern: consolidation is concentrating around data and AI capability, not audience or media supply.

#The week's big bet

The clearest tell this week was capital fleeing application-layer software (Adobe, Salesforce, The Trade Desk at 52-week lows) and piling into AI infrastructure, while Nvidia demonstrated a nuclear-powered data center to solve the energy bottleneck. The market is betting that value in AI accrues to whoever controls compute and the energy to run it, and that seat-priced software sitting on top is exposed. For digital leaders in travel and MarTech, the implication is to treat the data and activation layer you own as the durable asset, and to assume the execution tools riding on top will keep getting cheaper, more agentic, and more commoditized.

#The convergence watch (loyalty data → media networks)

The defining pattern held steady this week: carriers continue to build the loyalty-data-into-ad-inventory business, led by United's Kinective Media, which now scales brand reach across the United app and inflight screens and personalizes MileagePlus member offers, with a roster spanning Norwegian Cruise Line, Macy's, Chase, TelevisaUnivision, IHG, and Dentsu. The parallel signal is infrastructure: Databricks CustomerLake launched as an agentic CDP embedded in the lakehouse with Adstra supplying identity resolution. That matters for convergence because the hard part of monetizing loyalty data as media is clean, privacy-safe identity matching at scale, and the tooling to do it inside the data layer is maturing fast. The trend line for any airline without a media network: the gap between United and everyone else widens each quarter this compounds.

#Airline loyalty & digital

  • WestJet Rewards overhaul: The closest Canadian competitor is making its biggest change in 15 years, moving from a currency-based to a points-based system (100 points per prior $1) and, for the first time, allowing members to reach up to Platinum status without flying via RBC World Elite Mastercard spend. Signal: WestJet is explicitly decoupling elite status from flown revenue and tying it to card spend, the same lever Aeroplan relies on, so the Canadian status-competition front is now a co-brand card fight.
  • Air Canada Aeroplan spend-based earning: The shift to earning points on dollars spent rather than distance flown (effective Jan 1, 2026) is now the baseline both major Canadian carriers share. Signal: distance-based earning is dead in Canada, the battleground moves to who has the richer card ecosystem and burn options.

#Hotel & travel loyalty

  • Marriott Bonvoy and United dual-currency offer: A stay-and-fly promotion (July 15 to Sept 30) lets members earn in both currencies across the partnership. Signal: cross-vertical loyalty bundling (hotel plus airline) is a lighter-weight path to the same "own more of the trip" goal that media networks chase.
  • Marriott Bonvoy points devaluation: A quiet 5 to 10% flash devaluation on award pricing plus Series-brand earning changes. Signal: even the top-ranked program is tightening economics, a reminder that dynamic point pricing is a margin lever hotels pull regularly and members increasingly notice.

#MarTech

  • Databricks CustomerLake: Agentic CDP embedded in the lakehouse with Adstra (identity) and Bloomreach as launch partners. Relevance to loyalty stacks: activation and identity resolution are moving into the data platform, which favors a "own the loyalty data layer, rent the activation" architecture over a monolithic marketing cloud.
  • ActiveCampaign and Typeface: New Google Ads/Performance Max connector and generative ad-resizing plus scene-level video editing. Relevance: owned-audience-to-paid loops and generative creative are now table stakes even in mid-market tools, raising the floor on what a lean loyalty marketing team can execute.

#AdTech & retail media

  • Software-to-infrastructure capital rotation: The Trade Desk, Adobe, and Salesforce all hit 52-week lows as investors rotated into AI infrastructure. Relevance to the convergence thesis: independent ad-tech platforms are under pressure, which strengthens the case for first-party data owners (airlines, hotels, retailers) to build media businesses on their own audiences rather than depend on the open programmatic middle.
  • Clean rooms as the convergence plumbing: Snowflake clean rooms and CustomerLake-style matching are the mechanism that lets a loyalty data owner and a brand transact media without exposing raw records. Relevance: this is the enabling layer that makes an airline media network sellable to sophisticated advertisers.

#Loyalty tech vendors

  • Adstra (via Databricks): Its embedded identity-resolution role inside CustomerLake is a build-vs-buy signal, identity is becoming a component you plug into your own data platform rather than a standalone product you route data through. For a carrier weighing loyalty-media infrastructure, the question shifts from "which CDP" to "which identity and activation layers wrap our existing lakehouse."

#Strategic implication

The two forces to watch converged this week: United keeps extending its media-network lead while the identity-and-activation plumbing (agentic CDPs in the lakehouse, clean-room matching) matures enough for followers to move. The single most important question for a carrier without a media network is no longer "should we build one" but "can we assemble the first-party data, identity, and clean-room layers fast enough before the category standard is set by United." Meanwhile the Canadian loyalty fight has narrowed to co-brand card economics as both Air Canada and WestJet decouple status from flying. Own the loyalty data layer, buy the identity and activation components around it, and treat a media network as the monetization endpoint of that data asset, not a separate initiative.
