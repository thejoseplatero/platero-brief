Executive Pulse485 words

#Macro signal

US-Iran escalation reprices energy risk: US forces struck Iranian targets late Tuesday after attacks on commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz, and the President declared the prior US-Iran understanding "over." Oil surged ~5% to ~$74 WTI, equities sold off (Dow -1.1%, S&P -0.5%), and the chip complex extended its rout. Why it matters strategically: this is a supply-shock risk, not a demand story. Leaders should stress-test plans against a sustained energy-cost step-up and a stronger-for-longer dollar, both of which compress margins and consumer discretionary spend. For travel, marketing, and product leaders, the implication is to model a scenario where fuel and input costs rise while consumer demand softens, and decide now which investments are protected versus deferred.

#Tech industry

  • Chip selloff extends: Semiconductors continued to sell off, with weak Samsung earnings failing to steady the Nasdaq. Strategic implication: after a long AI-led run, the market is separating durable compute demand from priced-in optimism. Don't read a multi-day chip drawdown as an AI-demand reversal; read it as valuation gravity meeting a geopolitical shock.
  • Nvidia's energy strategy goes physical: Nvidia and nuclear startup Valar Atomics ran a Blackwell chip on power from an advanced reactor in Utah (July 1) and are weighing a 30MW near-waterless data center. Strategic implication: the AI leaders are now vertically integrating into power and cooling. Compute strategy is becoming energy strategy, and boards should expect capex conversations to increasingly include grid and generation.

#AI at the leadership level

The C-suite is consolidating AI authority at the top while adoption strains the org beneath it. Nearly three-quarters of CEOs now call themselves their company's chief AI decision-maker (2x last year's share), and firms expect to roughly double AI spend to ~1.7% of revenue. Yet 79% of organizations report adoption challenges (up double digits), 54% of C-suite leaders say AI adoption is "tearing the company apart," and only 29% see significant GenAI ROI (23% for agents). The governance gap is stark: only 38% of large firms have a Chief AI Officer, with no consensus on reporting lines. The pattern: ambition is centralizing faster than the operating model can absorb it.

#Leadership & org design

The CDO mandate is widening from data stewardship to human-centered digital leadership. A recurring 2026 move (Forbes profiled one this month) is retitling the CIO as CDO to emphasize the human connection to technology, not just the infrastructure. The durable insight for org-builders: the winning digital leadership role is being defined by adoption and change management, not tooling. The scarce skill is getting an organization to actually use AI well, not selecting the model.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If oil stays above $70 and the dollar stays strong through the quarter, which two AI investments do we protect no matter what, and which two do we defer, and can everyone in the room name the same four?

#Try this now

  • Artifact-first AI workflows: Stop letting decisions live in chat threads. Capture every design decision, eval result, and model choice as a durable record (a Markdown log, a registry entry, a shared Project). The teams doing this report faster handoffs and dramatically faster incident response, because the "why" survives after the conversation scrolls away. Set it up once this week: one shared doc per initiative that logs decisions with dates and rationale, not transcripts.
  • Persistent shared context over siloed prompts: Claude Projects, ChatGPT Projects, Google Gems, and Copilot Studio now let a whole team work against one shared memory instead of everyone re-priming a blank chat. The 20-minute win: load your team's real context (brand voice, current roadmap, key constraints) into one shared Project and make it the default entry point. It kills the "explain the background again" tax on every AI task.
  • The AI-native product loop: Reframe the product lifecycle from staged phases (research → plan → build → measure) to a continuously running loop where signals, opportunities, validation, and prioritization are always live. Practically: pipe product telemetry and support signals into a standing weekly synthesis rather than waiting for quarterly research. It compresses the distance between a user signal and a shipped response.

#Case study worth stealing

H&M, AI live-chat deflection: H&M cut customer-service operating costs an estimated 30% annually with an AI live-chat agent, while satisfaction (not just handle time) became the metric that improved. The steal for any digital org: measure your AI service rollout by CSAT and resolution quality, not deflection rate alone. Vendor-quoted deflection (70-80%) runs 30-40 points ahead of real enterprise medians (~41%), so anchoring the business case on satisfaction keeps you honest and keeps the rollout defensible when the vendor numbers don't materialize.

#Framework or mental model

Vendor-marketing delta: The structural gap between a vendor's cherry-picked case-study numbers and the cross-program aggregate you'll actually get. In AI customer service it's a 30-40 percentage-point deflection gap. Apply it to every AI tool eval: discount the headline metric by asking "is this a best case or an average?" and budget against the aggregate, not the demo. It's a cheap way to avoid over-promising to your own leadership.

#Rabbit hole (optional)

JetBrains' new "AI for Teams" (announced July 7) is worth an hour if you run an engineering-adjacent org: it's a vendor-agnostic layer that gives shared context, reusable agentic workflows, org-wide governance, and cost management over whatever AI tools developers already use, including long-running agents in managed cloud environments. The interesting pattern isn't the IDE features; it's that "govern and share the agents your people already use" is becoming a product category of its own.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Salesforce → Informatica (~$8B): Salesforce closed its ~$8B acquisition of Informatica's AI-powered cloud data management platform (~$25/share, ~4.9x revenue on a mature ~$1.64B base). Signal: The CRM giants are buying the data-management layer, not just apps. Owning ingestion, quality, and governance is how they intend to feed agentic workflows. Data plumbing is now strategic real estate.
  • Adobe → Semrush (~$12/share, 77% premium): Valuing Semrush's ~108,000 customers at ~5.1x revenue. Signal: SEO, content intelligence, and creative tooling are converging into one stack. Adobe is betting that in an AI-search world, brands need content intelligence wired directly into creative production, not bolted on afterward.
  • Cordial launches AI-headless infrastructure: The enterprise marketing platform (Levi's, Tapestry, L.L.Bean, Boot Barn) now exposes every major platform capability as a service that AI agents can call and execute. Signal: "Agent-addressable" is becoming a platform requirement. If your martech capabilities aren't exposed as callable services, agents can't orchestrate them, and you're locked out of the automation layer forming above the app tier.

#Category watch

AI-native vs. traditional MarTech valuation split: The market is repricing the category in real time. Traditional martech traded at a ~7% discount to the broader SaaS M&A median in 2025 (3.8x vs 4.1x), while AI-native martech firms commanded 7.8x revenue, more than 2x the traditional multiple. The tell for anyone building a stack: acquirers are paying for AI-native architecture, not feature parity. Retrofitting AI onto a legacy platform does not earn the premium.

#Stack intelligence

Q1 2026 dealmaking cooled across adtech and martech overall, but the deals that did happen clustered tightly around three capabilities: incrementality measurement, identity resolution, and AI-driven performance optimization. That's the buy-vs-build tell for enterprise stacks right now. Measurement and identity are being bought (hard to build, trust-sensitive, standardizing), while performance optimization is where teams are still deciding. If you're building a martech org, treat identity and incrementality as procured infrastructure and concentrate internal engineering on the optimization layer that differentiates you.

#Foundations concept of the day

Incrementality measurement: The discipline of proving that a marketing action *caused* an outcome, versus taking credit for conversions that would have happened anyway. Last-touch attribution says "the ad got the click, so the ad gets the sale." Incrementality asks the harder question: run a holdout group that sees no ad, compare against the exposed group, and the *difference* is the true lift. It matters at scale because most attributed conversions are not incremental. A retailer running always-on branded-search ads may find that buyers would have found them anyway, meaning millions in spend is buying credit, not customers. Example: any large e-commerce brand can run geo holdouts, dark a channel in matched markets, and measure the revenue delta to size real contribution before renewing a budget.

#Watch list

  • Agent-addressable platforms: Cordial won't be the last. Watch whether the big CDP and marketing-cloud vendors ship "expose everything as a callable service" as a headline feature this quarter. It's the leading indicator of who survives the shift to agent-orchestrated marketing.
  • Data-layer M&A: With Salesforce buying Informatica, watch whether Adobe, HubSpot, or a hyperscaler responds with its own data-management or governance acquisition. The land grab for the layer beneath the apps is on.
