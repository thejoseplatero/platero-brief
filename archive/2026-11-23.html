#Macro signal

The Treasury Secretary warned the Fed in public, markets bounced, and a rate hike is still priced in. On CBS on Sunday, Scott Bessent said the Fed risks a policy mistake by overtightening and pointed to tighter financial conditions and the stock sell-off. He did not say what he wants on 9 December. The two-year Treasury yield had its biggest drop since August 2025, and by 5:19 a.m. ET Monday futures were up 2.8 percent on the S&P 500 and 3.6 percent on the Nasdaq-100, after a week in which the S&P lost 8.6 percent and about $8 trillion in value has been erased since October. CME FedWatch still shows roughly a 96 percent chance of a quarter-point hike from the current 3.75 to 4.00 percent range. The strategic read is that the bounce changes sentiment and leaves the cost of money where it was. Leaders setting 2027 budgets this month should plan on higher rates and a nervous consumer, and treat a relief rally as a reason to hold the plan steady. (Yahoo Finance, CNBC, investingLive)

#Tech industry

  • Governors asked Congress for one AI rulebook that leaves state laws standing. On 22 November the National Governors Association's AI council, led by Wes Moore of Maryland with Mike Braun of Indiana, Josh Stein of North Carolina and Spencer Cox of Utah, published six principles. Washington would handle national and cross-border risks, including independent safety reviews of advanced models and incident reporting by developers. States would keep consumer protection, anti-discrimination and workforce rules. The governors also said data centers should largely pay for their own energy. This cuts against the White House's push to preempt state AI laws, and no congressional sponsor has been named. For any company deploying AI to customers in several states, the planning assumption for 2027 is a patchwork of state rules with a federal layer added on top, so compliance should be built to the strictest state the business operates in. (Bloomberg, The Next Web)
  • Anthropic is now a public company that refuses ad revenue. Its shares priced at $83, opened at $111.50 on Thursday, closed Friday at $88.91, and were indicated near $92.80 before Monday's open. Its prospectus excludes advertisers and reports about $895.4 billion in compute commitments, some running to 2045, against third-quarter revenue of $17 billion that is 80 percent usage-based. ChatGPT's ads reached a $1 billion annualized run rate in under 200 days. Every board with an AI vendor strategy now has a live test of whether a subscription and usage model can carry infrastructure costs of that size under quarterly scrutiny, and should expect pricing and packaging changes from all the labs as that test plays out. (CNBC, Digiday)

#AI at the leadership level

An AI coding agent leaked a credential, and customer prompts were downloaded before anyone caught it. Together AI disclosed on 21 November that an agent working on its public xorl repository committed a credential that opened a private Hugging Face repository of customer prompt samples from June and July. Scanning caught the commit and it was reverted, but an outside party had already taken samples. The next day OpenRouter removed three inference providers and withdrew zero-data-retention status from 125 endpoints after researchers seeded canary prompts and found some being stored and stolen. Both accounts come mostly from secondary reports, and Together says its review continues. The governance lesson holds either way. A retention promise is made by one company and kept, or broken, by every provider behind it. Boards should ask which providers actually process the company's prompts, who verifies each one's retention claim, and whether agents with write access to public repositories can touch secrets at all. (Notebookcheck, Mezha)

#Leadership & org design

Airline groups are putting loyalty, marketing and media under one leader. Lufthansa Group will merge its loyalty and marketing functions from 1 January 2027 under Clemens Strauss, who will oversee the loyalty ecosystem including Miles & More. IAG launched IAG Media on 12 November modelled on its loyalty company, and United folded its media team into MileagePlus partnerships in October. Three large groups have reached the same structure within weeks of each other. The reasoning is that the member relationship, the message, and the data sold to partners are one asset, and separate owners optimize each against the others. Research from MIT Sloan Management Review points the same direction for AI: among heavy users of agentic AI, 66 percent expect their operating model and roles to change, against 42 percent of non-adopters. Former Cisco CEO John Chambers put the ownership question bluntly: "It's got to be owned by the CEO." (Business Travel News Europe, Shattered)

#Question worth asking your team this week

Which outside companies process the prompts and customer data we send to AI tools, and who on this team has verified each one's data retention claim instead of reading it on a pricing page?

#Try this now

  • Run a shopping agent through your own checkout before Black Friday: A Salesforce study of more than 2,500 holiday shoppers in the US, UK, Canada and Australia, cited in a guide released 20 November, found that 22 percent of people who use a personal AI agent have had it blocked from completing a purchase, 27 percent say an agent bought something too expensive, and 73 percent are uncomfortable handing an agent their payment details. The test takes 20 minutes: give an agent a real task on your site (find a product, apply a promo code, reach the payment step) and write down where it stalls. Bot protection, login walls and CAPTCHA are the usual causes. Black Friday is 27 November, so the fix list needs an owner today. (GlobeNewswire)
  • Ask Meta campaign questions in plain language through AWS Quick: On 21 November AWS added a Meta ads connector to Quick that uses Meta's ads MCP server. A marketer can pull performance, diagnose a catalog or a Conversions API problem, and draft or edit campaigns by typing a request, then combine the result with first-party data in a dashboard or deck. Quick asks for approval before any write action such as a budget change. It is free for three months for up to 10 teammates. Start with read-only questions an analyst answers every Monday and compare the answers with the current report. AWS published no time-savings figures. (AWS)
  • Read the terms before claiming your ChatGPT Business Hub profile: Business Hub, launched 10 November, shows a company how ChatGPT mentions and links to it, which makes it the closest thing yet to a search console for AI answers. Lawyers told Digiday the original Section 10 appeared to make businesses give up certain legal claims against OpenAI. OpenAI rewrote it on 18 November so the release covers only claims tied to a company's Business Agent chatbot and its use of customer data. Mandatory arbitration and a class action waiver remain, and AdExchanger reports the new version adds a waiver of the right to seek a court order stopping OpenAI's use of certain customer data. Send the current terms to legal with one question: which rights does the company give up in exchange for the dashboard? (Digiday, AdExchanger)

#Case study worth stealing

Grab and Careem, a game for the minutes customers spend waiting: Ride-hailing and delivery apps hold a customer's attention while a car or an order is on its way, and most show a map and nothing else. Mobile monetization consultant Felix Braberg reported on 20 November that when Grab and Careem put mini-games into that wait, 63 percent of visitors became players, sessions averaged 6.2 minutes, 41 percent came back on day seven, and a Careem Formula 1 game converted 4.4 percent of the users it reached. The figures are Braberg's and he does not cite a primary source, so treat them as directional. The tactic transfers to any app with a predictable wait: a boarding gate, a delayed flight, a hotel check-in queue, an order in transit. List the waits in the product, measure how long each lasts, and test one optional thing to do there, with rewarded video or points as the payoff. (Felix Braberg)

#Framework or mental model

The agent profile: Walmart said on 19 November that it now gives each customer's third-party AI agent its own profile. The customer sets what the agent may do, sets a spending limit, sees agent purchases tracked separately from their own, and can revoke access at any time. That is four controls: scope, limit, a separate ledger, and an off switch. Walmart's own assistant, Sparky, saw US usage rise 129 percent year over year with baskets 40 percent larger, so the company is opening the door to outside agents while keeping the customer in charge of them. Any team deciding whether to block or admit customer agents (United blocked unauthorized ones on 11 November) can use the four controls as the design brief for the third option, admitting them under the customer's own rules. (CX Dive, PYMNTS)

Signal: Meta is letting other companies' assistants operate its ad accounts. The interface for buying media is moving out of the platform's own ads manager and into whichever assistant the marketing team already uses, which weakens the ads manager as a point of lock-in and raises the value of the data the assistant can see. (AWS)

#Category watch

Agent checkout and payments: Three launches in three days moved agent payments from pilots toward plumbing. Klarna and Adyen said on 20 November that Adyen merchants can offer Klarna when an AI agent checks out for a shopper, the first payment option beyond cards in Adyen's agent suite, with the shopper confirming each purchase. AWS Marketplace opened purchasing to AI agents on 19 November through the x402 payment protocol, with spending limits set in advance and every agent transaction logged. Walmart described agent profiles with customer-set permissions and spending limits the same day. The category is settling on a common pattern of a spending limit, a log, and a human confirmation, and a martech org should expect its checkout, fraud and consent teams to share ownership of it. (Business Wire, AWS, GlobeNewswire)

#Stack intelligence

One buying agent across publishers, measured by the seller. Philips Sonicare consolidated its streaming TV buys inside Amazon for the 2025-26 upfront and used Amazon Ads Agent to buy across Paramount+, Roku, Tubi, Peacock, TelevisaUnivision and Warner Bros. Discovery, where it used to deal with each publisher separately. It combined Amazon's data with its own to build segments, and found that 86 percent of the oral care audience was not considering Philips. Reported results were 24 percent incremental reach, a 75 percent lower cost per acquisition, and a 15 times higher purchase rate for people who saw streaming ads. Amazon's own director noted that the 15 times figure came from customers who saw ads on both streaming and Amazon's site. The pattern is consolidation of buying, data and measurement in one retail media platform. The efficiency is real and so is the dependency, because the platform that sold the media also reported the lift. Teams adopting this should keep one measurement the seller does not control. (AdExchanger)

#Foundations concept of the day

Rewarded video: An ad format where a person chooses to watch a short video, usually 15 to 30 seconds, in exchange for something of value inside the app: an extra life in a game, a free article, loyalty points, a discount. The app's publisher is paid each time a video is completed, and the advertiser gets an audience that opted in and watched to the end. Completion rates are far higher than for ads that interrupt, which is why the format pays publishers more per thousand views (the eCPM, or effective cost per mille). It matters at scale because it is one of the few ad formats users rate positively, and because the reward can be a company's own currency. Mobile games built the format. It is now spreading to apps with waiting time: startup PlaysOut embeds mini-games with rewarded video in other companies' apps, and Snapchat's games reached 255 million monthly players in the first quarter of 2026. A grocery or airline app could offer points for a completed partner video during an order wait or a boarding delay, with the app owner controlling which advertisers appear. (Felix Braberg, AdExchanger)

Stack insight

Every layer reported a constraint that money alone does not remove: export licenses on chips and on rented compute, a leaderless Nvidia under investigation, a grid growing a tenth as fast as demand, and voters refusing sites. Demand is intact, as Korea's record chip exports show. The next 12 to 18 months of AI capacity will be set by permits, power and governance more than by how many chips can be bought, so capacity will arrive later and less evenly than the spending plans imply. Enterprises should expect compute prices and availability to vary by region and provider, and should avoid plans that depend on a single one.

#The convergence watch (loyalty data → media networks)

Lufthansa Group will merge its loyalty and marketing functions from 1 January 2027 under one executive, Clemens Strauss, who will oversee the loyalty ecosystem including Miles & More. Strauss has been with the group since 2014, most recently running customer experience, product and marketing at Eurowings. The move was reported by Business Travel News Europe earlier this month and was not covered here last week. Lufthansa has been developing its own media proposition, so the group has now put the member base and the marketing voice under a single owner before it sells either to advertisers (Business Travel News Europe, Wise Marketer).

That makes three large airline groups reorganizing around the same idea inside six weeks. United folded Kinective Media's sales and operations into MileagePlus partnerships in October. IAG launched IAG Media on 12 November, modelled on IAG Loyalty and built on Starlink logins across 122 million travellers a year (IAG). Lufthansa is now joining loyalty to marketing. The structures differ and the logic is shared: the loyalty relationship, the brand's own messaging and the audience sold to partners are treated as one asset with one owner.

The retail benchmark showed both the upside and the open question. Walmart reported on 19 November that Walmart Connect grew 36 percent, down from 43 percent the quarter before, with US advertising up 37 percent and global advertising up 25 percent. On the same call Chief Growth Officer Seth Dallaire said Walmart is still learning how conversational prompts change product discovery and how brands reach shoppers inside those exchanges. Walmart's assistant Sparky saw usage rise 129 percent with baskets 40 percent larger, and the company now gives third-party agents their own profiles with customer-set permissions and spending limits (PPC Land, CX Dive). What it signals: the network airlines are copying is growing by more than a third a year and does not yet know where an ad goes when an assistant does the shopping. An airline media network built on seatback screens and Wi-Fi portals has a captive human audience for hours, which is an advantage retail does not have, and a reason to build inventory around the journey more than around search.

A new kind of inventory surfaced: the wait. Mobile monetization consultant Felix Braberg argued on 20 November that ride-hailing, delivery and travel apps hold large audiences during dead time and show them nothing. He cites Grab and Careem mini-games converting 63 percent of visitors into players with 6.2-minute sessions, figures that are his own and unsourced (Felix Braberg, AdExchanger). What it signals: an airline app owns some of the longest predictable waits in consumer life, at the gate, in a delay and in the air. Opt-in games or rewarded video paid in miles would be first-party, logged-in inventory that fits a loyalty-led media network.

#Airline loyalty & digital

  • Singapore Airlines (announced 17 November): From 1 January 2027 KrisFlyer members no longer receive upgrade certificates at 75,000 and 100,000 Elite miles. Those milestones become redemption discounts of 10,000 and 20,000 miles. Solitaire PPS members lose the upgrades at 60,000 and 75,000 PPS Value and keep one at 100,000, exchangeable for two 50,000-mile discounts. An offline service fee of US$50 or 5,000 miles starts 1 December 2026 (Mainly Miles, Singapore Airlines, InsideFlyer). What it signals: a premium carrier is replacing a scarce, cabin-constrained benefit with a currency discount it can price. As premium cabins sell for cash, elite upgrades are the first perk to be converted into points.
  • Ethiopian Airlines ShebaMiles (18 November): The airline told members of a possible data exposure and its CEO confirmed an investigation to Reuters. It calls the incident limited, says there is no evidence financial data was accessed, and has not said how many members are affected. ShebaMiles had more than 5.38 million members when it partnered with Etihad Guest in 2025 (TimesLIVE, teiss). What it signals: a loyalty database that is about to be sold as an advertising audience is also a larger target. Every partner integration and data-sharing deal widens the surface, and the security case needs to be part of the media business plan.
  • United and Delta on AI agents: United's 11 November restriction on unauthorized third-party AI agents in its booking systems drew a second week of scrutiny, including AFAR's 18 November look at its effect on fare shopping. Delta says an AI tool that can authenticate a passenger's credentials can complete a booking for them (AFAR, Fast Company). What it signals: the logged-in loyalty account is becoming the credential that decides whether an agent may act. Programs that define what a member's agent can do, and log it, keep the relationship. Programs that only block will be routed around through intermediaries.

#Hotel & travel loyalty

  • Choice Hotels (18 November): Launched Access by Choice Privileges, where members earn and redeem points on concerts, sports and live events from 2,000 points, paying with cash, points or both. It has three parts: tickets (NBA, NFL, MLB, the NHL Winter Classic, concerts and theatre) through ticketing partner TFL, tours from GetYourGuide, and auctioned experiences such as sideline passes at college football games. First-time users get 1,000 bonus points (Hotel Dive, Hospitality Net). Worth stealing: a low entry price of 2,000 points gives infrequent guests something to redeem between stays, and the partners supply the inventory. A midscale brand has assembled an experiences marketplace without owning any of it.
  • IHG One Rewards: Expanded loyalty earning to group stays, according to Business Travel News (Business Travel News). Details were not available beyond the headline. Worth watching: group and meetings travel is a large pool of stays that programs have left unrewarded, and attaching earn to it enrols travellers who never booked direct.

#MarTech

  • Intuit sells Mailchimp to Bending Spoons (19 November): $3.68 billion, against the roughly $12 billion Intuit paid in 2021, with closing expected in early 2027. Mailchimp revenue was $313 million last quarter, down 1 percent. Bending Spoons reported 125 percent revenue growth with 3 percent organic growth and carries about $4 billion in acquisition debt (AdExchanger, FourWeekMBA). Relevance: email service providers are being run for cash. Any loyalty stack with a standalone email or engagement tool should check who owns it and what the owner's record on pricing has been.
  • Salesforce and Braze: Ahead of a close expected in Salesforce's fiscal 2028, a 17 November analysis says Salesforce plans to connect Braze with Marketing Cloud, Data 360 and Agentforce, and has not said how the two engagement products will coexist or what happens to pricing (CDP.com, Klaviyo). Relevance: many airline and hotel programs run Braze for app and push messaging. Renewal terms signed before the close should fix pricing and data portability.
  • AWS Quick adds a Meta ads connector (21 November): Marketers can analyze and edit Meta campaigns in natural language through Meta's ads MCP server, with approval required for changes (AWS). Relevance: campaign operations are moving into general-purpose assistants. The control that matters in a loyalty stack becomes who can approve a write action, since the assistant can already read everything.
  • New York City bans surveillance pricing (12 November): The City Council approved Introduction 891-A, barring algorithmic pricing that uses personal data to charge different customers different prices. The release does not say how loyalty programs are treated (NYC Council). Relevance: personalized offers and dynamic award pricing draw on the same member data. Programs should be able to show that a member-specific price is a disclosed benefit of membership.

#AdTech & retail media

  • Philips Sonicare and Amazon Ads Agent (18 November): Philips consolidated streaming TV buying inside Amazon and used its agent to buy across Paramount+, Roku, Tubi, Peacock, TelevisaUnivision and Warner Bros. Discovery. Reported results were 24 percent incremental reach and a 75 percent lower cost per acquisition, measured by Amazon (AdExchanger). Relevance to the thesis: a retail media network is becoming the buying desk for media it does not own, using its shopper data as the targeting layer. An airline network with verified traveller data could play the same role for travel advertisers buying off-property.
  • Google Connected Apps (20 November): Albertsons, Safeway, Shaw's and Target now take orders and checkout inside Google Search and Gemini through the Universal Commerce Protocol (Google). Relevance: retailers are placing their checkout inside the assistant. No airline or hotel was in this batch, and travel is an obvious next category. The loyalty login is the asset to protect in that integration.
  • Klarna and Adyen (20 November): Adyen merchants can offer Klarna when an AI agent checks out for a shopper, with the shopper confirming each purchase (Business Wire). Relevance: financing and payment choice are following the customer into agent checkout. Co-brand card and pay-with-points options need a place in that flow or they will be left out of it.

#Loyalty tech vendors

  • Ascenda (22 November): Powers CommBank Yello, Commonwealth Bank of Australia's program, which has moved to a points-based model. More than 9 million customers can earn points across home loans, deposits, cards and insurance and redeem them with Australian brands (PR Newswire). Build-vs-buy signal: Australia's largest bank bought its points engine and redemption network from a vendor. That also puts a new points currency with 9 million holders into a market where airline programs have been the default.
  • Velocity Logic Group: Sunoco will move its Go Rewards and Journie Rewards programs, and its co-brand card, onto one platform across the US and Canada under a multi-year agreement (Mobility Plaza). Build-vs-buy signal: operators with two programs after an acquisition are consolidating on a bought platform, with the card moving alongside the program.
  • TFL and GetYourGuide: Supply the ticketing and tours behind Choice's new experiences platform. Build-vs-buy signal: redemption catalogs are being assembled from specialist partners through APIs, which shortens launch time and leaves the program owning the currency and the member relationship.

#Strategic implication

The move that matters most this week is organizational. Lufthansa, IAG and United have each put loyalty, marketing or media under shared ownership within six weeks, and the retail network they are imitating told investors it does not yet know how advertising works when an assistant shops. The question for a CDO or VP Digital at a major carrier is who owns the member relationship across four surfaces at once: the program, the brand's own messaging, the audience sold to advertisers, and the agents members will send to act for them. A carrier that settles that ownership first can design inventory around the journey, including the hours of waiting it already has, and can set the terms on which outside assistants use a member's login. A carrier that leaves those surfaces with separate owners will have each optimized against the others while Singapore-style benefit conversions and partner-built catalogs make the programs themselves easier to compare.
