Executive Pulse438 words

#Macro signal

Oil back as a strategic variable: WTI rose roughly 4% on the week on fresh US-Iran strikes and a broken ceasefire, settling near $71 after a mid-week spike. For any leader with fuel, logistics, or discretionary-demand exposure, the signal is that geopolitical supply risk is repricing faster than the macro data. With CPI and PPI landing this week and Treasury yields above 4.5%, the strategic posture is to stress-test plans against an energy shock and a higher-for-longer rate path at the same time, rather than assuming either resolves cleanly.

#Tech industry

  • Microsoft stands up a 6,000-person AI consulting arm: Microsoft launched Microsoft Frontier Company, a $2.5B initiative deploying 6,000 industry experts to help enterprises plan and deploy AI. Strategic implication: the frontier labs and hyperscalers have concluded that the bottleneck to AI revenue is not model capability, it is enterprise adoption. When your platform vendor starts selling you the change management, read it as an admission that the technology is ready and the organizations are not.
  • Enterprise becomes the AI revenue engine: Enterprise now exceeds 40% of OpenAI revenue and is tracking toward parity with consumer by year end. Implication: the center of gravity in AI is shifting from consumer novelty to enterprise deployment, which rewards vendors with security, governance, and integration over those with the flashiest demo.

#AI at the leadership level

The adoption data is a leadership indictment, not a technology story. 79% of organizations report challenges adopting AI, up double digits year over year, and 54% of C-suite executives say AI adoption is tearing their company apart. The root cause is structural: 48% introduced AI without redesigning the workflows or roles it sits in, and only 12% have redesigned at scale with a new operating model. AI dropped onto an unchanged org chart produces friction, not leverage. The differentiated companies are treating adoption as operating-model redesign, not a tools rollout.

#Leadership & org design

The 12% who redesigned their operating model around AI are the control group worth studying. The pattern is not "add AI to every role," it is redrawing where decisions are made, which steps get automated, and which keep a human in the loop, then rebuilding roles around that. Spending over $1M a year on AI while leaving the workflow untouched, which many are doing, is how you get high investment and low return in the same quarter.

#Question worth asking your team this week

Where have we bolted AI onto an unchanged process, and what would this function look like if we redesigned the workflow around the AI instead of the reverse?

Practitioner Pulse431 words

#Try this now

  • Context engineering over prompt engineering: The field has quietly moved past clever wording. Treat the model's input as a managed system, not a sentence. Four moves to run this week on any agent or GPT you own: write (persist context outside the window so it survives a reset), select (retrieve only what the current step needs), compress (summarize running history to save tokens), isolate (give each sub-task its own clean context). The counterintuitive rule: models take 1M-token windows, but answer quality degrades past roughly 50K tokens of genuinely relevant context. Stuffing the window is now an anti-pattern. Audit one production prompt and cut everything that is not load-bearing.
  • Measure agents like a P&L, not a demo: The teams pulling ahead track tokens per successful task, context-window utilization, retrieval precision and recall, and cost per task against a fixed evaluation set. If you run any AI workflow, stand up a 20-case eval set this week and start logging success rate and cost per run. You cannot improve what you are not scoring, and "it feels better" is not a metric a CFO accepts.
  • Treat prompts as company assets: The smartest operators are versioning prompts, scoping them to narrow well-defined jobs, keeping human review on anything customer-facing, and storing the winners as reusable IP. The advantage is shifting from who has the best model to who structures decisions, approvals, and feedback loops best.

#Case study worth stealing

CVS Media Exchange, closed-loop measurement on loyalty data: CVS launched CorIQ, an AI-driven measurement platform built directly on ExtraCare loyalty data, tying ad exposure to actual in-store and online purchase. The move any digital team can adapt: the asset is not the ad inventory, it is the identity spine that connects a logged-in customer across exposure and conversion. If you hold a loyalty or account relationship, you already own the one thing advertisers and internal stakeholders will pay for, closed-loop proof that spend moved behavior. Build the measurement layer on your first-party identity before you build more media.

#Framework or mental model

Write, Select, Compress, Isolate: The four primitives of context engineering for any AI agent. Write persists state beyond the current window. Select pulls in only what this step needs. Compress summarizes to protect the token budget. Isolate hands sub-tasks their own uncluttered context so one messy step does not poison the whole run. Digital leaders can apply the same discipline to human workflows: most bad decisions come from the same failure modes, no memory, too much irrelevant input, no summarization, and no separation of concerns.

AI Stack Daily524 words

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Agency holdcos are buying the data layer: WPP acquired data clean room vendor InfoSum, folding it into its WPP Open operating system, while Publicis grabbed identity and data company Lotame to bolt onto Epsilon's 2.3 billion profiles. Signal: the agencies have stopped renting first-party data infrastructure and started owning it. When the middlemen vertically integrate into identity and clean rooms, the message to every enterprise is that the data collaboration layer, not the creative layer, is where durable margin now sits. InfoSum also hands WPP a large, diverse dataset for training AI, so the clean room doubles as a model-training moat.
  • CVS Media Exchange ships CorIQ: A retailer stood up an AI-driven closed-loop measurement platform on its own loyalty data. Signal: retail media is maturing from selling impressions to selling proof. The networks that win the next 18 months are the ones that can attribute exposure to purchase on first-party identity, which pressures every non-retailer with a logged-in audience to ask why they are not doing the same.

#Category watch

Data clean rooms and CDPs converging: The old framing of CDP versus clean room is collapsing into "you need both." The CDP resolves and activates your own first-party data; the clean room lets you match it against a partner's without either side exposing raw records. With cookie deprecation and privacy law forcing the issue, the enterprise stack is reorganizing around a resolution-and-collaboration core, and vendors that only do one half are the acquisition targets, not the acquirers.

#Stack intelligence

MarTech M&A ran 37 to 47 deals per quarter through 2025 with over $25 billion in tracked deal value, and two catalysts drive nearly all of it: acquiring AI-native capability, and acquiring first-party data infrastructure (CDPs, identity resolution, consent management) ahead of cookie loss. The practical read for anyone building a stack: the buy-versus-build line has moved. Identity and collaboration are being bought because they are too slow to build against a regulatory clock, while activation and orchestration are increasingly built in-house on top.

#Foundations concept of the day

Data clean room: A secure, neutral environment where two parties match and analyze their data against each other without either seeing the other's raw records. Party A uploads its customer list, Party B uploads its own, the room returns only aggregated overlap and insights, never row-level data. It matters at scale because it is how a brand and a publisher (or two retailers) collaborate on audiences in a cookieless, privacy-regulated world. Example: a consumer packaged goods brand and a grocery chain use a clean room to find shared shoppers and measure whether the brand's ads drove in-store sales, without the grocer handing over its loyalty file or the brand exposing its CRM.

#Watch list

  • TSMC Q2 earnings, July 16: guidance on CoWoS advanced-packaging capacity and any capex revision is the clearest read on whether the AI infrastructure buildout is still accelerating or hitting a ceiling.
  • Retail media measurement standardization: as more networks ship closed-loop tools on loyalty data, watch for the first credible cross-retailer measurement standard. Whoever sets it controls the category's currency.

#Macro pulse

Weekend, markets closed. The read carries from Friday's close. Canada: the S&P/TSX Composite gained 0.3% to 35,305, near a record high after a stronger-than-expected jobs report, with financials mixed as a firm economy eased pressure on the Bank of Canada to cut. USA: the S&P 500 rose 0.4% to finish near its highs ahead of this week's June CPI and PPI prints, with Treasury yields holding above 4.5% and the Fed leaning hawkish. Oil: WTI settled at $71.41 Friday, down under 1% on the day but up about 4% on the week on fresh US-Iran strikes and a broken ceasefire, the kind of geopolitical supply move that hits airline margins directly.

#AI infrastructure watch

TSMC reports Q2 earnings July 16, the week's single most important infrastructure read. Watch three things: CoWoS advanced-packaging capacity (the current chokepoint in the AI chip supply chain), full-year revenue guidance, and any revision to the $52B to $56B 2026 capex budget, already the largest in the company's history at roughly 37% above 2025. Whether that number moves up or holds is the clearest available signal on whether the AI buildout is still accelerating.

#Signal or noise?

The oil move is signal, not noise: renewed US-Iran conflict reintroduces a supply-risk premium that had faded, and it lands the same week as inflation data, so energy and rates are set to move demand-sensitive sectors together.

#Watch tomorrow

US June CPI and PPI this week, and the start of earnings season with the big banks Tuesday. Both set the tone for whether the near-record equity levels hold.
