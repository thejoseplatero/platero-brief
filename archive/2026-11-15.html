#Macro signal

The US air traffic system was grounded by a router swap during a modernization project. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said Friday's nationwide ground stop began when a router being replaced during a copper-to-fiber migration started flooding other routers in the air traffic control network. Controllers saw inconsistent radar data and aircraft communications at 3:36 p.m. ET, departures were halted at 3:54 p.m. and resumed at 8:15 p.m. About 9,100 flights were cancelled Friday and more than 6,400 more on Saturday, per FlightAware, with airlines expecting full schedules by Sunday night. Duffy pointed at contractor L3Harris, which says it is cooperating, and officials found no evidence of a cyberattack. The strategic lesson is that modernization itself is the risk window. Every organization replacing legacy infrastructure, whether a reservation system, a data platform or a network, runs its highest outage exposure during the cutover, and the cutover is usually staffed by a contractor. Leaders should ask whether their migration plans have a blast-radius limit and a tested rollback, not just a go-live date. (NBC News, Yahoo News)

#Tech industry

  • Reuters reported that Jensen Huang misled Nvidia's general counsel before his exit: Per sources and correspondence Reuters reviewed, Huang told general counsel Tim Teter and CMO Alison Wagonfeld in October that his dealings with Alice Huang and Megaspeed were "standard business dealings with customers." Separately, CNBC reported that his departure threatens a $500 billion Nvidia-backed plan with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to turn GPUs into tradable debt, which has produced no deals since August. Strategic implication: when a founder's personal guarantee sits under a financing structure, the governance failure becomes a capital markets problem. Boards should map which commitments depend on one person's credibility. (Reuters via AI Weekly, CNBC via AI Weekly)
  • Canada substantially concluded free-trade talks with ASEAN while a US deal remains open: On 14 November in Manila, Ottawa announced the ASEAN talks were substantially concluded, and Prime Minister Carney said a bilateral deal with the US remained possible after a "substantial" call with the president. Strategic implication: Canadian firms with Asia exposure, including carriers and travel brands, get a second trade corridor to plan around while the US relationship stays unresolved. (Hashtag Investing)

#AI at the leadership level

The frontier labs now face a lawsuit asking a court to stop training. Four nonprofits, The Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative and Alliance for Secure AI, sued OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and SpaceX's AI unit in San Francisco Superior Court on 13 November under California's Unfair Competition Law. They want an emergency injunction halting frontier training until the companies can prove human control. The suit is unlikely to stop training, but it adds legal exposure to the vendors in enterprise AI stacks in the same week Anthropic prices its IPO. On the same day, Anthropic put self-hosted Claude Managed Agents into private beta, letting enterprises run the agent harness, database and sandboxes in their own cloud and route requests to Anthropic, Bedrock, Google, Microsoft Foundry or their own gateway. Read together, vendor risk is rising and vendors are answering by offering portability. Boards should require that any agent deployment can move between model providers without a rebuild. (Al Jazeera via AI Weekly, Help Net Security via AI Weekly)

#Leadership & org design

OpenAI dissolved its standalone robotics team for the second time in six years. Fortune reported that researchers move into Mark Chen's research organization, while applied robotics and hardware staff move into Greg Brockman's Applied group. The pattern is common: a separate unit is set up to protect a new capability, then folded back once it needs the core's resources, or once the core needs it. The useful design choice is to decide in advance what milestone ends the separation, so the fold-in is planned rather than a reorganization that reads as retreat. Innovation labs, AI centres of excellence and digital factories inside large enterprises face the same question. (Fortune via AI Weekly)

#Question worth asking your team this week

Which of our current migrations could take down a customer-facing system if one component misbehaves during cutover, and who would notice first, us or our customers?

#Try this now

  • Run an agent checkout test against your own site before ChatGPT Wallet scales: OpenAI switched on ChatGPT Wallet on 13 November with American Express, Mastercard and Visa. Its agents pay with a network-issued stand-in credential after the user confirms with Face ID or a fingerprint, so the real card number never reaches the agent. It is limited to US ChatGPT Pro subscribers for now, but OpenAI's Commerce Partner Playbook already asks merchants to accept signed agent requests through Web Bot Auth. A Blend test found bot protection blocked an AI shopping agent at 29 of 200 retailers. Spend 20 minutes with your security and ecommerce leads: check whether your bot rules can tell a signed agent from a scraper, and decide which agents get through to checkout and which get stopped at browse. The decision is cheaper to make now than after a holiday-week incident. (PPC Land, CNBC via AI Weekly)
  • Check whether the Amazon Ad Tag just put a new cookie on your site: The Amazon Ad Tag now sets a 30-day first-party cookie called aatSession on advertiser websites in North America. It is on by default, Amazon says it respects consent signals, and opting out requires adding code. The documentation does not explain what the identifier contains or how Safari's cookie limits apply. Open your tag manager, find the Amazon tag, and confirm three things: that your consent platform categorizes it correctly, that your privacy notice covers it, and whether you want it on. A default you did not choose is still a decision your legal team owns. (PPC Land)
  • Use YouTube's new outlier score to find what to make more of: YouTube Studio's Analytics became Insights on desktop on 13 November, with plain-language summaries, a Research tab that replaces Trends, and outlier detection. A 5x score means a video drew five times the channel's usual views for that format. For any brand channel, sort the last 12 months by outlier score, list the top five, and write down what they share in topic, length and opening seconds. That list is a sharper creative brief than a quarterly content calendar. (PPC Land)

#Case study worth stealing

A points program that rewarded customers nobody had, rebuilt around the second purchase: A MarTech case describes a brand whose points program had run for years. About seven in 10 issued points were too small to redeem, and nearly nine in 10 customers had made a single purchase, while rewards required a fourth. The program was paying people who were already buying and was invisible to everyone else. The author estimated that moving the same budget to second-purchase incentives would return about seven times what the program earned, so the effort shifted to the second order, with a small, timely reward at the next purchase decision instead of a distant balance. Any team running a loyalty or rewards scheme can run the same check this week: plot customers by purchase count against the purchase number where the first reward unlocks, and see how many ever reach it. (MarTech)

#Framework or mental model

Four questions before funding a loyalty program: The same piece gives a test any loyalty proposal should pass. First, does the product build anything a customer would lose by leaving, such as saved settings, history or status. Second, who will enroll, since best customers join first and make members look better than they are. Third, has the typical customer bought often enough for points to matter. Fourth, what result would shut it down, measured against a holdout group. Digital leaders can apply it to any retention mechanic, from a status tier to a subscription perk, before the budget is committed rather than after the dashboard looks good. (MarTech)

#Rabbit hole

Claude and Gödel's shorthand notebooks: Logician Jan von Plato spent years decoding Kurt Gödel's 1,009 pages of Gabelsberger shorthand at roughly one page a week. He told the Guardian that Claude processed the full set in a couple of days in September and surfaced 663 theorems, hundreds of them previously unknown. It is a long read on what changes when a decade-long expert transcription job becomes a weekend, and on what the expert still has to do to verify the output. (The Guardian via AI Weekly)

#Vendor & platform moves

  • OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet with American Express, Mastercard and Visa: Live since 13 November for US ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and over. Agents pay with a per-transaction network token after biometric confirmation, and merchants are asked to accept signed agent requests via Web Bot Auth under OpenAI's Commerce Partner Playbook. This is OpenAI's second attempt at checkout. Instant Checkout launched in September 2025 and closed in March 2026. OpenAI cites 1.2 billion weekly ChatGPT users. Signal: Version one tried to own the checkout. Version two hands identity and payment to the card networks and asks merchants to authenticate agents. Agentic commerce is settling into a three-party model of agent, network credential and merchant, and the merchant's bot policy becomes a revenue decision, not a security setting. (PPC Land)
  • Amazon's ad business reached $78.0 billion and is now selling defaults, not inventory: PPC Land's special edition documents the change. DVA+ is the pre-selected route among three buying paths, automated campaigns are capped at 20 products, Full-Funnel Campaigns at $100 a day let advertisers choose neither channels nor audiences, Amazon's 16 benchmark recommendations never suggest cutting budgets, and the campaign-level agency fee setting disappears after 31 January 2027. Amazon's DSP fees sit around 1 to 2 percent of spend against 12 to 15 percent at The Trade Desk, which reported its first revenue decline since its 2016 IPO this month. Signal: The cheapest DSP in the market makes its money back on the defaults. When the platform chooses the channel, the audience and the benchmark, the advertiser's leverage moves to the only thing left, which is its own measurement. (PPC Land)
  • Italy fined Meta 805 million euros over Facebook's access to Android photo galleries: The Garante found the Android app read photos, videos and metadata for a gallery suggestions feature without specific consent, affecting about 20 million Italian users. Meta must ask for separate consent, keep the feature off by default and report back at one, two and three months. The case stayed in Italy because the ePrivacy rules have no one-stop-shop mechanism. In the same week India opened consent manager registration with a 2 crore rupee net worth floor. Signal: Device-level data access is being enforced country by country, outside the GDPR lead-regulator route that let large platforms deal mainly with Ireland. Any app that touches on-device data needs per-market consent design, not one global flow. (PPC Land, PPC Land)

#Category watch

Agent payments and agent identity: The pieces now exist on every side. Card networks issue tokenized agent credentials, OpenAI's Wallet sits on top of them, Web Bot Auth gives agents a cryptographic signature, and merchants such as eBay are enforcing bans on agents that do not identify themselves. The open questions are who carries liability when an agent buys the wrong thing, how chargebacks work when the person never saw the checkout page, and whether merchants get the customer data or the agent keeps it. For a loyalty or commerce org, the practical question is which agents are allowed to transact, and whether members buying through an agent still earn and get recognized.

#Stack intelligence

Every default is a negotiated position, so audit them on a schedule. In one week Amazon set a first-party cookie on advertiser sites by default, pre-selected its automated buying path, made automated Search campaigns the default in Google Ads Editor, and opted new Demand Gen video ads into text overlays by default in Google Ads API v26.2. None of these require the advertiser to act, and that is the point. Enterprises that keep a quarterly default register, listing every setting a platform switched on, who reviewed it and the decision taken, catch these before they show up as consent issues or budget drift. Enterprises that do not are running a configuration their vendors chose. (PPC Land, PPC News Feed)

#Foundations concept of the day

Principal media: In a normal agency model the agency buys media on the client's behalf and passes the cost through, taking a fee. In principal media the agency buys inventory itself, at its own risk, and resells it to clients at a price it sets, keeping the margin. The client usually cannot see what the agency paid. It works at scale because agencies can negotiate bulk discounts and fill campaigns quickly, and it lets agencies earn more as their fees get squeezed. The ANA found 58 percent of marketers used principal media in the past year, up from 47 percent, and Forrester estimates it is about a third of agency billings. One independent agency described paying 17 percent to a platform plus 12 percent to a reseller. Retail is a clear example: a consumer goods brand buying retail media through an agency's principal deal may get lower headline CPMs while giving up the transparency needed to compare that spend against buying direct. (PPC Land)

#Watch list

  • Agentic bid management priced on savings: Lunio and Revvim launched Agentic Bid Optimization on 12 November. It cuts Google brand bids when no rival is in the auction and restores them when one appears, and customers pay a share of the savings. One food delivery client reportedly reclaimed $6.4 million in 2025 with 76 percent lower cost per click on 15 brand terms, with no baseline or control published. Outcome-priced agents that sit on top of the platforms are the next layer of the stack. (PPC Land)
  • Thought leadership read by machines first: Edelman and LinkedIn found 84 percent of 1,135 B2B executives use AI to summarize thought leadership, and 59 percent say weak thought leadership got a vendor dropped, yet only 46 percent of suppliers optimize for AI answers. The study's authors sell related services and gave no margins of error. (PPC Land)

#Macro pulse

Weekend, markets closed. Friday's closes stand: TSX about 36,106, nearly flat; S&P 500 7,561.67, down 0.4 percent; Nasdaq 25,136.27, down 0.9 percent; Dow 52,347.55, up 0.1 percent. USD/CAD ended near 1.4269, close to the 2026 high around 1.43, and WTI near $88.56. (La Nación/AP)

Canada: The weekend news is trade. Ottawa said on 14 November it had substantially concluded free-trade talks with ASEAN, and Prime Minister Carney said a US deal is still possible but not done. The Bank of Canada raised to 2.50 percent on 28 October, its first hike in 18 months, and decides next on 10 December. A weak loonie plus a hiking central bank squeezes Canadian carriers on US-dollar fuel and leases while softening domestic demand. (Hashtag Investing, Rain City Properties)

United States: The FAA disruption carried through Saturday, with more than 6,400 US cancellations on top of about 9,100 on Friday. Airlines expected full schedules by Sunday night. Expect the cost to show in US carriers' fourth-quarter numbers. (NBC News)

#Today's industry move

AI developer tooling raised at a premium while the frontier lab IPO approaches. LangChain closed a $300 million Series C at a $3 billion valuation on Friday, led by CapitalG with GIC, DFJ Growth, IVP and Sapphire, more than double the $1.25 billion valuation of a year ago. Mirendil, founded by former Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind and xAI researchers, raised a $1 billion Series A at $5 billion to automate frontier AI research, five months after a $200 million seed. Anthropic's prospectus sets pricing for 18 November and a Nasdaq debut on 19 November under ANT. (TechCrunch via AI Weekly, PR Newswire via AI Weekly, Anthropic)

#AI infrastructure watch

  • Riot Platforms named Anthropic as the tenant for its entire 910MW Corsicana, Texas campus: A 15-year powered-land lease worth about $28 billion over the initial term and up to $54 billion with both five-year extensions. Bitcoin miners with grid connections are becoming AI landlords, because power access, not chips, is the slowest input. (DatacenterDynamics via AI Weekly)
  • Nscale's updated prospectus shows a $1.69 billion third-quarter loss on $307.5 million of revenue, up 1,344 percent year over year, after a $1.02 billion second-quarter loss. Microsoft walked away from an English data centre. (CNBC via AI Weekly)
  • The $500 billion GPU-as-collateral financing pact with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR has produced no deals since August, and partners had been pushing Nvidia to raise its 25 percent residual-value backstop before Huang's exit. (CNBC via AI Weekly)

#Signal or noise?

Signal: capital is flowing to whoever controls power and to the tooling layer, while the financing of GPUs themselves is getting harder, so the AI buildout is being priced by megawatts, not chips.
