Executive Pulse585 words

#Macro signal

Fed holds unanimously, but the tone stays hawkish into a decelerating-yet-sticky inflation print: The Fed held at 3.50 to 3.75 percent in a rare 12-0 vote, its first unanimous decision since November 2024, and entered its blackout period on a hawkish note ahead of the July 28-29 FOMC. June CPI cooled to 3.5 percent from 4.2 percent in May, real progress, but still a full point above target with a cooling labor market underneath. The strategic read: the "one cut is coming soon" narrative that anchored many 2026 plans is not guaranteed. Leaders building H2 budgets on cheaper capital should stress-test the case where rates stay put through year-end, and prioritize investments that pay back on operating leverage rather than on a financing tailwind that may not arrive.

#Tech industry

  • Big Tech's AI capex hits an estimated $725B for 2026, up roughly 77 percent, as free cash flow across the top hyperscalers is projected to approach zero by Q3: Earnings season is now the referee. Alphabet reports July 22 and Microsoft July 29, and Wall Street is explicitly demanding "receipts", proof that the spend converts to revenue, with Azure growth below 35 percent likely read as a severe deceleration. Strategic implication: the market is shifting from rewarding AI ambition to pricing AI ROI. Expect vendors to reprice, bundle, and push consumption-based contracts harder in H2 to show monetization. If you are a buyer, this is leverage. If you are a builder, the bar on demonstrating payback just moved up.

#AI at the leadership level

The adoption-to-governance gap is now the defining board risk. Roughly 55 percent of enterprises are actively deploying AI, but only 26 percent say their governance frameworks are fully aligned with that pace, and while ~41 percent are already running agentic AI in production, only about 15 percent say they are fully prepared to. Directors are convening specifically on oversight: not whether AI works, but how fast to move and where value diverges from risk. The CDO mandate is visibly shifting from data stewardship toward enterprise risk operator, owning AI dependency maps, decision-chain documentation, and cross-functional governance boards. Board takeaway: the competitive question in 2026 is no longer "are we using AI" but "can we govern it fast enough to keep deploying it," because the first serious incident is what stalls adoption, not the technology.

#Leadership & org design

"Forward Deployed Engineering" is hardening into an operating model, not a buzzword. Microsoft committed $2.5B and 6,000 employees to embed engineers directly inside client operations, two days after Amazon put $1B behind a similar FDE initiative, with Anthropic and OpenAI having stood up FDE groups earlier this year. The signal for org-builders: value is moving to the teams that sit inside the customer's workflow and ship in their context, not the ones that sell a license and leave. Meanwhile, AI-cited restructurings (a fintech cutting ~20 percent of headcount over two to three years, others explicitly redeploying people into AI roles) show the reorganization is real, not rhetorical. Practical move: audit where your highest-value talent sits relative to the customer's actual work, and consider whether your best people are close enough to the point of use to compound.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If our biggest AI vendor had to prove payback to us the way Wall Street is forcing them to prove it this earnings season, which of our current AI deployments would survive that review, and which are we funding on faith?

#Try this now

  • LinkedIn AI ad variants + Brand Kit: Campaign Manager now has Draft with AI, Brand Kit, and AI ad variants live for all advertisers. Load your colors, fonts, tone, and key messages into Brand Kit once so generated creative stays on-brand, then spin up 5+ headline and intro variants per campaign. LinkedIn's own data shows campaigns running five or more variants get 20%+ higher CTR than single-ad campaigns. Twenty minutes to set the Brand Kit and launch a variant test that would have taken a designer a week.
  • Adobe Advertising Custom Algorithms: Adobe's DSP now trains your bidding model directly on raw Adobe Analytics data instead of the thin slice APIs usually pass through. The lever for practitioners is defining High Value Actions (video views, newsletter signups, checkout steps) so the model optimizes toward real engagement signals, not just last-click conversions. Worth doing this week if you have meaningful first-party traffic and are tired of renting Google/Meta's black-box optimization. This one bids on your data without owning the inventory.
  • PRD-to-prototype one-pager: Instead of a full PRD, write a focused one-pager (user, goal, success criteria, failure modes) and hand it to an AI prototyping tool to generate a clickable prototype in hours. Teams doing this report 30-50% less time from concept to testable artifact. Run one this week on a stalled backlog item and put a real prototype in front of five users before the next sprint planning.

#Case study worth stealing

EQUIVA, omnichannel loyalty at the center: The retailer rebuilt around loyalty optimization as the connective tissue between online and store, rather than treating the program as a points bolt-on. Result: 23% lift in average transaction value and 42% more transactions per customer. The adaptable part for any digital team is sequencing, they made loyalty the data spine that identifies and personalizes across channels, so every touchpoint feeds the same profile. If your loyalty program lives in a silo next to the CRM, this is the argument for wiring it into the identity layer instead.

#Framework or mental model

Product judgment over documentation: As AI makes code cheap, prototypes fast, and docs trivial to generate, the scarce skill moves upstream to deciding what is worth building, what ships first, and what gets ignored. The "product builder" is whoever can move from insight to working artifact fastest and let real feedback, not files, measure progress. Digital leaders should re-weight their teams and their own week toward judgment calls and user tests, and stop rewarding volume of specs.

#Rabbit hole (optional)

The PM role is visibly splitting in 2026. Hands-on "product builders" who ship prototypes are separating from the broader strategy-and-alignment remit, and the consensus is that strategy and business acumen, not tool fluency, are the durable skills for the next two to three years. Worth thinking about how you structure your team as AI collapses the execution layer: fewer people writing PRDs, more people owning outcomes end to end.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Publicis Groupe / LiveRamp: Publicis is acquiring identity and data-collaboration vendor LiveRamp for roughly $2.2B all-cash ($38.50/share, a ~30% premium). Signal: A holdco is buying the identity spine rather than renting it. The pitch is "data co-creation" across brands and publishers without leaning on US platform gatekeepers. When an agency group vertically integrates the identity resolution layer, it reframes identity from a neutral utility every DSP taps into a strategic asset one buyer controls. Everyone downstream should re-examine whether their identity partner is now a competitor's asset.
  • Salesforce Agentforce Commerce: Shopper, Buyer, and Merchant agents are now generally available, with native integration into ChatGPT and Google (AI Mode, Gemini) arriving in coming months. Signal: The commerce surface is moving off owned properties and into third-party AI assistants. If a Buyer Agent transacts inside ChatGPT, the retailer's site stops being the funnel and the martech job shifts from optimizing a page to feeding structured product and inventory data into agents you do not own.
  • 6sense MCP Server (open beta): 6sense buying-intent data is now callable from any MCP-compatible agent (Claude, ChatGPT, Writer, Agentforce), GA targeted for August. Signal: Model Context Protocol is becoming the integration pattern that replaces bespoke API connectors. Vendors that expose their data as an MCP endpoint make themselves consumable by whatever agent a customer already runs, which is a defense against being locked out of a rival's walled agent ecosystem.

#Category watch

Agentic CRM and engagement: The race to rebuild the suite around persistent agents is now the whole competitive field, not a feature. Salesforce, Adobe (Experience Cloud rebranded to CX Enterprise with agent "Coworkers"), and HubSpot are all repositioning from tool-centric software to goal-oriented, agent-run workflows. MoEngage buying Aampe to form an "agentic customer engagement platform" shows the same logic hitting mid-market. For anyone building a martech org, the question is shifting from "which tools do we license" to "which agents do we let act autonomously, and on whose data." Only a small fraction of teams run campaigns where multiple agents operate without a human in the loop, so the gap between the vendor narrative and operational reality is wide and worth pressure-testing before you buy the vision.

#Stack intelligence

The token economics of agentic pipelines are quietly forcing an architecture decision. A single daily agent pipeline can burn thousands of tokens per run and exhaust a flat subscription in an afternoon, which pushes cost from predictable SaaS seats to volatile usage. The emerging pattern: keep raw data in a warehouse you own (Snowflake, BigQuery, Postgres) and use cheap non-LLM filtering to select the relevant slice before anything hits the model, cutting token spend by more than half while keeping quality. The strategic framing is "do you want to pay for the work, or own the infrastructure and pay only for the reasoning." This favors warehouse-native, provider-agnostic builds over deep consolidation into one vendor's agent stack, because owning the context layer lets you swap models by config instead of migration.

#Foundations concept of the day

Identity graphs: An identity graph is a database that stitches the many signals a single person leaves behind (emails, device IDs, cookies, loyalty IDs, hashed logins) into one persistent profile, so a customer looks like one person across channels instead of a dozen strangers. It works by matching deterministic keys (a login you know is the same person) and probabilistic ones (same household, same device pattern) into a linked record. Why it matters at scale: with third-party cookies still fading in relevance, the graph built on your own first-party logins is what lets you recognize, target, and measure people across web, app, email, and connected TV. Cross-industry example: a retailer connects in-store loyalty scans to website behavior and app purchases through one graph, so a shopper who browses on mobile and buys in store counts as one journey rather than three disconnected events. This is exactly why LiveRamp commands a $2.2B price and why The Trade Desk is investing in warehouse-activation players like Hightouch. Whoever owns the graph owns the ability to resolve and value an audience.

#Watch list

  • MCP as the martech integration standard: 6sense exposing intent data over Model Context Protocol, alongside Salesforce and Adobe wiring agents into ChatGPT and Gemini, points to MCP becoming the connective tissue between data vendors and agents. If it holds, the moat shifts from proprietary API integrations to whoever owns the underlying data an agent must call. Watch which vendors publish MCP endpoints next.
  • Warehouse-native activation: The Trade Desk's stake in Hightouch (round valuing it at $2.75B, more than double 14 months prior) plus the direct integration piping raw DSP impression logs into brands' own data warehouses signals that activation is moving to the warehouse rather than a separate CDP. Watch whether the standalone CDP category keeps consolidating into data-cloud and DSP ecosystems.

#Today's industry move

The macro signal for travel and loyalty is the fuel line, not a single ticker. A Houthi blockade threatens Saudi crude that transits pipelines bypassing the Strait of Hormuz, the relief valve the market has counted on. Sustained Brent near $90 pressures carrier cost structures into peak summer demand and tilts the yield-vs-load-factor calculus toward holding fares. For Canadian international routes, a flat loonie means no FX offset on those higher fuel bills.

#AI infrastructure watch

Monday's selloff started in semiconductors before rippling out, a reminder that the AI trade now sets the risk tone for the whole tape. Nothing structural broke: NVIDIA's most recent quarter was $68.1B revenue (up 73%) with $62.3B from data center, AMD data center at $5.8B (up 57%), TSMC May revenue up 30% year over year. The pullback reads as positioning and valuation air (NVIDIA at roughly 43x forward), not a demand crack in the chips-to-data-center stack.

#Signal or noise?

Mostly noise on equities, real signal on oil: the chip wobble is a one-day valuation reset inside an intact buildout, but the Red Sea and Saudi blockade escalation is a genuine new supply front that could keep crude elevated for weeks.

#Watch tomorrow

Whether US-Iran mediation reports firm up or the Saudi blockade hardens. That single headline sets crude, and crude sets the airline cost line for the back half of summer.
