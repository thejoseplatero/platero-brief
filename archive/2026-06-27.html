Executive Pulse589 words

#Macro signal

U.S. re-frames AI as a national security asset, and the clock is ticking: The White House's AI Executive Order ("Promoting Advanced AI Innovation and Security," signed June 2) is now entering its active enforcement window, with major agency deliverables due July 2 and August 1. The EO establishes a voluntary framework requiring frontier AI developers to give the federal government 30-day pre-release model access before sharing with partners, and directs agencies to deploy AI-enabled cyber defenses on aggressive timelines. The strategic implication: AI has crossed from productivity play to sovereign capability. Any executive with AI in their roadmap is now operating in a policy environment where "who controls frontier models" is a geopolitical question, not just a commercial one. Expect increasing friction between open model access and national-security-first posturing, especially for companies operating across borders.

#Tech industry

  • Florida sues OpenAI, state-level AI liability is here: Florida became the first U.S. state to sue OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman personally, alleging ChatGPT contributed to user harm and accusing the company of prioritizing growth over safety. This opens a new front beyond federal regulation, product, legal, and trust-and-safety leaders must now track 50 potential state-level liability frameworks. Companies shipping AI-facing consumer products (including airlines) need a defensible duty-of-care posture, not just terms of service.
  • Alphabet commits $80B; Anthropic moves toward IPO; Qualcomm eyes Tenstorrent ($8-10B): Capital concentration in frontier AI is locking in fast. For leaders in travel, retail, and media, industries adjacent to Big Tech, the gap between AI haves and have-nots widens every quarter. The companies spending at this scale are not catching up; they are pulling away. Your AI roadmap is competing against players spending more in one quarter than most companies spend in a decade.

#AI at the leadership level

CEOs have officially become the AI function's owner: 75% are now the primary decision-maker on AI strategy, and companies are doubling AI spending as a share of revenue (0.8% → 1.7%) in 2026 (IBM CEO Study, BCG AI Radar). But a structural warning is emerging beneath this ambition: AI is quietly eliminating the entry-level roles that have historically trained future leaders. Junior hiring is down dramatically at AI-adopting organizations, graduate unemployment is climbing twice as fast as the broader workforce, and HBR research confirms companies are cutting entry-level positions based on AI *promises*, not proven capability. The leaders who see this now and build deliberate internal development pathways will have a structural talent advantage in 3-5 years. The ones who don't will find their bench hollow when they need it.

#Leadership & org design

The "big freeze" and the disappearing leadership pipeline: Yale, WEF, and HBR research are converging on the same signal, AI isn't just displacing workers, it's collapsing the apprenticeship model that creates leaders. Organizations are not mass firing, but they have stopped hiring at entry level, which is where leaders have always developed judgment, stakeholder fluency, and domain depth. For a Director or VP today, this creates two obligations: (1) build an internal development track that substitutes for the missing junior reps your team used to grow through; and (2) audit whether your AI-assisted team is actually developing capability or just throughput, because those diverge sharply when the tools fail.

#Question worth asking your team this week

> "If 30-40% of our entry-level work is absorbed by AI in the next 18 months, what is our deliberate plan for developing the next generation of product, design, and marketing leaders, and who owns that plan today?"

Practitioner Pulse517 words

#Try this now

  • RAG your design system: If you have a design system (you do), the most immediately valuable thing you can do is make it AI-readable. Export your component documentation as structured Markdown or JSON, then use it as the retrieval layer for any AI design tool. Figma AI and Galileo AI can ingest this context. Result: AI-generated UI that actually matches your system instead of hallucinating components. 20 minutes of setup, permanent payoff.
  • Nudge for AI search visibility: Go to nudge.ai and check how your brand's products appear inside ChatGPT and Gemini shopping recommendations. It's free to audit. For Air Canada, this means checking how flights, Aeroplan, and travel packages surface in AI-driven travel planning queries. This is the new SEO audit, most brands haven't done it yet.
  • Write a "pilot-to-production" one-pager: Before your next AI initiative, write one page that defines: what does working look like in production (not the pilot), what metric proves it, who owns it. This is the discipline that separates teams generating AI value from teams stuck in POC theater. Teach this framework, it works in any BrainStation session on AI in product.

#Case study worth stealing

Warner Bros. Discovery, Full adtech stack rebuild on agentic AI: WBD didn't layer AI onto their existing ad stack. They rebuilt the entire thing, media planning, audience forecasting, attribution, order management, campaign optimization, from the ground up with agents handling the workflow under human oversight. The result is a unified linear + digital advertising system. What to steal: the decision to rebuild rather than retrofit. Every org faces this choice when AI arrives. WBD chose rebuild. For Jose building a martech/adtech org from scratch at Air Canada, this is permission to design for AI-native from day one, not to build a legacy stack and add AI later.

#Framework or mental model

"AI in the value chain, not on top of it": The orgs generating real ROI from AI in 2026 are embedding it deep in operations, supply chain, core workflows, decision loops, not using it as a productivity surface on top of existing processes. The surface-level use (AI writing emails faster) generates marginal value. The deep use (AI restructuring how decisions are made and executed) generates structural advantage. Where to apply: when evaluating any new AI initiative, ask "is this on top of our process or inside it?" On top = marginal. Inside = strategic.

#Teach this tomorrow

The pilot-to-production gap, 75% of companies stuck in POC theater despite heavy AI investment. Use the WBD case study as the contrast: a company that went full production. Ask the class: "what's the difference between a pilot and a production deployment?" Then work backward to the discipline required. Lands in any AI, PM, or product strategy session.

#Rabbit hole

Morgan Stanley's AI market trends report (mid-2026): ~$2.9 trillion in global data center construction through 2028. AI is now a macro variable, not a tech story. Worth an hour if you want the full economic framing for why the infrastructure stack matters as much as the application layer.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • WPP acquires InfoSum / Publicis acquires Lotame: Two of the largest holding companies are buying data clean room and identity infrastructure vendors, not agencies or creative shops. Signal: the holding companies are repositioning as data infrastructure players. Identity resolution is the new battleground. If you're building a martech stack, your clean room and identity layer decision is now a 5-year strategic commitment, not a point solution.
  • ActiveCampaign Active Intelligence 2.8: Brand memory across AI sessions, voice, colors, logos, strategic priorities persist across automated workflows. Signal: the "brand AI" category is forming. Platforms that can hold and express brand identity at scale across automated outputs will win the mid-market.
  • Nudge launches Agentic Commerce Platform: Tracks SKU-level visibility inside AI chat engines. Signal: a new measurement category is emerging, "AI search presence" alongside SEO and SEM. Expect Google and Adobe to acquire in this space within 18 months.

#Category watch

Identity resolution is the hottest category in MarTech right now. With cookies gone and walled gardens tightening, the ability to recognize and reach the same person across channels without third-party cookies is the core infrastructure problem. WPP/InfoSum and Publicis/Lotame are both bets on this. The players: LiveRamp (public, the incumbent), InfoSum (now WPP), Lotame (now Publicis), Neustar, and a dozen well-funded startups. For Air Canada: Aeroplan's loyalty data is one of the most valuable first-party identity assets in Canadian travel. How you activate it in a cookieless world is a category-defining decision.

#Stack intelligence

The consolidation pattern in 2026: Large platforms are acquiring point solutions and folding them into suites. Agencies are acquiring data infrastructure. The result: the number of viable independent martech vendors will shrink significantly by 2028. For org builders, this means betting on platforms with acquisition momentum (Adobe, Salesforce, HubSpot) over best-of-breed point solutions that may not exist in 3 years. Or going open/composable so you're not locked in when consolidation happens.

#Foundations concept of the day

Clean Rooms: A clean room is a secure, privacy-safe environment where two parties (e.g. a brand and a publisher, or two brands) can combine and analyze their first-party data without either party ever seeing the other's raw data. Think of it as a vault where you can run computations on combined data but neither party can take the other's data home. Example: Air Canada and a hotel chain could combine loyalty data to find overlapping customers and build joint campaigns, without sharing customer lists. Google Ads Data Hub, Amazon Marketing Cloud, and InfoSum are the main players. This is the infrastructure that replaces third-party cookies for audience matching and measurement.

#Watch list

  • Retail media networks: Amazon, Walmart, Target, and now airlines are all building ad networks on top of their first-party data. Air Canada has the ingredients (Aeroplan data, high-intent travel audience). Watch how other loyalty-heavy brands monetize their data assets.
  • Adobe at Cannes Lions: Adobe's GenStudio enterprise push with agency partnerships is the most aggressive enterprise martech play of the summer. Watch for customer wins in Q3.
Markets Signal361 words

#Today's move

MarTech/AdTech indexes in pain: LUMA's ad tech and MarTech indices fell 21% and 27% respectively in Q1, massively underperforming the Nasdaq's 7% decline despite solid Q4 earnings. Deal volume down 14%, scaled deals down 60%. The market is pricing in uncertainty, not fundamentals. Buyers are only moving on AI, data, and measurement capabilities, everything else is stalled.

NVIDIA goes after Intel and AMD: NVIDIA unveiled its Vera CPU and RTX Spark superchip, a direct move into the CPU market for AI agents and PCs. Dell and Lenovo are launching it this fall. NVIDIA is no longer a chip company. It's becoming the full AI compute stack.

#AI infrastructure watch

The grid is the bottleneck, not the chips: US power interconnection queues have hit 2,100 gigawatts, exceeding total grid capacity. Result: 30-50% of planned 2026 data center capacity will slip to 2028. The hyperscalers are spending $635-690B combined in 2026 capex, but 75% of that goes to physical infrastructure (power, cooling, land, networking), not chips. The constraint isn't silicon anymore. It's electricity and copper.

Helium shortage hitting fabs: Strikes on Qatari helium production (one-third of global supply) have doubled spot prices. TSMC and Samsung are rationing helium. This is the kind of upstream supply chain move that doesn't make tech headlines but directly impacts chip production timelines.

Nuclear is accelerating: SMRs (Small Modular Reactors) are moving from concept to contract. Westinghouse has an $80B US government agreement to build new reactors for AI deployment. NuScale is the only SMR design with NRC certification, targeting hyperscale data centers specifically. The Microsoft/Constellation Energy nuclear playbook is becoming the industry template.

#Signal or noise?

The grid and energy constraint is structural, not cyclical, this is a multi-year bottleneck that will determine which AI capabilities actually ship at scale. The MarTech downturn is cyclical with a structural AI disruption layer on top, the category is being repriced, not killed.

#Watch tomorrow

TSMC's CEO warned global chip production capacity may fail to meet market needs for several years. Watch for any Q2 capacity announcements and whether AMD's server CPU record share (one-third of market) holds as NVIDIA expands into CPU territory.
