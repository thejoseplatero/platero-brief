Executive Pulse611 words

#Macro signal

Fed decides today with a hike, not a cut, on the table: The FOMC announces at 2:00pm ET, with consensus a fifth straight hold at 3.50 to 3.75 percent but markets pricing roughly a 30 percent chance of a rate increase. The June dot plot already flipped the median year-end 2026 rate up to 3.8 percent from 3.4 percent in March, and the IMF's July outlook says global disinflation has stalled, with headline inflation projected to rise to 4.7 percent this year. The direction of surprise is now up, not down. Every AI capex and infrastructure bet is being underwritten at a discount rate that could climb rather than fall, so any 2027 budget defense needs to show payback that survives a hike, not just a hold.

#Tech industry

  • The AI race became a war for electricity, financing, and fiber, and investors started pricing the risk: July 28 brought a cluster of capital moves. Amazon committed roughly 200 billion dollars to AI infrastructure in 2026, Nvidia backed a 50 billion dollar lease for a Texas facility running its own chips, AMD locked up 2.5 gigawatts of capacity from Core Scientific, and Verizon signed a 1 billion dollar dark-fiber deal with Google. At the same time AI chip stocks sold off as the market shifted from "is there demand" to "can trillion-dollar capex clear an adequate return, and when." The bottleneck has moved from models to power, real estate, and the balance sheet, and vendor financing where chipmakers underwrite their own customers' buildouts is circular-demand risk boards should now be probing.
  • The open-weight frontier went Chinese-led: Moonshot AI released Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter model under a modified MIT license, giving away frontier-scale capability and undercutting proprietary API economics. Google separately delayed Gemini 3.5 Pro after it missed on coding and reasoning in testing, and Alphabet fell about 4 percent on execution concerns. API-pricing moats are eroding from below while the incumbent leader stumbles on delivery, which makes multi-model architecture a board-level resilience question, not an engineering preference.

#AI at the leadership level

The accountability gap is the story. Roughly 76 percent of organizations now have a Chief AI Officer, up from 26 percent a year ago, but only 14 percent have defined who is accountable for AI results at the C-suite level, and 48 percent of AI projects miss their business objectives, most often because responsibility is undefined between CIO, CDO, and the business units. The org-chart response to AI has outrun the accountability response. The leadership move is not another exec title, it is a written accountability map naming who owns outcomes, plus a continuous-monitoring reporting line to the board, since AI is probabilistic and drifts rather than staying validated once.

#Leadership & org design

McKinsey's State of Organizations 2026, surveying more than 10,000 senior executives across 15 countries, finds 75 percent of organizations are failing to build high-performance cultures, with limited career progression the top barrier at 47 percent, ahead of weak incentives and disengagement. The specific move: the single-leader hierarchy is now treated as the obstacle, with leadership shifting toward teams of leaders holding distributed accountability. The live risk is that AI-driven delayering, visible this week in Visa cutting 2,600 tech and product roles citing automation, kills the very progression paths the data flags as the number-one performance lever. Cut layers without cutting the ladder.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If the board asked tomorrow who personally owns the return on our largest AI investment, and by when it pays back at a discount rate that rises rather than falls, could we name the person and the date?

Practitioner Pulse645 words

#Try this now

  • Record a Skill in Claude Cowork: Screen-record yourself doing a repetitive task once while narrating the steps aloud, and Cowork captures the screen activity, clicks, typing, and voice-over together, then turns it into a structured, rerunnable skill in your library. No prompt writing, no step lists. In the desktop app, use the plus menu, "Record a skill," pick one genuinely repetitive weekly chore (pulling a campaign report, reformatting a brief, QA-ing a UTM sheet), record it once, and rerun it with a click. It removes the automation-authoring bottleneck for non-engineers, the first mainstream teach-by-showing tool, and turns a 30-minute weekly task into a one-click rerun.
  • Route tasks across a multi-model stack: Instead of standardizing on one vendor, send each task to the cheapest model that clears the quality bar, high-value reasoning to a frontier model (Opus 5 shipped July 24 at roughly half the price of Opus 4.8), high-volume grunt work to cheap Flash-tier models. Audit your team's recurring AI tasks, tag each as reasoning-heavy or volume, assign a model tier, and track cost-per-output from day one. Cost efficiency lives in the multi-model stack, not single-vendor lock-in, and only 7 percent of orgs can currently prove AI ROI, so the number matters before your next budget review.
  • Automate AI-answer share-of-voice monitoring: Replace manual spot-checking of how your brand shows up in ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, and Gemini with continuous tracking of citation frequency, sentiment, and competitor share. Stand up a weekly automated query set of your top 20 category questions, log which brands get cited and in what tone, and pair it with an agent-ready catalog audit (clean structured attributes, complete descriptions, machine-readable policies) so shopping agents can actually recommend you. As generative platforms become the first point of contact for buyers, "are we even in the answer" is the new rank-tracking, and it got sharper this week with ChatGPT's self-serve ad platform going live July 27.

#Case study worth stealing

Schnucks, agentic commerce that keeps the data layer in-house: The regional US grocer is embedding an AI shopping assistant directly inside its existing Schnucks Rewards app, powered by VitalityIP's intelligence layer built on billions of lines of shopping, health, and nutrition data mapped to ingredient-level product attributes. The structural detail is the lesson: Schnucks keeps ownership of the customer relationship, shopper data, and brand experience while renting the AI and the database. Any digital team can adapt the tactic, embed the agent inside your own owned surface, the app or loyalty program, rather than handing customers to a third-party assistant, and contractually keep the data and the relationship. For travel and loyalty, swap nutrition guidance for trip planning and points optimization and the pattern is identical.

#Framework or mental model

Context engineering, the four moves: The discipline replacing prompt engineering as the named skill of 2026, now formalized by both the Thoughtworks Technology Radar and LangChain. Where prompt engineering asks what to tell the model, context engineering asks what the model needs to know, using four moves on everything in the context window: Write (persist state outside the prompt), Select (retrieve only relevant data and examples), Compress (summarize to fit the window), and Isolate (split work across separate agents so they do not pollute each other). When an AI workflow gives inconsistent output, the fix is almost always context, not a cleverer prompt, and because all four moves work in a plain chat window with zero code, this is a team-wide literacy play, not an engineering one.

#Rabbit hole

Anthropic's own usage data shows the overwhelming majority of Claude Cowork activity is non-technical knowledge work, scheduling, files, and ops, not software, a useful gut-check on where the real AI-adoption market is versus where the discourse points. VentureBeat's write-up ("most Cowork users aren't coding") is the short version worth reading before your next tooling debate.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • HubSpot: Opened Agent Hub and Agent Builder to all Professional and Enterprise customers. Agent Hub is a single console showing every HubSpot agent by GTM outcome (build demand, win deals, delight customers, scale growth), and Agent Builder is a low-code canvas where non-technical staff assemble agents from their own prompts, knowledge, and CRM data. Custom agents burn metered "HubSpot Credits." Signal: the CRM suites are racing to become the agent orchestration layer, not just the system of record, and the move to consumption pricing is a quiet break from seat-based SaaS economics.
  • Salesforce: Made its Agentforce Commerce Shopper, Buyer, and Merchant agents generally available, with native ChatGPT integration live and Google Search and Gemini following. Signal: Salesforce is planting commerce agents inside third-party AI surfaces rather than only its own UI, because the battleground is moving to where the buyer's agent actually shops. Whoever owns the merchant catalog and checkout wins agentic-commerce distribution, and the agentic CRM race is now the defining suite competition.
  • Google: AI Max exited beta with real numbers, 500,000-plus advertisers and a 15 percent average conversion lift, while Alphabet's Q2 ad revenue grew 14 percent to 81.6 billion dollars. Signal: Google is proving AI-native ad products both convert and monetize at scale, a counterweight to the bearish standalone-chatbot ad narrative, and with model prices collapsing (Opus 5 shipped at half its predecessor's price) the unit economics of embedding agents keep falling.

#Category watch

Agentic advertising: In early July, Meta, Google, Amazon, and OpenAI all shipped agentic ad tooling in the same week, and the framing across trackers is that AI ad tooling crossed from experiment to default. But the revenue thesis is being repriced hard: eMarketer pegs the entire US standalone chatbot ad market at under 1 billion dollars in 2026 and only 5.4 billion by 2030, against OpenAI's reported 100 billion dollar 2030 target. The category is splitting two ways, agentic buying inside the walled gardens is real and monetizing now, while standalone chatbot advertising is being marked down. For a stack builder: invest in agentic execution where inventory and measurement already exist, and treat chatbot ad inventory as experimental, not a budget line.

#Stack intelligence

The composable-CDP value proposition is getting swallowed by the lakehouse. Databricks launched CustomerLake, an agentic CDP embedded directly in the lakehouse, occupying the exact "activate data where it already lives" position that warehouse-native CDP vendors built their pitch on, and Snowflake and BigQuery equivalents are the natural next move. The buy-versus-build line has shifted: the question is no longer "which CDP" but "does my cloud data platform's native agentic CDP make a standalone CDP redundant." For anyone on Databricks, Snowflake, or BigQuery, the default is drifting toward native activation plus a thin best-of-breed layer, and standalone CDP vendors become consolidation targets.

#Foundations concept of the day

Composable CDP: A traditional Customer Data Platform is a standalone system that copies customer data out of your source systems into its own store, then unifies identities and pushes segments to marketing tools. A composable CDP inverts that. Instead of a separate database, it runs unification, identity, and activation directly on top of the cloud data warehouse or lakehouse you already operate, so the data never leaves its system of record. It matters at scale because duplicating billions of customer rows into a separate CDP is expensive, slow, and a governance liability, whereas activating in place keeps one source of truth. A retailer running its transaction history in Snowflake can add identity resolution and audience activation as a layer rather than exporting everything into a second platform, and in 2026 that same layer increasingly ships an agent on top of it.

#Watch list

  • Consumption pricing spreading across CRM suites: HubSpot Credits and Salesforce Agentforce consumption both meter agent usage. If agent cost scales with usage rather than seats, budget planning and vendor-cost forecasting for martech orgs change materially, so watch for cost-predictability tooling and buyer pushback.
  • Retail media's scaled second tier: Amazon and Walmart Connect still take roughly 88 percent of US retail media spend, but DoorDash Ads and Instacart Ads have each crossed about 1 billion dollars in annual US ad revenue. As total US retail media hits 71 billion dollars this year, the next inventory land-grab is in that second tier of delivery and grocery platforms.

#Macro pulse

Canada: The TSX Composite closed Tuesday at 35,749.70, up 0.51 percent, a fresh record high led by technology. USD/CAD sits near 1.408, roughly flat on the day and range-bound between 1.406 and 1.412 all week, so no international-revenue-translation flag today, though the loonie remains persistently weak in trend terms.

USA (July 28 close): The Dow rallied 1.03 percent to 52,747 on strong earnings and rotation out of semis, the S&P 500 edged up 0.21 percent to a near-record 7,428, and the Nasdaq slipped 0.22 percent, dragged by the chip selloff. The FOMC decision lands today at 2:00pm ET with the presser at 2:30pm, consensus a fifth straight hold at 3.50 to 3.75 percent. Treat the hold as expected, not fact, until the release.

Asia (July 29 session): Seoul's Kospi is the standout, down roughly 6.8 percent intraday as SK Hynix fell about 13 percent (record profit but a forecast miss) and Samsung dropped nearly 8 percent, an AI-capex reality check hitting the chipmakers hardest. The Nikkei fell 1.7 percent, Shanghai slipped 0.5 percent, and Hong Kong's Hang Seng was the lone gainer, up 1.7 percent.

Oil, flag: WTI closed near 81 dollars Tuesday after an 8 percent Monday plunge on US-Iran diplomatic hopes, but reversed hard today as fresh Middle East fighting erupted, with Brent up about 4 percent toward 88 dollars and crude broadly up more than 2 percent. The de-escalation that had cut jet-fuel input costs is unwinding, putting fuel risk back on the table.

#Today's industry move

JetBlue embedded installment flight financing from fintech ClarityPay directly into its TrueBlue loyalty program, with customers earning points on financed bookings. The more strategic Skift thread underneath it: travel customer acquisition is shifting away from Google's open auction toward private bilateral "private treaty" deals that trade loyalty-member access and inventory. For an airline CDO, that is the loyalty data layer becoming the acquisition currency, exactly the asset worth owning rather than renting.

#AI infrastructure watch

The chip stack is the whole story. Tuesday's US session saw AMD fall roughly 10 percent, Broadcom down 4 percent, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index off more than 4 percent, triggered by SK Hynix's miss, signs of progress in China's advanced chipmaking, and optics around NVIDIA reportedly in talks to provide OpenAI a 250 billion dollar backstop to fund a 10-gigawatt Ohio data-center campus. Critics read the backstop as circular vendor financing, NVIDIA underwriting its own largest customer, while for OpenAI it is a first step toward owning infrastructure rather than renting from Microsoft, Amazon, and Oracle. Context: the four hyperscalers are on track for up to 725 billion dollars of mostly-AI capex this year.

#Signal or noise?

Signal. The two-day chip rout is investors repricing AI capex for return timelines rather than doubting demand, and it rhymes with the same ROI scrutiny showing up in the Fed's higher-for-longer stance, a larger trend, not a one-day blip.

#Watch tomorrow

Today's oil reversal is the key watch-item into Air Canada's Q2 results on August 12, and the 2:00pm Fed decision plus any hint of a hike bias could compound the AI-capex repricing already underway.
