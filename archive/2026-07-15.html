Executive Pulse456 words

#Macro signal

Softer inflation reopens the rate-cut case: June inflation came in weaker than expected, and US equities rallied on July 14 (S&P 500 +0.4%, Nasdaq +0.9%) as bets on further Fed rate hikes eased. Underneath, oil kept climbing on US-Iran tensions, so the read is not "all clear," it is "disinflation and geopolitical cost pressure at the same time." For leaders, that means the planning environment is bifurcated: cheaper capital may be coming, but input and energy costs carry a tail risk. Build the 2027 plan with a cheaper-money base case and an oil-shock hedge, not one or the other.

#Tech industry

  • The AI budget reckoning has started: Uber reportedly burned its entire 2026 AI budget in four months, and Starbucks killed an AI inventory system after nine months. Strategic implication: the era of uncapped AI experimentation is closing, and the second-order winners are the leaders who instrumented ROI before scaling spend. Expect Q3 CFO reviews to separate AI programs tied to measurable decisions from the ones that were headcount of pilots.
  • TSMC Q2 earnings, July 16, as the AI bellwether: TSMC's report is being treated as a health check for the whole AI boom, with capex guidance and packaging capacity the tells. Strategic implication: the physical supply chain, not model quality, is now the binding constraint on how fast AI ambitions can actually be delivered.

#AI at the leadership level

Nearly three-quarters of CEOs now say they are their organization's primary decision maker on AI, more than double last year's share, and corporate AI spend is set to roughly double to about 1.7% of revenue. The signal is that AI has moved from a delegated IT initiative to a CEO-owned agenda, which means governance, org design, and authority are being redrawn at the top. The risk in that centralization is speed without instrumentation: CEOs owning the decision does not guarantee anyone owns the measurement.

#Leadership & org design

The organizations pulling ahead are redesigning how decisions get made and how authority is distributed, not just bolting agents onto existing structures. Cisco's move to put AI agents in the hands of all 90,000 employees, framed by its CFO in finance terms, is the tell: the win comes from tying deployment to a P&L outcome and instrumenting it, not from seat count. The practical pattern for any exec team is to pick one high-volume process, measure it before deploying, and report the delta, then let that number justify the next expansion.

#Question worth asking your team this week

For every AI initiative we are funding, can you name the specific decision it improves and the single metric that decision moves? If not, why is it still in the budget?

Practitioner Pulse416 words

#Try this now

  • Claude Cowork / ChatGPT Work for the boring long-running job: Both shipped this month (Cowork July 7, ChatGPT Work July 9) and both target the same thing: hand off a multi-hour task that runs even when you close the laptop. The highest-leverage first use is not a demo, it is a recurring operational chore. Point one at "turn this folder of vendor contracts into a renewals tracker with risks and dates flagged," or "build tomorrow's client deck from these call transcripts and the pipeline export." Twenty minutes to set up a task you run every week is the test of whether agent mode earns a seat in your workflow or stays a toy.
  • Trigger inside the tools your team already lives in: The quieter July release was the Slack + Salesforce agent integrations. The lesson for a practitioner: adoption dies at the context switch. If the AI action fires from a Slack message or a CRM record instead of a separate app, manual handoffs and approval delays drop without retraining anyone. When you scope any internal AI workflow this quarter, put the entry point where the work already happens, not in a new destination.

#Case study worth stealing

Cisco, agents to all 90,000 employees: Cisco is rolling AI agents out to every employee, with the CFO framing it in finance terms rather than IT terms. The tactic worth copying is the framing, not the rollout. They tied the program to measurable finance outcomes (cycle time, spend, close speed) instead of a headcount of seats deployed. Any digital team can adapt this: pick one process with a number attached, instrument it before you deploy, and report the delta in that number. It reframes AI from a cost line into a P&L conversation, which is the only version executives keep funding.

#Framework or mental model

Measure by decision outcomes, not deployment counts: The sharpest line from this month's enterprise coverage is that as CFO reviews of AI spend intensify, the leaders in a stronger position are the ones who built measurement frameworks tied to decision outcomes rather than the number of tools or seats deployed. Seat counts and pilot counts are vanity metrics that survive exactly until the first budget review. Apply it by defining, for every AI initiative, the specific decision it improves and the metric that decision moves, before you deploy anything. If you cannot name the decision, you do not have a project, you have a demo.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Databricks CustomerLake: Databricks entered the marketing category with CustomerLake, an agentic CDP built directly on the lakehouse and governed by Unity Catalog, consolidating identity resolution, audience building, campaign automation, and activation into one AI-native layer. Its open partner list already spans Adobe, LiveRamp, The Trade Desk, Epsilon, Acxiom, Braze, Magnite, and more, with HP, Circle K, and AB InBev as early adopters. Signal: the CDP is being absorbed into the data platform. When the warehouse ships identity, audiences, and activation as native agents, the standalone CDP loses its reason to exist as a separate purchase. The buying question shifts from "which CDP" to "does my data platform already do this."
  • The agent super-apps land in the enterprise: Anthropic's Claude Cowork (July 7) and OpenAI's ChatGPT Work (July 9) both shipped long-running task agents that plug into email, calendar, files, and connected SaaS. Signal: the application layer is consolidating into agent surfaces that sit above your existing tools. For a martech org this means the interface to your stack is starting to move from dashboards to agents, and vendors that only sell a UI, not data or activation, are the most exposed.

#Category watch

Agentic CDPs: The CDP category is being redefined in real time from a database you query to a workforce of agents that continuously analyze, decide, and act on customer data. Databricks is the loudest entrant, but the pattern (identity plus activation plus autonomous agents on top of first-party data) is where every player is heading. For anyone building a martech org, the near-term decision is whether to keep a best-of-breed CDP or let it collapse into the data platform you already run.

#Stack intelligence

The consolidation logic this year is gravitational: data-heavy platforms are pulling application functions down into the layer that already holds the data. A CDP that lives on top of the warehouse now competes with the warehouse itself shipping the same features natively and governed by one catalog. The practical read for stack owners: assume any function that is "just logic over your first-party data" (audiences, identity, segmentation, activation) will get absorbed by the platform that stores the data. Buy specialized tools for genuine differentiation, not for work the data layer will commoditize within 18 months.

#Foundations concept of the day

Identity resolution: The process of stitching many fragmented signals (an email, a loyalty ID, a cookie, a device, an app login, a phone number) into a single persistent view of one customer. It works by matching deterministic keys where they exist (a logged-in ID) and probabilistic signals where they do not (device, behavior, location), then maintaining that graph as new signals arrive. It matters at scale because everything downstream (personalization, frequency capping, attribution, suppression) is only as good as your ability to know that these ten touchpoints are one person. Real example: a grocery chain that reconciles its app, loyalty card, and e-commerce logins into one profile can cap ad frequency and personalize offers across channels; a competitor treating each as a separate user wastes spend re-targeting the same shopper three times.

#Watch list

#Macro pulse

  • USA: Stocks rose July 14 after softer-than-expected June inflation eased Fed rate-hike bets. S&P 500 +0.4% to 7,543.59, Nasdaq +0.9% to 26,107.01, Dow essentially flat (+0.02%) at 52,508.27. Tech and chips led the rebound after Monday's rout.
  • Canada: TSX had closed roughly flat Monday (~35,253) after US-Iran military exchanges; the risk-on US session sets a firmer tone into midweek.
  • Asia: Hang Seng edged +0.2% to ~24,214 with tech giving back gains late; broadly steady rather than directional.
  • Oil: WTI up ~1% to ~$80.21 on US-Iran tensions. Not a 2%+ move, but the direction matters: energy cost pressure is building even as inflation cools, a squeeze on airline margins to watch.

#Today's industry move

Databricks formally entered martech with CustomerLake, an agentic CDP on the lakehouse, pulling identity, audiences, and activation into the data platform and partnering across LiveRamp, The Trade Desk, Epsilon, and Adobe. The market signal: the CDP category is consolidating into the data warehouse, pressuring standalone customer-data vendors.

#AI infrastructure watch

TSMC reports Q2 earnings July 16, treated across the Street as the health check for the entire AI buildout. It controls 90%+ of leading-edge production, plans $52-56B in 2026 capex (up ~25%), and has told Apple, Nvidia, and Qualcomm to expect 5-10% price hikes on advanced nodes. Memory stocks plunged even as TSMC sales jumped ~36%, a sign demand is concentrating in leading-edge AI silicon while the rest of the chip stack wobbles.

#Signal or noise?

Signal. The inflation-driven rally and the AI-capex concentration are both trend, not blip: disinflation is reopening the rate-cut path while compute demand keeps funneling into a single leading-edge chokepoint.

#Watch tomorrow

TSMC Q2 earnings (July 16): capex guidance and CoWoS packaging capacity will set the tone for AI hardware sentiment and, by extension, how much runway the agent-feature wave has.
