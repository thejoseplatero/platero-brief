Executive Pulse558 words

#Macro signal

Higher-for-longer is now the base case, not the risk case: With the funds rate held at 3.50-3.75% and 2026 inflation revised up to 3.6%, futures now price the path drifting toward 4% by year-end under a more hawkish Fed. The strategic read: the era of assuming rate relief is bailing out weak unit economics is over. Growth plans that only pencil out with cheaper capital next year need to pencil out at today's cost of money. For tech and product leaders, that means ruthless prioritization of the roadmap toward things that generate cash inside 12 months, not 36. Discount-rate discipline is back as a board conversation.

#Tech industry

  • AI infrastructure demand is real and it is bifurcating the market: Micron posted fiscal Q3 revenue of $41.5B (up from $9.3B a year prior) on DRAM and NAND tied to AI buildout, and semis led the tape higher. The strategic implication is a widening gap between companies selling AI capacity and companies still trying to prove AI ROI. If you are a buyer, supplier pricing power is rising. Lock capacity and terms now rather than assuming the AI cost curve bends in your favor next quarter.
  • Palantir's 84.7% revenue growth signals where budget is actually landing: The money is moving to platforms that operationalize AI against proprietary data, not standalone models. The lesson for any org: differentiation is your data plus your workflows, not access to a frontier model everyone else can also rent.

#AI at the leadership level

The governance gap is now a board liability, and the regulatory clock is doing something unusual: moving. Fresh data shows 55% of enterprises actively deploying AI but only 26% with governance frameworks that keep pace, and 60% of executives expect their board to intervene over a botched AI strategy. Meanwhile the EU AI Act's high-risk obligations, long fixed to August 2, 2026, are now slated for deferral under the Digital Omnibus (stand-alone high-risk systems pushed toward December 2027), pending formal publication before the August date. Do not read the delay as a reprieve. The transparency obligations and enforcement architecture still land, and 78% of organizations have taken no meaningful compliance steps. The board move: treat AI governance as a standing committee item with a named owner, not a legal cleanup you can time to a deadline that keeps shifting.

#Leadership & org design

The flattening is producing measurable results when paired with real enablement, not just headcount math. Average span of control has climbed from roughly 8 in 2013 to 12+ today and is trending toward 25 by 2028. The instructive case: one large industrial reported customer teams doubling revenue in a single 90-day cycle and a product reaching market a year early after cutting management layers from 11-12 down to 6-7. The nuance that separates winners from cargo-cult flatteners: wider spans only work when you invest in the conditions (clear decision rights, better tooling, AI-assisted coordination) that let a manager actually lead 14 people. Removing the layer without funding the enablement just moves the bottleneck up.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If our cost of capital stays near 4% and never comes down, which two things on our roadmap do we kill this quarter, and which one do we double down on because it pays back inside a year?

#Try this now

  • Corvic AI V5 (prompt-to-workflow): Turns a plain-language prompt into a repeatable, versioned workflow instead of a one-off answer. Take one ad-hoc request your team keeps re-doing by hand (weekly campaign QA, a recurring competitive scan, a data pull) and describe it once. You get a standardized process you can rerun and hand off. Worth 20 minutes because it converts tribal knowledge into a durable asset, not another throwaway chat.
  • Wrike Conversational AI Agent Builder: Build custom task automations in Wrike by describing them in natural language, no scripting. If your team already lives in Wrike, spend 20 minutes automating one intake-to-assignment path (brief submitted, fields validated, owner assigned, due date set). It removes the manual triage tax on every new request.
  • Akeneo Agentic Ziggy (shipped July 8): An agent layer over the product catalog that runs continuous completeness checks across millions of SKUs and auto-simplifies retailer syndication errors into plain guidance. For any commerce or content team, point a data-quality agent at your worst catalog and let it flag the gaps that suppress discovery. The practical hook: as AI-driven discovery replaces browse and search, product data quality is now a ranking input, not back-office hygiene.

#Case study worth stealing

Salesforce, Agentforce Commerce GA (July 6): Salesforce shipped three specialized agents, Shopper (discovery to checkout to service), Buyer (B2B inside WhatsApp and SMS), and Merchant (catalog and merchandising in plain language), and reports early customers seeing double-digit lifts in both conversion and add-to-cart. The load-bearing stat for any digital team: AI-referred traffic converts at roughly eight times the rate of social, and retailers running their own shopper agents grew sales 59% faster than those sitting it out. You do not need Salesforce to steal the move. Split the shopping journey into narrow agent roles rather than one do-everything bot, and instrument AI-referral as its own high-intent traffic source so it stops getting buried in your generic organic bucket.

#Framework or mental model

MAP (Model, Assumptions, Plan): Before executing, write down your model of how the thing works, the assumptions that model depends on, and only then the plan. It forces the hidden assumptions into the open where they can be tested cheaply, which is exactly where most product and marketing bets quietly fail. Use it on your next roadmap or campaign call: spend the first ten minutes listing assumptions and the riskiest one becomes the thing you validate this sprint instead of six weeks in.

AI Stack Daily787 words

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Omneky: Shipped a public API and an MCP (Model Context Protocol) server today, exposing its autonomous ad-creative engine to any AI agent. Pass a single brand URL and it reads positioning, colors, and fonts, writes headline and CTA, and renders on-brand ads in every aspect ratio. Signal: Creative generation is becoming a callable service inside agent workflows, not a seat in a tool. The unit of adtech is shifting from a UI a marketer logs into to an endpoint an agent invokes. Plan your stack around who owns the orchestration layer that calls these endpoints.
  • BlueConic / Blueshift: BlueConic (capture and unify first-party behavioral data) acquired Blueshift (cross-channel decision-and-execute engine for email, SMS, push, in-app, web). Combined base is 600-plus customers across CPG, retail, DTC, and travel. Signal: The CDP category is collapsing the wall between the data layer and the activation layer. A CDP that only unifies profiles is now a feature, not a product. Buyers should re-scope RFPs around closed-loop decisioning, not schema unification.
  • Databricks / CustomerLake: Databricks' agentic CDP, launched mid-June, runs Customer 360, identity resolution, audience building, and activation natively on the lakehouse with no separate CDP data store, governed by Unity Catalog. Private preview names HP, Circle K, and AB InBev. Signal: The data-warehouse vendors are attacking the standalone CDP from below. If the customer data already lives in the lakehouse, the composable-CDP thesis (rent activation, own the data layer) gets a native home and the packaged CDP loses its reason to hold a copy of your data.

#Category watch

Agentic CDP: The customer-data layer is being redrawn in a single quarter. Databricks CustomerLake, BlueConic-Blueshift, and warehouse-native players are all converging on the same claim: unify the data, then let agents decide and act on it continuously rather than in scheduled campaigns. For anyone building a martech org, the practical shift is that "CDP" now spans two jobs that used to be separate purchases (data unification and real-time decisioning), and the incumbents that only do the first are acquisition targets or feature-fodder. Evaluate on where the decisioning loop lives and whether it can run without exporting your data to a vendor's black box.

#Stack intelligence

Enterprises are increasingly building the customer-data layer where the data already sits (the lakehouse or warehouse) and treating activation, messaging, and creative as swappable services called on top of it. The tell is the MCP server: Omneky, Mirakl Ads, and Dynamics 365 Commerce all shipped MCP-native surfaces this cycle, which means the stack is being wired for agent-to-agent invocation, not human-to-UI. The org implication is that your leverage moves upstream to owning the profile store, the identity spine, and the governance layer, while execution tools become interchangeable behind an orchestration agent. Design for portability at the activation edge and defensibility at the data core.

#Foundations concept of the day

Retail media networks (RMNs): A retail media network is an advertising business a retailer builds on top of its own store, app, and first-party purchase data. Instead of only selling products, the retailer sells ad placements (sponsored listings on-site, off-site programmatic, in-store screens) to the brands that want to reach its shoppers, and it targets and measures those ads using its own transaction data. It matters at scale because it is one of the few high-margin, first-party-data-native ad channels left standing as third-party cookies decay: the retailer already knows who bought what, so attribution is closed-loop and does not depend on external tracking. Cross-industry, this is no longer just Amazon and Walmart. Grocery, specialty retail, and food-delivery players are standing up networks (LiveRamp is piloting agentic tooling specifically for grocery and delivery commerce media), and the same logic extends to any business with a logged-in audience and transaction history: a bank, an airline, or a travel platform can run the identical playbook by monetizing its own audience for partner brands. The strategic question for a leader is not whether to buy RMN inventory but whether your own first-party data qualifies you to build one.

#Watch list

  • MCP as the adtech integration standard: A finalized MCP spec is expected July 28. If it lands, expect a wave of "MCP-native" ad servers and creative engines through Q3, and integration strategy stops being about API connectors and starts being about which agent orchestrates the calls. Watch who publishes servers first, because early standard-setters shape the category.
  • Fox / Roku: Fox's $22B agreement for Roku (100M-plus streaming households, first-party CTV data, expected close first half 2027) consolidates content, a connected-TV platform, and a data spine under one owner. Watch for the identity and clean-room implications: another walled garden of logged-in TV viewership tightening around a single ad stack.

#Today's industry move

SK Hynix debuts on Nasdaq under SKHYV (converts to SKHY July 13), pricing 177.9M ADSs at $149 to raise ~$26.5B. Largest ADR offering in market history, bigger than Alibaba's 2014 listing. This is the memory maker behind the HBM stacked next to nearly every AI accelerator at Microsoft, Amazon, Google, and Meta. A foreign chipmaker choosing US markets to fund fabs is a direct read on where the AI capital is priced. Separately, Delta beat with $1.56 EPS vs $1.48 expected on $17.67B revenue, and guided Q3 to $2.00 to $2.50 with mid-teens revenue growth. Bastian's tell for the whole travel sector: fares stay strong on disciplined capacity, and the industry now passes fuel costs through to consumers rather than eating margin. DAL still fell ~2.4% on the fuel line, but the pricing-power thesis is the durable signal for any premium carrier.

#AI infrastructure watch

The SK Hynix listing is the infrastructure story today. HBM is the physical bottleneck on AI capacity, and a $26.5B raise earmarked for new fabs and equipment tells you the memory shortage is real enough to fund at scale. Korea-listed Hynix is up ~770% over twelve months, outrunning Micron's ~700%. Chip stocks softened into the debut on nerves about whether this marks a top. The quieter structural shift underneath: Broadcom's custom ASICs for Alphabet and Meta are eating NVIDIA's narrative, with custom silicon forecast to compound faster than merchant accelerators. NVIDIA is up only ~3% YTD while AMD has ripped past 170%. The accelerator monopoly is fragmenting toward custom.

#Signal or noise?

Signal on infrastructure, blip on the tape: the SK Hynix mega-listing and the ASic-versus-GPU divergence are structural repricings of the AI stack, while today's flat indices and the oil dip are noise.

#Watch tomorrow

Where SKHYV settles after its first full session. A strong close narrows the "Korea discount" and validates the AI trade; a fade would be the first crack in the memory-shortage narrative.
