#Macro signal

The Fed debate has moved from whether to hike to how fast, because inflation is now spreading into services that have nothing to do with energy or tariffs: Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan, a voter this year, said in Houston on 10 November that the FOMC "needs to implement at least two more 25-basis-point rate increases," that the pace could quicken this winter, and that the Fed should pause and assess only after both. Governor Michelle Bowman warned that "price pressures may be broadening," while Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack still argue for a gradual path. The evidence behind the hawks is the composition of the October data: doctor visits, childcare, dining, hotels and streaming all rising, smartphone prices up a record 1.4 percent in a single month, and computer and TV prices climbing against their long-run trend. Markets now price roughly 90 percent odds of a December hike, and odds of a second hike in January have roughly tripled to more than 64 percent. The 10-year Treasury touched 5.02 percent on 12 November before settling near 4.99 percent.

The strategic read: when inflation sits in services and in categories that historically deflate, it is a pricing-behaviour problem, not a supply shock that fades. For product and marketing leaders that cuts two ways. Suppliers, agencies and SaaS vendors will push escalators into 2027 renewals, so lock multi-year terms now where leverage exists. And customer price sensitivity will rise just as your own cost base does, so any 2027 plan that assumes price increases pass through cleanly should be tested against a segment-by-segment elasticity view before it goes to the board.

#Tech industry

  • Anthropic set its IPO range at $70 to $78 a share, implying up to a $1.404 trillion valuation, with pricing expected 18 November and Nasdaq trading as "ANT" around 19 November: The updated filing on 10 November covers 825 million Class A shares, raising $57.75 to $64.35 billion, plus a $26 billion non-voting private placement to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC, with Google and Nvidia taking $10 billion each. The filing shows an $81 billion revenue run rate at the end of October, $17 billion of third-quarter revenue, a nine-month net loss of $104 billion, $132 billion in cash and short-term investments, and $900.9 billion in compute and infrastructure commitments. Founders hold 50.1 percent of voting power and a Long-Term Benefit Trust elects the board majority, so public Class A holders get limited influence. Strategic implication: a core AI model supplier is about to become a quarterly-reporting public company whose suppliers are also its shareholders. That brings more disclosure, which helps vendor due diligence, and more pressure to show margin, which tends to show up as pricing and packaging changes for enterprise customers. Contracts signed this quarter should anticipate that.
  • United Airlines banned third-party AI agents from booking, changing or servicing its tickets, effective 11 November: The new rule covers assistants built into search, social and messaging platforms, AI browsers, standalone agents, and any AI tool reaching United through a travel agency or aggregator, and it gives the airline authority to cancel reservations made that way. The official rationale is credential security, unintended purchases or MileagePlus redemptions, and federal check-in requirements. CEO Scott Kirby was more direct: "To be honest, I don't want to create another middleman." Southwest has taken the opposite position and opened its inventory to ChatGPT users. Strategic implication: this is the first major consumer brand to treat agentic commerce as a distribution threat rather than a channel, and it frames the decision every brand with a direct channel now faces. The question is not whether agents will transact, it is who owns the customer relationship and the economics when they do. Brands that block early keep pricing and loyalty data but risk being invisible where demand starts; brands that open early gain reach but may be negotiating referral fees with a new intermediary within two years. Either way, this needs a deliberate policy owned at the executive level, not a default set by a terms-of-service update.

#AI at the leadership level

Two studies released on 12 November describe the same gap from different angles. Genpact surveyed more than 2,000 executives across 17 industries at companies with $1 billion to over $50 billion in revenue: 79 percent have AI agents in production, but only 15 percent have linked AI investment to P&L results, 49 percent of funded AI projects in the past 24 months met value expectations, and just 9 percent have completed a major operational transformation. Notably, 92 percent of companies running agents in production say their human headcount stayed the same or grew. HFS Research and Rackspace surveyed 502 senior AI decision-makers: only 33 percent met or exceeded their AI business case, 96 percent report significant governance gaps, 78 percent see governance failure as a bigger threat to scaling than technology failure, and 44 percent lack a single consistent way of running AI in production. Success rates rise from 37 percent when AI lives in one function to 56 percent when it is embedded in standard operations.

The implication is that deployment is no longer the hard part. Genpact's CTO put it plainly: "Enterprises have become increasingly skilled at deploying AI agents. The real challenge lies in operating them." The organizations getting returns are treating AI as an operating model question, with one accountable owner for value, a common production standard across functions, and P&L attribution designed in from the start. If an AI portfolio review still counts use cases launched rather than dollars attributed, it is measuring the part that no longer differentiates.

#Leadership & org design

Nvidia's CEO resigned on 12 November with no successor in place, and the board's response is a case study in governance built too late. Jensen Huang stepped down as president, CEO and director effective immediately, following his admission of an affair with an executive at Megaspeed whom US intelligence suspects of working for China's Ministry of State Security, and a second reported relationship with a direct report. CFO Colette Kress becomes interim CEO while remaining CFO, former Synopsys CEO Harvey Jones becomes the company's first-ever board chair, and the board will search internal and external candidates. Third-quarter results remain scheduled for 17 November. The shares fell nearly 10 percent, pushing market value below $4 trillion, after losing more than $450 billion since the investigation was announced on 8 November. Former Nasdaq chairman Robert Greifeld said the board "has done a horrible job of succession planning."

The lesson is structural. A founder-led company at the center of a global supply chain ran for three decades without an independent chair and without a visible successor, and both had to be created in a single afternoon under crisis conditions. Most organizations have smaller versions of the same exposure: a function, platform or customer relationship that depends on one leader, with no named successor and no separation between the person who runs it and the people who oversee it. Succession coverage for every role that would move the business if vacated overnight is the cheapest risk control a leadership team can buy, and it only works if it exists before it is needed.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If the leader of our most critical function or platform were gone tomorrow, who runs it on Monday, who decides, and how long before customers or partners notice?

#Try this now

  • Rebalance AI search effort toward your own product pages: Peec AI published the first edition of Primary Sources on November 11. It combines a September survey of 700 marketing decision-makers in the US, UK, and Germany with more than 100 million AI chats across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, Gemini, and Claude from July to September. Between those months, company websites rose from 52 to 58 percent of citations, product pages went from 13.8 to 20.1 percent, and "Best X" listicles fell from 16.2 to 7.3 percent. Meanwhile 89 percent of marketers raised AI search spend and only 24 percent can show a return. The 20-minute exercise: take your five highest-revenue products or routes, ask each assistant the comparison question a customer would ask, and check whether your own product page gets cited or whether a third-party roundup does. If it's the roundup, the fix is structured specs, prices, and policies on your own page, not more placements in listicles.
  • LoyaltyLion's branded agent and MCP connection, as a model for opening a loyalty program to assistants: On November 11 LoyaltyLion added an MCP connection, so compatible assistants including Shopify Sidekick can query program configuration, member data, and orders. It also added a Branded Agent that recommends optimizations drawn from 10,000+ programs, with brand review required and an audit log, plus Drops for one-off, time-limited rewards to a segment. Even if you don't use LoyaltyLion, copy the governance shape: the agent proposes, a human approves, and every change is logged. Write those three rules down before any assistant gets write access to your loyalty rules.
  • Riskified Agent Identity Risk Intelligence, for service teams already fielding agent traffic: Announced November 11 with Q3 results. It identifies the AI agent behind an order or a customer service request, resolves it to the consumer it acts for, and returns a real-time risk signal. A Zendesk partnership puts that signal inside the service workflow. Ask your CX lead one question this week: can we tell today whether a refund or change request came from a person or an agent acting for them? If the answer is no, that gap is where the first agent-driven fraud and goodwill-abuse cases will show up.

#Case study worth stealing

Square, its own agent at the front door and an open door for everyone else's: On November 12 Square made Squaremates generally available to US sellers as the main entry point to the Square Dashboard. It runs multistep work in its own workspace: finding lapsed customers and re-engaging them with targeted marketing, bulk catalog updates, schedules built from labor cost and sales trends, finished sales reports and branded emails. The same release shipped a Square MCP server so sellers can authorize third-party assistants, a CLI for scripting against Square APIs, and a beta restocking feature that drafts supplier orders for approval. The pattern to adapt is building both surfaces at once: a first-party agent where your customers already work, plus a governed MCP endpoint for the assistants they bring, so you aren't forced to pick between owning the experience and being reachable. Any digital team with a logged-in dashboard, member portal, or partner extranet can map its top five repetitive tasks onto both surfaces.

#Framework or mental model

Capability first, distribution second (B2A): In an essay published November 11, two a16z partners argue that every platform shift pairs a new capability with a new distribution channel: directories for phones, search for the web, app stores for mobile, and now agents. They say AI has delivered the capability but not yet the distribution layer, and that businesses that spent years blocking bots will now compete for their business. They cite agent traffic up 8x between August and October 2026 on HUMAN customer sites, with Meta's Muse at 59 percent of agent requests, and AI-referred traffic converting 60 percent better in July. Treat the numbers carefully, since critics point out the traffic chart counts all automation rather than AI agents alone. The useful move for digital leaders is to ask which past shift your org handled worst (SEO, app store presence) and make sure you don't repeat the same delay with agents.

#Rabbit hole (optional)

Skift, Citi Says AI Companies Should Pay When Their Agents Book the Wrong Travel (November 11): Citi's head of value cards, lending and commerce argues that liability for agent booking errors should sit with the agent's maker, enforced when agents register with Visa or Mastercard to transact. The industry is building a way to embed the traveler's original instructions inside tokenized transactions, so issuers and merchants can check intent when a chargeback lands. A Visa study of 75+ agents found 43 percent made errors and about half of those completed the purchase anyway, and even the best performers reached only 82 percent accuracy. Read it next to MCR Hotels' CEO saying the same day he wants to block AI bots because they resist impulse upsells, and Skift's finding that only 10 of 50 hotel brand sites address AI crawlers in robots.txt. Together they sketch the liability, merchandising, and access questions every travel and loyalty team will be asked about in 2027. https://skift.com/2026/11/11/citi-says-ai-companies-should-pay-when-their-agents-book-the-wrong-travel/

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Google is stacking local retailer ads inside AI Mode: PPC Land reported on 12 November that AI Mode served a "Sponsored" pack of four golf shoes introduced as "options available from local retailers" (DICK'S Sporting Goods, Golf Galaxy, Golf Headquarters, Worldwide Golf Shops, Carls Golfland), with a second paid layer labelled "Sponsored stores" in the product panel. One analyst counted four distinct paid placements in a single answer. Google's own help page lists local inventory ads in AI experiences as an experiment and has not commented on the screenshots. The context is Alphabet's Q3: Search revenue grew 12%, down from 17% in Q2, and management blamed "lower monetization we were seeing in AI Mode." AI Mode also shows roughly 95% fewer product listings than classic search on the same query. Signal: Google is closing its AI Mode monetization gap by adding paid density to fewer slots. Fewer organic listings plus more sponsored layers means local inventory feeds and store data become paid media inputs. Any retailer with physical stores should treat local inventory feed quality as an ad-ops responsibility now, because the answer box is turning into a shelf that gets rented.
  • TikTok Shop launched a paid membership to its top 1 million US shoppers: TikTok Shop Plus went live the weekend of 8 to 9 November (first reported by Modern Retail on 9 November, followed by Digiday on 11 November) at $9.99 a month after a 30-day trial, undercutting Amazon Prime ($14.99) and Walmart+ ($12.95). Perks include free shipping on eligible items, an extra 10% off select products, three monthly coupons, instant store-credit refunds and holiday-exclusive deals. It lands as Best Buy, Ulta Beauty and Sephora sell on TikTok Shop for their first holiday season. Signal: every platform that owns an ad business wants a paid loyalty layer on top, because a subscription identifies the highest-value buyers and makes them logged-in, deterministic and measurable. Prime is the template: membership data feeds the retail media network. Expect TikTok to use Shop Plus behaviour to sharpen GMV Max targeting and pitch closed-loop measurement on its most valuable cohort.

#Category watch

AI-era attribution: The IAB released its AI-Era Attribution Blueprint on 12 November. It asks three questions at the moment of conversion (who created the intent, who made the decision, who completed the purchase) and sorts each conversion into Human-Owned, AI-Assisted or AI-Executed. It asks AI platforms and measurement vendors to disclose whether recommendations were paid or organic, reveal which sources informed them, and label AI-originated traffic consistently. It is built to sit next to existing attribution models, not replace them. The IAB's own framing is that the clicks and referrals attribution depends on "are thinning out or disappearing," and 45% of ad buyers name it a top measurement problem. The pressure is real: a Peec AI survey of 700 marketing decision-makers in the US, UK and Germany released 11 November found 89% raised AI search spend in the past year and only 24% can show what it returns. For a martech org, this is a taxonomy before it is a tool. Teams that start tagging conversions with these three labels now will have a year of baseline data by the time vendors productize it.

#Stack intelligence

Server-side tagging just changed from a privacy control into a privacy default you have to defend. Google notified advertisers in early November, and PPC Land followed up on 12 November, that from 11 January 2027 server-side Google Tag Manager will pass full visitor IP addresses to Google Ads, Display & Video 360, Search Ads 360 and Campaign Manager 360 tags unless the container owner redacts them. Google Analytics tags keep receiving a redacted IP. This is opt-out, the reverse of the AdSense full-IP control added in June 2026, which shipped switched off. IP addresses are personal data under EU case law, and Google's notice puts the consent burden on the advertiser. The practical move: inventory every sGTM container before January, decide per tag whether full IP is justified by a lawful basis, and use Transformations to strip it where it is not. The broader pattern is that the server container, which many organizations adopted to regain control of data flows, is now a place where vendor defaults can widen data sharing without a code change on the website. Whoever owns the container owns the compliance exposure, so that ownership needs to sit with a named team, not with whoever set it up.

#Foundations concept of the day

Server-side tagging: A tag is a small piece of code that sends data about a visitor's action (a page view, an add-to-cart, a purchase) to a marketing or analytics vendor. Traditionally every vendor's tag ran in the visitor's browser, so a site with 30 vendors made 30 separate calls, each one able to read whatever the browser exposed. Server-side tagging moves that work to a server the company controls: the browser sends one stream of events to the company's own endpoint, and that server decides what to forward to each vendor, in what form, after removing or transforming fields. Why it matters at scale: pages load faster, data survives browser restrictions and ad blockers better, and the company gets a single choke point to enforce consent and strip personal data before it leaves. The trade-off is that the server becomes critical infrastructure with hosting costs, monitoring needs and its own default settings, as the Google IP change above shows. Example: a large online retailer running a holiday sale can send the full order value to its own server, forward revenue but no email address to an ad platform, and forward hashed email only to the platforms where the customer gave consent, all from one event, without adding a single new script to the checkout page.

#Watch list

  • Agent-originated traffic as a labelled channel: The IAB blueprint asks for consistent labelling of AI-originated traffic, and fraud vendors are already building the plumbing on the commerce side. Watch for analytics suites and CDPs to add "agent" as a first-class source dimension next to organic, paid and referral. The first vendor to ship it natively sets the default schema everyone else maps to.
  • Proof of AI ROI as a budget gate: Validity's Winter 2026-2027 survey of 250+ marketers, released 11 November, found 61% face C-suite pressure to prove AI gains, 26% of 2026 AI investments fell short or showed no measurable return, and AI tools and agents ranked second (16%) behind paid media (20%) as the line most likely to be cut mid-year. AI tooling is moving from innovation budget into the same scrutiny as media spend.

#Today's industry move

Imprint raised $130 million at a roughly $1.9 billion post-money valuation, led by Khosla Ventures with Thrive, Ribbit, Hedosophia and Spice participating, per a martech news roundup. Imprint builds co-branded credit cards for consumer brands (Albertsons, Fanatics, Kroger, Shell) and says cardholder spend more than doubled from 2025 to 2026. It also launched an MCP server so AI agents can read member rewards, issue virtual cards and request authorization for agent-initiated purchases. The co-brand card, the economic engine of airline loyalty, is being rebuilt as an API that agents can transact against. Loyalty programs that cannot expose earn, burn and payment to an agent will be routed around.

#AI infrastructure watch

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO and board member effective immediately, after reports of two extramarital affairs, one of which has raised US national security concerns. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. Shares fell 8.61 percent after hours Wednesday, opened down as much as 14 percent Thursday and were down roughly 7 to 10 percent across morning snapshots, putting Nvidia below a $4 trillion market value. Rotation was immediate: Broadcom up as much as 6 percent and AMD up about 2 to 3 percent, while CoreWeave fell about 11 percent, giving back much of Wednesday's 24.7 percent jump, and Micron, Intel and TSMC declined. The chips have not changed, but the market is now pricing key-person and governance risk at the top of the stack, and custom silicon (Broadcom) and second-source GPUs (AMD) are the immediate beneficiaries.

#Signal or noise?

Signal: the market just showed that a single governance event at Nvidia can erase an entire AI rally overnight, which is why hyperscalers have been diversifying into custom silicon, and a 10-year above 5 percent makes that capex more expensive to finance.

#Watch tomorrow

Cisco and Applied Materials report after the close today; Applied's equipment outlook is the first read on whether chip capex plans hold through Nvidia's leadership transition.
