Executive Pulse543 words

#Macro signal

AI is now a macroeconomic variable, not a tech story. Morgan Stanley Research estimates nearly $3 trillion of AI-related infrastructure investment will flow through the global economy by 2028, with more than 80% of that spending still ahead. Goldman Sachs raised 2026 AI-related spending forecasts to $800B (up from $650B annualized in Q1). Global M&A is on track for $4T in 2026, strongest year since 2021, with megadeals over $5B now nearly half of total value. Strategic read: At this scale, AI spend is a GDP input, a capex cycle, and a geopolitical variable simultaneously. Leaders making budget decisions should model AI investment the way they model energy infrastructure, long cycle, high switching cost, strategic rather than tactical.

#Tech industry

  • SpaceX IPO priced at $1.75T valuation, the largest IPO in history, raising up to $75B, trading on Nasdaq as SPCX. A non-tech industrial company becoming the highest-valued IPO ever signals that the market is now treating space infrastructure and connectivity as foundational tech, not a speculative vertical. Watch what this does to satellite internet expectations for aviation (in-flight connectivity as a commodity).
  • Anthropic hit $965B valuation in Series H (late May 2026), surpassing OpenAI in private AI valuation for the first time. Anthropic, Blackstone, and Goldman Sachs launched a new enterprise AI services company to deploy Claude into core business operations. Signal: The frontier model layer is consolidating around two players (Anthropic, OpenAI) while the services/deployment layer is opening up. The model is commoditizing faster than the integration expertise.

#AI at the leadership level

The C-suite is the bottleneck, not the workforce. Fortune's June 2026 analysis is direct: AI is turning workers into superhumans, but most leadership teams haven't redesigned the structures around them. Entry-level hiring has dropped ~80% per quarter since 2023 at AI-adopting orgs, the entry-level experience that traditionally trained the next generation of leaders is being systematically eliminated. The WEF flags this as a 5-year leadership crisis: Gen Z is being shaped by AI-augmented work from day one, but their leadership pipelines are broken. Practical implication: The org design question is no longer "how do we use AI", it's "how do we develop judgment in a generation that never had to do the rote work that built ours."

#Leadership & org design

91% of CHROs rank AI and digitization of the workplace as their top concern (Gloat Q2 2026 AI Workforce Trends). More than half of talent leaders plan to add autonomous AI agents to their teams this year. The HBS framing for what leaders need now: "change fitness", not a strategy for one AI transition, but the organizational muscle to absorb continuous transitions. The skills that matter most are the ones AI cannot replicate: contextual judgment, trust-based communication, ethical reasoning under uncertainty, and creative synthesis. These are not trainable in a workshop, they develop through repeated exposure to consequential decisions.

#Question worth asking your team this week

The entry-level pipeline is being hollowed out by AI. Five years from now, where are your future senior leaders getting the judgment-building experiences that the rote work used to provide?

--- *Sources: Morgan Stanley midyear outlook, Goldman AI capex, Fortune C-suite bottleneck, WEF AI leadership crisis, Gloat AI Workforce Q2, PWC M&A mid-year*

#Try this now

  • Context Engineering (Anthropic guide): Stop thinking about prompts as single questions and start thinking about what the model *sees*. Anthropic published a practical engineering guide covering how to structure context windows for agents: put your most critical instructions at the top and bottom (models lose attention in the middle), use XML tags to delineate sections (<background>, <instructions>, <examples>), and start minimal then add based on failure modes. For digital leaders running AI workflows or reviewing vendor AI outputs, this framework will immediately sharpen why your outputs are inconsistent, and what to change. Worth 30 minutes: Anthropic context engineering guide.
  • Propensity-gated campaigns: Most marketing teams still blast by demographic cohort. The specific tactic worth stealing from Crocs India's 13X ROI case: use propensity models to score purchase intent *before* targeting, then feed only high-intent segments into your messaging agent. The lever isn't better creative, it's routing. If your ESP or CDP supports propensity scoring (Braze, Salesforce, Customer.io), you can wire this in without a custom build. This week's action: pull your last campaign's conversion data by segment and identify which cohort you should have excluded entirely.
  • Canva Magic Studio bulk generation: Canva's 2026 update to Magic Studio supports bulk social asset generation, upload a spreadsheet of variants, export a batch of formatted assets. Marketing teams report ~3 hours saved per week on social production. Useful for A/B testing creative at volume without design bottlenecks. Works for LinkedIn carousels, email headers, and ad creatives simultaneously.

#Case study worth stealing

Crocs India, multi-agent precision marketing at scale: Crocs India hit a growth plateau with standard cohort-based campaigns. They deployed Netcore's multi-agent setup: a Segment Agent to identify high-intent buyers using propensity models, and a Content Agent to personalize messaging per segment. Result: 13X ROI, 3X WhatsApp campaign performance versus traditional segments, and ₹50L+ in monthly incremental revenue. The portable insight for any digital org: the two-agent structure (one for *who to reach*, one for *what to say*) maps cleanly onto any stack that separates audience tools from content tools. You don't need a custom build, you need to stop treating these as a single campaign step and start treating them as sequential agent decisions.

#Framework or mental model

Positionless Marketing: McKinsey's reframe for AI-era marketing teams, now operationalized by Optimove's AI Hub launch. The core idea: the goal isn't to remove roles, it's to remove the *handoffs* between roles. When a data analyst has to pass a segment to a strategist who passes a brief to a copywriter who passes assets to a campaign manager, the latency and translation loss kills speed. Positionless Marketing means AI handles the transitions, native AI inside the platform, MCPs extending into external tools, custom apps for org-specific logic. McKinsey cites 88% campaign efficiency improvement in early adopters. Where digital leaders should apply it: map one campaign workflow end-to-end, identify every handoff point, and ask which ones exist because of tooling friction vs. actual judgment requirements. The friction ones are AI candidates today.

#Rabbit hole

McKinsey's agentic marketing workflows report breaks down the full five-step framework for deploying marketing agents, from taxonomy mapping to redefining human oversight roles. The estimate that agentic AI will power two-thirds of current marketing activities by 2027 sounds big, but the taxonomy exercise alone (mapping which tasks are data-retrieval vs. judgment vs. execution) is a useful diagnostic for any team. Reinventing marketing workflows with agentic AI.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • CartographAI, vendor mapping at scale: A new tool letting marketers explore and compare 1,000+ MarTech and AdTech vendors against their specific stack needs. Already being piloted by Reckitt, LinkedIn, and Mars. Signal: The MarTech complexity problem is large enough that a dedicated navigation layer is now a product category. When 1,000 vendors is table stakes, the selection problem becomes as important as the solution problem. For any org rebuilding a stack, this is a useful diagnostic before committing to architecture.
  • Zeta + Palantir (live update): The Zeta/Palantir Foundry rearchitecture is now operationally active, connecting customer and operational data with real-time AI decision-making across enterprise marketing. Signal: This is the clearest live example of governance-as-moat in MarTech. A versioned, auditable data layer is what earns autonomous AI the right to act inside a regulated enterprise. Palantir's ontology layer isn't just governance, it's the audit trail that satisfies legal, compliance, and the board simultaneously.
  • "Custom AdTech Renaissance" emerging: PWC's mid-year M&A outlook and multiple agency holding co signals point to more brands choosing to build and control essential stack layers, data, decisioning, delivery, and measurement, rather than rent them. Signal: The pendulum is swinging from "buy everything" back toward "own the strategic layers." This mirrors the composable architecture thesis exactly. Brands that built on walled gardens and SaaS-only stacks are now paying platform tax; the ones who own their data layer have optionality.

#Category watch

Agentic marketing platforms: The "positionless marketing" concept (McKinsey, operationalized by Optimove) is moving from whitepaper to product. The idea: remove the handoffs between roles, not the roles themselves. When the latency and translation loss of analyst → strategist → copywriter → campaign manager is eliminated by native AI, campaign efficiency compounds. McKinsey cites 88% efficiency improvement in early adopters. The buyer question shifting from "which marketing cloud" to "which platform removes the most handoffs in my specific workflow."

#Stack intelligence

The "build vs. buy" decision is polarizing fast. Mid-market orgs are buying thick platforms (Salesforce, Adobe, HubSpot) for speed. Enterprise orgs with mature data teams are building thin activation layers on top of owned data foundations (warehouse + identity graph + model). The gap between these two strategies is widening, and switching costs are going up in both directions. The window to make the right call for a large org is now, before agentic tooling gets embedded into platform contracts.

#Foundations concept of the day

Programmatic advertising basics, DSP, SSP, and the auction: Programmatic advertising is the automated buying and selling of digital ad impressions in real time. When a user loads a webpage, an auction happens in milliseconds: a DSP (Demand-Side Platform, e.g. The Trade Desk, DV360) bids on behalf of the advertiser; an SSP (Supply-Side Platform, e.g. Magnite, PubMatic) represents the publisher and maximizes their yield. The winning bid's ad is served before the page finishes loading. Why it matters at scale: programmatic now accounts for ~90% of all digital display ad spend. The data layer that informs the DSP bid, who this user is, their propensity, their value, is where first-party data and identity graphs plug in. An airline's loyalty data, when properly structured and activated through a DSP, means bidding higher for a known high-value traveler on a competitor's site without ever sharing that data externally. The loyalty data is the bid signal.
