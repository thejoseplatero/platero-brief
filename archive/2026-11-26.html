#Macro signal

A central bank raised rates and rebuilt its growth forecast on AI investment, in the same statement. The Bank of Korea lifted its policy rate a quarter point to 3.25 percent on Thursday, in a 5 to 2 vote according to several outlets, and raised its growth forecast for 2027 to 4.1 percent from 2.9 percent and for 2026 to 3.9 percent from 3.3 percent. The reason is chips. South Korean exports rose 94 percent year on year in October to $115.3 billion, semiconductors were nearly half of them, and SK Hynix and Samsung hold about 80 percent of the market for the high-bandwidth memory used in AI servers, per Counterpoint. The bank said the outlook depends on "the AI investment outlook" and on "developments in the Middle East," and that inflation will "stay elevated for some time." The decision came hours after the Federal Reserve's Beige Book reported that US consumer spending "was little changed, as households remained price sensitive and were increasingly selective in discretionary purchases," and that firms were "hiring primarily to replace for attrition." A Fed hike on 9 December is almost fully priced. The strategic reading: AI capital spending has become large enough to set national growth forecasts and interest rates, while the consumer who funds everything downstream is flat and choosing carefully. A 2027 plan is now exposed to AI investment twice, once through its own technology costs and once through the borrowing costs and currencies that investment is moving. Leaders should price next year's demand from the Beige Book consumer and next year's cost of capital from the central banks, and should not assume the two will move together. (Al Jazeera, Seoul Economic Daily, Federal Reserve)

#Tech industry

  • Nvidia's governance questions reached its credit rating. S&P Global placed Nvidia's AA rating on CreditWatch Negative, citing management and governance risk, according to the CappNotes closing recap for Wednesday, an AI-assisted summary worth confirming against S&P's own notice. The shares fell 3.9 percent on the day after being down more than 9 percent, and are down about 27 percent in November. The AP noted that questions about governance and the influence of a US president who owns shares in the company "have added to concerns." Boards usually treat succession and oversight as reputational matters. When a rating agency cites them, they become a cost of borrowing, for the company and for the customers and suppliers whose financing leans on its paper. (CappNotes, BNN Bloomberg)
  • The market sold AI in the morning and bought everything else by the close. On Wednesday the S&P 500 fell as much as 3.2 percent and the Nasdaq 4.6 percent before finishing down 0.25 and 0.62 percent. About three quarters of S&P 500 stocks rose, the equal-weight index gained 0.83 percent, and mid-cap stocks gained 1.23 percent. Venu Krishna of Barclays told CNBC, "We just feel that this is panic right now." The breadth is the part that matters for executives outside technology: investors are separating the AI trade from the wider economy, so a company's valuation and financing terms depend less on an AI narrative than they did in October. (CappNotes, CNBC)

#AI at the leadership level

Approval is being delegated to software, with the policy written as a file. Anthropic launched Claude Ship this week, as reported by TechCrunch and summarized in Sam on AI's 24 November brief. It bundles the existing Code Review agent with two in beta. PR Steward fixes failing checks, merge conflicts and review feedback, and comments after each push on what changed and how to roll it back. Claude Approvals applies a plain-language approval policy stored as a file in the code repository, and can approve routine changes on its own. Anthropic recommends starting in observe-only mode on one repository with monthly spending limits. It is rolling out to Team and Enterprise plans for GitHub repositories. The governance point reaches beyond engineering. An approval policy that lives in a file is explicit, versioned and auditable in a way that a manager's habit is not, and it makes someone decide in writing which changes count as routine. Every function that wants agents to act will need the same artifact. The useful first step is to ask who in the organization would own that file, and who could change it. (Sam on AI, TechCrunch)

#Leadership & org design

Employers say they are investing in AI skills, and most employees have not noticed. Payscale's AI Workforce Impact report surveyed 500 HR and compensation leaders and 1,000 full-time employees at organizations with more than 100 people, fielded in August 2026. Seventy-four percent of employers plan AI upskilling in the next 12 months, and 29 percent of employees perceive it. Fifty-six percent of employees expect a pay increase for AI skills, and 16 percent say they have received one. Sixty-one percent of employers are rewriting job descriptions, and 48 percent say their pay benchmarks no longer reflect AI skills. Employees are twice as likely as employers to expect layoffs, 20 percent against 10 percent. The findings are self-reported and come from a vendor that sells compensation data. The pattern matches the Fed's report of firms hiring only to replace attrition: organizations are changing the work faster than they are changing the deal. The practical move is to publish, for each role family, which AI skills change pay or progression and by when, before the job descriptions are rewritten. (Payscale, Federal Reserve)

#Question worth asking your team this week

If an agent were allowed to approve routine decisions in our function tomorrow, could we write down in one page what counts as routine, and who would sign that page?

#Try this now

  • Check which local campaigns still locate people by IP address: A 51Degrees study published on 26 November looked at all 2.4 billion broadband IP addresses and found that the number giving a city-level location fell 30 percent in a year, to 203 million, or 8.4 percent of the total. In the EEA and UK, 56 percent of broadband addresses now point to an area covering 90 percent or more of a country. The firm sells IP intelligence, so treat the figures as directional. The check takes 20 minutes: list every campaign on desktop, connected TV or streaming audio that targets a city, a postal code or a store radius, and ask each platform or vendor which signal sets the location and what confidence radius comes with it. Where the answer is IP alone, move the targeting up to region or country, or add a second signal such as device location or a logged-in address. Apply the same question to store-visit measurement and to any personalization that swaps content by city. (ExchangeWire, 51Degrees)
  • Add six questions about AI connectors to every vendor review: MCP, the Model Context Protocol, is the standard that lets an assistant such as Claude or ChatGPT operate an outside tool. This week's MarTech release roundup lists new MCP servers from Iterable, Basis, Cision, Versium, Janney AI and Webflow, and one from Xnurta that can write changes to live Amazon ad campaigns. Ryan Phelan's 25 November column gives the questions to ask: Do you have an MCP server? What can it do? Is it headless? What experience do I get when I plug in my own assistant? How easily can I import and access data? What controls do you use? Send them to the three vendors holding the most customer data this week. For any connector that can write, add one more: who can switch it on, and where is each action logged. (MarTech, MarTech)
  • Label every line of an AI persona as Known, Inferred or Simulated: Escalent's new Persona Intelligence product lets teams question interactive personas built on its research, and attaches a Persona Confidence Scorecard that marks each output as Known, Inferred or Simulated. The labels work without the product. Take the persona or synthetic-research summary a team is currently using, and tag each claim: Known if it traces to a study, interview or behavioral data, Inferred if it was reasoned from known facts, Simulated if a model produced it. Decisions about pricing, positioning or roadmap should rest on Known claims, and a Simulated claim that matters should become the next research question. (MarTech)

#Case study worth stealing

Anthropic, using live production traffic to check a rewrite done by agents: Anthropic published an engineering account on 25 November of moving its Messages API to Rust. The team did not rely on reading the new code to decide whether it was right. It ran the new system in the shadow of the old one on live traffic, compared each request-handling step, recorded the shape of each difference in place of the text to protect privacy, and grouped the differences by fingerprint so that thousands of mismatches collapsed into a short list of causes. Agents worked in nested loops: fast checks in a sandbox, then shadowed production traffic, then rules written from human corrections. Reported results were about 70 percent less CPU per request and about 20 percent lower median time to first token. The post's summary line is "Verification is the new bottleneck." Any team replatforming a booking flow, a pricing engine, a CMS or a customer data pipeline can copy the method: run old and new side by side on real inputs, compare outputs automatically, and sort the differences into classes before anyone reviews a single case. (Anthropic)

#Framework or mental model

Who is advertising to whom: Shirley Marschall's 25 November ExchangeWire column pulls together two grids that belong on one page. LUMA's State of Digital describes four kinds of advertising that will coexist: human to human, human to bot, bot to human and bot to bot. The IAB's AI-era attribution blueprint sorts purchases into human-owned, AI-assisted and AI-executed. Read together they say that every impression and every sale now needs two labels, one for who saw the message and one for who made the purchase. Her closing line: "In advertising, it's getting harder to rule a human in." Digital leaders can apply it to the measurement plan for 2027 by asking, for each channel, which of the four audience types it reaches and whether current reporting can tell an AI-assisted sale from a human one. (ExchangeWire)

#Rabbit hole (optional)

Three LLM inflection points in twelve months: Simon Willison published the write-up of a keynote on 24 November. His three points are that coding agents became reliable around November 2025, that the newest class of models can solve a problem when given a goal and the right tools, and that 3D modelling and games improved sharply. The working advice is to state a goal, supply tools, let the model iterate, and prompt on quality as well as correctness. A companion post the same day quotes historian Mark Humphries, who says a model identified the creator of an 1808 sketchbook in 22 minutes by proposing a hypothesis and naming the archive source that would confirm it. That pattern, asking for the hypothesis and the source that would test it and then checking the source by hand, transfers directly to market and customer research. (Simon Willison, Simon Willison)

#Vendor & platform moves

  • ChatGPT Ads picked up its integration layer in one week of release notes: MarTech's 26 November roundup of vendor releases lists three separate ChatGPT Ads connections. Dreamdata, a B2B attribution tool, now identifies the companies that click ChatGPT ads, pulls daily cost and impression data, and syncs pipeline-stage conversions back to OpenAI Ads for bidding. InMobi launched Accelerate, a self-serve product with seven agents that plan, build, launch and optimize campaigns across Google, Meta, Microsoft, TikTok and ChatGPT Ads, using its own pixel to deduplicate conversions. Pinewood.AI added ChatGPT Ads as a channel in its automotive marketing tool. None of the entries gives pricing or availability. Signal: a new ad channel becomes a line in the media plan when third parties build conversion feedback and cross-channel buying around it. ChatGPT Ads reached that stage in under a year, and the conversion data now flowing back to OpenAI is the same signal advertisers already hand to Google and Meta. (MarTech)
  • Google connected Wallet to AI Mode: Google began rolling out an opt-in that links Google Wallet to Personal Intelligence in AI Mode, with Drive and the Gemini app to follow in the coming weeks. Gmail, Calendar and Photos could already be connected. Robby Stein, VP of Product for Google Search, wrote that AI Mode "can only read information from your accounts; it can't move, manage, or invest your money." TechCrunch reports a Personalization icon will show which context an answer used. Signal: the search assistant is assembling a consented, read-only view of a person's inbox, calendar, files and wallet. Wallet is where many consumers keep loyalty cards, tickets and boarding passes, so a brand's own program data can now inform answers on a surface the brand does not control. Google has not said which Wallet items AI Mode reads. (Google, Sam on AI)
  • Meta's Muse appeared as a commerce and monitoring target in three releases: In the same MarTech roundup, Destination XL Group made its catalog available through Meta's Muse using commercetools, Brandi AI extended its brand monitoring to Muse along with Bing AI Mode and Bing AI Overviews, and Constructor's new Commerce AEO product tracks shoppers arriving from ChatGPT, Claude, Muse and Instinct through to an on-site purchase. Signal: vendors now treat the list of assistants a retailer must feed and monitor as four or five names long. Catalog syndication and answer monitoring are being built per assistant, which repeats the cost structure of managing separate retail media networks. (MarTech)

#Category watch

AI visibility tools: The category started as monitoring: ask the assistants a set of questions and count how often a brand appears. This week's releases move it in two directions. Constructor's Commerce AEO ties assistant referrals to on-site purchases and uses purchase data to rewrite product attributes, descriptions and buying guides. Scrunch, now part of Sitecore, added Citation Optimization, which finds the third-party publishers and reviewers that assistants cite and drafts outreach to them, and Imposter Detection, which flags sites impersonating a brand in AI answers and starts takedowns. Shirley Marschall's 25 November ExchangeWire column collects the sizing: the IAB counts more than 20 vendors selling AI visibility tools while 16 percent of brands track it systematically, Forrester's 2026 guidance is to move at least 15 percent of content or digital spend into AI search visibility, and Adobe reported that shoppers referred from AI converted 60 percent better in July. For someone building a martech org, the category is splitting into measurement that connects to revenue, earned-media work on the sources assistants cite, and brand protection. Those sit in three different teams today. (MarTech, ExchangeWire)

#Stack intelligence

An MCP server is now a standard line in a release note, and write access is arriving with it. MCP, the Model Context Protocol, is the open standard that lets an AI assistant call an outside tool. In the 26 November MarTech roundup, Iterable shipped a hosted MCP server for Claude, ChatGPT and Cursor, Basis added one to its Compass layer so outside agents can work with its buying platform, Cision published one for Brandwatch Consumer Research, Versium and Janney AI each shipped one over their identity and creator data, Webflow added a connector for Microsoft Copilot, and ActiveCampaign opened its assistant to any MCP server. Xnurta's entry is the one to read twice: its server supports both read and write for Amazon Sponsored Products, Brands and Display, which means an outside assistant can change live campaigns. Ryan Phelan argued on MarTech the day before that MCP now belongs in vendor selection, and listed the questions to put in an RFP: whether the vendor has an MCP server, what it can do, whether it is headless, what the experience is when a company plugs in its own assistant, how easily data moves in and out, and what controls apply. He also reports Claude declining a task because a website's terms did not allow it, and says vendors should update their terms for AI use. The practical consequence for a stack owner is that permissions written for human users in each tool do not cover an assistant acting through a connector. Each write-capable connector needs an owner, a scope and a log before it is switched on. (MarTech, MarTech)

#Foundations concept of the day

IP geolocation: Every device on the internet connects through an IP address, a number assigned by the internet provider. IP geolocation is the practice of estimating where a user is from that number, using databases that map address ranges to places. It needs no consent prompt, no app and no cookie, which is why it sits underneath a large share of advertising: city and postal-code targeting on desktop and connected TV, regional frequency caps, store-visit measurement, fraud checks and the regional licensing rules of streaming services. The estimate is only as good as the link between one address and one place. When a provider shares a single address among many households, a common way to stretch a limited supply of addresses, the address stops pointing to a city and starts pointing to a region or a country. A study published on 26 November by 51Degrees, which sells IP intelligence, examined all 2.4 billion broadband IP addresses and found that those giving a city-level result (an area of 10,000 square kilometres or less) fell 30 percent in a year, from 290 million to 203 million, or 8.4 percent of the total. In Europe the typical area an address points to more than doubled to 194,000 square kilometres, and 56 percent of broadband addresses in the EEA and UK now point to an area covering 90 percent or more of a country. The company attributes the change to providers increasing IP sharing. Its CEO, James Rosewell, said: "On its own, you can no longer rely on IP to target a localised ad campaign with any real hope of success." It matters at scale because location is often the one signal left when identity signals are unavailable. A grocery chain running a connected TV campaign for a single metro store opening may be paying for households several hundred kilometres away and then counting none of them as store visits. The source has a commercial interest in the finding, so the direction is worth more than the exact figures. (ExchangeWire, 51Degrees)

#Watch list

  • Verification vendors still file declared AI agents as invalid traffic: Marschall notes that IAS and DoubleVerify classify declared AI agents as general invalid traffic while brands are paying to be seen by those agents. HUMAN Security reported agent and agentic-browser traffic up 7,851 percent in 2025, with media sites taking 19 percent of it, and Imperva put automated traffic at 53 percent of the web. A buyer who blocks all non-human traffic and also funds AI visibility is paying on both sides of the same visit. Watch for the first verification product that reports agent traffic as its own class. (ExchangeWire)
  • Retargeting from outdoor screens: Vistar Media described a workflow in which play logs from programmatic digital out-of-home screens go to its partner Spotzi, which finds mobile devices within a set radius when the ad played and passes them to the advertiser's DSP as an audience. About 40 percent of devices typically match, the data takes about two weeks to arrive, and a third UK campaign is running with no uplift figures published yet. It gives outdoor a place in a sequenced plan, and it depends on mobile location data whose consent basis buyers should ask about. (ExchangeWire)

#Macro pulse

Canada: The TSX closed Wednesday up 0.64 percent at 34,179.99, a gain of about 218 points, after an early drop of roughly 700. Materials rose about 3 percent and energy about 1 percent. The loonie traded near 70.2 US cents on Wednesday morning per The Canadian Press, and USD/CAD was quoted at 1.432 before Thursday's open, so the two-day move is small and under the 0.5 percent threshold. Bank of Canada Governor Tiff Macklem's Toronto remarks were read as hawkish: headline inflation could finish the year below the Bank's October forecast as refinery margins ease, and the delayed pass-through of earlier energy price spikes remains a threat. Toronto trades today while New York is shut, so expect thin volume. The Bank's rate decision is 9 December. (Ad Hoc News, Investing.com)

USA: Wednesday's session reversed almost completely. The S&P 500 fell as much as 3.2 percent and closed down 0.25 percent at 7,182.78. The Nasdaq Composite fell as much as 4.6 percent and closed down 0.62 percent at 23,585.80. The Dow erased a loss of more than 1,100 points to close up 0.33 percent at 50,182.95, and the Russell 2000 gained 0.71 percent. Breadth was strong, with 370 S&P 500 stocks up and 128 down. Materials led at plus 2.07 percent and technology was the weakest sector at minus 0.83 percent. The VIX closed at 28.29 after topping 30. The 10-year Treasury yield fell about 6 basis points to roughly 4.85 percent. The Fed's Beige Book said eight of twelve districts grew slightly to moderately and four were flat, consumer spending "was little changed," and "tourism and travel activity was solid while entertainment and hospitality spending was mixed overall." A quarter-point Fed hike on 9 December is priced at about 98 percent. US markets are closed today for Thanksgiving and close at 1 p.m. ET on Friday. (ABC News, CappNotes, Federal Reserve)

Asia: Thursday was a partial rebound led by chip stocks. The Nikkei 225 rose 0.84 percent to about 57,492 per Investing.com. The Kospi closed up about 0.3 percent near 4,711 after Wednesday's 6.2 percent fall, on the day the Bank of Korea raised its policy rate a quarter point to 3.25 percent and lifted its 2027 growth forecast to 4.1 percent from 2.9 percent. Taiwan's Taiex gained 2.5 percent. The Hang Seng was reported up roughly 1.5 to 1.9 percent and the Shanghai Composite about flat. Published closing figures differ between reports for every index except the Nikkei, so treat the levels as approximate. (Investing.com, Al Jazeera, IC Markets)

Oil: WTI settled Wednesday up 1.99 percent at $83.68, and Brent rose 3.51 percent to $94.61 after a Houthi attack on Ras Tanura, the Saudi export terminal. WTI was quoted near $84.50 early Thursday. That is two straight sessions of gains of about 2 percent in WTI and a larger move in Brent, which is the benchmark most jet fuel outside North America prices against. Gold fell 0.71 percent to $3,534.70 and touched its lowest level since August 2025. (CappNotes, Investing.com)

The recap is AI-assisted and the detail should be confirmed against S&P's notice. Nvidia closed down 3.91 percent after falling more than 9 percent intraday and is down about 27 percent in November. Anthropic rose 1.07 percent to $82.79, still just under its $83 IPO price and about 28 percent below its first-day peak of $115 per the AP. #AI infrastructure watch

  • Chips: Micron fell 4.53 percent and Oracle 5.13 percent. Broadcom recovered from a drop of more than 6 percent to close down 0.1 percent, and Microsoft from more than 3 percent to minus 1.2 percent. CNBC reported AMD, Intel, Marvell, CoreWeave and Nebius lower without figures. Dell rose 0.60 percent, holding its post-earnings level. (CappNotes, CNBC)
  • Memory supply, seen from the exporter: South Korea's October exports rose 94 percent year on year to $115.3 billion, with semiconductors nearly half. SK Hynix and Samsung hold about 80 percent of the high-bandwidth memory market per Counterpoint. This is the other side of HPE's warning on Tuesday that memory stays constrained into 2028: the shortage is large enough to raise a country's growth forecast by more than a point. (Al Jazeera)

Peter DeSantis, who leads custom silicon at Amazon: "Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip." A hyperscaler selling its own accelerators is a direct second source to Nvidia at the moment Nvidia's governance is being marked down. (Sam on AI, TechCrunch)
  • Energy: US natural gas fell 1.62 percent to $3.269 per million BTU on warmer forecasts, giving back part of Tuesday's 15 percent jump. (CappNotes)

#Signal or noise?

The intraday plunge was noise and the rotation under it is signal: three quarters of the S&P 500 rose on a day technology fell, which says investors are pricing AI hardware as its own risk and no longer as a proxy for the whole market.
