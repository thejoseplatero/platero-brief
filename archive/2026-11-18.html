#Macro signal

A cluster of infrastructure failures is making resilience a board topic again. In one week: a nationwide FAA ground stop after an air traffic control upgrade overloaded old equipment, a United Airlines system outage of more than 90 minutes that delayed departures on 16 November, a Chase on-premises hardware bug that disrupted online banking for its customers and anyone transacting with them, a global Outlook and Teams outage on 12 November that left more than 80 percent of Microsoft's gateway servers unresponsive, and brief failures at Marriott's sign-in. Chase and United say there is no evidence of a cyberattack, and the Secretary of State said officials found "zero evidence" linking the incidents. Satya Nadella opened Ignite by apologizing: "I know we have work to do to provide that resilience that you can count on." The strategic read is that the failures are coming from routine change, upgrades, failovers and latent bugs, not attackers. For tech and product leaders, the questions boards will ask next are about change management and concentration: how many customer journeys stop when one vendor or one deploy fails. (Yahoo News, Fox News, UC Today)

#Tech industry

  • Nvidia beat and raised in its first report without Jensen Huang: Revenue of $115.8 billion rose 103 percent, against guidance of $108 billion. Data center was $107.6 billion, and the AI clouds, industrial and enterprise segment grew 195 percent, faster than hyperscale at 71 percent. Fourth-quarter guidance is $130 billion, plus or minus 3 percent, assuming no China data center revenue, and fiscal 2028 growth guidance rose to about 75 percent. The chair said the board "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," and Heidrick & Struggles is running the search. Interim CEO Colette Kress said guidance "considers data center readiness" and customers' access to capital. Shares rose 3 to 5 percent after hours. Strategic implication: demand is broadening from hyperscalers to enterprises and neoclouds, but Nvidia now carries $118 billion of guarantees, including $105 billion of credit support for one data center campus. The constraint on AI capacity is shifting from chips to power, buildings and financing. (mlq.ai)
  • Software sold off on the seat-pricing question as Anthropic prices its IPO: Salesforce fell more than 9 percent, HubSpot and ServiceNow about 7 percent and Workday about 5 percent on 17 November with no company news, as investors questioned per-user licensing in a world of agents and raised cash for Anthropic's offering, priced today at a $70 to $78 range implying about $1.4 trillion, with trading expected Thursday. Strategic implication: the vendors most enterprises run on are under pressure to change how they charge. Leaders with large renewals in 2027 should prepare to negotiate on outcomes and consumption, and model what happens to their own cost base if agents replace seats. (mlq.ai, CNBC)

#AI at the leadership level

Agents are getting org chart status, with named owners and budgets. At Ignite, Microsoft put Agentic Users into Teams in public preview: agents join a team under their own identity, can be @mentioned like a colleague, and each has an accountable human owner and admin controls. Agent 365 added spending limits and alerts and a Business Analytics view tying AI spend to outcomes, which Nadella called "your ERP system for AI usage." Defender now scans local AI coding tools for hidden malicious instructions. The governance shift: when the dominant workplace vendor treats an agent as a governed identity with an owner and a budget, boards will expect the same of every agent the company runs, whatever the platform. The minimum standard to set now is an inventory of agents, each with an owner, a spend cap and an action log. (UC Today, Fast Company Middle East)

#Leadership & org design

Baidu's CEO says AI value comes from redrawing jobs, not adding tools. Speaking at the APEC CEO Summit in Shenzhen on 17 November, Robin Li said companies must overhaul their organizational structure to benefit from AI, described "radical" attempts to break down traditional job boundaries at Baidu, and said applications are spreading more slowly than expected despite fierce competition. AI-related revenue has exceeded half of Baidu's total for three straight quarters. Bank of America's 2026 business owner survey points the same way from the other end: 84 percent of owners use AI, 60 percent expect it to move employees into new roles, and 33 percent expect headcount cuts. The practical point: adoption stalls when AI is layered onto job descriptions written for the old workflow. Leaders seeing slow uptake should look at role boundaries and handoffs before blaming the tools. (Caixin Global, Agile Brand Guide)

#Question worth asking your team this week

If our single largest technology vendor, or our next routine deploy, took down a core customer journey for 90 minutes tomorrow, which journeys would stop, who would know first, and what would customers see?

#Try this now

  • Test whether your site actually honors Global Privacy Control, end to end: PPC Land reports California fined Hertz $1.55 million for ignoring GPC browser signals, with data still reaching 13 ad tech companies. The same month CalPrivacy settled with FedEx for $2.975 million on the same theme, after Disney ($2.75 million) and Ford. The pattern is consistent: regulators are not reading the privacy policy, they are turning on GPC in a browser and watching the network tab. Twenty minutes: open the booking or checkout flow in a browser with GPC enabled, open developer tools, and list every third-party tag that still fires and every pixel that still receives an identifier. Hand the list to whoever owns the tag manager and consent platform. Travel and rental brands are clearly in scope. (PPC Land)
  • Give AI assistants scoped access to your systems through an MCP server that inherits existing permissions: Two launches on 17 November show the pattern worth copying. Salesforce in Copilot entered public preview with more than 38 prebuilt sales skills (Pipeline Review, Deal Review, Renewal Radar), connecting through authenticated Salesforce MCP access, and users must confirm CRM write-back actions. Impartner's MCP server connects Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot and Gemini to partner pipeline data, where access follows each user's existing permissions, sensitive fields are withheld, and custom objects are discovered automatically. If your team is building an internal agent on top of a loyalty, CRM or campaign system, use these as the spec: per-user authentication, inherited permissions, withheld sensitive fields, and a human confirm step on any write. (Agile Brand Guide)
  • Screen AI-agent orders as their own category before peak season: Spreedly and Riskified now let merchants put Riskified's fraud decisioning in front of payment processing, identify orders placed by AI shopping agents, and set separate approve, decline or flag policies for them. Spreedly's own survey of 2,000 US adults found 52 percent cite fear of fraud as the top reason they will not let AI shop for them, and 31 percent do not know who is responsible when an AI purchase goes wrong. Whatever your payment stack, the move is the same: add an "agent-initiated" flag to orders and bookings now, so you can report on them, set policy on them, and answer the liability question before a customer asks it. (Agile Brand Guide)

#Case study worth stealing

Similarweb, selling the data instead of the dashboard: Similarweb booked its first GAAP profit in Q3, $3.5 million on revenue up 15.6 percent to $83.0 million. AI-related revenue rose to nearly 20 percent of the total from 11 percent at the end of 2025, and drove roughly two-thirds of new ARR. The tactic: customers buy datasets through APIs and MCP rather than seats in a web interface, the CFO says deals entering through those interfaces are larger from day one, and expansion happens one dataset at a time. Net retention climbed from 98 to 104 percent. Any team sitting on proprietary data, from route demand to loyalty behavior to content performance, can adapt the question: what would this look like as a feed an agent consumes, priced per dataset, rather than a dashboard a human logs into? (PPC Land)

#Framework or mental model

Every agent is a named user with an accountable owner: Microsoft's Ignite launch of Agentic Users in Teams makes agents join a team under their own identity, with a named human owner and admin controls, and its Autopilot agent's actions are tied to its own agent ID. Agent 365 adds cost limits and a "Business Analytics" view that ties agent spend to outcomes. The model is simple: an agent without its own identity cannot be audited, and an agent without a named owner cannot be held to account. Digital leaders can apply it today, whatever the platform: no agent goes live in marketing, service or operations without a service identity, a named owner, a spend cap and a log of what it did. (UC Today)

#Rabbit hole

A field experiment on what Google's AI Mode does to clicks: A preprint from UPenn and Northeastern switched 1,100 US Google users fully to AI Mode. They clicked out to websites 18.8 percentage points less often, visited news sites, Reddit and Wikipedia less, rated Google lower on trust and usefulness, and were 11.2 points more likely to use another search engine. Ad clicks fell 42.7 points. It is not peer reviewed and the sample skews young and educated, but it is one of the first randomized looks at the question every traffic forecast now depends on. Worth an hour for anyone who owns organic or paid search targets for 2027. (PPC Land)

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Salesforce fell more than 9 percent with no company news, and seat-priced software went with it: Salesforce closed 17 November near $194.50, its worst day since 2024 and down about 26 percent this year. HubSpot fell about 7 percent, ServiceNow about 7 percent, Workday about 5 percent, and the iShares software ETF 4.4 percent. Reports cite fear that AI agents shrink paid seats, doubts about how fast Agentforce converts to revenue and about the $5.7 billion Braze deal, funds raising cash ahead of Anthropic's IPO pricing, and a Bank of America survey showing managers cutting tech at the fastest pace since 2006. Anthropic makes up about 45 percent of Salesforce's $11.3 billion strategic portfolio. Signal: The market is pricing the per-seat model, not the products, as the risk. Expect every major suite to bring outcome or consumption pricing into renewals over the next year, which gives buyers leverage to restructure contracts away from seat counts now. (mlq.ai, Motley Fool)
  • Microsoft and Salesforce put CRM inside Copilot ("Copilotforce"): Announced at Ignite on 17 November: Salesforce in Copilot is in public preview with 38-plus sales skills over authenticated Salesforce MCP access, OneLake to Data 360 federation is generally available, Data 360 to OneLake sharing is in preview, and Sales Cloud in Teams is in pilot. Accenture is the implementation partner. No pricing. Signal: The CRM is becoming a system of record that agents reach from wherever the user already works. The interface layer, and the seat attached to it, is moving to the productivity suite, which is exactly the fear the stock market expressed the same day. (Agile Brand Guide)
  • Bloomreach buys AthenaHQ, a generative engine optimization platform: Terms undisclosed. AthenaHQ, whose customers include Coinbase, finds where brands can appear in AI answers and recommends fixes. Bloomreach already pushes its product discovery into ChatGPT through Loomi Connect, so the deal covers both earning visibility in AI answers and converting it. First combined use cases arrive in early 2027. Signal: After Adobe and Semrush, GEO is being absorbed into engagement suites rather than standing alone. The open question MarTech itself flags is still unanswered: whether anyone can prove an AI citation drove a sale. (MarTech)

#Category watch

Agent-facing commerce infrastructure: A new layer is forming between brands and AI shopping agents. Knotch's Ace for Agents splits site content into modular units tagged by topic, audience, funnel stage and intent, and serves them to verified agents through the CDN without changing the human site. Spreedly and Riskified let merchants identify agent-placed orders and set separate fraud policies for them. Google published guidance on letting its acting agent through the WAF, and Blend found bot protection blocked a shopping agent at 29 of 200 retailers. The category has three jobs: let the right agents in, show them structured content, and decide which of their transactions to trust. For a martech org, that is one roadmap spanning web, security and payments teams that rarely plan together. (Agile Brand Guide, PPC Land)

#Stack intelligence

Data vendors are being bought as feeds, not platforms. Similarweb's AI-related revenue reached nearly 20 percent of the total and two-thirds of new ARR, sold through APIs and MCP to AI developers and enterprises that pipe it into their own models and agents. ZoomInfo's new ChatGPT plugin does the same for B2B contact and intent data, and Impartner exposed partner pipeline data through an MCP server that inherits user permissions. The architecture consequence: third-party data increasingly enters the stack through the agent layer, not through the CDP or a vendor UI. Procurement and data governance need to catch these contracts, because a dataset licensed "for the agent" can sidestep the controls built around the CDP. (PPC Land, Agile Brand Guide)

#Foundations concept of the day

Global Privacy Control (GPC): GPC is a browser setting that sends a signal with every page request saying "do not sell or share my personal data." Under California law and a growing number of other US state laws, that signal counts as a legal opt-out, the same as clicking a "Do Not Sell" link, so a site must stop sending that visitor's data to advertising partners without being asked again. It matters at scale because the signal is automatic: every visitor using a privacy browser or extension opts out on arrival, and regulators can test compliance in minutes by turning it on and watching which tags fire. The failure is almost always in the plumbing, where the consent platform records the opt-out but pixels in the tag manager keep firing. Cross-industry example: PPC Land reports California fined Hertz $1.55 million for ignoring GPC, with data reaching 13 ad tech companies, and FedEx, Disney and Ford have settled similar cases. (PPC Land)

When the platform vendor ships agent cost controls, AI spend is about to move from IT experiment to a line finance reviews monthly. (UC Today)

Retail media build-outs are spreading past the US and UK grocery leaders into regional chains. (Agile Brand Guide)
