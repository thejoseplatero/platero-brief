Executive Pulse1,042 words

#Macro signal

The global bond sell-off is repricing the cost of capital underneath every plan approved this year: The US 10-year Treasury yield reached 4.81%, up from 4.20% in January. The 2-year sits at 4.40%, against 3.50% in early 2026. Japan's 10-year hit 3.02%, its highest level since 1996. Equities followed, with the Nikkei down 2.9% and the Kospi down 4% overnight, the S&P 500 down 0.71% and the Nasdaq down 1.03% on September 1. Brent crude is at $94.90 against roughly $72 before the war began in February. Odds of a Fed hike on September 15 to 16 jumped to about 66% after hawkish Jackson Hole remarks naming July PCE at 3.7% concerning.

The strategic point is not the daily move, it is which direction rates are moving. Every multi-year investment case built in the first half of this year assumed a falling or flat discount rate, because that had been the consensus for eighteen months. Payback periods that cleared at 4.20% do not automatically clear at 4.81%, and the projects most exposed are exactly the ones with the longest horizon and the least certain terminal value, which in most organizations means the AI transformation portfolio. The immediate implication for technology and marketing leaders is that the internal argument is about to change from "is this the right bet" to "does this bet pay back inside a shorter window," and the leaders who have already instrumented time to value will win that argument by having an answer rather than a conviction.

#Tech industry

  • Anthropic signed a $35 billion cloud agreement with Nvidia-backed Lambda, roughly 350 megawatts in Nueces County, Texas, in a structure where Hut 8 builds, Nvidia holds the lease, Lambda operates and Anthropic buys. Strategic implication: the AI supply chain is now financed the way commercial real estate and aircraft are, with the manufacturer standing inside the lease to make the customer's obligation bankable. That is a mature-industry financing pattern arriving in a category most boards still discuss as emerging technology, and it means vendor concentration risk in AI now runs through balance sheets, not just through APIs.
  • Apple holds its iPhone event on September 9, the first under a new chief executive, following a run of senior executive departures. Strategic implication: leadership transitions at platform companies are a supplier risk event for everyone downstream, because platform policy, not platform hardware, is what determines the economics of mobile acquisition, attribution and payments for tens of thousands of businesses. The right question in a partner review is not what the new device does, it is who now decides the rules.

#AI at the leadership level

The EU AI Act's teeth came out on August 2, on schedule and without the delay many organizations were quietly banking on. Article 50 transparency duties and the AI Office's enforcement powers over general-purpose AI providers are live. Interactive systems must disclose they are not human, deepfakes must be labelled, and AI-generated or altered content must carry machine-readable marks. Maximum penalties reach 35 million euros or 7% of worldwide annual turnover.

The governance consequence is more specific than "we need a policy." Disclosure and machine-readable marking are product requirements with implementation cost and design consequences, and they sit on surfaces owned by marketing and customer experience rather than by legal or by a central AI function. An organization whose AI governance lives in a steering committee will discover that the obligation lands on a chatbot script, an ad creative pipeline and a content management system, none of which report to the committee. The pattern to check for is a governance body with an inventory of models and no inventory of the customer-facing surfaces those models write to.

#Leadership & org design

New research on agentic automation in customer experience puts a precise number on a gap most leaders can feel but cannot size. Ninety-eight percent of organizations have deployed AI somewhere in the customer journey. Only 15% combine agentic AI with cross-departmental orchestration to resolve a customer need end to end. Sixty-four percent have specialized agents handling functions such as identity, billing or technical support, and those agents operate in silos. Just 5% say they can clearly quantify AI's impact on business outcomes, and 52% cite trust in AI decisions as a primary barrier to handing over control. Organizations that do orchestrate are roughly four times more likely than the next maturity tier to report major CSAT or NPS gains, and nearly twice as likely to automate higher-value work such as churn prevention and returns.

The org design lesson is that agents inherit the org chart. A billing agent and an identity agent that cannot resolve a case together are not a technology failure, they are an accurate rendering of two departments that never had a shared handoff, now automated and running faster. Deploying agents inside existing functional boundaries makes the boundaries more expensive rather than less. The organizations pulling ahead did not buy better agents, they gave one accountable owner the authority to resolve an outcome across functions, then let the agents follow that line. Corroborating evidence sits alongside it: 79% of organizations report challenges adopting AI, 59% invest at least a million dollars a year, and only 29% see significant returns.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If our discount rate is 60 basis points higher than when we approved this year's plan, which three initiatives no longer clear the bar, and are we the ones deciding that or is finance going to decide it for us in November?

#Try this now

  • Claude Fable 5.1, and the cache read price cut that should change how you build prompts: Shipped September 1 with a one million token context window by default, 128k max output, and always-on adaptive thinking. Input and output pricing did not move, at $10 and $50 per million tokens. Cache reads dropped to $0.25 per million, a 75% cut. The practical consequence is that the cheapest architecture is now the opposite of what most teams built. Instead of assembling a tight bespoke prompt per request, load a large stable corpus once, brand guidelines, the product catalog, past campaign performance, the tone rules, the policy constraints, and re-read it on every call at a quarter of the previous cost. Spend twenty minutes finding the prompt in your stack that gets rebuilt from scratch on every invocation and turn its stable portion into a cached prefix. Two new beta controls are worth the same session: per-message effort settings, so a cheap classification step in a chain does not run at the same reasoning depth as the hard step, and turn-scoped system messages that clear at the next user turn, which is the clean way to inject situational instructions without permanently polluting context.
  • DeepSeek Harness, an open agent runtime where every layer is swappable: Open-sourced in August under MIT, roughly 165,000 GitHub stars inside a week, which is a near-unprecedented adoption curve for a developer tool. The design premise is that there is no privileged core. The model adapter, tool registry, session log, sandbox, storage, scheduler, UI and the agent loop itself are all plugins mounted beside each other and replaceable from configuration. Run it with npx @deepseek-ai/dsh web for a local UI at port 3080. The reason a senior leader should spend twenty minutes here is not to adopt it. It is to see, concretely, what an agent architecture looks like when nothing is locked, then go ask your vendor which of those nine layers you are actually allowed to replace. That question is a faster procurement diligence test than any RFP section on extensibility.
  • Show-level targeting and reporting in CTV, now live in a major DSP: Gracenote's content IDs, metadata and taxonomy became available inside The Trade Desk on September 1, so campaigns can be targeted and reported at the level of the individual program rather than the channel or app. If you buy CTV, the immediate action is a reporting request rather than a media plan change. Pull program-level placement for a recent flight and look at where the impressions actually landed. Ninety-one percent of programmatic traders say the lack of show-level data limits CTV, and most teams have never seen this view of their own spend. The first look at it usually settles an internal argument that has been running for a year.

#Case study worth stealing

The 15% who orchestrate, and what separates them: New research on agentic automation in customer experience found that 98% of organizations have deployed AI somewhere in the customer journey, but only 15% combine agentic AI with cross-departmental orchestration to resolve a need end to end. Sixty-four percent already run specialized agents for functions like identity, billing or technical support, and those agents sit in silos. The measurable separation is large. Organizations with orchestration are roughly four times more likely than the next maturity tier to report major CSAT or NPS gains, and nearly twice as likely to automate genuinely valuable work such as churn prevention, personalized recommendations, and returns and refunds, rather than deflection. Only 5% of all respondents can clearly quantify AI's impact on business outcomes at all.

The adaptable tactic is narrow and unglamorous. Pick one customer outcome that currently crosses at least two functions, a refund that requires identity verification, or a churn save that requires billing authority, and give a single owner the mandate to resolve it end to end before adding a single new agent. The pattern in the data is that the silos were there first and the agents inherited them. Automating inside an existing boundary makes the boundary more expensive, because now two systems fail to hand off at machine speed. The teams pulling ahead redrew one accountability line, then let the agents follow it.

#Framework or mental model

Cost per completed workflow, not cost per token: Token prices across frontier providers fell roughly 280 times between 2024 and 2026, and enterprise AI spend rose 320% over the same window. The FinOps Foundation found 73% of enterprises exceeded their original AI cost projections, and the share of FinOps practitioners responsible for AI spend went from 31% in 2025 to 98% in 2026.

The mental model that resolves the paradox is that unit price and unit consumption are set by different people. Price is set by the vendor and is falling fast. Consumption per task is set by your own engineers through three choices, how much modality you allow, how much autonomy you grant an agent, and how long you let a reasoning chain run. Every one of those is a design decision made in a pull request, not a budget decision made in a planning cycle, which is why the bill surprises finance.

Apply it by changing the denominator on your reporting. Stop tracking spend per vendor per month, which nobody can decompose or act on, and start tracking cost per completed unit of work: cost to resolve a service ticket, cost to produce a campaign variant, cost to qualify a lead. That number is comparable to the human cost of the same work, which makes it the only version of the metric that can settle a build decision, defend a budget, or tell you honestly when an agentic workflow is more expensive than the process it replaced.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Gracenote and The Trade Desk, show-level intelligence inside programmatic CTV: Announced September 1. Gracenote, Nielsen's content intelligence business, is making its content IDs, metadata and standardized taxonomy available inside The Trade Desk for targeting and reporting, so a buyer can target and report at the level of the individual program rather than the channel or the app. This is Gracenote's first DSP partnership. The supporting research is blunt: 91% of programmatic traders say the absence of show-level data limits CTV, and 95% of media planners say show-level reporting would be valuable. Signal: CTV has been sold for years as the premium end of programmatic while running on a signal set thinner than display. The bid request usually does not carry what was actually on screen. Making content the addressable unit does two things at once. It gives advertisers a privacy-durable targeting axis that needs no household identifier, and it gives publishers a reason to expose metadata they previously withheld to keep inventory fungible. That second effect is the one to watch, because the moment content becomes a priced attribute, undifferentiated CTV inventory loses its cover.
  • Anthropic, Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1: Shipped September 1. One million token context by default, 128k max output, always-on adaptive thinking. Input and output pricing unchanged at $10 and $50 per million tokens, but cache reads dropped to $0.25 per million, a 75% cut. New beta controls include per-message effort settings, turn-scoped system messages that clear at the next user turn, and progress display between tool calls. Alongside it, Enterprise Frontier Safeguards offers customer-controlled cloud storage with zero data retention equivalence and automated safety monitoring with no vendor human review, phasing in from fall 2026. Signal: the headline price did not move and the economics did. When cache reads get four times cheaper, the cost curve stops rewarding short prompts and starts rewarding large stable context that gets reused across many calls. That is an architectural instruction, not a discount. It favors stacks that keep a durable context corpus, brand rules, product catalog, policy, prior campaign performance, resident and re-read, over stacks that assemble a fresh prompt per request. The compliance packaging matters just as much for regulated buyers, because zero retention plus no human review removes the two objections that have kept large enterprises on smaller deployments.
  • Anthropic and Lambda, $35 billion cloud agreement: Roughly 350 megawatts in Nueces County, Texas. Hut 8 builds it, Nvidia holds the lease, Lambda operates the chips. Signal: the compute layer of the martech stack now has visible term structure. Anyone signing a multi-year commitment with an AI-native vendor is indirectly underwriting a twenty-year power and property obligation several parties upstream. That belongs in vendor risk review, and almost nobody has it there.

#Category watch

Contextual targeting has stopped being the cookieless fallback and become the CTV primary: The category was written off as a downgrade for a decade, the thing you bought when you could not buy the audience. The economics inverted. Contextual advertising now exceeds $300 billion globally with a projected 20% plus compound growth, outpacing identity-dependent models, and on CTV it is structurally better rather than merely acceptable. Television is a shared-household device, so person-level identity was always partly fiction there, while content signal is exact. The competitive field is forming around who owns the content identifier: Gracenote via Nielsen's catalog and now a DSP integration, IRIS.TV with over 90 million IRIS IDs for shows and movies, Peer39 and Viant pushing toward scene-level and sentiment-level analysis of dialogue and visuals. The constraint is plumbing, not appetite. CTV bid requests generally do not carry the content signal, so value accrues to whoever can attach it at the request. For anyone building a media org, the practical read is that the CTV targeting roadmap should now be budgeted as a metadata integration project rather than an audience data purchase.

#Stack intelligence

Unit price is no longer a useful proxy for AI cost, and most stack budgets are still built as though it is. Token prices across frontier providers fell roughly 280 times between 2024 and 2026. Enterprise AI spend over the same period rose 320%. The FinOps Foundation's 2026 report found 73% of enterprises exceeded their original AI cost projections, and the share of FinOps practitioners responsible for AI spend went from 31% in 2025 to 98% in 2026. The Linux Foundation launched a Tokenomics Foundation at FinOps X this year to write the practices that do not yet exist.

The mechanism is worth naming precisely, because it is a stack design problem rather than a procurement problem. Cost per token collapsed, and consumption per task exploded, because three things happened simultaneously: modality expanded from text to images and video, agents were given more autonomy so a single request became hundreds of tool calls, and reasoning chains got longer as extended thinking became default. A martech stack that adds an agentic layer to campaign operations does not add a line item, it converts a fixed software cost into a metered one whose volume is set by how much autonomy was granted, and autonomy is a design decision made by an engineer rather than a budget decision made by a finance partner. The practical architecture response is to instrument at the workflow level rather than the vendor level, so cost attaches to "resolve a service ticket" or "produce a campaign variant" and not to a monthly API bill that nobody can decompose. Anyone who lived through the shift from licensed on-premise software to unmanaged cloud spend already knows how this ends if the meter is not attached early.

#Foundations concept of the day

Contextual targeting: Contextual targeting decides which ad to show based on what the surrounding content is about, not on who the viewer is. The original version was crude keyword matching on a web page. The modern version classifies content on multiple axes, topic, genre, sentiment, brand safety, and in video down to the individual scene, then matches inventory to advertiser criteria without ever touching a user profile.

Two properties make it strategically different from audience targeting. First, it needs no consent to a personal identifier, because it describes the content rather than the person, which is why it survived cookie deprecation and why it works in jurisdictions where behavioral profiling is restricted. Second, it works on shared devices. Audience targeting on a living room television assumes one household equals one person, which is false roughly every evening.

The catch is that the signal has to reach the auction. A programmatic bid request describes the ad slot and usually not the content around it, so contextual works only when a content identifier is attached at the request level, which is exactly the integration Gracenote and The Trade Desk just built and the reason IRIS.TV built a separate ID space for programming.

The cross-industry example makes the value legible. A financial services brand running video wants to appear in coverage of markets and personal finance and stay out of segments about layoffs, fraud and bankruptcy. Audience targeting cannot express that, because it selects viewers rather than moments, and the same person watches both. Contextual expresses it exactly. The pattern generalizes: whenever an advertiser's real constraint is about the moment rather than the person, contextual is the correct instrument and audience targeting is the workaround.

#Watch list

  • Whether content becomes a priced dimension in CTV inventory, and what happens to the inventory that has none: The moment a buyer can pay more for a known program, undifferentiated CTV supply becomes visibly undifferentiated. Watch whether publishers who have withheld metadata start exposing it, which would signal that the pricing premium on transparency now exceeds the value of keeping inventory blendable. That is the same inflection that reshaped display supply a decade ago.
  • Whether cost per completed workflow shows up as a governance metric rather than an engineering one: The tell that token FinOps has matured is not a dashboard, it is an approval gate. Watch for the first large stack that requires a cost-per-outcome estimate before an agent gets production autonomy, the same way privacy review is required before a data flow ships. Everything before that is reporting on spend that already happened.

#Macro pulse

Canada: The TSX closed September 1 at 35,785, down 1.34%, roughly 450 points off, its second consecutive heavy session. USD/CAD sits at 1.3894, up 0.29%, so the loonie gave back yesterday's gain. That is the wrong direction for a Canadian carrier on the exact day crude repriced, because fuel is bought in US dollars and a weaker loonie widens the cost move rather than cushioning it. The two-day round trip in the currency is not the story. The story is that the currency is now a second-order effect sitting on top of a first-order energy shock.

USA: The S&P 500 fell 0.71% to 7,631.47, the Nasdaq dropped 1.03% to 26,099.77, and the Dow shed 419.02 points, or 0.79%, to 52,766.88. Tech led the selling, which is the pattern that matters: the Nasdaq's loss was roughly triple the S&P's, and duration-sensitive assets sell hardest when the discount rate moves. Futures were down again early September 2.

Asia: The overnight session was the sharpest move on the board. Nikkei 225 fell 2.9% to 64,325.64, the Kospi lost 4% to 6,562.72, Shanghai Composite dipped 1% to 3,941.39, the Hang Seng was nearly flat at 25,311.21, ASX 200 fell 1% to 8,978.40, Taiex fell 1.7%. USD/JPY strengthened to 159.84. When Korea and Japan drop three to four percent while Hong Kong holds flat, the seller is not trading a China view, it is trading a rates view, and the heavy semiconductor and export exposure in Seoul and Tokyo is what gets sold first.

Europe: FTSE 100 down 0.6% to 10,726.68, DAX down 0.6% to 25,798.01, CAC 40 down 0.4% to 8,260.77. EUR/USD at 1.1571.

Bonds, and this is the actual headline: The US 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.81%, up from 4.75% Monday and 4.20% in January. The 2-year sits at 4.40%, up from 3.50% in early 2026. Japan's 10-year government bond yield reached 3.02%, its highest level since 1996. The stated driver is elevated inflation plus growing government debt, with investors demanding more return for holding sovereign paper. A Japanese long bond at levels not seen in thirty years is not a Japan story. It is the removal of the global anchor that made every other duration asset look reasonably priced, and it pulls capital home out of the carry trades that funded a decade of risk-taking elsewhere.

Oil: WTI is at $90.34 after jumping 5.9% on September 1 to $90.82. Brent is at $94.90, against roughly $72 before the war began in February. The trigger was US military strikes on Iranian targets around the Strait of Hormuz following attacks on two oil tankers in the transit route, with Tehran promising a response "many times greater." A 32% move off the pre-conflict base is no longer a risk premium sitting on a normal price. It is the price.

Rates: The Fed meets September 15 to 16. Odds of a 25 basis point hike jumped to roughly 66% on Monday per CME FedWatch, close to double where they sat before the Jackson Hole speech on August 28, where the Fed chair called July PCE at 3.7% concerning, named inflation the predominant focus, downplayed labor market risk, and still declined to give forward guidance. Markets are pricing a hike off tone rather than off a commitment.

#Today's industry move

Anthropic signed a $35 billion cloud computing agreement with Lambda, the Nvidia-backed provider, covering roughly 350 megawatts of capacity for Claude training and inference. The structure is the interesting part. The Texas facility in Nueces County is being built by Hut 8, the bitcoin miner turned data center developer. Nvidia holds the lease. Lambda installs and operates the chips. Anthropic buys the compute. Four balance sheets, one building, and the chip vendor is standing in the middle of the lease obligation rather than simply selling hardware into it. Hut 8 shares rallied on the news.

#AI infrastructure watch

The Lambda deal is the same pattern that showed up in the Nvidia guarantee of $105 billion in lease and power obligations for the Ohio site serving OpenAI, run again with a different set of counterparties. Read it as a financing innovation, not a compute announcement. Model labs cannot carry twenty-year power and property commitments on their own credit, so the vendor with the strongest balance sheet in the chain is absorbing the term risk in exchange for locking in demand. It works cleanly as long as inference revenue grows into the obligations. It concentrates a remarkable amount of the sector's counterparty risk in one company if it does not.

Scale context: Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Oracle have collectively committed between $660 billion and $690 billion of capital expenditure in 2026, close to double 2025. That number was underwritten when the 10-year sat at 4.20%. It is now 4.81%. Nobody re-runs a multi-year capex model on a sixty basis point move, but everybody who has to refinance into it will notice.

#Signal or noise?

Signal, and it is one signal wearing two costumes. Bond yields at multi-decade highs and crude 32% above its pre-war base are both the market repricing the cost of physical inputs, money and energy, at the same time. Airlines feel it on fuel and on discretionary demand. AI infrastructure feels it on the cost of the debt funding the buildout and on the power contracts underneath it. The two industries that look least alike on a sector screen are currently exposed to exactly the same two variables.

#Watch tomorrow

Whether the Japanese 10-year holds above 3%. That level is the tell for whether this is a repricing or a dislocation, because a sustained break forces Japanese institutional capital to repatriate out of US and European credit, and that unwind moves far more money than the Fed's September decision does.
