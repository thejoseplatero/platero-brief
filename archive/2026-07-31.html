Executive Pulse637 words

#Macro signal

Stagflation risk moves from talk to data. The Fed held rates at 3.5-3.75% on Wednesday, but with three of twelve FOMC members dissenting in favor of a hike, the split itself is the story. Q2 GDP came in at a 1.5% annual rate, down from 2.1% and below the 1.8% consensus, while PCE inflation is still running 3.7% year over year. Slowing growth with sticky prices is the setup the Fed has no clean tool for. The Dow fell 2.2% on the decision, its worst day since April 2025, and 30-year yields pushed above 5.2%. Strategically, the era of assuming rate cuts will bail out weak demand is over. Leaders planning 2027 budgets should model a scenario where capital stays expensive and top-line growth is soft at the same time. That kills the "grow into it" logic behind a lot of AI capex and headcount plans, and rewards teams that can show margin, not just momentum.

#Tech industry

  • Microsoft now sells implementation as the product. Microsoft stood up Frontier Company, a $2.5B subsidiary with roughly 6,000 engineers and specialists embedded directly inside enterprise clients (LSEG, Unilever, Land O'Lakes, Accenture) to deploy and optimize AI systems, and notably to help customers pick the best model mix whether it comes from Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, or open source. The strategic signal: the hyperscalers have concluded the bottleneck is no longer model access, it is making models useful inside messy real companies. The margin is moving from the model to the deployment. Every vendor pitching you a platform is quietly discovering the money is in the services layer they said you would not need.
  • AI agents cross into production, and so does the risk number. Major platforms shipped agent frameworks trustworthy enough for narrow jobs (invoice processing, customer triage, data entry), moving AI from answering questions to completing work. Gartner attached a figure to the downside: $234B in enterprise software spend is now at risk from agentic AI displacing incumbent tools. The implication for boards is that your existing SaaS stack is no longer a safe default line item. Agents don't just automate labor, they collapse whole categories of software you currently pay seat licenses for.

#AI at the leadership level

The gap on AI is now inside the boardroom, not between the company and the market. BCG's 2026 survey of 625 CEOs and board members found 35% of CEOs think their boards overestimate what AI can replace, while 60% of CEOs think their boards are too impatient with the pace of transformation. The tell is cadence: only 26% of boards discuss AI at every meeting, but among companies reporting strong AI returns, 63% put it on every agenda. AI performance is tracking governance rhythm, not budget size. If AI is a quarterly update in your boardroom, you are already structurally behind the firms that treat it as a standing agenda item.

#Leadership & org design

The consulting model is being restructured by the tool it sells. McKinsey is cutting roughly 10% of staff, concentrated in back-office, junior research, and practice areas where generative AI has compressed delivery timelines, with work that once took analyst teams now done in hours. The durable lesson for any org builder: the leverage is shifting from headcount to a smaller number of people who can operate AI as a force multiplier. The relevant design question is no longer "how many analysts," it is "how few operators, each amplified." Firms whose economics depend on billing junior hours by the head are watching their own product commoditize their staffing model.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If capital stays expensive and demand stays soft through 2027, which of our current AI investments still pays for itself on margin alone, without assuming faster growth to justify it?

Practitioner Pulse646 words

#Try this now

  • LinkedIn Campaign Manager AI creative suite (Ads Personalization + AI Variants): LinkedIn's July creative tools resurfaced this week with hard numbers. Campaigns running five or more ad variants saw over 20% higher CTR, and Ads Personalization (tailoring by job title, company, industry) lifted CTR 1.4 points on website conversion and 2.4 points on video lead-gen for SMB advertisers. This week, take one live campaign, load your fonts, colors, and tone into Brand Kit so the AI stays on-brand, then spin up five variants instead of one. Worth 20 minutes because it removes the "we only have bandwidth for one creative" excuse that caps most B2B ad performance. Caveat: LinkedIn has not published sample size or method behind the 20% figure, so treat it as a hypothesis to A/B, not a guarantee.
  • Plain-English AI evals you own, not approve: The reframe landing across product teams this month is that the PM owns the eval, not just signs off on it. Write evals as four plain-English parts: role, context, goal, and labels (pass/fail criteria), no code required. Pick the single AI feature you shipped last quarter, write 20 real user inputs, and score the outputs yourself against those labels before your next release. Worth 20 minutes because user tolerance for generic AI output has cratered, and an eval set is the only way to catch quality regressions before customers do.
  • A persistent context workspace before any AI workflow: The foundational move under evals, agents, and prototyping is a shared workspace (Claude Projects, Google Gems, ChatGPT Projects, Copilot Studio) that holds your personas, constraints, brand rules, and metric definitions. Spend 20 minutes loading yours once, and every downstream prompt stops starting from zero. This is the difference between AI that guesses about your product and AI that reasons from your actual context.

#Case study worth stealing

Netflix and Adidas, dynamic creative optimization via Cortex: The two brands partnered with AI marketing platform Cortex to serve personalized ad creative built from viewing history, past purchase data, and contextual signals rather than one fixed asset per audience. Instead of a designer hand-building a handful of variants, the system assembles and targets creative per viewer against real behavioral inputs. The steal for loyalty and travel teams: your CRM already holds the behavioral signal (trips booked, tier, dwell time, cart) that most brands still ignore at the creative layer. Wire that signal into creative assembly, not just audience selection, and you personalize the "what and why," not only the "who."

#Framework or mental model

AI-first, not AI-only: This month Klarna, Duolingo, and Shopify all walked back aggressive "AI-first" claims after automation underdelivered, with Klarna resuming human hiring when its chatbot's lower-quality responses hurt satisfaction. The usable model that replaces the hype: map one messy process end to end, insert AI where it removes toil, keep a human review gate, and prove time saved or errors reduced before scaling. Digital leaders should apply it to the loudest automation ask in their org right now by scoping it to a single workflow with a measured before-and-after, instead of an org-wide "AI-first" mandate that has no baseline to defend.

#Rabbit hole

Agentic commerce is quietly rewriting the top of the funnel. AI-referred shoppers converted 31% higher over the 2025 holidays (Adobe Analytics), AI-driven orders grew roughly 15x from January 2025 to January 2026, and AI platforms are projected to drive around $20.9 billion in US retail spend this year, nearly four times 2025. Salesforce's July B2C Commerce release added Agentic Commerce Search and native OpenAI feed distribution so a storefront's catalog stays accurate inside ChatGPT. The practitioner question worth an afternoon: is your product, rate, or inventory data structured and fresh enough for an agent to read and transact against, or are you invisible the moment a customer shops through an assistant instead of your homepage?

AI Stack Daily748 words

#Vendor & platform moves

  • OpenAI + The Trade Desk: Reports this week (via The Information) surfaced early talks for OpenAI to lean on The Trade Desk to automate and scale ad sales inside ChatGPT, as OpenAI projects advertising could roughly double consumer ChatGPT revenue toward $17B this year. TTD stock rallied on the news. Signal: The next demand surface is the chat interface, and even a would-be walled garden with $25B in revenue is choosing to rent programmatic plumbing rather than build a full ad stack from scratch. Buy-side infrastructure keeps its relevance precisely as AI threatens to disintermediate it.
  • Walmart / Vibe.co: Walmart is absorbing self-serve CTV platform Vibe.co (reported near $1.2B, expected to close by end of FY2027) and folding it into Walmart Connect. Vibe lets a mid-market advertiser launch live TV campaigns in under five minutes from $50/day. Signal: Retail media, connected TV, and SMB self-service are collapsing into one motion. The moat is first-party purchase data from 150M+ customers wired directly into CTV targeting and closed-loop sales attribution, which is something a pure DSP cannot replicate.
  • MoEngage: The engagement platform raised $100M (Goldman Sachs Alternatives, A91 Partners) to scale its Merlin AI agent suite, then followed with roughly $180M more weeks later. Signal: Capital is flowing to the agentic execution layer that sits on top of customer data, not to another system of record. The bet is that orchestration and next-best-action, not storage, is where marketing value concentrates next.

#Category watch

The CDP is splitting in two. The category is bifurcating into martech CDPs built for internal lifecycle orchestration (Adobe, Hightouch, Zeotap) and adtech CDPs, increasingly rebranded as collaborative audience platforms, built for privacy-first activation across the ad ecosystem (Decentriq, Permutive, Mediarithmics). If you are building a martech org, this ends the era of buying one CDP to do everything. You now make a deliberate call about whether your primary need is orchestration inside your own funnel or safe audience collaboration with partners and publishers, because the tooling for those two jobs is diverging fast.

#Stack intelligence

Warehouse-native is winning the architecture argument. Composable and warehouse-native CDP vendors grew headcount 7.8% against a 1.3% industry average, roughly 6x, which is where talent votes with its feet. The emerging pattern: keep the customer data in the warehouse (Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery) as the single source of truth, then layer activation and increasingly agentic next-best-action on top rather than copying data into a monolithic suite. Databricks entering with an agentic CDP validates the direction. The buy-vs-build line has moved: buy the activation and agent layer, own the data layer, and stop paying a suite vendor to hold a second copy of data you already store.

#Foundations concept of the day

Data clean rooms. A clean room is a secure environment where two parties (say a brand and a retailer, or an advertiser and a publisher) can match and analyze their customer data together without either side seeing or exporting the other's raw records. Instead of handing over a customer list, each party loads data into a neutral space that only permits aggregated, privacy-safe outputs: overlap counts, audience segments, campaign measurement. It matters at scale because third-party cookies and device IDs are decaying, regulation is tightening, and the richest data now sits locked inside walled gardens and retailers. Clean rooms are how first-party data gets combined without the legal and privacy exposure of moving it. Amazon Marketing Cloud is the canonical example: advertisers run analytics across pseudonymized Amazon signals plus their own inputs and never touch the underlying rows. AWS Clean Rooms and Snowflake now support cross-cloud collaboration with zero data movement, so a travel brand, a bank, and a media network can measure a shared campaign without any of them exposing a customer file.

#Watch list

  • The agentic CDP. Databricks and MoEngage's Merlin are early signals that the CDP conversation is shifting from where data lives to what autonomously acts on it. Watch whether these move past pilot stage. BCG's CMO survey found 96% say AI is transforming marketing but only 8% run genuinely multi-agent autonomous campaigns. The gap between narrative and production is the real signal to track.
  • AI chat as an ad surface. If the OpenAI and Trade Desk talks mature, conversational interfaces become a new inventory type with no cookies, no page, and a very different attention model. Anyone building a media plan should start asking what a "placement" even means inside a chatbot.
Markets Signal348 words

#Macro pulse

Canada: TSX closed near 35,506 (+0.49%), lifted by banks and gold miners. USD/CAD sits around 1.40, with the loonie firm after the Fed held rates this week, up from a 1.42 low on Monday. A stronger CAD trims the domestic value of USD-denominated international fares for Canadian carriers. USA: Records again. S&P 500 +0.67% and Nasdaq +1.32% to fresh highs, Dow +0.38%. Amazon's blowout quarter carried tech higher, while Apple fell on a soft forward outlook and rising component costs. Asia: Broadly green. Shanghai Composite +0.76% to ~3,834 on stimulus hopes, Nikkei 225 +0.71% near 61,867, Hang Seng roughly flat +0.20%. Oil: WTI quiet near $84, off about 0.5%. No margin signal for airlines today.

#Today's industry move

Amazon's Q2 reset the cloud narrative. AWS grew 37% year over year to $42.2B, its fastest since 2021 and well past the ~31% Street expectation, now at a $169B annualized run rate. AI and custom-chip businesses each cleared $25B run rates growing triple digits. Management said even at raised spend it still cannot meet all 2026 demand, and expects the same in 2027. The read for travel and loyalty leaders: cloud reacceleration means the AI capacity crunch is a demand story, not a hype story, and compute pricing power stays with the hyperscalers.

#AI infrastructure watch

The tell was in the capex line. Amazon lifted 2026 cash capex to about $220B from roughly $200B, and named higher memory cost as the driver, on top of data centers, networking, and AI chips. Memory is emerging as the new bottleneck in the stack, ahead of raw GPU supply. When a hyperscaler raises a $200B budget by 10% mid-year and points at DRAM and HBM pricing, that pressure flows downstream to every buyer of AI-heavy SaaS.

#Signal or noise?

Signal. This is the AI capex supercycle broadening from GPUs into memory and power, with cloud demand outrunning supply into 2027.

#Watch tomorrow

Whether the memory-cost flag in Amazon's guide shows up in other hyperscaler and chip commentary, and whether USD/CAD holds sub-1.41 into next week's data.
