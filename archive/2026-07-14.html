Executive Pulse623 words

#Macro signal

Inflation cools, but the risk moved to the tape, not the CPI: June CPI came in at 3.5% annual versus the 3.8% expected, and odds of a July Fed hike collapsed from 42% to 17% overnight. The relief was immediate but narrow. Oil pushed higher on new Strait of Hormuz shipping fees and an Iranian port blockade, so the next inflation shock is now geopolitical, not demand-driven. For leaders, this reframes the planning question. The domestic disinflation story is largely won, which means the exposures worth hedging in the back half of 2026 are energy-linked and supply-chain-linked, not rate-linked. Warsh's first Humphrey Hawkins testimony this week sets the tone for how much of this the Fed is willing to look through.

#Tech industry

  • Apple sues OpenAI over trade secrets, and the fight is really about the post-phone interface: Apple alleges OpenAI obtained confidential hardware and manufacturing knowledge through two former Apple executives now building OpenAI's Jony Ive-designed AI-native device. The strategic read is that Apple is defending the device layer before it exists, because whoever owns the primary AI interface owns the customer relationship that Apple has monetized for fifteen years. Every incumbent with a direct-to-user surface should be asking who is quietly building the interface that makes theirs optional.
  • Meta and the hyperscalers are vertically integrating the entire AI stack, down to the power plant: Meta begins production of its custom "Iris" chip in September, targeting 14 gigawatts of compute by 2027 against $145B in AI infrastructure spend this year, cutting Nvidia dependence. In parallel, a $5.34B Blackstone-led consortium is funding behind-the-meter power generation to route around multi-year grid interconnection delays. The signal for boards: compute and electricity are now strategic scarcities that the largest players are buying their way out of directly. Anyone renting capacity from them is a price-taker on the two inputs that will define AI cost structure for the decade.

#AI at the leadership level

The accountability for AI has fully migrated to the CEO seat, and it is now personal. Roughly 72% of CEOs identify as their company's primary AI decision-maker, double last year's share, and 80% believe their own job is at risk by year-end if the AI strategy underperforms, with 72% citing direct board pressure to prove ROI. This is a governance shift, not a mood. When the top of the house owns AI outcomes personally, delegation patterns invert. The risk is that fear-driven CEOs over-index on visible AI activity to satisfy boards rather than on operating-model change that actually compounds. The board's job this quarter is to reward the second and see through the first.

#Leadership & org design

The pyramid is being hollowed from the middle, not the bottom. The pattern across 2026 org redesigns is that intermediate roles, the layers that existed to coordinate and control rather than to create value, are being absorbed into the agent layer, while senior orchestrators run teams of agents across stages that used to require separate teams. BCG's 2026 work shows firms that redesign the operating model around AI hit up to 60% cost and 80% cycle-time reduction, but most are instead layering agents onto a breaking model, described aptly as taping over the cracks. The practical test for each proposed agent deployment is three questions a leadership team can settle in an afternoon: who owns it, who defines and checks "good," and who catches the edge cases. If those have no owner, it is not deployed, it is exposed.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If we removed one full layer of management this quarter, which one exists to create value and which one exists only to move information that an agent could move faster?

Practitioner Pulse574 words

#Try this now

  • AI Media Buyer by Creatify (launched July 13): Paste a product URL and it generates video ads, scripts, and voiceovers tuned for conversion, then iterates on the variants that perform. Worth 20 minutes to test against your current paid-social creative pipeline. If it produces a shippable ad from a live PDP in one pass, that reframes who owns creative production on a growth team. Treat the output as a first draft to human-edit, not a final asset.
  • Fudge MCP (launched July 13): A design reference engine that plugs into your AI agent via MCP and lets you search 10,000 sites by font, color, and visual similarity. Instead of prompting "make it look premium," you point the agent at concrete references it can pull from. Useful the moment you connect it to a coding or design agent that already reads MCP, since it swaps vague taste language for retrievable examples.
  • GPT-Live API waitlist: OpenAI shipped GPT-Live to ChatGPT Voice on July 8, a full-duplex model that listens and speaks at the same time, backing chips like "mhmm," interrupting, and calling tools mid-sentence. The API is not out yet, but the waitlist is open now. Get on it before a competitor does if you run any voice IVR, drive-thru, or phone-support surface, because turn-based voice bots will feel broken next to this.

#Case study worth stealing

Adobe Analytics, quantifying the AI-referral flip across 130-plus North American retailers: Adobe measured over a trillion visits and found that by March 2026 traffic arriving from AI assistants converted 42 percent better than non-AI traffic, a full reversal from converting 38 percent worse a year earlier. AI-referred shoppers also spent 48 percent longer on-site and generated 37 percent more revenue per visit, because they arrive pre-qualified after researching inside the chat. The tactic to steal is narrow and cheap: Adobe flagged that roughly a quarter of retailer homepage content is still not structured for AI discovery, so audit your homepage and top category pages for machine-readable product facts, specs, and answers this quarter. You are not chasing a new channel, you are removing the reasons an assistant skips or misreads you.

#Framework or mental model

Context engineering as information architecture for a model: The 2026 reframe, pushed by Shopify's Tobi Lutke and echoed by Anthropic, is that the headline AI skill is no longer wording a clever prompt, it is deciding what configuration of context makes the desired output most likely. Chroma's "Context Rot" finding gives it teeth: across 18 models, the longer the input, the less reliably the model uses it, so more context is not better context. For digital leaders this is a design discipline you already own, picking references, setting hierarchy, cutting noise, and deciding what to show and hide, applied to what you feed an agent rather than what you feed a user.

#Rabbit hole

The AI-referral conversion reversal is worth an hour across sources. Adobe's dataset says the flip is real and large, but Practical Ecommerce collected mixed reports where some merchants see negligible AI traffic, and PYMNTS notes AI referrals still convert nearly 50 percent higher on Shopify product pages while outperforming organic in 23 of 25 categories. The open question worth sitting with: is AI referral a genuine high-intent channel to instrument now, or a small-volume signal being over-read because the year-over-year growth curve looks dramatic. Read all three before you reallocate budget.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • ActiveCampaign: Shipped a Google Ads connector for its Active Intelligence engine, letting mid-market marketers spin up Performance Max campaigns from a conversational prompt without leaving the platform or logging into Ads. Gated to Plus plans and up. Signal: the connector, not the campaign builder, is becoming the product. Value is migrating from "where you compose the ad" to "who owns the pipe between your first-party data and the walled garden's buying engine." Every MarTech vendor is now racing to be that pipe before the ad platforms close it off.
  • SAS: Launched 360 Marketing AI, positioning it so marketing teams can build and deploy ML models with no data-science support. Signal: the analytics incumbents are attacking the same "no code, prompt it" surface as the startups. When a legacy stats vendor and a growth-marketing SaaS converge on the identical promise (self-serve modeling for non-technical marketers), the differentiator stops being the model and becomes the proprietary data it is trained on.
  • Zappi: Released Amplify AI, a predictive ad-testing tool that scores hundreds of social video variants pre-launch at a claimed 84% accuracy versus human survey panels. Signal: creative testing is collapsing from a post-flight measurement cost into a pre-flight generation input. Once you can predict performance before spend, the testing budget and the media budget start to merge, which reshapes how agencies price the work.

#Category watch

Retail media, onsite plus offsite unification: The category is consolidating around a single control plane that spans a retailer's owned inventory and the open web. Dollar General's DGMN, wiring Kevel for onsite ad serving to The Trade Desk for offsite activation, is the template: one framework, full-funnel across CTV, digital audio, and onsite display, with consistent measurement, managed and self-serve. The strategic shift is that mid-tier retailers no longer build a media network, they assemble one from an infrastructure layer (Kevel), a DSP (The Trade Desk), and a clean room (LiveRamp). That lowers the barrier to launch and guarantees a long tail of retail media networks, which in turn fragments the buy side and raises the premium on tooling that abstracts across dozens of networks at once.

#Stack intelligence

The dominant enterprise pattern right now is assemble, not build. A retail media network that would have required a two-year internal platform effort in 2023 is now a three-vendor integration: serving layer, demand layer, identity and clean-room layer. The same decomposition is spreading to marketing ops broadly, where the stack is increasingly a thin owned data spine plus rented execution modules snapped on through connectors and Model Context Protocol profiles (RainFocus shipped MCP Profiles this week to expose live event data to agents). The buy-vs-build line has moved decisively toward buy for execution, build for the data layer. The one thing worth owning outright is the first-party data spine and the identity resolution on top of it, because that is the only asset the connectors cannot commoditize.

#Foundations concept of the day

Global Privacy Control (GPC): A browser-level signal that broadcasts a user's opt-out of data sale and sharing automatically, so the person does not have to click a "do not sell my data" link on every site. Think of it as a standing instruction the browser sends on every request, rather than a per-site cookie banner choice. It matters at scale because a growing set of US state laws now treat GPC as a legally binding opt-out that companies must honor, not a suggestion. Kentucky, Rhode Island, and Indiana began requiring GPC recognition on January 1, 2026, joining California and Colorado. Enforcement has shifted from writing laws to bringing cases, with per-violation fines in the $7,500 to $10,000 range. Cross-industry example: a travel brand running personalized retargeting across e-commerce, a co-brand card, and a loyalty app has to honor a single GPC signal consistently across all three surfaces, or each surface becomes an independent enforcement exposure. The practical consequence for an org-builder is that consent state has to live in the shared data spine, not in each channel tool, because a signal respected in email but ignored in the ad platform is still a violation.

#Watch list

  • Kevel: The retail media serving layer showing up underneath third-party networks. If the assemble-not-build pattern holds, the infrastructure provider that becomes the default onsite ad server for the long tail of mid-tier retailers captures a structural position, the way a payments processor sits under thousands of merchants.
  • Model Context Protocol as a distribution channel: Vendors are shipping MCP profiles so agents can pull their live data directly. Watch whether MCP becomes the connector standard that replaces bespoke API integrations, because whoever's data is easiest for agents to reach becomes the default source of truth in an agent-driven buying workflow.

#Macro pulse

The tape is being driven by one thing: the Strait of Hormuz. US-Iran fighting entered a third straight day, and Trump's move to call the peace deal "over" and impose a shipping blockade has crude leading everything.

  • Canada: TSX under pressure alongside US equities, tracking the risk-off and chip selloff. USD/CAD held soft near 0.7072 US cents (the loonie is not the story today, oil is).
  • USA: Monday's close was ugly. S&P 500 -0.79% to 7,515.34, Nasdaq -1.55% to 25,873.18, Dow -0.26% to 52,498.64. Semiconductors led the drop, Philadelphia Semiconductor Index off more than 5%. Energy names cushioned the Dow.
  • Asia: Split tape Tuesday. Nikkei 225 clawed back an afternoon deficit to close +0.75% at 67,743.50. Hang Seng -0.72% to 24,039.32, and Chinese equities hit a three-month low on slowdown worries ahead of Wednesday's Q2 GDP print (growth seen slowing to 4.5% from 5%). Shanghai Composite finished near 3,967.
  • Oil: WTI back near 79.50 and Brent up as much as 3.8% Tuesday to around 85.90, a one-month high, after a 9.6% surge the prior session. Hormuz transits dropped more than 50% week over week. This is a direct margin hit for carriers.

#Today's industry move

The oil spike is an airline story. On the last leg higher, American -5%, United -4%, Delta and JetBlue -3%. Carriers built 2026 guidance around roughly 4.00 to 4.30 per gallon jet fuel, and every leg up in crude compresses margins that capacity discipline had just started to protect. Delta is the least exposed of the group, with loyalty, premium, and refinery streams making up 62% of adjusted revenue. The read for Canadian carriers: a soft loonie plus a jet fuel spike is a double squeeze on international unit costs.

#AI infrastructure watch

TSMC reported Q2 revenue of NT$1.27 trillion (about 39.6B USD), up 36% year over year, with June sales up nearly 68%. First-half revenue is running 35.6% ahead of last year. The demand signal at the foundry is as strong as ever, yet the chip equities sold off hard on geopolitics, not fundamentals: NVIDIA -3.39%, AMD -2.64% on the day. The gap between real AI capex momentum (TSMC, foundry loadings) and stock price action (macro fear) is the tell. On energy, the nuclear-for-AI pattern keeps compounding: Valar Atomics and NVIDIA are scoping a nearly waterless 30MW data center in Utah, and Terrestrial Energy plus Riot Platforms are exploring co-located SMR sites in Texas and Kentucky.

#Signal or noise?

Mostly geopolitical noise on the equity tape, but the oil move is a real trend with a real cost line attached for travel. TSMC's numbers say the AI buildout is not slowing, whatever the chip charts do this week.

#Watch tomorrow

China Q2 GDP print. A sub-4.5% number stacks demand-slowdown fear on top of the Hormuz risk premium, and Asia-exposed travel and consumer names feel both.
