#Macro signal

The AI trade broke on Friday, and the Fed is still expected to hike. The S&P 500 fell 3.7 percent and the Dow 1.9 percent, both into correction; the Nasdaq dropped 5.3 percent and wiped out its year; the TSX lost about 2.5 percent for a seventh straight decline. At the same time the New York Fed called inflation "stubbornly high" and markets price a hike on 9 December. Why it matters: the wealth effect that has carried premium discretionary spending through 2026 is shrinking just as credit gets more expensive, and the companies that led the market up are the ones now leading it down. For tech, product and marketing leaders, expect finance to revisit 2027 AI budgets in the next few weeks. Have the business case for each AI line ready in margin terms, not adoption terms, before the question arrives. (CNBC, Axios)

#Tech industry

  • Nvidia is reportedly under SEC accounting scrutiny, and the market erased about $491 billion in a day. Reuters reported that SEC enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues; no charges, no SEC comment, focus unknown. It follows the CEO's departure and a 23 percent weekly drop. Strategic implication: the single most concentrated supplier in the AI stack now carries governance risk on top of supply risk. Boards that have treated GPU access as a pure procurement question should ask what a prolonged inquiry, or restated numbers, would do to pricing, allocation and the financing behind their cloud providers' capacity. (Motley Fool)
  • Alibaba cut sales and marketing spend 38 percent while revenue grew 10 percent. In the quarter to 30 September, marketing fell from 26.8 to 15.2 percent of revenue, while merchant revenue on Taobao and Tmall fell 5 percent and AI cloud revenue rose 53 percent. At Temu's owner PDD, advertising income (RMB59.8bn) now exceeds commissions (RMB48.5bn). Strategic implication: the largest commerce platforms are moving money out of buying demand and into the AI and ad layers that monetize it. (PPC Land)

#AI at the leadership level

The model is not the moat, said the CEO of a company that builds one. Alibaba group CEO Eddie Wu, discussing agents on the call: "if all the apps are basically running on the same model, then those apps are very easy to replicate." It is a useful line to take into any AI steering committee. Most enterprise AI roadmaps are a list of features built on the same three or four frontier models every competitor can license. The board-level question is what the company owns that a competitor with the same model cannot copy in a quarter: proprietary data, distribution, a workflow customers already depend on, or regulatory position. AI investment that compounds one of those is strategy. AI investment that does not is cost parity. (PPC Land)

#Leadership & org design

Middle managers are now the change function. Korn Ferry's survey of more than 1,300 talent and senior leaders found 66 percent expect managers to spend more time helping teams through change than overseeing daily work, and described them as the organization's "shock absorbers." Many of those managers already run larger teams after years of delayering. Mercer's 2026 data puts the tension plainly: 98 percent of executives plan org design changes within two years, but only 32 percent think their organization can combine human and machine work well. The practical move is to treat manager capacity as a constraint in AI rollout plans: count the change load per manager, and fund it, before cutting another layer. (Fortune, Tech.co)

#Question worth asking your team this week

If a competitor licensed the same AI models we use tomorrow, which of our 2027 AI initiatives would still give us an advantage, and which would we be funding just to keep up?

#Try this now

  • Ask the assistants for your promo code before Black Friday: PPC Land reports that Google, ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and Claude are already showing coupon codes directly inside answers ahead of 27 November. Independent tests show the codes are often wrong: Coupons.com asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude for working codes at 101 US retailers and found ChatGPT suggests codes confidently while Claude usually declines, with similar working rates overall; a SimplyCodes study found 47 percent of Claude's codes worked against far more, less reliable, codes per answer from ChatGPT (both testers sell coupons, so weigh the numbers accordingly). Spend 20 minutes this week typing "[your brand] promo code" and "[your brand] Black Friday deal" into each assistant. Log every code shown, check whether it is live, expired or invented, and brief customer service on what shoppers will quote at them. Expired codes that still circulate are a margin leak; invented ones are a service ticket. (PPC Land, Coupons.com, SimplyCodes)
  • Defer-load the tools in any agent with a long tool list: Google added Tool Retrieval to the Gemini API on 19 November, in preview in the Interactions API for Gemini 3.6 and later. Add a tool_search tool and set defer_loading: true on the rest; the model sees only names and descriptions (PPC Land puts idle tools at about 26 tokens each) and pulls a full schema only when a task needs it. Setting it on an MCP server defers every tool that server exposes. Two limits: retrieved schemas cannot be unloaded, and the full tools array is resent each request. Worth it for any team whose internal agent has grown to dozens of connectors: cheaper calls and fewer wrong-tool picks. Rewrite tool descriptions first, because they are now the only thing the model reads when choosing. (Google, PPC Land)
  • Check where your Microsoft Ads now appear: Search Engine Land reported on 20 November that Microsoft Advertising ads can now run alongside AI Overviews on Bing in all markets except China, and that Microsoft is adding "Made with AI" labels to some AI-generated ad images. Pull this week's placement and search term reports, flag queries that trigger AI Overviews, and compare click-through and conversion there against standard results before Black Friday budgets go out. If your creative team uses Microsoft's generative image tools, review which assets will carry the label and whether that matters in regulated categories. (Search Engine Land, Search Engine Land)

#Case study worth stealing

Alibaba, letting a model decide who gets the incentive: For Double 11 in 2025, Alibaba used AI to allocate 50 billion yuan (about $7 billion) of coupons, and reported a 15 percent lift in conversion over its previous allocation methods. A year on, its quarter to 30 September shows sales and marketing down 38 percent, from 26.8 to 15.2 percent of revenue, with group revenue still up 10 percent. Alibaba has not said how much of the cut came from smarter targeting versus ending subsidy wars, and merchant revenue fell 5 percent, so this is a direction, not proof. The tactic any digital team can adapt: stop issuing discounts by segment rule and test a model that predicts incremental purchase per customer, with a holdout group that receives no offer, so the saving is measured on purchases the incentive actually caused. (Caixin, PPC Land)

#Framework or mental model

The same-model test: Alibaba CEO Eddie Wu put it simply this week: "if all the apps are basically running on the same model, then those apps are very easy to replicate." The test for any AI feature is to assume a competitor has the same model tomorrow and ask what is left: your data, your distribution, your place in the customer's workflow, or nothing. Apply it at roadmap review: features that score "nothing" can still be worth shipping, but fund them as cost parity, not as differentiation. (PPC Land)

#Macro pulse

Canada: The S&P/TSX Composite fell 838.88 points, or about 2.5 percent, to 33,189.85 on Friday, its seventh straight loss and 8.8 percent below the 11 November level. Technology, mining, consumer cyclicals and energy led the decline, with almost every sector deep in the red. (Yahoo Finance / Canadian Press, Motley Fool Canada)

USA: The worst day of the drawdown so far. The S&P 500 fell 3.7 percent to 6,910.39 and the Dow 1.93 percent to 48,147.33, both closing in correction, at least 10 percent below their peaks. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28 percent to 22,617.73 and erased its 2026 gain, and the Nasdaq-100 is now 20 percent off its high, which is bear market territory. It was the S&P 500's seventh straight loss and its worst week in more than 18 months. The Fed backdrop has not softened: New York Fed President John Williams said this week that inflation is "stubbornly high," and markets price a quarter-point hike on 9 December. (CNBC, Yahoo Finance, Axios)

Asia: Friday's session already carried the damage: the Nikkei 225 closed down 5.06 percent and the Hang Seng extended its five-month low. Monday's open will be the first read on whether Asia absorbs Friday's 5 percent Nasdaq drop.

Oil: The expiring December WTI contract settled up 0.9 percent at $83.98, while the new front-month January contract closed near $81.56 as recovering Middle East exports weigh on prices. Brent sits around $90. Crude is the one input not getting worse for airline cost lines. (MarketScreener / OPIS, EnergyNow)

#Today's industry move

Anthropic gave back most of its debut pop on day two. ANT fell to an intraday low of $83.05, five cents above the $83 IPO price, before closing around $88 to $89, down roughly 9 percent from Thursday's $97.17 (reported closes differ slightly). Morningstar's $92 fair value estimate now sits just above the market price. The comparison 24/7 Wall St. drew is useful: Facebook and Uber both fell 11 percent on day two. A frontier lab trading as a normal high-beta tech stock in a risk-off tape is a reminder that public pricing will now discipline AI vendor behavior quarter to quarter. (Motley Fool, TradingKey, 24/7 Wall St.)

#AI infrastructure watch

  • Nvidia lost about $491 billion in one session on a reported SEC accounting inquiry. Reuters, citing two people briefed on the matter, said SEC enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues; the timing and focus are unknown, the SEC declined to comment and no charges have been filed. Shares fell roughly 13 to 14 percent Friday and about 23 percent on the week, roughly $1 trillion of value, already down more than 6 percent before the report. Two days after a revenue beat, the company at the center of the AI supply chain is trading on governance: a CEO departure and now a regulator. (Motley Fool, Yahoo Finance)

#Signal or noise?

Signal: corrections in two U.S. indices, a bear market in the Nasdaq-100 and a governance shock at the AI bellwether, with central banks still leaning toward hikes, is a repricing of the AI trade, not a one-day blip.

#Watch tomorrow

Weekend headlines from Nvidia or the SEC that could set the tone for Monday's Asian open, and whether ANT holds its $83 IPO price on Monday.
