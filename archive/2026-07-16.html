Executive Pulse530 words

#Macro signal

Inflation prints soft two months running, and the market has moved on from rate hikes: Back-to-back cool readings (June CPI down 0.4% headline, PPI down 0.3%) pushed traders to an ~85% probability the Fed holds at 3.50-3.75% this month. Ten and two-year yields fell, and megacap tech led the S&P higher, with Apple hitting an all-time high. Strategically, the "when do rates fall" debate is quietly replacing the "how high do they go" debate, which changes the calculus for capital deployment. If the cost of capital has plateaued, the case for deferring AI and infrastructure investment weakens. Leaders who have been waiting for macro clarity to commit budget are running out of reasons to wait, and the competitors already spending are compounding a lead.

#Tech industry

  • The trillion-dollar bet moves from models to implementation: Anthropic's Blackstone-backed venture Ode (100 engineers, over half former founders) and OpenAI's $4B Deployment Company (second acquisition, Northslope) are both racing to build forward-deployed engineering, the same play Deloitte and Accenture are now staffing. Strategic implication: the bottleneck to enterprise AI value is no longer model quality, it is integration into real operations, and the scarce resource is elite generalist talent, not compute. Buying a model license solves the smallest part of the problem.
  • EU AI Act enforcement went live July 10: Chatbot disclosure is now binding with real penalties, following the Digital Omnibus final approval July 9. Any customer-facing conversational AI touching EU users must now disclose it is AI. Strategic implication: this is a compliance line item today, not a 2027 planning item, and it sits across marketing, product, and legal at once.

#AI at the leadership level

The AI leadership seat is consolidating, not proliferating. The CDO, CIO, and CDAO titles are folding into a single accountable owner, and the Chief AI Officer, now most often reporting directly to CEO or board, is being asked to own value creation rather than just governance. Boards are increasingly assuming someone is in charge of AI risk and escalation paths without confirming who. The gap to close this quarter: a board that cannot name the single executive accountable for an AI failure, and the escalation path when one happens, does not actually have AI governance. It has an org chart.

#Leadership & org design

The AI-native restructuring is now visible in headcount, and the shape matters more than the size. Over 87,000 tech job losses this year cite AI. The instructive moves are structural: Coinbase flattened to five layers below the CEO and is piloting "one-person teams" that fuse engineering, design, and product, and Groupon is embedding agents into every function as a deliberate operating-model rebuild. The pattern is not headcount reduction for its own sake, it is collapsing coordination layers so fewer people wielding agents can own end-to-end outcomes. The lever is span of control, not just span of cuts.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If our biggest AI constraint is implementation talent rather than model access, are we still budgeting as if buying licenses is the hard part, and who exactly owns the outcome when an AI system we deployed fails a customer?

Practitioner Pulse550 words

#Try this now

  • GPT-5.6 Programmatic Tool Calling (shipped this week in the Responses API): instead of one tool call per round trip, the model writes a small JavaScript program that loops, filters, dedupes, and aggregates across your connected tools inside a sandbox, then calls the model once to interpret. The practitioner move: point it at ad-platform data plus your CRM and ask it to pull the raw pulls, dedupe, and return only structured counts and a few representative samples. You stop paying for the model to re-read thousands of rows, and you get a clean growth-opportunity readout instead of a token bill. Worth 20 minutes to wire one report you rebuild every week.
  • GPT-5.6 multi-agent (Sol as orchestrator, Luna/Terra as workers): Sol plans and synthesizes while cheaper sub-models do the fetching and simple analysis. For competitor teardowns or multi-market scans, spin up parallel sub-agents (one per competitor or region) and let Sol merge the findings in a single request. Reported 60 to 80 percent cost reduction per run when routing is deliberate, so route the grunt work down and keep the expensive model for the thinking.
  • Audit your longest prompts, then cut them to 150-300 words: the highest-ROI prompting habit this month is subtraction, not addition. Take the three prompts you reuse most, strip them to context, task, and output format (the CTF pattern), and delete the rest. Shorter prompts are cheaper, faster, and in practice more accurate because you stop burying the instruction. Fifteen minutes, immediate payback.

#Case study worth stealing

& Other Stories, AI fit recommendations plus 3D product guides: The fashion brand paired an AI-powered fit recommender with interactive three-dimensional guides on its knitwear line and cut return rates by 32 percent. The lever was not the model, it was attacking the single most expensive post-purchase moment (the wrong-size return) at the point of decision. Any team with a costly downstream failure, refunds, cancellations, support escalations, can copy the pattern: instrument the decision that causes the failure, then inject AI-driven guidance right there rather than bolting a chatbot onto the homepage.

#Framework or mental model

Agent-as-labor workflow selection: Before building any agent, score the target workflow on five traits: high frequency, a clear finish line, existing software it can touch, learnable edge cases, and pain the buyer actually feels. If a workflow misses two or more, it is a bad first agent no matter how impressive the demo. Digital leaders can use this as a one-page filter to decide which of their team's repetitive processes to automate first, and which to leave to humans for now.

#Rabbit hole (optional)

Context engineering is eating prompt engineering. The 2026 discipline shifts the question from "what do I say to the model" to "what does the agent know, see, and remember at the moment it acts": its memory, tool outputs, policies, prior-step history, and the visibility boundaries between sub-agents. In multi-agent setups each sub-agent gets its own isolated context window and system prompt so it cannot contaminate the primary thread, which is why Claude Code-style subagent isolation and the new GPT-5.6 sub-agents are converging on the same architecture. If your AI outputs are inconsistent, the fix is almost never a cleverer prompt. It is deciding, deliberately, what each agent is allowed to see.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Zoom / Common Room: Zoom is acquiring Common Room, the AI-native go-to-market intelligence platform used by Anthropic, Notion, and Snowflake, folding person-level buyer signals into Zoom's AI revenue platform. Terms undisclosed, close expected in weeks. Signal: The buyer-intelligence layer is being absorbed into communication and workflow platforms. A video vendor now wants to identify your buyers before the first call. Standalone GTM data tools are becoming features inside larger revenue suites, not a category you buy separately.
  • Databricks / CustomerLake: Databricks entered martech with CustomerLake, an agentic CDP announced at its Data + AI Summit. The pitch is unified customer data plus AI decisioning plus autonomous execution, built on the lakehouse rather than as a bolt-on. Signal: The data-infrastructure vendors are moving up the stack into activation, collapsing the line between warehouse and CDP. If your customer data already lives in Databricks or Snowflake, the CDP increasingly comes free with the platform you already own.
  • ActiveCampaign / Google Ads: ActiveCampaign shipped a Google Ads connector for its Active Intelligence engine, letting operators launch Performance Max campaigns directly from their marketing automation data. Signal: Mid-market automation platforms are quietly becoming ad-buying interfaces, pulling paid media activation into the same tool that holds the audience.

#Category watch

Agentic CDPs: The category is reorganizing around a clear generational frame. CDP 1.0 unified profiles, CDP 2.0 went composable, and the current wave (Databricks CustomerLake, BlueConic's June acquisition of Blueshift, Hightouch at a 2.75B valuation) adds AI agents that decide, create, and execute against the data, not just store it. For anyone building a martech org, the buy question is shifting from "where does the data live" to "who owns the decision loop." The risk is buying three overlapping agentic layers that each want to be the brain of your stack.

#Stack intelligence

The warehouse is eating the CDP. When Databricks and Snowflake ship native activation, the "composable CDP" argument (keep data in the warehouse, rent execution) stops being a philosophy and becomes the default architecture. The practical implication for stack-builders: stop treating the CDP as the center of gravity. The center is the data platform you already run, and the CDP is a decisioning and activation layer you can now swap or even skip. Buy for the activation and governance surface, not for storage you are already paying for twice.

#Foundations concept of the day

Data clean rooms: A clean room is a secure, neutral environment where two parties combine their data to find overlap without either side seeing the other's raw records. A retailer and a brand can learn "these 40,000 people bought your product and also watched this show" without the retailer handing over its customer list or the brand exposing its ad exposure logs. It works through matching on hashed identifiers and returning only aggregated, privacy-safe outputs, so no individual is exposed. Why it matters at scale: as third-party cookies fade and privacy law tightens, clean rooms are how first-party data gets activated across companies that legally cannot share it directly. Cross-industry example: Walmart, Amazon, and Disney all run clean rooms so consumer brands can measure and target against retail and viewing data, and under GDPR and the EU Data Act this is the compliant path because consent given to a retailer does not transfer to a media platform. The catch is fragmentation. Every walled garden runs its own clean room with its own ID graph, so without a neutral translation layer you get many small matches instead of one scaled audience.

#Today's industry move

The martech/adtech software complex hit fresh multi-year lows on fears AI erodes seat-based licensing. Adobe sank to an eight-year low near $196, Salesforce to a three-year low around $154 (AI agents seen cannibalizing per-user seats), and The Trade Desk to a six-year low near $18, down roughly 74% over twelve months. AppLovin is the exception, up about 34% over the year on AI-native ad performance. Read-through for a travel brand: the vendors you license your customer data platform and campaign stack from are being repriced on the thesis that agentic AI collapses seat-based software. Own the data layer, rent execution.

#AI infrastructure watch

TSMC reported Q2 today: revenue up 36% YoY to about $40B, net profit up 77% to a record ~$22B, beating estimates by double digits. High-performance computing is now 66% of revenue. Management called AI demand "extremely robust" on the shift from generative to agentic AI, raised full-year revenue growth guidance above 40%, and lifted capex to $60 to $64B. CoWoS advanced-packaging remains the supply bottleneck, and TSMC has told NVIDIA, AMD, Apple and Qualcomm to expect 5 to 10% wafer price increases. The chip layer is booming even as memory stocks crack, a widening split in where AI hardware demand actually holds.

#Signal or noise?

Signal: the software-vs-silicon divergence (adtech at lows, TSMC at records) is a structural repricing of the AI stack, not a one-day blip.

#Watch tomorrow

Whether the Thursday semiconductor futures dip deepens into a broader tech pullback, or TSMC's blowout guidance re-anchors the AI-hardware trade and lifts NVIDIA and AMD.
