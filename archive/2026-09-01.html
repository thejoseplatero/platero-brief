Executive Pulse1,115 words

#Macro signal

The energy shock has become a demand problem, and the Fed no longer has a clean move: WTI is at $86.57, up 32% year over year, driven by capacity that has been removed rather than sentiment that can reverse. Global observed oil inventories fell 69 million barrels in July on Gulf and Caspian export disruption, with renewed risk premium around the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb. That has now transmitted into the consumer. The Conference Board Consumer Confidence Index fell to 89.4 in August from 90.2, with energy costs cited heavily, and the Expectations Index dropped 5.8 points to 68.2. The Conference Board's own threshold is that a reading below 80 is generally associated with a recession within the year. The University of Michigan measure fell to 51.7 from 55.2, against a record low of 44.8 set in May.

The strategic point is not the direction of any one indicator, it is the shape of the box. Energy-driven inflation is a supply shock, and rate policy has no instrument for supply. Markets are split roughly 48% cut against 49% hold going into the September 15 to 16 meeting, and at least one major bank has moved to expecting a hike. When the central bank's next move is genuinely a coin flip two weeks out, the planning implication for leaders is to stop building a single 2027 plan around a rate assumption and start identifying which line items are sensitive to input cost versus consumer demand, because this environment can hit both at once. Meanwhile equities finished August up, with the S&P 500 gaining 2.6% and the Nasdaq 3.9%, their first monthly gains since May. Asset prices and household expectations are pointed in opposite directions, and that gap is where forecasting errors live.

#Tech industry

  • Google put commerce inside the answer, and declined the commission: AI Mode now tracks flight prices across more than 300 airlines in over 180 countries, books hotels end to end with Google Pay, and prices flights and stays in points and miles. It takes no cut of the transaction. Strategic implication: the monetizable event has moved one step earlier, from conversion to consideration. Google is selling placement in the shortlist an agent produces, which is a lighter, higher-margin position than being the merchant. Any executive whose business depends on a customer arriving at a branded surface should be asking who now controls the step before that arrival, and what it will cost to be present there. The answer will not be found in a marketing review, because it is a distribution question.
  • The application layer is agreeing to be called rather than opened: Salesforce made Claude an available reasoning model across its agent stack and simultaneously made Salesforce a plugin inside Claude with 37 prebuilt skills, so users can act on live CRM data without opening the product. IBM has embedded OpenAI's frontier models into IBM Consulting Advantage and is certifying tens of thousands of consultants. Strategic implication: incumbents are choosing data gravity and governance over interface ownership. That is probably correct, and it has a pricing consequence nobody has resolved. Seat-based revenue assumes people open the software. If the work moves into a model surface, the vendor keeps the system of record and loses the meter. Anyone with a renewal cycle inside eighteen months should model what their own vendors' repricing looks like before those vendors present it.

#AI at the leadership level

Governance structure is running ahead of governance capability. An IBM study found 76% of surveyed organizations now have a Chief AI Officer, up from 26% a year earlier. Deloitte's CDAO research shows 94% of data and analytics leaders expect their influence to grow over the next twelve months and 78% say AI has already increased their power as decision makers. The role is real and it is accumulating authority quickly.

The risk in that speed is specific. A newly created executive role with expanding scope and no established review discipline tends to acquire decision rights faster than it acquires the evidence base to exercise them. The useful board-level question is not whether the organization has an AI leader, it is whether that leader's decisions are being scored. If the AI function has authority over spend, vendor selection and workforce design but no record of which of its prior calls turned out right, the org has created accountability on the org chart and nowhere else.

#Leadership & org design

The layer absorbing the cost of AI adoption is middle management, and most operating models have not acknowledged it. Gartner projects that 20% of organizations will use AI to eliminate more than half of current middle-management roles by the end of 2026, while the managers who remain are being handed a materially different job: validating agent outputs, catching errors that arrive at machine speed, coaching teams through new tools, and deciding when to trust an agent and when to override it. HBR's reporting on the same population is blunt about the arithmetic. Those responsibilities were added, delivery targets were not reduced, and no formal support structure was created.

The practical failure this produces is quiet and predictable. Overloaded managers rationally stop doing the reviewing, because reviewing is the only part of the job with no deadline attached. That is the mechanism by which an organization ends up with unverified agent output flowing into decisions while every dashboard still shows adoption climbing. The design fix is unglamorous: make verification an explicitly resourced activity with named owners and time allocated, rather than an assumed behaviour layered on top of a full workload.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If a customer's decision is now being made inside somebody else's model, before they ever reach our surface, what do we actually own in that moment, and what are we currently paying for that we no longer need?

Practitioner Pulse1,165 words

#Try this now

  • Run an AI-answer presence check on your own category before your competitor does: Google's AI Mode now handles flight price tracking across more than 300 travel sites in over 180 countries, completes hotel bookings end to end with Google Pay, and prices inventory in points and miles. Travel is first because travel has structured inventory and live pricing, and every category with a feed is next. The exercise takes an afternoon and does not require a vendor. Write down the ten questions a customer actually asks before buying from you, phrased the way a person talks rather than the way a keyword tool renders it. Run each one in an AI answer surface. For each result record three things: whether your brand appears at all, whether the specific facts about you are correct, and which intermediary is standing between the answer and your transaction. The output is a two-column list, one column of places you are absent and one of places you are present but misdescribed. Those are different problems with different owners. Absence is a data and feed problem for whoever controls your structured inventory. Being present and wrong is a content problem, and it is the one that is genuinely urgent, because a confidently wrong answer at the consideration step is worse than no answer.
  • Give a browser agent one real workflow with a scoped account and time it against a person: Anthropic made Claude in Chrome generally available on paid plans on August 26, and the meaningful change is that it can now take actions autonomously rather than requesting approval per step, with a safety classifier checking each action against the original instruction. The useful test is not a demo task, it is a real one that a person on your team currently does badly. Good candidates share a shape: high volume, low judgment, spread across several web tools, and nobody's favourite part of the job. Competitive rate checks across a set of sites. Pulling campaign numbers out of four platforms that will never have a shared API. Auditing a hundred landing pages for a compliance string. Set it up properly, which means a dedicated browser profile and an account scoped to exactly the systems the task needs, not somebody's admin session. Then measure two numbers, the time it takes and the error rate against a human-checked sample. Most teams argue about agent capability in the abstract for months and could have had both numbers by Thursday.
  • Apply the write, select, compress, isolate taxonomy to the agent that is currently unreliable: Context engineering has largely displaced prompt engineering as the operative skill, and LangChain's four-way split is the most usable framing of it because it turns a vague quality complaint into four separate diagnosable failures. Write is what you author, the instructions and the standing context. Select is what gets retrieved into the window for this specific call. Compress is what you do to keep the window from filling with waste. Isolate is keeping unrelated context in separate scopes or sub-agents so it cannot contaminate. Take one agent that people describe as flaky and ask which of the four is broken. Almost nobody's answer is write, which is where almost everyone's effort goes. It is usually select, meaning the agent is retrieving the wrong things, or isolate, meaning three concerns are sharing one context window and bleeding into each other. Both have engineering fixes. Neither is fixed by rewriting the prompt, which is what the team has probably been doing.

#Case study worth stealing

Google, launching an agentic booking product with the intermediaries it was supposed to disrupt: For eighteen months the hospitality industry has narrated agentic AI as a disintermediation story, where the traveler asks an agent, the agent books direct, and the OTA that has taxed hotel distribution at 15 to 25 percent finally gets cut out. Google shipped the product on August 27 and the founding partner list includes Booking.com, Expedia and Priceline alongside Marriott, Hilton, IHG, Choice, Wyndham, Hotels.com and Trip.com. Google also takes no commission on the booking and accepts none of the merchant obligations, no payments risk, no fraud, no refunds, no customer service.

The tactic worth stealing is the sequencing, and it generalizes well outside travel. When you enter a market that has powerful incumbent intermediaries, the expensive path is to fight them for the transaction, because the transaction is where all the operational cost and regulatory exposure sits. The cheaper path is to take the step immediately upstream of it, the one where the customer's choice actually narrows, and let the incumbents keep fulfilling. They will help you build it, because from their position it looks like a new distribution channel rather than a new landlord. Any team currently writing a strategy that begins with going direct should first ask whether there is an upstream step they could own instead, one where they capture the decision without inheriting the cost of the fulfilment. The answer is often yes, and it is usually a step nobody has priced yet because it was never separately purchasable before.

#Framework or mental model

Placement, not commission: monetize the decision, not the transaction: A commission ties revenue to a transaction. A placement fee ties revenue to the decision, which sits one step earlier and is both cheaper to serve and harder to disintermediate. This is the clearest available explanation of why Google built a hotel checkout it takes no cut of. The value is not in the booking, it is in the narrowing, because when an agent reduces a thousand options to three, the narrowing is the entire game and everyone holding inventory has to bid to survive it.

The reason this matters beyond travel is that it inverts a default assumption in most digital strategy. Teams optimize conversion because conversion is the step they can measure and control, and they treat the consideration set as an input they inherit. In an agent-mediated market the consideration set is the product, and it is being auctioned. The practical application for digital leaders is to look at your funnel and ask which step you are actually paid for. If your margin depends on being chosen, and choosing now happens on a surface you do not own, then your conversion rate optimization program is a well-instrumented effort applied downstream of where the decision was made. The budget conversation that follows is uncomfortable and worth having early, because the cost of placement in these surfaces is currently low and will not stay that way.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Google, agentic hotel booking and flight tracking inside AI Mode: Rolling out from August 27. Users can track airfares conversationally across more than 300 airlines and travel sites, with price tracking live in over 180 countries. Hotel booking is rolling out in the US in English, where a user can compare rooms, read cancellation terms and pay with Google Pay without leaving the AI surface. Launch partners are Booking.com, Choice, Expedia, Hilton, Hotels.com, IHG, Marriott, Priceline, Trip.com and Wyndham. AI Mode can also now price flights and stays in points and miles, with Alaska and Hawaiian, American, Choice, Hilton and Wyndham at launch. Signal: read the partner list before reading the press release. Two of the largest OTAs on earth are founding partners in what the industry has spent eighteen months describing as an OTA disintermediation event. Nobody is being bypassed. Google is not taking a cut of the booking, which means it is not competing with the OTAs on transaction economics at all. It is monetizing the step before the transaction, the narrowing of a thousand options to three, and selling placement inside that narrowing to everyone who holds inventory. The supply side does not consolidate, it gets a new auction stacked on top of it. Anyone whose brand shows up in an AI answer today is going to be paying for that position within a year.
  • Salesforce and Anthropic, Claudeforce: Announced August 26, open beta expected this month. Claude becomes an available reasoning model across the Atlas Reasoning Engine, Agentforce Vibes, Agentforce Coworker and Agent Builder. In the other direction Salesforce becomes a plugin inside Claude, shipping 37 prebuilt sales skills that query and act on live CRM data without opening Salesforce. This is the first time Salesforce has attached its "force" suffix to another company's product. Signal: the system of record is volunteering to become a tool call. The strategic bet is that the durable asset was never the interface, it was the data model, the business logic, the permissions and the audit trail, and those survive being invoked from somewhere else. It is a defensible bet and it is also a concession, because seat-based pricing assumes people open the application. Every enterprise martech vendor now faces the same fork: expose your logic as a skill surface inside somebody else's agent and keep the data, or defend the UI and watch usage migrate. The ones who wait for the answer to become obvious will negotiate the integration from a worse position.
  • Perion, acquiring PRN for up to $12 million: Announced August 26, moving Perion into in-store retail media inside a US retail media market it sizes above $70 billion. Signal: small deal, clear direction. The contested ground in retail media has moved from the retailer website to the physical aisle, because online retail media inventory is close to saturated and in-store screens are the largest unmonetized surface with a verifiable audience standing in front of them. Expect the measurement fight to follow, since in-store has no equivalent of a session and the industry has not agreed what an impression is when the screen is on a shelf.

#Category watch

Agentic commerce is settling into a placement market, not a transaction market: The assumption underneath most agentic commerce strategy decks is that whoever owns the agent will take a cut of the sale. Google's travel launch says otherwise. By declining merchant responsibility, payments, fraud, refunds and service, Google holds the lightest position in travel distribution and still captures value, because it charges for consideration rather than conversion. That reframes what brands should be optimizing. Conversion rate optimization assumes the customer is already on your surface. In a placement market the decisive event happens before that, inside a model's shortlist, and the levers are structured data quality, feed freshness, API-accessible inventory and whatever a model uses to decide who makes the final three. The vendors positioning here today are the feed and product information companies, the loyalty and rate distribution layers, and a new set of visibility tools claiming to measure presence in AI answers. Most of that measurement is unverifiable right now. The underlying shift is not.

#Stack intelligence

The new leakage layer is agent to agent, and no existing control sits there. As marketing stacks add autonomous agents, data stops moving through the two paths enterprises actually monitor, user to system and system to system, and starts moving through three unmonitored ones: agent to model, agent to tool, and agent to agent. An agent that reads customer records from the CDP and passes them to an external analytics API through a second tool is not an abuse case, it is the pattern the frameworks encourage. The speed is the part that breaks existing controls, since agents run thousands of tool invocations a minute with nobody reviewing what moved where, and data loss prevention tooling built for human-paced egress does not see it.

The architectural consequence is that enforcement has to move to the communication layer rather than the endpoint. Practically, that means a stack needs a gateway that all agent traffic transits, per-agent identity rather than shared service credentials, and a log that records not just which system was called but what payload crossed the boundary. Very few marketing stacks have any of the three. The uncomfortable version for anyone running an approvals process: your privacy review evaluates a data flow diagram drawn at design time, and agents compose new flows at runtime, so the diagram is describing a system that no longer exists by the time it is approved.

#Foundations concept of the day

Walled gardens: A walled garden is a platform that holds a large logged-in audience, sells advertising against it, and measures the results itself, without letting the data or the measurement leave. Google, Meta, Amazon and increasingly Apple, TikTok and the large retail media networks all operate this way. The mechanics are simple and the consequences are not. Because the audience is authenticated, the platform has stable identity without cookies, so it kept working when third-party tracking degraded. Because it owns the measurement, an advertiser cannot independently verify what they were charged for. And because reporting stops at the wall, a brand running across five gardens has five separate reports, each counting the same person as a different reach, each claiming credit for the same conversion. There is no valid way to sum them, which is why sophisticated advertisers stopped trying and moved to marketing mix modelling and incrementality tests, methods that treat each garden as an opaque input and measure the lift from spending in it rather than believing the report that comes out of it.

The reason to understand this today is that a new garden is being built in front of everyone. An AI answer surface has the same three properties: an authenticated audience, an auction the platform runs, and measurement the platform defines. A cross-industry example makes the cost concrete. A retailer spending across Amazon Ads, Meta and its own site sees Amazon claim a sale, Meta claim the same sale, and the site analytics claim it too, so the reported return across channels can exceed total revenue. Nothing is being falsified. Each garden is honestly reporting what it saw inside its own walls, and the walls are the product.

#Watch list

  • Whether hotel and airline loyalty programs get an API for agents, or just a placement fee: Points and miles pricing inside AI Mode launched with five partners. The interesting question is what those partners had to expose to be there, and whether the redemption path stays proprietary or becomes another integration. A loyalty currency that an agent can price but not transact is a display asset. One an agent can spend is distribution.
  • Whether an agent gateway becomes a named layer in martech architecture diagrams: Right now agent traffic control is a security team concern discussed after vendor selection. Watch for it appearing as its own box in reference architectures alongside the CDP and the activation layer. That is the tell that the category is real, and it will happen faster than the equivalent shift did for consent management.

#Macro pulse

Canada: The TSX closed August 31 at 36,180, down 1.02% on the session, the sharpest single-day drop among the majors and a reminder that a resource-heavy index does not automatically win on an oil rally when the same rally is squeezing the consumer. USD/CAD sits at 1.3865, down 0.28% on the day, so the loonie strengthened slightly. Directionally helpful for a Canadian carrier buying fuel in US dollars, immaterial at that size, and swamped by the crude move underneath it.

USA: The S&P 500 slipped 0.33% to 7,686.14 on August 31, the Nasdaq fell 0.12% to 26,370.89, and the Dow dropped 374.09 points, or 0.7%, to 53,185.90. The more useful number is the month rather than the day. All three finished August higher, with the S&P up 2.6% and the Nasdaq up 3.9%, their first monthly gains since May. Equity markets spent August pricing recovery while the consumer data went the other way.

Asia: Hang Seng fell 0.95% to 25,324 on September 1 and is down 2.64% over the past month. Shanghai Composite was effectively flat, up 0.03% to 3,987.56. Nikkei 225 traded near 66,000 with little daily movement. The split between Hong Kong and mainland China has persisted long enough now to stop reading as noise. China exposure is being priced by market of listing rather than by country of operation.

Oil: WTI is at $86.57, up 0.94% on the day, up 7.75% over the past month and up 31.98% year over year. Brent is at $91.28, up 0.87%. The year over year number is the one that matters for anyone modelling 2027. This is not a spike sitting on top of a normal base, it is a repriced base. The drivers are structural rather than sentiment led: global observed inventories fell 69 million barrels in July on Gulf and Caspian export disruption, and risk premium has returned around the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb transit routes. Supply that has been removed by capacity loss does not come back on a headline.

#Today's industry move

Salesforce and Anthropic move Claudeforce toward open beta this month, after announcing the partnership on August 26. The structure runs in both directions. Claude becomes an available reasoning model across the Atlas Reasoning Engine, Agentforce Vibes, Agentforce Coworker and Agent Builder, and Salesforce becomes a plugin inside Claude with 37 prebuilt sales skills that let a user query and act on live CRM data without opening Salesforce. Salesforce shares rose 23% on the earnings print that carried the announcement. The market did not reward a new feature. It rewarded a distribution answer to the question that has been sitting on every application-layer software company since the model vendors started shipping agents, which is what happens to seat-based revenue when the model becomes the place work starts.

#AI infrastructure watch

Nvidia is underwriting demand rather than just supplying it. The company announced financing platforms with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR intended to mobilize more than $500 billion of third-party capital into AI infrastructure, and separately agreed to guarantee up to $105 billion in conditional lease and power payment obligations to SB Energy for an Ohio data center serving OpenAI. That project envisions 9.2 gigawatts of new gas-fired generation, with SB Energy and SoftBank targeting at least 10 GW of new generation overall. Context: Nvidia reported Q2 FY2027 revenue of $96.2 billion on August 26, up 18% sequentially.

The financing structure solves the capital cost of GPUs. It does not solve grid interconnects, transformers, turbines or permitting, and those are the constraints with multi-year lead times that money does not compress. On the silicon side, AMD introduced Helios, a rack-scale system pairing sixth-generation Epyc 9006 CPUs with Instinct MI455X GPUs, competing explicitly on tokens per dollar rather than raw performance. When a challenger picks unit economics as the axis, it is a sign the buyer has started asking about cost per unit of output rather than capability.

#Signal or noise?

Signal, and the two signals are connected. A vendor guaranteeing over $100 billion of a customer's power obligations and an oil market up 32% year over year are the same story told from opposite ends: energy has become the binding input on both the AI buildout and the travel cost structure, and neither constraint resolves inside a planning cycle.

#Watch tomorrow

The Fed's September 15 to 16 meeting, and specifically how fast the market repositions into it. Odds are currently split roughly 48% cut against 49% hold, while at least one major bank has moved to expecting a 25 basis point hike on energy-driven inflation. A market that cannot decide between a cut and a hike two weeks out is not pricing a policy path, it is pricing a coin flip, and that shows up as volatility in exactly the rate-sensitive consumer sectors that carry discretionary travel demand.
