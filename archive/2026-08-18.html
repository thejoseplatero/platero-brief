#Macro signal

Energy has become the transmission channel for inflation again, and it is running on a geopolitical variable: Crude extended its advance Tuesday as hopes for a US and Iran agreement faded and Washington ruled out extending the temporary ceasefire, with WTI at roughly $85 and Brent above $91, both near six week highs. The second-order effect landed a day earlier in Canada, where July CPI came in at 3.0 percent year over year against a 2.9 percent consensus, up from 2.8 percent in June, with gasoline up 25.7 percent year over year doing most of the work. Core measures were far calmer, CPI-median at 2.0 percent and CPI-trim at 1.9 percent. That gap is the strategic point. Underlying inflation is close to target while headline inflation is being set by a conflict nobody in the building can forecast. For leaders this argues against treating the current print as a demand signal at all. It means input cost plans, fuel and freight hedging, and any pricing decision indexed to headline CPI now carry embedded geopolitical risk that should be modelled as a scenario range rather than a point estimate. It also means central banks are more likely to look through the headline than to react to it, so do not read a hot print as a rate signal.

#Tech industry

  • Nvidia guarantees up to $105 billion in lease and power obligations for OpenAI's Ohio data center (August 17): OpenAI signed a ten year lease on a facility at the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus in Pike County, Ohio, that will eventually deliver 8 gigawatts of compute and require at least 10 gigawatts of new generation. SoftBank's SB Energy builds and owns it, Nvidia supplies the chips and backstops the obligations, and Nvidia is separately putting $1.5 billion into SB Energy. SB Energy and SoftBank will invest at least $4.2 billion into regional grid infrastructure. Capacity phases in from 2028. Strategic implication: this is vendor financing at a scale that changes what a moat is. Competitors have narrowed Nvidia's technical lead, so Nvidia is deploying the asset they cannot match, which is balance sheet. Guaranteeing a customer's power and lease obligations converts a chip sale into a decade-long structural lock. The pattern generalizes beyond semiconductors: in any category where the product advantage is compressing, the incumbent's next move is to absorb the customer's financing risk. Worth asking whether a key supplier in your own stack is starting to sell you capital rather than capability, because the switching cost profile is completely different.
  • Google donates the Agent2Agent protocol to the Agentic AI Foundation (August 18): The interoperability layer that lets agents from different vendors discover each other and delegate work moved to neutral governance. Strategic implication: the agent interop standard is being settled early rather than fought over for five years, which pulls forward the date at which "can your systems be delegated to by an agent we control" becomes a procurement requirement rather than an architecture preference.

#AI at the leadership level

Two large studies now point at the same structural change from different angles. Mercer's Global Talent Trends 2026 finds 98 percent of executives planning organizational design changes in the next two years and 99 percent expecting AI to produce at least some workforce reduction, while employee thriving fell from 66 percent in 2024 to 44 percent in 2026. IBM's CEO study of 2,000 chief executives finds 77 percent saying talent and technology leadership roles are converging, with 29 percent of employees expected to need reskilling into a different role and 53 percent needing upskilling in their current one between 2026 and 2028. Read together, the near-unanimity on redesign combined with a 22 point collapse in thriving says the redesign is being announced faster than it is being absorbed. The governance question this raises for a board is not whether AI reduces headcount. It is who is accountable for the transition capacity, because a plan that assumes 29 percent of people change roles inside two years is a workforce logistics program, and almost nobody has staffed one. The convergence finding is the actionable half: if talent and technology leadership are merging, the org chart that separates them is generating the coordination cost, and that is fixable this quarter.

#Leadership & org design

Synchrony's OpenAI announcement on August 17 is more useful as an org design case than as a product one. The external artifact is a ChatGPT plugin exposing financing and store card offers from partners including Amazon, Walmart, and Lowe's, with the transaction completing inside the assistant. The precondition is that essentially the entire professional workforce has been using AI tools since 2024, with job-relevant fluency training run as a deliberate program, and staff now moving onto ChatGPT Enterprise and ChatGPT Work. The sequencing is the lesson. Two years of internal adoption depth is what made an externally facing agentic product buildable, staffable, and defensible in front of a regulator. Organizations running the reverse order, external AI product first and internal fluency later, end up with a launch nobody inside can operate, extend, or explain. The diagnostic is simple and uncomfortable: count how many people in your org could specify, review, and sign off on an agentic customer-facing workflow without external help. That number, not the roadmap, sets what you can actually ship.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If a customer could discover, finance, and complete a purchase entirely inside an assistant we do not own, which parts of our margin still depend on them landing on our property first, and what is our plan for those specifically?

#Try this now

  • Similarweb AI Ads (launched August 17): An ad intelligence dataset covering paid placements inside ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, and Google AI Overviews, three surfaces that carry advertising with no public ad library behind them. It is built from real user panel conversations rather than synthetic prompts, which is the methodological difference that matters. The baseline numbers it publishes: 26 percent of ChatGPT responses already carry a sponsored ad on the Free and Go tiers, click-through sits at 0.50 percent, 66.3 percent of ChatGPT ads appear after the second prompt or later, nearly 30 percent of ad-eligible Google AI Mode queries show ads, and over 40 percent of Google searches now trigger an AI Overview. Worth 20 minutes because a competitive scan tells you whether your category's rivals are already buying there, and the answer changes a 2027 budget conversation from speculative to comparative. Read it with one caveat: panel size, geography, and device mix are undisclosed, so treat directional share as directional.
  • IAB AI Transparency and Disclosure Framework, Version 2 (published August 18): The first version, from January, leaned toward disclosing AI involvement broadly. Version 2 replaces that with a materiality test. Disclose only when AI materially affects authenticity, identity, or representation in a way that could mislead. That puts synthetic voices and avatars, photoreal generated imagery and video, digital twins of real people in fabricated situations, and chatbots mistakable for humans inside the line. It puts post-production, internal workflows, copy generation, generic synthetic voice, and stylized avatars outside it. Use it this week as a classification pass over your live creative library, because the enforcement dates are already behind you: New York's synthetic performer law took effect in June, California SB 942 and Article 50 of the EU AI Act both on August 2.
  • Cloudflare Kitesurf browser runtime: A browser runtime purpose-built for agents rather than humans, claiming three to seven times less CPU and memory than Chromium. If any workflow in your org runs headless Chromium at volume for scraping, QA, or agent-driven web tasks, the unit economics change materially at that ratio. This is the kind of substitution a platform team can pilot on one job in an afternoon and either prove or kill by the numbers.

#Case study worth stealing

Synchrony, putting the financing offer inside someone else's checkout: On August 17 Synchrony announced an enterprise collaboration with OpenAI that does two distinct things at once. Outward, a ChatGPT plugin surfaces promotional financing, deals, and store card offers from partner brands including Amazon, Walmart, and Lowe's, so a shopper can complete the purchase in the assistant without being redirected to a Synchrony property. Inward, Synchrony is deploying current OpenAI models across enterprise operations, with essentially all professional staff already using AI tools since 2024 and now moving onto ChatGPT Enterprise and ChatGPT Work. The stealable part is the sequencing. Two years of internal fluency came first, which is what made an external agentic commerce product a shippable thing rather than a press release. The second stealable part is the strategic concession: they gave up owning the destination in order to keep owning the offer. Any loyalty, credit, or ancillary business should run that trade explicitly, because the alternative is defending traffic to a page the customer no longer needs to visit.

#Framework or mental model

The four-mechanism cost ladder: Greg Kihlstrom's piece in MarTech on August 18 separates AI implementations into four mechanisms ordered by cost and capability. Rule-based is if-this-then-that logic, near-zero operating cost, predictable, brittle outside anticipated cases. Predictive scores inputs against historical patterns, cheap per call, weak outside training data. Generative produces new text or images from open-ended input, higher per call, prone to fluent hallucination. Agentic wraps a generative model in a loop with tool access and planning, and is the most expensive by a wide margin because context gets reprocessed every iteration. The number that makes the point: a customer service interaction that cost roughly four cents in 2023 as simple chat costs roughly $1.20 in 2026 as an agentic workflow, about 30 times more for the same job. The discipline is to pick the lightest mechanism that actually meets the need, ask every vendor which mechanism sits under the demo, and price pilots at production volume before signing. Marketing is where this compounds fastest, because 200,000 product descriptions refreshed quarterly or 400,000 tickets a year turn a fraction of a cent into a budget line.

#Rabbit hole

The UAE announced a national program on August 18 to move 50 percent of federal services to agentic AI within two years, with more than 50 federal entities enrolled and agents running procurement, tax auditing, customer service, and technical support. Set aside whether the target lands. This is the largest single natural experiment anyone has run on agentic systems in a bureaucracy, and bureaucracies are where the interesting failure modes live: accountability for an agent's decision, appeal rights, audit trails, and what happens when a policy changes faster than the agent's tooling. Every enterprise about to put agents into procurement or claims will face the same questions at a smaller scale and with less public reporting. Worth an hour watching what governance artifacts they publish, because those will be the first real templates.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • IAB publishes Version 2 of the AI Transparency and Disclosure Framework (August 18): Version 1, from January, pointed toward broad labeling. Version 2 replaces that with a risk-based materiality test, requiring disclosure only where AI materially affects authenticity, identity, or representation. Synthetic voices, photoreal generated media, digital twins of real people, and human-mistakable chatbots are in scope. Post-production, internal workflow, copy generation, and stylized avatars are out. Signal: the industry chose classification over tagging, and that is a bigger operational change than it sounds. Blanket labeling is a pipeline feature someone ships once. Materiality is a per-asset judgment that needs an owner, a definition, and an audit trail, in a compliance environment where New York, California SB 942, and EU AI Act Article 50 are all already in force. The stack implication is that creative asset management systems now need a provenance field, not a checkbox, and most do not have one.
  • Similarweb launches AI Ads (August 17): An ad intelligence dataset covering paid placements in ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, and Google AI Overviews, built from real user panel conversations rather than synthetic prompts, with an AI Ads Gallery of live creative. Published baselines: 26 percent of ChatGPT responses carry a sponsored ad on Free and Go tiers, 0.50 percent click-through, 66.3 percent of ChatGPT ads landing after the second prompt or later, nearly 30 percent of ad-eligible Google AI Mode queries showing ads. Signal: measurement is arriving on assistant inventory ahead of the ad libraries and transparency reporting that every prior channel got first. That inverts the usual sequence, where a platform publishes an ad library and third parties build intelligence on top. Here a third party is building the library the platforms did not, which means the reference numbers for this channel will be set by a vendor with a panel rather than by the sellers. Whoever the buy side accepts as the source of truth in the next two quarters gets to define the denominator for share of voice in assistants.
  • Synchrony and OpenAI collaboration, plus Omneky self-service ChatGPT advertising (August 17): Synchrony is shipping a ChatGPT plugin exposing promotional financing and store card offers from partners including Amazon, Walmart, and Lowe's, with checkout completed inside the assistant. Omneky added self-service ChatGPT ad buying alongside Meta, Google, LinkedIn, TikTok, and Reddit, with auto-generated creative and cross-channel reporting. Signal: two different layers of the same channel maturing in the same week. Financing and payment credentials moving into the assistant is the part that makes assistant commerce a transaction surface rather than a discovery surface, and self-service buying is what turns a channel from a partnership into a media line anyone can access without a sales call. A channel usually needs both before it shows up in a planning template.

#Category watch

Agentic commerce, specifically the payments and financing layer: The discovery layer of assistant commerce arrived first and got most of the attention, product carousels, citations, referral traffic. The layer being built now is the one that determines whether the transaction completes without leaving the assistant, and it is being built by financial institutions rather than by martech vendors. Synchrony's move puts promotional financing and store card credentials inside ChatGPT. That is a different competitive position than an ad placement, because financing terms are a reason for the assistant to surface one merchant over another, and they are not something a retailer can replicate on its own. For anyone building a martech org, this reframes what "owning the customer relationship" means when the relationship is mediated. The brand that supplies the offer keeps its margin and its data even when it loses the destination. The brand that only supplied the destination loses both. Every loyalty program, co-brand card, and ancillary business is holding an asset that is more portable into an assistant than its website ever was, and most are still budgeting as though the website is the thing to defend.

#Stack intelligence

Google moved the Agent2Agent protocol to the Agentic AI Foundation as a hosted project on August 18. A2A is the layer above Model Context Protocol: MCP connects an agent to tools and data, A2A lets agents discover each other, delegate, and coordinate across frameworks without bespoke integrations. Two things are worth reading from the donation. First, the interop layer for agents is being pushed to neutral governance early, before any single vendor's implementation became the de facto standard, which is the opposite of how identity, tag management, and server-side tracking each played out. Second, it means the integration question on a vendor renewal now has two parts, not one. Does this system expose its data over an open protocol, and can its agent be delegated to by an agent we own. A vendor can pass the first and fail the second, and the second is what determines whether their agent participates in your workflow or requires you to enter theirs. Same week, Salesforce continued embedding Agentforce and Atlassian shipped Robo, both moving from tools a person operates to systems where an agent does the work. Those are exactly the products where the delegation question decides whether you are composing a stack or entering someone's product.

#Foundations concept of the day

Content provenance and C2PA credentials: Provenance is the record of where a piece of media came from and what was done to it. C2PA, the Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity, is the cross-industry technical standard for attaching that record to the asset itself. Mechanically, a Content Credential is a cryptographically signed manifest embedded in or bound to the file, listing the capture device or generating model, the edits applied, and each signing party, so a downstream system can verify the chain rather than trust a label someone typed. It matters at scale for a reason that is operational rather than ethical. Disclosure regimes now in force, EU AI Act Article 50, California SB 942, New York's synthetic performer law, and the IAB's updated framework, all require an assertion about how an asset was made. If that assertion lives in a spreadsheet or a trafficking field, it breaks the first time an agency reformats a file, and the liability sits with whoever ran the ad. If it travels with the asset, verification is a lookup. A cross-industry example: a bank running a national campaign through three agencies and a stock library needs to answer, per asset, whether a synthetic voice was used, six months after the campaign ended and after the agency of record has changed. Provenance metadata answers that. A workflow checkbox does not. The practical readiness test for any martech org is whether your DAM preserves C2PA manifests through ingest, resize, and transcode, because most asset pipelines strip metadata on transform by default.

#Watch list

  • Who gets to define share of voice in assistants: Similarweb is establishing baselines for a channel where the sellers publish no ad library. Watch whether OpenAI or Google responds with first-party transparency reporting, or whether a measurement body standardizes the metric first. A vendor-defined denominator that goes unchallenged for two quarters becomes the industry number by default, and everyone's 2027 planning gets built on a panel nobody audited.
  • Simulation as a media planning primitive: Uniphore's Marketing AI, announced August 17, builds per-customer digital twins as fine-tuned small language models and simulates campaign outcomes before budget is committed. Acoustic shipped an agentic teammate on 20 years of behavioural data the same week. If pre-flight simulation gets credible enough to move budget, it competes with incrementality testing rather than with personalization tools, which is a different buyer and a much larger line item. The thing to watch is whether anyone publishes simulated versus actual results, because a category built on unvalidated forecasts is one bad quarter from being unsellable.

#Today's industry move

Higgsfield raised a $400 million Series B at a $5.4 billion valuation, announced August 17, led by DST Global with Tribe Capital, Goldman Sachs Alternatives growth equity, Smash Capital, Fifth Wall, Valor, Intel Capital, Liberty Global Tech Ventures, Mirae Asset Capital, and NTT DOCOMO Ventures participating. Annualized revenue reached $700 million this month, up from $20 million a year ago, with the platform now used in visual production by 390 of the Fortune 500 and running over 20 million content generations a month. Valuation has more than quadrupled from $1.3 billion at the Series A earlier this year, and users of its agentic products have grown 42-fold in three months since the May Supercomputer rollout. The signal is not the multiple, it is the revenue slope: a 35x annualized revenue increase in twelve months in creative production means the spend is coming out of existing production budgets at speed, not out of innovation pilots. Anyone with a content P&L should be able to say where that displacement is landing in their own numbers.

#AI infrastructure watch

The largest single infrastructure commitment of the cycle landed August 17. OpenAI signed a ten year lease on an 8 gigawatt compute campus at PORTS-Pike in Pike County, Ohio, built and owned by SoftBank's SB Energy under a 20 year lease structure, requiring at least 10 gigawatts of new power generation. Nvidia supplies the chips and is guaranteeing up to $105 billion of the lease and power obligations, plus a $1.5 billion direct investment into SB Energy. SB Energy and SoftBank commit at least $4.2 billion to regional grid infrastructure. Capacity phases in from 2028. This follows Nvidia's $3 billion Lancium investment tying compute directly to power. The through-line across the whole stack this month is that the binding constraint has moved off the die and onto the grid, and the chip vendor is now underwriting the power build to clear it.

#Signal or noise?

Signal, and a structural one. Nvidia guaranteeing a customer's lease and power obligations is a different competitive instrument than a better chip, and it appears precisely as AMD and Google have narrowed the technology gap. When the product lead compresses, the incumbent monetizes the balance sheet instead, which locks demand in for a decade rather than a product cycle.

#Watch tomorrow

The EIA weekly petroleum status report. Last week's 17.4 million barrel build was the largest since January 2023 and is the only thing currently offsetting Hormuz risk premium. A second consecutive large build caps crude near $85 and takes pressure off airline fuel plans. A draw against this geopolitical backdrop is what pushes WTI through the July high, and jet fuel crack spreads with it.
