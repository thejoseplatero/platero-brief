Executive Pulse591 words

#Macro signal

Weak June jobs report lands on top of an energy-driven inflation shock: The US added just 57,000 jobs in June, well below expectations, with unemployment at 4.2%. This lands while the Strait of Hormuz closure from the Iran war is still working through the system, jet fuel is up 106% y/y, diesel up 58%, and Brent has been trading near $120 before easing back toward $68 WTI as tensions cool. The combination is classic stagflation risk: soft labor demand plus a supply-side inflation shock the Fed can't fix by cutting rates. For leaders, this means input-cost and freight/travel-cost planning can't assume energy reverts to pre-conflict baselines even as headlines calm down, build scenario ranges, not point estimates, into H2 budgets. Canada is directly exposed via energy pass-through and the parallel US trade war, so this is not a US-only story.

#Tech industry

  • AI for Good Global Commission launched (July 2): Rwanda's President Kagame, Salesforce's Marc Benioff, and the ITU's Doreen Bogdan-Martin convened 40+ Founding Members, heads of state, industry CEOs, UN agency heads, to shape global AI access and trust norms. Strategic implication: AI governance is consolidating into cross-industry, cross-government coalitions faster than most corporate boards are organizing internally, expect pressure to show alignment with an external framework, not just an internal policy.
  • Anthropic shipped Claude Sonnet 5 as the new default (July 1) and the Commerce Department lifted export controls on Fable 5 and Mythos 5 the same day. Strategic implication: the frontier-model cadence and export posture are both accelerating simultaneously, vendor lock-in decisions made even six months ago should be revisited against a faster-moving capability and availability curve.

#AI at the leadership level

Chief AI Officer adoption has tripled in a year, 76% of organizations now have a CAIO office, up from 26% in 2025 (HSBC, Lloyds among recent adds). But authority hasn't followed structure: 72% of CEOs now say they personally need to own AI decisions (2x last year), yet only 28% actually take direct governance responsibility, and just 17% of boards do. The gap between "we created the role" and "someone is actually accountable" is the real exposure, a CAIO title without CEO/board-level ownership behind it is theater that won't hold up under an incident.

#Leadership & org design

Deloitte's 2026 Tech Trends data: 93% of AI-related budget is going to technology, only 7% to people, yet Deloitte's separate study of high-performing teams finds the differentiator is human capability (curiosity, resilience, connected teaming), not tool access. Agentic AI is also starting to invert the org pyramid, junior-does/senior-reviews is flipping toward AI-does-volume/humans-do-oversight, creating net-new roles like Agent Supervisor and Exception Handler that don't map to existing job families. Leaders who keep funding models 10:1 in favor of tech over people development are optimizing the wrong constraint.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If an AI agent made a bad customer-facing decision tomorrow, who on this team owns the accountability chain, not just the fix, but the "why did we let it get there" review? If you can't name the person in under five seconds, that's the gap to close before it's forced on you.

Worth 20 minutes if you've ever waited on a designer for a stakeholder deck or a quick concept mock.
  • Claude Code's /dataviz skill: New skill for chart and dashboard design guidance, feed it a metrics dump and it applies real visualization principles instead of default chart-library output. Useful for anyone who ships their own reporting decks without a data viz specialist on call.
  • ActiveCampaign's Active Intelligence 2.8: Now retains brand voice, colors, logos, and strategic priorities across AI-generated sessions instead of re-explaining brand guidelines every time. If your team fights AI-generated content drift, this pattern (persistent brand memory layered on generation) is worth replicating even if you're not on ActiveCampaign, check whether your own content tools support saved brand context.

#Case study worth stealing

Alaska Airlines, identity resolution turned into trigger-based revenue: Alaska partnered with Amperity to unify siloed reservation, loyalty, and app data into single customer profiles, then built a four-touch pre-trip messaging sequence (upgrades, meal choice, loyalty enrollment) triggered by route, aircraft, and cabin. Result: +198% loyalty sign-up conversion and a 61.1% lift in email open rates. When Alaska acquired Hawaiian Airlines in 2025, the same infrastructure (Amperity Bridge) merged both airlines' customer data in two days. The transferable move: identity resolution isn't a data-hygiene project, it's the prerequisite for any trigger-based personalization program, and it pays off again at M&A speed.

#Framework or mental model

Context engineering: The 2026 shift in how practitioners work with AI models, instead of hand-crafting a single clever prompt, you design the full context the model sees before it answers (retrieved docs, memory, prior decisions, structured data). Anthropic's own guidance frames this as the successor discipline to prompt engineering. Digital leaders should apply this at the team level: audit what context your AI tools actually have access to (knowledge bases, brand guidelines, past decisions) before blaming bad outputs on "the wrong prompt."

#Rabbit hole

The State of AI Design Report 2026 is worth an hour: 91% of design orgs now use AI weekly (up from 54% in 2025), Claude has overtaken ChatGPT as the default tool for design work, and coding tools have become a core part of the design process rather than a downstream handoff. If you're setting AI tooling policy for a design org, this is the primary source to cite instead of vibes.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Zeta Global x Palantir: Zeta Global is rearchitecting its Data Cloud on Palantir Foundry, a strategic partnership to fuse customer and operational intelligence onto shared AI infrastructure. Signal: CDP vendors are outsourcing the compute/AI layer to infrastructure specialists rather than building it in-house, a "buy the brain, keep the data" pattern that will show up in other martech stacks as AI features become table stakes rather than differentiators.
  • RAD Intel acquires majority stake in AVCommunications: RAD Intel (an AI marketing intelligence platform) took a controlling stake in AVC, a Canadian multicultural marketing agency with 20 years of reach across North America, Asia, and Europe. Signal: AI-native platforms are buying distribution and audience expertise instead of building it, which is the inverse of the usual "agency buys a tech layer" M&A pattern, a sign that AI intelligence is now considered the scarcer asset.
  • Adobe expands GenStudio at Cannes Lions: Adobe announced new technology and agency partnerships to extend GenStudio into end-to-end content creation, compliance review, and campaign analytics in one suite. Signal: Adobe is pushing further into owning the full content-to-measurement loop, tightening the walled-garden dynamic that Jose already tracks as a lock-in risk for Air Canada.

#Category watch

Retail media networks (RMNs): US retail media spend is forecast at ~$69 to 71B in 2026 (up from ~$59B in 2025) and on pace to hit $100B by 2028, already surpassing traditional TV ad spend. The real shift underway is retailers extending their first-party identity graph *offsite*, into CTV, programmatic, and paid social, rather than staying confined to on-site sponsored placements. Walmart's tie-up with Disney (Walmart audiences activated on Disney streaming, measured via clean room) is the template other verticals will copy: travel and hospitality loyalty programs are sitting on the same shape of asset and haven't monetized it nearly as aggressively yet.

#Stack intelligence

Clean rooms are shifting from a nice-to-have measurement add-on to expected deal infrastructure in retail media contracts, yet only ~48% of RMNs currently offer clean room capability, meaning most of the market is still promising privacy-safe collaboration it can't yet deliver. For anyone building a martech org now, the practical lesson is to treat clean-room readiness as a go/no-go gate when evaluating retail media or co-marketing partners, not an assumed baseline.

#Foundations concept of the day

Attribution models: Attribution is the method for assigning credit for a conversion (a sale, a booking, a sign-up) across the multiple touchpoints a customer had before converting. Last-touch gives 100% of the credit to the final click or ad before conversion, simple, but it systematically overvalues bottom-of-funnel channels like retargeting and undervalues awareness. Multi-touch attribution (MTA) splits credit across touchpoints (linear, time-decay, or data-driven/algorithmic weighting) to better reflect the real path. Media mix modeling (MMM) takes a step back and uses aggregate, privacy-safe statistical regression across all spend and outcomes over time, without needing to track individual users at all, which is why MMM is resurging as cookies and device IDs erode. Why it matters at scale: the attribution model you choose literally decides which channels look like they're "working," which shapes next year's budget. Cross-industry example: a retailer using last-touch attribution might defund upper-funnel CTV spend because it never shows the "last click", even though MMM would reveal it's driving the site visits that retargeting later converts. An airline evaluating loyalty vs. paid search vs. brand CTV needs the same discipline: the wrong attribution model can starve the channel that's actually building the identity graph everything else depends on.

#Watch list

  • Palantir Foundry as martech infrastructure: watch whether more CDP/CDP-adjacent vendors (beyond Zeta) start rearchitecting on Foundry rather than building proprietary AI/compute layers, a sign the CDP category is bifurcating into "data layer" vendors and "compute layer" vendors.
  • Clean room capability gap in retail media: with only ~48% of RMNs offering clean rooms today, the networks that close that gap first will have a real differentiation window before it becomes table stakes across the category.
