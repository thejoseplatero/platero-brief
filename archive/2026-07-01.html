Executive Pulse629 words

#Macro signal

New round of "forced labor" tariffs on 60 trading partners: USTR proposed 10% tariffs on 16 economies (Canada, Mexico, EU, Taiwan, UK) and 12.5% on 44 more (China, Japan, India, South Korea, Switzerland), this time justified via forced-labor enforcement probes rather than the reciprocal-tariff mechanism the Supreme Court struck down earlier this year. Public hearing July 7; levies could land within weeks after. This is the administration re-arming its tariff toolkit through a different legal channel after losing the first one, expect this pattern (new legal basis, same economic effect) to repeat. For leaders with cross-border supply chains or vendor contracts, the lesson isn't "wait for the ruling", it's that tariff exposure is now a standing operating risk to model into pricing and sourcing, not an event to react to.

#Tech industry

  • PayPal to cut ~20% of workforce (4,500+ jobs) over 2 to 3 years as part of an AI-driven turnaround and org simplification. Cisco is cutting ~5% (4,000 jobs) to reallocate spend toward silicon, optics, security, and AI. Neither is a distress signal, both are profitable companies pre-funding AI capacity by shrinking headcount elsewhere. Strategic implication: boards are now expecting AI investment to be self-funded through workforce reallocation, not treated as incremental budget ask.
  • The COO is overtaking the CIO/CTO/CMO as AI's key internal champion in many large enterprises this year. AI is being treated less as a technology rollout and more as an operating-model change, which explains why operations leaders, not technologists, are driving it.

#AI at the leadership level

BCG's 2026 survey of 625 CEOs and board members finds real misalignment on AI strategy, speed, and expected ROI, everyone agrees AI matters, few agree on how fast or how much to bet. McKinsey data backs this up structurally: only 28% of organizations say their CEO takes direct ownership of AI governance, and just 17% say the board does. Meanwhile the EU AI Act's documentation, risk-management, and human-oversight requirements for high-risk AI systems become mandatory in August 2026, six weeks out. Any org with EU-touching operations (including EU routes, EU customer data, or EU-based vendors) should already have an accountable owner and a paper trail, not a plan to build one.

#Leadership & org design

The organizations pulling ahead on AI aren't the ones deploying the most models, they're the ones that have redesigned decision rights, team structure, and accountability around AI-augmented workflows. Deploying tools into an unchanged org chart is the pattern separating this year's AI winners from the many companies quietly writing off failed pilots. Separately, a $500M+ retraining coalition (RAISE US, backed by former Commerce Secretary Raimondo) launched this week specifically to catch workers displaced by this wave of AI-driven cuts, a signal that "AI transition cost" is becoming a named line item companies are expected to underwrite, not an externality.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If a board member asked "who personally owns AI governance and ROI accountability for this function, not a committee, a name", could we answer in one sentence, and would our EU AI Act documentation survive an audit six weeks from now?

#Try this now

  • Claude Tag in Slack: Anthropic shipped Claude Tag this week, you can now add Claude as a member of a Slack channel, @-mention it like a teammate, and it holds persistent context across the thread instead of starting cold each time. Karpathy called it a third UX paradigm for LLMs (after "website you visit" and "app you download"): now it's a standing presence with org-wide tools and memory. It's in beta for Claude Enterprise/Team. Pilot it in one working channel this week, a project standup or content review thread, and watch whether persistent context actually reduces the "let me re-explain everything" tax that kills most AI-in-Slack experiments.
  • Context quarantine, pruning, and offloading: Simon Willison's three concrete techniques for managing long-running AI agent sessions, surfaced widely this week as the practical follow-through on "context engineering." Quarantine = isolate newly-ingested information (a new doc, a new tool output) so it doesn't contaminate the rest of the context. Pruning = actively strip irrelevant turns from a long conversation rather than letting them accumulate. Offloading = move information to external storage (a file, a scratchpad) instead of keeping it in the live context window. If you're running any multi-step AI workflow that degrades over a long session, these are the three levers to pull first, no new tooling required, just discipline in how you manage the conversation.

#Case study worth stealing

Iberia, booking inside ChatGPT, not just a chatbot on your own site: Iberia launched "Iberia GPT," a conversational travel app that lives inside ChatGPT rather than on iberia.com. Users start a prompt with "Iberia," get natural-language destination inspiration or precise date-driven search, see a visual calendar of price/availability, and route directly into Iberia's existing booking flow, no separate app, no new login flow to build from scratch. The portable insight: instead of building your own AI assistant and fighting for adoption, meet the customer where the 800-million-user platform already is, and treat that surface as a new booking channel with your existing backend behind it. Any digital team with a transactional flow (bookings, orders, reservations) should be asking whether a ChatGPT app is a faster distribution channel than another owned-channel redesign.

#Framework or mental model

The governance paradox: New Sinch research found 74% of enterprises have rolled back a live AI customer-communications agent after deployment, and the rollback rate is *higher* (81%) among organizations with the most mature AI guardrails. The counterintuitive read: a high rollback rate isn't proof AI agents are broken, it's proof your monitoring is working. Sinch's CPO put it well, "the most advanced organizations aren't failing less, they're seeing failures sooner." For any digital leader deploying customer-facing agents: don't benchmark success by rollback count. Benchmark by time-to-detection. If your team can't tell you how fast it would catch a bad agent output today, that's the gap to close before you scale anything further.

#Rabbit hole

Karpathy's original thread reframing LLM interaction paradigms (website → app → persistent teammate) is worth the full read if you're deciding how deep to integrate AI into team workflows this year, the argument is that each paradigm shift changed *where* trust and permissions had to be engineered, not just the interface. Karpathy on X.

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Zeta Global x Palantir: Zeta is rearchitecting its Data Cloud on Palantir Foundry, giving its Athena AI decisioning engine access to a much broader slice of enterprise data (operational, not just marketing) for real-time campaign decisions. Signal: the line between "marketing data infrastructure" and "enterprise data infrastructure" is dissolving, CDPs are positioning themselves as consumers of general-purpose AI/data platforms rather than standalone systems of record.
  • BlueConic acquires Blueshift: Combines a CDP with decision-intelligence/AI-orchestration tech to ship unified customer data + AI-driven engagement decisioning as one product. Signal: mid-market CDP consolidation is happening around "data plus the AI that acts on it", a CDP without a decisioning layer is becoming an incomplete pitch.
  • Viant acquires TVision (~$40M): Folds TVision's attention-measurement signals directly into Viant's programmatic buying platform ("Intelligence Layer"). Signal: attention data is graduating from a measurement add-on to a first-class bidding signal inside the DSP itself, another sign that pure measurement vendors are getting absorbed into buying platforms rather than surviving standalone.

#Category watch

Retail media networks: The IAB's Connected Commerce Summit this week released a blunt warning, "every network that doesn't make a deliberate strategic choice will have one made for them, by advertisers, by consolidation, or by quiet irrelevance" within 24 to 36 months. Spend remains heavily concentrated in Amazon and Walmart; smaller networks are struggling to pull national brand budgets beyond trade dollars. The "gold rush" phase (launch a network, any network) is closing; what follows is a smaller set of RMNs that can prove incremental measurement and access non-shopper ad budgets.

#Stack intelligence

Overall martech/adtech deal volume is down (Q1 2026 martech deals -14%, scaled deals -60%), but the deals still happening cluster tightly around three capabilities: incrementality measurement, identity resolution, and AI-driven performance optimization. Meanwhile AWS is moving up-stack into martech-native territory, adding a native data clean room and (soon) identity resolution service. Read together: the "buy" side of buy-vs-build is narrowing to specialist AI/data capability, while the commodity infrastructure underneath (clean rooms, identity graphs) is being absorbed by hyperscalers. For an org being built from scratch, this argues for renting the commodity infrastructure layer (increasingly a hyperscaler or CDP default) and reserving build/buy scrutiny for the differentiated decisioning layer on top.

#Foundations concept of the day

CDP vs. DMP: A DMP (Data Management Platform) was built for the third-party-cookie era, it ingests anonymous, cookie-based audience segments (often third-party) mainly to power ad targeting, and typically retains data for only 90 days since cookies expire. A CDP (Customer Data Platform) ingests known, first-party, PII-linked data (purchases, logins, loyalty, support tickets) from your own systems, persists it indefinitely, and is built to power the full customer relationship, service, product, and marketing, not just ad buys. The practical shift: as third-party cookies and cross-site tracking keep eroding, DMPs are becoming obsolete and CDPs are becoming the load-bearing data layer for anyone running an addressable business. Cross-industry example: a bank's DMP might once have bought lookalike audiences for a credit-card campaign; its CDP is what lets it recognize that a specific known customer just had a mortgage application declined and should get a different next-best-offer rather than a generic ad. An airline's loyalty database is CDP territory by definition, it is known, first-party, and permanent, which is why building the org around CDP-grade data discipline (not DMP-era audience buying habits) is the higher-leverage bet.

#Watch list

  • AWS's native clean room + upcoming identity resolution service: hyperscalers absorbing martech infrastructure primitives that used to require dedicated vendors (LiveRamp, InfoSum), worth tracking how fast this commoditizes the identity layer.
  • CDP + decisioning bundling (BlueConic/Blueshift, Zeta/Palantir): two different paths to the same endpoint, CDP vendors are racing to own the "what do we do with this data" layer, not just the storage layer. Whoever wins that layer captures the margin.

#The convergence watch (loyalty data → media networks)

The Trade Desk announced (June 17) an expanded commerce media ecosystem stitching together Kinective Media (United), MARRIOTT MEDIA, Booking Holdings brands (Booking.com, Agoda, KAYAK, Priceline), and Uber Advertising, letting advertisers activate travel/loyalty signals programmatically across the open internet, not just inside each brand's walled garden. Marriott's SVP of Media called it deliberate: "our owned channels give us a deep understanding of traveler intent... working with The Trade Desk allows us to activate those signals beyond our own ecosystem." This is the convergence thesis moving from proof-of-concept to infrastructure, travel loyalty data is now a first-class programmatic inventory source, not a bespoke experiment.

Underlying the deal: Kinective Media ran 130 campaigns for 100+ brands in its first 18 months (Norwegian Cruise Line, Macy's, Chase, TelevisaUnivision, IHG among them), and United's loyalty revenue grew 9% in FY2025 to a record $59.1B in total operating revenue. Loyalty-as-media is no longer a side bet, it's showing up in the top-line numbers that justify the next round of investment.

What it signals: the moat is shifting from "who has a media network" to "whose network is easiest to buy programmatically." Airlines and hotels sitting on first-party loyalty data but without a Trade Desk-caliber integration risk becoming second-tier inventory even if their audience quality is strong.

#Airline loyalty & digital

  • Aeroplan × Club Avolta (June 30): new North American partnership lets Aeroplan members earn points at 900+ Avolta airport/travel-retail locations by linking accounts. Signals coalition programs are still expanding earn surface area even as core award pricing tightens elsewhere.
  • Aeroplan award chart devaluation (effective June 1): partner premium-cabin awards up 20 to 67% in some transpacific/transatlantic bands (e.g., 87,500 → 102,500 points one-way on ANA/EVA/Singapore/Asiana routings); one economy transatlantic band actually dropped. Net: more increases than decreases, a real-money margin grab dressed as "award chart update."
  • Hawaiian Airlines joins oneworld: Atmos Rewards (Alaska/Hawaiian) members now earn/redeem and hold elite status across all 14 oneworld carriers. Extends oneworld's reciprocal-perks strategy (see also oneworld × Taj InnerCircle NeuPass) as a scale play against SkyTeam/Star Alliance without a merger.
  • WestJet / Porter: both cut loyalty fuel surcharges (WestJet $60→$40, Porter to $20) as fuel prices ease, a direct, visible lever airlines are pulling to improve perceived award value without touching base earn rates.

#Hotel & travel loyalty

  • World of Hyatt replaced its 3-tier award pricing (Off-Peak/Standard/Peak) with a 5-tier system effective May 20: 112 hotels moved to higher categories vs. 24 lower, with Standard awards devalued 17 to 38% and Peak up to 67% more expensive. Same devaluation pattern as Aeroplan this cycle, worth watching whether this is coordinated seasonal repricing across the industry or coincidence.
  • MARRIOTT MEDIA: now the anchor hospitality partner in The Trade Desk's travel network (see convergence section), leveraging 200+ targetable attributes across ~237M Bonvoy members and ~9,500 properties.

#MarTech

  • Braze Data Platform update (June 16): zero-copy sync with Databricks via Cloud Data Ingestion, plus Databricks' new CustomerLake (an agentic CDP embedded natively in Databricks) that pushes governed cohort audiences straight into Braze. Signals the CDP layer is migrating into the data warehouse itself rather than sitting as a separate SaaS tier, relevant for any team weighing a standalone CDP purchase.
  • Klaviyo launched a distinct CDP tier, formalizing its move beyond ecommerce-only positioning and into head-to-head competition with Braze/Iterable on enterprise engagement.
  • Salesforce (Connections 2026) and Adobe (CX Enterprise, rebranded from Experience Cloud) are both leaning hard into agentic campaign orchestration, Adobe's "CX Enterprise Coworker" coordinates multi-agent workflows (audience, creative, performance) against defined business goals rather than manual campaign building.

#AdTech & retail media

  • The Trade Desk's travel/hospitality network expansion (above) is this week's dominant AdTech story and is functionally the same story as the convergence item, worth reading together.
  • CTV continues to benefit from the cookie transition: U.S. CTV spend projected past $30B in 2026, with programmatic CTV buying growing faster than direct sales as advertisers lean on ACR data, IP household graphs, and Unified ID 2.0/RampID-style identity in place of cookies.

#Loyalty tech vendors

  • Comarch named a Representative Vendor in Gartner's 2026 Market Guide for Loyalty Program Vendors (May 27) and won Best Supplier/Vendor Innovation at the 2026 International Loyalty Awards, reinforcing its position as a credible buy-side option for multisolution loyalty builds.
  • Plusgrade/Points launched new Buy/Gift/Reinstate miles products for China Airlines' Dynasty Flyer, the ancillary-revenue-as-a-service model continues to expand program by program.
  • Budget signal: CMOs allocated just 4.3% of marketing budgets to loyalty program management in 2026, down 22% YoY. That's in tension with everything above, loyalty data is becoming more strategically valuable (as media inventory) even as direct loyalty-tech spend contracts. Read as pressure toward consolidating vendors or building in-house rather than expanding vendor rosters.

#Strategic implication

The Trade Desk's travel network expansion is the clearest signal yet that loyalty-data-as-media is graduating from bespoke, in-house ad platforms (Kinective, MARRIOTT MEDIA) to programmatically buyable inventory on the open web, which lowers the barrier for any carrier or hotel group to monetize first-party data without building a full ad-tech stack from scratch. At the same time, shrinking loyalty-tech budgets (4.3% of marketing spend, down 22% YoY) alongside two more devaluation cycles (Aeroplan, World of Hyatt) suggest the category is optimizing the loyalty *cost* side while the *monetization* side gets a new external distribution channel. The open question for any digital leader in this space: is your loyalty data pipeline clean and permissioned enough to plug into a Trade Desk-style integration today, or would you be building that foundational layer from a standing start while competitors are already running campaigns on it.
