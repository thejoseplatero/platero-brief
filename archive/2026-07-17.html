Executive Pulse394 words

#Macro signal

AI capex faces its first real credibility test: markets sold chips hard after Alphabet's Gemini 3.5 Pro slipped months behind and TSMC lifted capex guidance to $60-64B. The two stories together sharpen the board-level question of 2026: will the enormous AI buildout earn its return, or is the sector spending ahead of demand? For tech, product, and marketing leaders, the implication is to stop treating AI investment as a blank check and start demanding a return thesis on every major AI line item. The narrative has shifted from "spend to keep up" to "prove the payback."

#Tech industry

  • Google's compute shortage: the Gemini delay is being attributed to a lack of compute, not a lack of ideas. Strategic implication: even the most resourced companies are supply-constrained on AI infrastructure. If Google can be gated by compute, your organization's AI roadmap depends on capacity you may not control. Bake infrastructure risk into your planning, not just model capability.
  • TSMC's $100B Arizona expansion: profit up 77% and spending up sharply. Strategic implication: the AI supply chain is concentrating geographically and financially into a handful of chokepoints, which is now a geopolitical and continuity risk for any AI-dependent business.

#AI at the leadership level

The Gemini stumble is a governance lesson in disguise. Boards that let a function standardize on a single frontier model just watched roadmap risk become real. The mature posture is model-agnostic architecture at the org level: own your data and orchestration, treat any one model as replaceable. This is a CDO-level design principle now, not an engineering detail.

#Leadership & org design

The Fed's June minutes flagged that strong AI-related demand is itself one of the forces keeping inflation elevated, alongside tariffs and Middle East risk, with nine of eighteen FOMC members now expecting at least one hike this year. Read that as a signal to leaders: the AI investment cycle is large enough to move macro policy, which means the cost of capital funding your AI bets could rise even as you place them. Plan for AI initiatives to be financed in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

#Question worth asking your team this week

If our most important AI vendor slipped their roadmap by six months tomorrow, like Google just did, what breaks in our plan, and how fast could we swap them out?

Practitioner Pulse376 words

#Try this now

  • Plain-English workflow builders: Unit21 shipped an Agentic Task Builder that lets compliance teams describe an investigation check in natural language instead of hard-coding it. The reusable lesson for any digital team: the next automation win is not writing prompts, it is capturing a repeatable process once, in words, so an agent runs it every time. Pick one recurring 30-minute task this week (a QA pass, a brief review, a data pull) and write it as a step-by-step instruction an agent can execute.
  • Voice agents in minutes, not sprints: xAI's Voice Agent Builder claims a production-ready voice agent in under two minutes, no code. Worth 20 minutes to prototype a customer-service or internal-helpdesk flow and see where the quality actually lands before you scope a build.
  • Model-agnostic by default: with Gemini 3.5 Pro slipping months behind, the practical move is to route your AI workflows through a thin abstraction (an orchestration layer or router) rather than calling one vendor's API directly. It costs a day now and saves a rebuild when a model stalls or a better one ships.

#Case study worth stealing

A.S. Watson, AI skincare advisor: customers complete a short questionnaire and upload a selfie, and computer vision scores 14+ skin metrics to generate a personalized routine and product set. Shoppers who used the advisor converted 396% better than those who did not, spent 4x more, and lifted average order value 29%. The steal for any digital team: replace the generic product grid with a short diagnostic that makes the customer do a little work in exchange for a genuinely tailored result. The act of answering raises intent, and the personalization closes it. This pattern travels to travel, finance, and B2B, anywhere choice overload kills conversion.

#Framework or mental model

Own the data, rent the model: this week's Gemini delay is the clearest argument yet for a two-layer stack. Treat your first-party data and orchestration logic as owned, durable assets, and treat any frontier model as a hot-swappable commodity that will change vendors more than once. Where digital leaders apply it: every AI build decision becomes "does this deepen our data moat or does it just deepen our dependency on someone else's model?" Favor the former.

AI Stack Daily443 words

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Alphabet / Google Cloud: Gemini 3.5 Pro is months late and short of internal coding benchmarks, and the delay is being tied publicly to a compute shortage. Signal: the model layer is now gated by infrastructure, not talent. Enterprises standardizing their martech and data stack on a single hyperscaler's frontier model just learned that roadmap risk is real. Multi-model architecture is the defensive posture.
  • Salesforce: continuing to fold advertising and GTM automation into Data 360 (formerly Data Cloud), with real-time AI sales agents via the Qualified acquisition. Signal: the CDP is quietly becoming an agent runtime. The value is shifting from "where the data sits" to "what autonomously acts on it."
  • TSMC: raised 2026 capex to $60-64B and flagged another $100B for Arizona. Signal: the whole application stack, martech included, is downstream of one foundry's spending decisions. Compute scarcity will price into AI feature roadmaps for the next 18 months.

#Category watch

CDP as connective tissue: Segment, ActionIQ, and Treasure Data are increasingly positioned not as destinations but as the normalization layer that activates identity across both media (adtech) and sales (martech) environments. The category is collapsing the old martech/adtech/salestech split into one activation fabric. For anyone building a stack, this means the CDP decision is now the single highest-leverage architecture call you make.

#Stack intelligence

The pattern this week: buy the model, rent nothing you can't swap. Google's stumble is a live case for why frontier-model lock-in is a strategic liability. The durable enterprise design is a thin, model-agnostic orchestration layer over your own first-party data, with the LLM as a hot-swappable component. Own the data and the routing logic. Treat the model as a commodity input that will change vendors more than once.

#Foundations concept of the day

Clean rooms: a secure, neutral environment where two parties (say a retailer and a brand) match and analyze overlapping customer data without either side exposing raw, individual-level records. Each party uploads hashed identifiers, the room finds the overlap, and only aggregated results come out. It matters at scale because it is how first-party data gets activated across companies in a cookieless, privacy-regulated world. Example: a CPG brand and a grocery chain measure whether a campaign drove in-store purchases, without the grocer ever handing over its shopper list. This is the plumbing underneath every retail media network.

#Watch list

  • Anthropic and OpenAI: with Gemini stalled, watch whether enterprise coding and agent workloads consolidate toward them, and how fast Google responds.
  • Agentic GTM tooling: Qualified-style real-time sales agents inside the CRM are the leading edge of where martech automation goes next.

Markets Signal284 words

#Macro pulse

Risk-off tone led by chips. Canada: TSX slipped 0.30% to 35,310, dragged by resource and financial names as gold eased and Middle East tension simmered. USD/CAD held near recent levels (CAD around 70.7 US cents), no material move. USA: Nasdaq fell 1.47% to 25,882 and the S&P 500 lost 0.51% to 7,534 on a semiconductor slide, while the Dow held up, off just 0.20% to 52,553 as defensives and healthcare rotated in. Asia: split tape. Nikkei 225 dropped 2.79% to 66,836 on the chip rout, while the Hang Seng rose 1.3% to 25,009, extending gains on softer US inflation. Oil: WTI took a breather near $79-80 after an 11% weekly surge on Middle East risk, still a margin headwind for carriers.

#Today's industry move

Alphabet fell more than 4% after Bloomberg reported Gemini 3.5 Pro, its flagship model, is months behind schedule and short of internal coding targets. The miss handed momentum to OpenAI and Anthropic and rattled the AI trade broadly.

#AI infrastructure watch

TSMC guided 2026 capex up to $60-64B (from $52-56B) after Q2 profit jumped 77%, and signaled another $100B for Arizona. The read-through: even a spending increase now reads as bearish for chip equities when demand certainty wobbles. Separately, compute cost estimates are trending toward roughly $100B per gigawatt of AI capacity, the real ceiling on how fast frontier models can scale.

#Signal or noise?

Signal. The Gemini delay plus TSMC's capex jump crystallize the market's core 2026 anxiety: whether AI capex will earn its return, and whoever controls compute controls the model roadmap.

#Watch tomorrow

Whether the chip selloff bleeds into cloud and hyperscaler names, or stays contained to semis and Alphabet.
