#Macro signal

A central bank put a number on the AI buildout's debt and called its financing opaque. The Bank of England's November Financial Stability Report, published on 26 November, describes AI financing as increasingly "complex, circular and opaque" and says that makes stress in one part of the system more likely to spread. Coverage of the report cites the Bank's estimate that hyperscalers' total potential exposure is just over $4 trillion once debt, leases, purchase commitments and guarantees are counted. Hyperscaler bond issuance is set to exceed $305 billion in 2026, about 8 percent of global investment-grade issuance. The Bank also said equity valuations still look "stretched" after this month's falls and depend on AI delivering the expected profits, per Reuters. The $4 trillion figure comes from secondary coverage and should be checked against the report. For technology, product and marketing leaders the practical point is about suppliers. Model and cloud prices, capacity commitments and vendor roadmaps all rest on financing that a regulator now flags as a stability risk. Multi-year AI business cases should include a scenario in which a key supplier raises prices or cuts back because its own funding tightened. (Bank of England, US News / Reuters)

#Tech industry

  • Nvidia stopped server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries. The Financial Times reported on 26 November, citing three people familiar with the matter, that Nvidia told manufacturing partners including Dell and Supermicro not to ship servers containing its AI chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. A US rule that took effect on 19 November requires export licences for advanced chips to those countries and Brazil, and gives cloud providers 90 days to obtain one. A cloud executive told the FT: "No one knows how to apply for this licence while the 90-day clock is ticking." It is unclear whether the halt is temporary. Strategic implication: compute location has become a licensing question. Any company that runs AI workloads in Singapore or Malaysia, or buys from a provider that does, should ask that provider how its expansion plans and existing capacity are affected. (US News / Reuters, The National)
  • Google wrote the rules for outside AI agents in Workspace and set a date. From 1 February 2027, agents must reach Gmail, Drive, Calendar and the other Workspace products through Google's own connectors, carry their own identity, and stop for user confirmation before sending email or changing file sharing. Billing for agent usage begins on 1 April 2027. The terms also bar permanent storage of raw Workspace data and limit how long a provider may keep indexes built from it to 30 days. Strategic implication: the platform that holds a company's working documents has decided that agents are a separate kind of user, with separate permissions and a separate price. Companies should expect the same from every system of record they run, and should decide their own agent access policy before each vendor decides it for them. (Help Net Security)

#AI at the leadership level

AI is showing up in hiring plans far more often than in layoffs. Anthropic's economics team interviewed 66 people at 58 US companies about nearly 300 AI deployments. Two of the 58 reported layoffs tied directly to automation. Two-thirds said they are hiring fewer people because of AI, nine cut outside contractors, mostly offshore software development, customer support, design and legal work, and five dismissed employees who refused to use AI. Respondents said 55 percent of deployments raised output or quality and 19 percent had produced no business value so far. The obstacles were internal. Too much AI output to review drew 49 mentions, employee resistance 43, and required expert review and testing 42. Regulation was mentioned four times. One employee told the researchers: "If I'm open to this, I'm essentially building my own coffin." The sample is Anthropic's own customers and the value figures are self-reported, so the direction matters more than the percentages. For boards, the finding moves the workforce question from severance to pipeline. A company that stops hiring at the entry level has made a decision about who its experienced reviewers will be in five years, and review capacity is the constraint the same study ranks first. (The Decoder, Anthropic)

#Leadership & org design

SAP is reported to be planning AI-led cuts of at least 10,000 roles, department by department. Bloomberg reported on 26 November that SAP is weighing a reduction of about 9 to 10 percent of a workforce of more than 110,000. Every department has been asked to identify where cuts could fall, with product, engineering and sales under the closest review. Sales chief Thomas Saueressig is leaving and two more executive board members will go by March. SAP has not confirmed the figure and said it is "continuously reviewing how our organization and workforce need to evolve." One report says German works councils hold an employment guarantee through 2028, and the final number could change after talks with employee representatives. The method is the part worth studying. Asking each department for a number produces cuts shaped like the current org chart. The Anthropic study above found the opposite pattern in the deployments rated most valuable: roles were widened so that one person or a small team owned a whole workflow. A reduction planned by department removes people from each step and leaves the handoffs between steps in place. (CNBC, Quartz)

#Question worth asking your team this week

Which roles did we decide not to fill this year because of AI, who made each of those decisions, and who will be qualified to review that work in 2030?

#Try this now

  • List every agent that touches Google Workspace before 1 February: Google's new Workspace AI Tools Addendum requires AI agents to reach Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Calendar, Chat and Tasks through Google's own MCP servers or its command-line tool. MCP, the Model Context Protocol, is the standard that lets an assistant call an outside tool. Technical enforcement starts on 1 February 2027, and ordinary applications can keep using the existing REST APIs. External agents must use their own OAuth client IDs so administrators can tell them apart from apps, and the supported routes force a user confirmation before an agent sends an email, changes Drive sharing or makes bulk edits. Quota billing starts on 1 April 2027. How to use it: pull the connected-apps list from the Workspace admin console, mark each entry as an app or an agent, and ask the vendor of each agent for its migration date. Worth 20 minutes because any inbox triage tool, meeting assistant or sales agent that reads Workspace through the old route has a hard stop date, and most teams have never counted how many they run. (Help Net Security, Google Cloud)
  • Give AI-referred visits their own row in the Cyber Week report: Adobe Analytics says visits to US retail sites from AI assistants are up 127 percent year on year so far this season, and it projected increases of 159 percent on Thanksgiving, 95 percent on Black Friday and 88 percent on Cyber Monday. In September those visits converted 90 percent better than other traffic and produced 83 percent more revenue per visit. How to use it: build a segment from the referrers of ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity and Copilot, then report sessions, conversion rate and revenue per visit beside paid search and email for Thursday through Monday. Worth 20 minutes because the 2027 planning conversation will ask how big this channel was, and the answer is cheapest to capture this week. Many assistant visits arrive with no referrer and land in direct, so treat the segment as a floor. (Digital Commerce 360, Adobe)
  • Swap one urgency device for one piece of proof before Cyber Monday: Lea Karam, founder of the research firm Mindscope, argues in ExchangeWire today that countdown clocks and flash deals cut short a pause shoppers take on purpose. Her firm's study with TikTok surveyed more than 3,000 consumers and analysed more than 420 video diaries. It found 62 percent wait for a discount before buying and 71 percent read reviews often or always. YouGov found 47 percent of likely Black Friday shoppers say that knowing whether a discount is genuine is among the hardest parts of the sales period. How to use it: pick the highest-traffic product page, remove the timer, and put the 30-day price history, the returns terms and the three most recent verified reviews in the same position. Compare add-to-cart and return rates against last week. The article is a contributed opinion piece, and the survey methods are not published in it. (ExchangeWire)

#Case study worth stealing

Therabody, a shopping assistant limited to claims the company can back: Therabody sells massage and recovery devices, so a wrong answer about pain is a legal and regulatory problem. Its assistant, called Jason, launched in October on AWS's Agentic Shopping Assistant after a build-versus-buy review, and took under 90 days to deploy. The team chose the build route because it allowed custom guardrails on what the assistant may say, which packaged tools did not offer. The assistant reads the existing Shopify product pages, drops out-of-stock items, starts checkout in the chat and hands payment to Shopify without ever collecting card details. Caitlin Berzok, SVP of digital: "when you're giving someone a recommendation on pain, you have to be right." No results are published yet. The company will judge it on whether holiday shoppers found the right product and bought it. What to adapt: write the list of things the assistant is forbidden to say before choosing a vendor, use that list as the selection test, and keep payment in the system that already handles it. (CIO Dive)

#Framework or mental model

Three ways to reshape work around AI: Anthropic's economics team interviewed 66 people at 58 US companies and catalogued nearly 300 AI deployments. Respondents rated a deployment as more valuable the more the organisation changed around it, and the report names three changes. The first widens roles so one person or a small team owns a whole workflow, which removes handoffs. The second moves people toward the work AI does poorly: strategic decisions, customer relationships and quality review. The third builds shared, secure data and infrastructure so non-technical staff start from clean data. The most frequently named obstacle was volume, with 49 mentions of more AI output than people could review. Digital leaders can use the three as a test for any stalled pilot: name which of the three changes was made, and if the answer is none, expect the result the study found for presentations and quick data queries, which was low value. All companies in the sample are Anthropic customers and the value ratings are self-reported. (The Decoder, Anthropic)

#Rabbit hole (optional)

Working with AI, the full report: The interview material is the reason to read past the summary. One company used AI to speed up application reviews so it could skip 90 percent of its planned hires, and some reviewers still spent over ten minutes on cases that should take two. An accountant built an app to automate an 80-hour audit, and months later it was still waiting for an outside contractor to build the production version. Several firms concluded that off-the-shelf software at $30 to $100 a month was less work to maintain than their own dashboards. One executive on brittle internal tools: "It's almost like we're trying to throw darts at a spinning dart board." (Anthropic, The Decoder)

#Vendor & platform moves

  • Google set a single entry point for agents in Workspace, with a meter on it: Under a new Workspace AI Tools Addendum, AI agents must reach Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Calendar, Chat and Tasks through Google's MCP servers or its command-line tool. MCP, the Model Context Protocol, is the standard that lets an assistant call an outside tool. Enforcement starts on 1 February 2027 and quota billing on 1 April 2027. Ordinary applications keep the REST APIs. Agent requests are checked against the organisation's permissions, data loss prevention policies and classification labels, and external agents need their own OAuth client IDs. Chandu Thota of Google Workspace and Brad Calder of Google Cloud wrote: "Policy checks, data boundaries, and human confirmation have to live in the request path." Secondary reports put the standard tier at 5,000 requests per day, per project, per product. Help Net Security does not give quota figures. Signal: a platform now treats agent traffic as a separate class of access with its own identity, rules and price. Every sales, service and marketing tool that reads a customer's inbox or calendar becomes a metered tenant on Google's terms. Expect the other suites that hold work data to copy the structure. (Help Net Security, Google Cloud)
  • OpenAI began piloting Private Inference with its first enterprise customers: The Decoder reported on 25 November that the pilot lets a company run queries on OpenAI's most capable models without OpenAI being able to read the data. Queries run on isolated hardware whose security can be independently verified, according to OpenAI. A related system, Private Safety Processing, scans for abuse without OpenAI storing customer content. Flagged material is encrypted and kept in the customer's own cloud. Sam Altman announced the pilot on X. Pricing, supported models and a general release date are not stated. Signal: the argument for keeping customer data away from a model vendor has rested on the vendor being able to read it. If the vendor can show it cannot, the build-versus-rent decision for regulated data, loyalty records and payment-adjacent data moves toward renting, and attestation reports become a document procurement asks for. (The Decoder)
  • Criteo's sale process has three known bidders and a year-end window: ExchangeWire's 27 November podcast reviewed the state of play. Reuters reported on 17 November that AppLovin is among the firms in talks to buy Criteo, joining Vista Equity Partners and Quinti Capital, which bid jointly in July. Criteo's market value is about $1 billion after its share price more than halved in five years, against roughly $136 billion for AppLovin. A deal could be announced before the end of the year and none is guaranteed. Signal: Criteo runs the ad technology behind many retailers' media networks. A buyer from mobile gaming advertising would be paying for retailer relationships and purchase data it does not have. Retailers that outsourced their ad stack should check what their contract says about a change of control. (ExchangeWire, Reuters via Investing.com)

#Category watch

AI-referred commerce traffic: This is the first holiday season in which assistant referrals are large enough to plan around. Adobe Analytics counts visits from AI assistants to US retail sites up 127 percent year on year so far this season, with a projected 159 percent rise on Thanksgiving. In September those visits converted 90 percent better than other traffic and earned 83 percent more revenue per visit. In July the conversion advantage was 60 percent. Barclays found 55 percent of Black Friday shoppers plan to use AI to find deals, and 67 percent of Gen Z. YouGov found almost half of AI shoppers worry that the recommendations are biased or sponsored. Therabody's SVP of digital told CIO Dive that traffic from ChatGPT "skyrocketed" last holiday season, which is what led the company to build its own assistant. For someone building a martech org, the gap is ownership. The visits arrive from a source that analytics tools label inconsistently, they convert like branded search, and the trust concern grows as assistants add advertising. Someone needs to own the measurement before the budget arrives. (Digital Commerce 360, ExchangeWire, CIO Dive)

#Stack intelligence

Data derived from a platform now carries a retention limit. Google's Workspace AI Tools Addendum bars providers from permanently storing raw Workspace data, from training models on it, and from unauthorised scraping. It also sets a 30-day retention limit on what it calls Google Technical Information: vector embeddings, memory indexes, user profiles and organisational graphs built from Workspace content. When a customer disconnects the integration or revokes access, the provider must delete the user data and the derived material. This reaches the part of an AI product that vendors usually describe as their own. A sales assistant's memory of an account, a meeting tool's index of past calls and a support tool's map of who works with whom are all derived data under these terms. The architectural consequence is that such stores have to be rebuildable from source on a rolling basis, and a vendor's claim that its product "learns your business over time" needs a second look where the learning sits on Workspace data. Two questions belong in the next vendor review: which of the product's stored indexes come from a third-party platform, and what happens to the product's quality when those are rebuilt every 30 days. (Help Net Security)

#Foundations concept of the day

Confidential computing: Data is normally protected in two states. It is encrypted when stored and encrypted when it travels. To be processed it has to be decrypted in a computer's memory, and at that moment the company that runs the computer can, in principle, read it. Confidential computing closes that third gap. The processing happens inside a sealed area of the chip called a trusted execution environment, or enclave, which keeps the data encrypted to everything outside it, including the operating system and the cloud provider's own staff. The second half of the idea is attestation: before sending anything, the customer receives a signed statement from the hardware saying exactly which code is running inside the enclave, and can refuse to proceed if it does not match what was promised. It matters at scale because it changes who has to be trusted. A contract says a vendor will not look at the data. An enclave with attestation is evidence that it cannot. Two examples from different industries: data clean rooms use enclaves so that a retailer and a consumer goods brand can match customer lists without either side or the clean room operator seeing the other's records, and banks use them to pool transaction data for fraud detection. OpenAI's Private Inference pilot, reported this week, applies the same approach to queries sent to a large AI model. The limits are worth knowing. Enclaves add cost and some loss of speed, attestation only proves which code is running and says nothing about whether that code is well written, and researchers have broken specific enclave designs in the past. (The Decoder)

#Watch list

  • Walmart's ad growth rate: Walmart's global ad sales grew 25 percent year on year in the third quarter, the slowest in two years per ExchangeWire, while the US ad business grew 37 percent and Walmart Connect 36 percent excluding Vizio and Vibe.co. The largest retail media network after Amazon is still growing fast, and the slope has turned. Watch whether fourth-quarter results show the same pattern at smaller networks, which have less traffic to sell. (ExchangeWire)
  • Marketing agents consolidating on one cloud in China: Tec-Do, which sells a marketing agent called Navos Pro to companies advertising abroad, signed a partnership with Tencent Cloud on 26 November covering models, compute and Tencent's WorkBuddy agent ecosystem. Tec-Do says its Tec-Chi models already train and run entirely on Tencent Cloud, and names Google, Meta, TikTok and OpenAI as media partners. The figures are the company's own. It is an early example of a marketing agent vendor tying its models to a single cloud, and a reminder that cross-border advertisers from China are building a parallel agent stack. (GlobeNewswire)

The loonie traded at about 70.2 US cents against 70.33 on Wednesday, a move of roughly 0.2 percent and under the 0.5 percent threshold. Crude and gold had no settlement price on Thursday because of the US holiday. (BNN Bloomberg, MoneySense)

Futures pointed to a strong open, with Nasdaq 100 contracts up about 2.5 percent before dawn, and the gains shrank in the first half hour. #Signal or noise?

Friday's bounce is noise on holiday volume, and the signal sits underneath it: in one week a regulator priced the AI buildout's debt at $4 trillion and an export rule froze shipments to the region where a large share of that capacity was being built.
